# Nicaragua tax system

The Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) administers income tax (impuesto sobre la renta, IR) and value added tax (impuesto al valor agregado, IVA). Amounts are in Nicaraguan córdobas (NIO, shown as C$). [Law 822, arts. 1 and 3](https://www.ine.gob.ni/DGE/digesto/leyes/Ley822.pdf)

## Income tax

- **Residence and source.** A national or foreign individual arriving from abroad meets the presence test after more than 180 days in Nicaragua in the calendar year. The days can be intermittent. Locating the main economic interests in Nicaragua can also establish residence, subject to the statutory rules for proving foreign tax residence. IR covers Nicaraguan-source income of residents and non-residents. The source rules can capture some work performed abroad, including work paid by a Nicaraguan resident or local permanent establishment. [Law 822, arts. 3 and 5](https://www.ine.gob.ni/DGE/digesto/leyes/Ley822.pdf) · [Law 891, amended arts. 7 and 12](https://www.mem.gob.ni/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/2014-12-18-Ley-No-891-Ley-Reforma-y-Adiciones-Ley-No-822-Concertacion-Tributaria.pdf)
- **Employment.** The annual resident scale exempts net employment income through C$100,000. The marginal rates above that band are 15% through C$200,000, 20% through C$350,000, 25% through C$500,000 and 30% above C$500,000. Employers withhold IR. An employee with one employer generally has no personal return for that income, subject to specified deductions and other taxable income. An employee with 2 or more employers generally files if combined employment income exceeds the exemption. [Law 822, arts. 19 and 22–29](https://www.ine.gob.ni/DGE/digesto/leyes/Ley822.pdf) · [Law 891, art. 5](https://www.mem.gob.ni/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/2014-12-18-Ley-No-891-Ley-Reforma-y-Adiciones-Ley-No-822-Concertacion-Tributaria.pdf)
- **Business income.** The ordinary IR rate is 30% of net taxable income from economic activities. Individuals and entities with annual gross income of C$12 million or less apply a separate schedule to net income. Its rates are 10% through C$100,000, 15% through C$200,000, 20% through C$350,000, 25% through C$500,000 and 30% above C$500,000. The rate for the applicable band applies to the whole net amount. Where the definitive minimum applies, compare the ordinary IR with the minimum and use the higher amount. The minimum uses gross taxable income at 3% for large taxpayers, 2% for principal taxpayers and 1% for other taxpayers. The law specifies a 2% rate for qualifying fishing activity on the Caribbean coast and provides exceptions to the minimum. [Law 822, arts. 52 and 55](https://www.ine.gob.ni/DGE/digesto/leyes/Ley822.pdf) · [Law 891, art. 5](https://www.mem.gob.ni/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/2014-12-18-Ley-No-891-Ley-Reforma-y-Adiciones-Ley-No-822-Concertacion-Tributaria.pdf) · [Law 987, amended arts. 59 and 61–63](https://www.mem.gob.ni/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/2019.02.28-Ley-987-Reforma-a-Ley-822-G-41.pdf)

## IVA and filing

- **IVA.** The standard rate is 15%, with zero rating for qualifying exports. File the ordinary monthly declaration by the fifth calendar day of the following month. Amended Article 139(1) sets the fifth calendar day as the ordinary payment deadline. The regulation sets the same date for principal and large taxpayers and lists the 15th for other taxpayers. Confirm the applicable payment date with the DGI before relying on the later date. Large taxpayers also make a separate advance payment. Law 1279 changed certain exemptions and consumption-tax provisions in April 2026. [Law 822, arts. 109 and 137–139](https://www.ine.gob.ni/DGE/digesto/leyes/Ley822.pdf) · [Law 987, amended art. 139(1)](https://www.mem.gob.ni/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/2019.02.28-Ley-987-Reforma-a-Ley-822-G-41.pdf) · [Decree 08-2019, amended regulation arts. 97–98](https://www.mem.gob.ni/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/2019.03.15-Decreto-08-2019-Reforma-y-Adiciones-al-Reglamento-de-la-Ley-822.pdf) · [DGI fiscal calendar](https://www.dgi.gob.ni/) · [Law 1279](https://www.spiex.gob.ni/media/4mpnovbp/2026-04-09-ley-1279-ley-de-reformas-y-derogacio-n-a-la-ley-822.pdf)
- **Annual business filing.** The ordinary year ends on 31 December. The DGI can authorise special periods of no more than 12 months. File and pay annual business IR by the last calendar day of the second month after the fiscal period ends, subject to any applicable deadline adjustment. This generally applies even if the return shows no tax payable. Simplified regimes have separate rules. [Law 822, art. 50](https://www.ine.gob.ni/DGE/digesto/leyes/Ley822.pdf) · [Law 987, amended art. 69(1)](https://www.mem.gob.ni/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/2019.02.28-Ley-987-Reforma-a-Ley-822-G-41.pdf)
