# Nicaragua Company Formation & Entity Choice

## Entity types and incorporation

- **Overview of business vehicles in Nicaragua** — The most common vehicle for business in Nicaragua is the Sociedad Anónima (S.A.), governed by the Commercial Code; companies register with the Registro Público Mercantil (commercial registry) and the DGI.

> **The Commercial Code is published and was read.** The **Digesto Jurídico
> Nicaragüense**, the National Assembly's official consolidated legal database,
> records the *Código de Comercio de Nicaragua* as **Vigente** — in force —
> published 20 October 1916, materia *Empresa, Industria y Comercio*. Its text is
> served from the Digesto's documentary collection. The entity and incorporation
> rules in §1 below are read from it; the fees, timeline and tax-compliance rows
> stay on the commercial source they had.
>
> **It is 1916 law and it reads like it.** Later investment, banking and sector
> legislation may qualify what follows — particularly the founder-eligibility
> ground and the capital rules — and a reviewer should check for that. The Digesto
> records the Code as current; it does not list every statute that touches it.

## 1. What the Código de Comercio requires

- **A sociedad anónima may be formed by two or more persons** — *"La sociedad
  anónima puede constituirse por **dos o más personas** que suscriban la escritura
  social que contenga todos los requisitos necesarios para su validez, según el
  artículo 124."* The previous version's "minimum 2 shareholders" is confirmed, and
  now cites the Code rather than a summary of it  _(Código de Comercio de Nicaragua, art. 202 — https://digesto.asamblea.gob.ni/consultas/normas/shownorms.php?idnorm=MjkyOTI=)_
- **Capital does have to be subscribed and partly paid — before the company may
  operate.** Article 206: *"Ninguna compañía anónima podrá comenzar sus operaciones
  mientras no tuviere suscrita siquiera **la mitad del capital social**, y en dinero
  efectivo, el **10%** del capital que consista en numerario."* **At least half the
  share capital subscribed, and 10% of the numerary capital in cash.** The previous
  version said capital "need not be deposited at incorporation" — the Code does not
  gate *incorporation* on it, but it gates **commencing operations**, which is the
  thing a founder actually cares about  _(art. 206 — https://digesto.asamblea.gob.ni/consultas/normas/shownorms.php?idnorm=MjkyOTI=)_
- **There is no legal personality until registration, and both documents must be
  published** — art. 204: the company *"no podrá gozar de personalidad jurídica"*
  while the escritura social and the Estatutos are not registered in the
  corresponding **Registro Mercantil**, and both are then published in the newspaper
  of the departmental capital or, failing that, in the official gazette. The
  publication step was absent from the step list below  _(art. 204 — https://digesto.asamblea.gob.ni/consultas/normas/shownorms.php?idnorm=MjkyOTI=)_
- **A judge may refuse to register, on five stated grounds** — art. 205 lists them,
  and the first bears directly on foreign founders: registration is refused *"si los
  fundadores no fueren de conducta notoriamente honrada, **naturales del país o
  extranjeros domiciliados en el mismo**"*. The others are an escritura not drawn to
  art. 124, provisions contrary to the Constitution, the laws, morality or public
  order, Estatutos not approved as the escritura provided or substantially
  contradicting it, and Estatutos that fail to give shareholders a regime of good
  administration, the means to oversee managers and the right to know how company
  funds are used  _(art. 205 — https://digesto.asamblea.gob.ni/consultas/normas/shownorms.php?idnorm=MjkyOTI=)_
- **So "100% foreign ownership" needs a qualification at the founding stage** — the
  Code makes a founder who is neither a national nor a **domiciled** foreigner a
  ground for refusing registration. That is about **founders**, not later
  shareholders, and it is 1916 law that later investment legislation may have
  displaced. It is flagged rather than resolved  _(art. 205, ground 1 — https://digesto.asamblea.gob.ni/consultas/normas/shownorms.php?idnorm=MjkyOTI=)_
- **The Estatutos have prescribed content** — art. 203 requires them to detail the
  powers of the Junta Directiva, the Junta de Vigilancia and ordinary and
  extraordinary general meetings; to establish a regime of good administration and
  of oversight of the managers; the shareholders' right to know the use of company
  funds; and the number of members and share of capital that must attend meetings
  reducing or increasing capital, or dealing with dissolution or modification  _(art. 203 — https://digesto.asamblea.gob.ni/consultas/normas/shownorms.php?idnorm=MjkyOTI=)_
- **A sociedad anónima is defined by art. 201** — a legal person formed by the
  gathering of a common fund supplied by shareholders liable only up to the amount
  of their shares, administered by **revocable** agents, and known by the
  designation of the company's object  _(art. 201 — https://digesto.asamblea.gob.ni/consultas/normas/shownorms.php?idnorm=MjkyOTI=)_
- **Branches register separately** — art. 207 requires any branch the company
  establishes to be registered in the **Registro de Comercio of the respective
  department**, presenting the registered social contract and the branch manager's
  appointment  _(art. 207 — https://digesto.asamblea.gob.ni/consultas/normas/shownorms.php?idnorm=MjkyOTI=)_

## 2. Steps and figures still on a commercial source
- **Sociedad Anónima (S.A.)** — Corporation with limited liability; the most common entity, requiring at least two shareholders  _(Código de Comercio de Nicaragua (https://www.bizlatinhub.com/business-structures-types-nicaragua/))_
- **Other entity types** — Sociedad Colectiva (general partnership), Sociedad en Comandita (limited partnership), branch of a foreign company, and sole trader  _(Código de Comercio de Nicaragua (https://www.bizlatinhub.com/business-structures-types-nicaragua/))_
- **Minimum shareholders (S.A.)** — 2  _(Código de Comercio de Nicaragua, art. 202 — https://digesto.asamblea.gob.ni/consultas/normas/shownorms.php?idnorm=MjkyOTI=)_
- **Minimum share capital (S.A.)** — Approx. C$10,000 (about USD 400) is commonly used; the Code sets no minimum *amount* NIO ((approx — confirm))  _(Código de Comercio de Nicaragua (https://www.bizlatinhub.com/company-formation-incorporation-in-nicaragua-a-six-step-guide/))_
- **But "no minimum" is not "nothing to pay"** — whatever figure is chosen, art. 206 requires **half of it subscribed and 10% of the numerary part in cash** before the company may begin operating. See §1  _(Código de Comercio de Nicaragua, art. 206 — https://digesto.asamblea.gob.ni/consultas/normas/shownorms.php?idnorm=MjkyOTI=)_
- **Step 1 — Deed of incorporation** — Draft and notarise the act/deed of incorporation and bylaws (escritura de constitución) before a Nicaraguan notary  _(Código de Comercio de Nicaragua (https://www.bizlatinhub.com/company-formation-incorporation-in-nicaragua-a-six-step-guide/))_
- **Step 2 — Corporate and accounting books** — Acquire and present the mandatory corporate and accounting books for registration  _(Código de Comercio de Nicaragua (https://www.bizlatinhub.com/company-formation-incorporation-in-nicaragua-a-six-step-guide/))_
- **Step 3 — Commercial registry** — Register the company and its books with the Registro Público Mercantil (commonly via the Ventanilla Única de Inversiones, VUI)  _(Código de Comercio de Nicaragua (https://www.bizlatinhub.com/company-formation-incorporation-in-nicaragua-a-six-step-guide/))_
- **Step 4 — Tax registration** — Register with the DGI to obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) and a municipal licence  _(Ley de Concertación Tributaria (Law 822) (https://www.bizlatinhub.com/company-formation-incorporation-in-nicaragua-a-six-step-guide/))_
- **Commercial registry fee** — 1% of registered capital, capped at approx. C$30,000 (about USD 1,200) NIO ((approx — confirm)) NIO (approx — confirm)  _(Arancel del Registro Público (https://www.bizlatinhub.com/company-formation-incorporation-in-nicaragua-a-six-step-guide/))_
- **Incorporation timeline** — Approximately 4–6 weeks ((approx — confirm))  _(Código de Comercio de Nicaragua (https://www.bizlatinhub.com/company-formation-incorporation-in-nicaragua-a-six-step-guide/))_
- **Foreign ownership** — 100% foreign ownership of a Nicaraguan S.A. is permitted; a local legal representative is required. **Read this against art. 205 in §1**: the Code makes a founder who is neither a national nor a foreigner *domiciled* in Nicaragua a ground for the judge to refuse registration. Ownership after formation and eligibility to be a founder are different questions, and the second is unresolved here  _(Código de Comercio de Nicaragua (https://www.bizlatinhub.com/company-formation-incorporation-in-nicaragua-a-six-step-guide/))_
- **Core annual compliance** — Annual IR return, monthly IVA and IR-withholding returns, monthly INSS/INATEC filings, municipal sales tax (1% monthly) and the annual municipal registration tax (2%)  _(Ley de Concertación Tributaria (Law 822) / Plan de Arbitrios Municipal (https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nicaragua/corporate/other-taxes))_
