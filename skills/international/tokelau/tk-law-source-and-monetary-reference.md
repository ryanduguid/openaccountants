# Tokelau: law sources and monetary conventions reference v0.3

**Tier 2 source-cited draft. Actual financial delivery is held.** This reference records selected statutory provisions and historical monetary illustrations. It is not a complete country companion under the universal workflow base. It remains outside the production corpus pending source and content review. No current tax, penalty, filing or professional conclusion follows from its examples.

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

A qualified practitioner must establish the requested-period law, the underlying obligation and all facts before using a provision for an actual position. The latest reading date does not make a historical consolidation current.

## Scope

Use this reference to assemble a Tokelau legal-source record and identify the conditions that must be resolved before a tax or compliance workflow can start. S1 is the official Tokelau Act 1948, shown as the latest version as at 28 October 2021. S2 is the official Legislation Act 2019 as at 2 November 2026. S3 is the official Income Tax Act 2007 Part Y as at 4 September 2026. S4 is a retained archived original of the Interpretation Rules 2003 from the consolidation as at 1 May 2016. The selected reading extent is stated in Sources.

The current General Fono amendment chain, substantive charging provisions, tax residence, net income, tax calculation method, operational forms and professional review remain open. No domain is established as not applicable. Incorporation, reporting, payroll, contributions, cross-border, property and every other applicable topic need their own qualified content.

## Ask the client first

Read redacted records and ask only for missing facts. Share at most 10 factual questions across the loaded bases. These 5 topics are a source-review intake, not a complete engagement interview. Keep identifiers, credentials, identity documents and bank details with the reviewer.

1. Requested obligation, period, place of activity and the person's legal form, using stable redacted references.
2. Complete underlying Rule, regulation or Act and each amendment or disallowance affecting that period, including commencement evidence.
3. Source of the text, its certified reprint status and date, its language of enactment and any substantive language disagreement.
4. For a monetary provision, its denomination, original amount, the precise conversion or charging provision and evidence for every temporal predicate.
5. Reviewer, missing evidence and the intended output. A client answer cannot establish unknown law or waive review.

## The method, step by step

1. Record the requested obligation and period. Keep a legal-source ledger separate from factual readiness. If the law is unresolved, return Needs Input for that issue and do not calculate an actual liability.
2. Apply the reception and hierarchy checks below to each proposed source. Record the complete relied provision, exceptions, amendments, commencement and any disallowance. A search result or an absent online copy cannot prove that a law is absent or remains in force.
3. Verify the consolidation date and reprint evidence. Retain the language issue separately. Resolve the complete operative chain for the requested period before using the historical provisions.
4. Read the specific country's charging and procedural content. This reference and the universal base do not supply an income-tax, contributions, formation or other domain companion.
5. Restrict the worked cases below to their fictional historical assumptions. For an actual engagement, complete the base's profile confirmation, evidence classifications, complete domain companion, working paper, reviewer brief, action list and review checklist, then obtain professional review. None of those engagement deliverables is completed here.

### New Zealand territorial language and residence [S2](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2019/58/en/latest/), [S3](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/97/en/latest/parts/DLM1520573/)

Legislation Act section 9 applies Part 2 to legislation forming part of New Zealand law unless the legislation provides otherwise or its context requires a different interpretation, and to the Act itself. Section 10 requires text, purpose and context, including the specified indications in the text. Subject to that application rule, section 13's territorial definition means the islands and territories within the Realm of New Zealand, excluding Tokelau, the Cook Islands, Niue and the Ross Dependency. This is a New Zealand interpretation provision; its quotation does not establish its extension as local law in Tokelau.

Income Tax Act YA 1 begins with its own context qualification. Its New Zealand definition is inclusive. Paragraph (a) adds the continental shelf. Paragraph (b) adds the water and airspace above a part of the continental shelf beyond New Zealand's territorial sea, if and to the extent that exploration or exploitation of that part or its resources is or may be undertaken, and that exploration, exploitation or a related matter involves or would involve activity on, in or in relation to that water or airspace. Those 2 conditions belong to paragraph (b); they do not qualify paragraph (a). That inclusive wording alone does not list every included or excluded place. Its New Zealand resident definition points to EY 49 and YD 1-YD 3B, with MA 8's special path for subparts MA-MG and MZ concerning family tax credits. The separately defined Tokelau International Trust Fund identifies the trust under the specified 10 November 2004 Deed; that definition alone establishes neither a general taxpayer exemption nor Tokelau's own tax rules.

## Boundary and exception table

| Proposed shortcut | Required treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Treat Tokelau's status as part of New Zealand as enough to use New Zealand rates | Establish the particular Act's territorial scope, extension, context, amendments and local modifications first. |
| Treat a missing online rule as no tax or no obligation | Keep the source gap open and use the statutory source-access and certification routes. Availability failure does not itself invalidate a rule. |
| Treat the inclusive YA 1 definition as a residence determination | Apply the separate residence provisions and their full conditions. No taxpayer residence is decided here. |
| Replace a printed income-tax boundary or choose a marginal method | Original tax text, amendments and the calculation method remain held. The 2016 republication is not enough for an actual calculation. |
| Convert every historical monetary penalty simply by dividing by $50 | Check rule 13(3)'s special pre-commencement, below-$50 case and the complete underlying law first. |
| Treat a reprint or a current reading date as current consolidated law | Establish certification, the source date and the complete later chain. The English-only consolidation does not resolve the enacted-language question. |

## Worked cases

These are fictional readings of S4's historical rule 13. They are not tax computations, assessments or amounts to pay. Assume solely for illustration that the underlying provision is validly within rule 13, the source text applies to the hypothetical period, the denomination is New Zealand dollars and no other law changes the result. Actual offences, commencement, currency handling and later law remain unverified. These 2 cases do not supply the universal base's complete engagement companion or its required full fictional positions.

### F1. Express a fictional same-value penalty in units

A hypothetical monetary penalty is NZD 250. It is outside rule 13(3)'s below-NZD-50 case. Under the stated historical assumptions, rule 13(1)-(2) gives 250 / 50 = 5 units and 5 × 50 = NZD 250. The conversion preserves value; it does not establish an offence or an actual penalty.

```oa-check
name: F1 historical same-value unit illustration
inputs:
  fictional_amount: 250
  historical_unit_value: 50
steps:
  - units: fictional_amount / historical_unit_value
  - reconstructed_value: units * historical_unit_value
expected:
  units: 5
  reconstructed_value: 250
tolerance: 0
```

### F2. Choose the special historical conversion only when its predicate is given

A second fictional enactment is assumed to have been in force immediately before rule 13 commenced and to refer to a NZD 40 penalty. Simple same-value division would give 0.8 units, equivalent to NZD 40. Because the stipulated case satisfies rule 13(3)'s temporal condition and is strictly below NZD 50, that special text instead gives 0.5 units, equivalent to NZD 25 under rule 13(2). The numerical difference is NZD 15. If the temporal or source conditions are unknown, neither path is an accepted actual result; return Needs Input. The example does not authorise reducing a real fine.

```oa-check
name: F2 historical special pre-commencement conversion decision
inputs:
  fictional_old_amount: 40
  historical_unit_value: 50
  historical_special_units: 0.5
steps:
  - same_value_units: fictional_old_amount / historical_unit_value
  - same_value_amount: same_value_units * historical_unit_value
  - special_amount: historical_special_units * historical_unit_value
  - illustrative_difference: same_value_amount - special_amount
expected:
  same_value_units: 0.8
  same_value_amount: 40
  special_amount: 25
  illustrative_difference: 15
tolerance: 0
```
