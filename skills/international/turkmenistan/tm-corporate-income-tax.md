# Turkmenistan Corporate Income Tax

## Corporate profits tax rates and base

> **The Tax Code has been read, and the guide was missing its highest rate.** The Tax
> Directorate of the Ministry of Finance and Economy publishes the
> ***«Salgytlar hakynda» Türkmenistanyň bitewi Kanuny*** (Unified Law of Turkmenistan
> "On Taxes") at [tax.gov.tm](https://tax.gov.tm/hukuknamalar) — 125 pages with a clean
> text layer. Every row below was previously sourced to one commercial site.
>
> **Article 172 sets three profit-tax rates, not the two this guide described, and the
> top one is 50%.** The 8%, 20%, 15% and 6% figures the guide carried are confirmed;
> what was absent is the 50% band and the conditions attaching to all of them. Each
> figure below was checked on the rendered page as well as in the text layer.
>
> _([«Salgytlar hakynda» Türkmenistanyň bitewi Kanuny, arts. 141, 170–172, 180](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_

- **Corporate profits tax — ordinary Turkmen legal entities** — **8 percent**, and the exclusions matter as much as the rate. Article 172(1) gives 8% to legal persons who are **residents of Turkmenistan**, but **not** to: entities whose share in charter capital held by the State **exceeds 50%**; **credit institutions**; **state-owned insurance organisations**; providers of **communications services** (satellite communications services are **not** within this exclusion), **standardisation, metrology and certification** services; or parties operating under the Law *"On Hydrocarbon Resources"* — in each case for the profit from that activity and its related non-sales transactions percent  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 172(1)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **⚠ Corporate profits tax — 50 percent, and this guide did not mention it** — Article 172(1) sets **50%** for **credit institutions** — with the parenthetical that credit institutions **not** in state ownership are **not** included — **state-owned insurance organisations**, and providers of **communications** (again excluding satellite communications), **standardisation, metrology and certification** services, on the profit from that activity and its related non-sales transactions. This is more than double the highest rate the guide previously stated, and it reaches the state banking and insurance sector and the telecoms sector percent  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 172(1)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **Corporate profits tax — all other legal persons** — **20 percent** (*beýleki edara görnüşli taraplar*). This residual band is where non-residents, state-majority entities generally, and — by the 50% band's own parenthetical — **privately-owned credit institutions** land, since the 8% band excludes credit institutions outright while the 50% band takes in only the state-owned ones percent  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 172(1)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **⚠ Small and medium enterprises — 2 percent, but on a base computed without deductions** — Article 172(4) sets **2%** for the parties in article 171(6): legal persons in **private ownership** that are subjects of small and medium entrepreneurship under the Law *"On state support of small and medium entrepreneurship"*, excluding parties operating under the Hydrocarbon Resources Law. Article 171(6) then provides that for those parties **the tax base is computed from total income without reducing it by the amount of deductions** (other than those in art. 154(2)(b), (ç) and (e)). The guide gave the 2% as though it were a profits-tax rate on net profit; it is a low rate on a near-gross base, which is a different calculation entirely percent  _([Bitewi Kanun, arts. 171(6) and 172(4)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **Income excluded from the SME gross base** — Article 171(6) keeps three categories out of the income it otherwise taxes without deductions: agricultural producers' income from selling their own products **other than industrially processed ones**; for the **first three years** from registration of an investment project, income from selling own-produced **poultry and quail meat** (with their edible and inedible by-products) and products of processing them; and income from producing **prosthetic-orthopaedic articles and special vehicles for disabled people**  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 171(6)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **Exemptions from profits tax** — Article 170(1) exempts, among others: investment pension funds; organisations restoring the health of disabled people; **educational and scientific institutions**; enterprises of public associations of disabled people where **at least 70%** of total employees are disabled people **and at least 20** disabled people work, whose charter capital belongs wholly to such associations; religious organisations; **agricultural enterprises**; legal persons providing **circus services** (resident or not); and international, intergovernmental and interstate organisations — though **not** their entrepreneurial income. Participants in **free economic zones** are exempt **for the first ten years** of activity, excluding tourist-recreational zones. The exemption does **not** extend to profit from selling goods subject to the additional payment, nor to the article 172(2) and (3) income types  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 170(1)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **The SME rate and the exemptions interact** — Article 170(1) closes by providing that where the parties it lists are **privately-owned** legal persons (other than hydrocarbon-law operators) belonging to small and medium entrepreneurship subjects, they are **exempt from paying the income tax at the article 172(4) rate** — so a qualifying SME inside an exempt category does not simply fall back to the 2%  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 170(1)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **⚠ The ten-year free-economic-zone exemption is not automatic, and it points the opposite way on foreign ownership** — Article 170(1)'s exemption runs to *participants* of a free economic zone, and the **Law "On free economic zones"** — also published by the Tax Directorate — says who those are. **Article 8(1)**: Turkmen legal persons ***"eýeçiliginiň we guramaçylyk-hukuk görnüşlerine garamazdan"*** — **regardless of their form of ownership and their organisational-legal form**; **foreign legal persons** operating in the zone **through branches and representative offices**; and private entrepreneurs. **Set that against the SME rate above, which excludes enterprises with foreign investment outright**: the two reliefs treat foreign participation in opposite directions, and a reader who generalises from one to the other will be wrong  _([Law "On free economic zones", art. 8(1)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/20/12.pdf))_
- **⚠ Participant status is conferred by contract and register entry, not by being in the zone** — **Article 8(2)**: the persons in article 8(1) are recognised as participants **only after the Committee enters them in the register of free-economic-zone participants on the basis of a contract**, and status is granted at that point; a **certificate of registration** issues **within five working days** of the contract being signed. **Operating physically inside a zone does not make a company a participant**, so it does not by itself reach the article 170(1) exemption. Where a candidate — a branch or representative office of a foreign legal person — is not yet on Turkmenistan's Unified State Register of Legal Persons, the Committee petitions the registration body on its behalf, and that body registers it **without any further permit and without agreement from other state bodies**; an unjustified refusal is appealable to court  _([Law "On free economic zones", art. 8(2)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/20/12.pdf))_
- **⚠ Who actually qualifies as an SME — a headcount test, split by sector, with no turnover limit at all** — Article 172(4) keys the 2% to being a subject of small and medium entrepreneurship *"under the Law 'On state support of small and medium entrepreneurship'"*, and **the Tax Directorate publishes that Law too**. Its **article 5(1)** defines the subjects **purely by average listed headcount** — there is **no revenue or asset threshold anywhere in the definition** — and the bands differ by sector, one set for **industry, electric power, construction, and gas and water supply**, another for **all other sectors**  _([Law "On state support of small and medium entrepreneurship", art. 5(1)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/14/6.pdf))_

| Category | Industry, power, construction, gas & water | All other sectors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Microenterprise** | up to **15** employees | no more than **10** |
| **Small enterprise** | **16 to 50** | **11 to 25** |
| **Medium enterprise** | **51 to 200** | **26 to 100** |
| **Private entrepreneur** (no legal person) | — | **not more than 10**, *including the entrepreneur* |

- **The headcount is counted wider than a payroll list** — Article 5(2): the average listed number is determined **for the calendar year** and includes **all** employees — those combining it with a main job elsewhere, **seasonal and temporary workers**, and the staff of **branches, representative offices and other separate subdivisions that are not themselves legal persons**. A group that keeps subsidiaries small by pushing staff into unincorporated units does not thereby stay within the bands  _([SME Law, art. 5(2)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/14/6.pdf))_
- **⚠ Losing SME status has a 1 April cliff, and regaining it takes a year** — Article 5(3): exceeding the article 5(1) headcount **over the calendar year terminates the right to the Law's reliefs**. Where the average is exceeded for a reporting year, the right ceases **from 1 April of the year following that reporting year** — not from the month the threshold was crossed. And if headcount is brought back within the limits, the right ***"dikeldilip bilner"*** — **may be restored only after one year has passed** since it ceased. So a company that briefly over-hires loses the 2% rate on a delayed, fixed date and then cannot requalify for a further year  _([SME Law, art. 5(3)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/14/6.pdf))_
- **⚠ Four categories are outside the SME Law entirely — including any enterprise with foreign investment** — Article 3 limits the Law to the article 5(1) subjects and states that it ***does not apply*** to: **state enterprises**; ***"daşary ýurt maýa goýumlary bolan kärhanalar"*** — **enterprises with foreign investment**; and enterprises carrying on **banking**, **insurance**, or **gambling and games of chance** activity. Because article 172(4) borrows this Law's definition, **a foreign-invested company cannot reach the 2% rate however small it is**. That is the single most consequential gate on this rate for the readers most likely to ask about it, and it sits in a different statute from the one setting the rate  _([SME Law, art. 3](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/14/6.pdf))_
- **The SME Law's own vintage** — *Kiçi we orta telekeçiligi döwlet tarapyndan goldamak hakynda*, Mejlis Maglumatlary 2009 No. 3 art. 58, as amended by the Laws of **29.08.2013 No. 429-IV**, **03.05.2014 No. 84-V**, **28.02.2015 No. 193-V** and **26.03.2016 No. 385-V**. The consolidated text the Tax Directorate serves carries those four amendments on its title page; **whether anything has amended it since 2016 is not established here**  _([SME Law, title page](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/14/6.pdf))_
- **Reductions in taxable profit** — Article 170(2) reduces taxable profit by profit from **scientific activity**; by agricultural producers' profit from selling their own products, **provided those products have not been industrially processed**; and by profit from receiving tourists within **national tourist zones** (accommodation, catering, personal and welfare services, organising entertainment other than gambling) and from managing and using tourism-industry objects there — a relief also given to holders of national tourist zone subject status when such services are supplied **outside** those zones  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 170(2)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **Petroleum / hydrocarbon operations** — Parties carrying on activity under the Law *"On Hydrocarbon Resources"* are **carved out of the 8% band by article 172(1) itself** and out of the SME regime by article 171(6), so the Bitewi Kanun routes them to a separate regime rather than rating them. The contract-level rate was **not** read and is not stated here (approx — confirm rate per contract)  _([Bitewi Kanun, arts. 171(6), 172(1)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf); Law of Turkmenistan on Hydrocarbon Resources)_
- **Gambling business — taxed by fixed daily amounts, not by rate** — Article 172(3) charges gambling income at **TMT 30 per gaming machine per day**, **TMT 370 per gaming table per day**, **TMT 7 per seat per day**, **TMT 3 per square metre** of premises used for the activity per day, or **TMT 38 per cash desk** accepting payments per day. Where more than one of these could apply to a single type of gambling activity, **the one producing the higher amount of tax applies**. Absent from this guide  _([Bitewi Kanun, arts. 171(5) and 172(3)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **Tax base** — Gross income less deductible business expenses (accounting profit adjusted under the Tax Code) — subject to the **SME exception above**, where deductions are not applied  _([Bitewi Kanun, arts. 149, 154, 171](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **Withholding tax — dividends** — **15 percent**. Article 172(2) sets 15% for dividends and other income in the form of funds received from participating as a shareholder in other legal persons percent (treaty rates may reduce)  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 172(2)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **Withholding tax — interest** — **15 percent**, as part of the general article 180(3) rate on Turkmen-source income of non-resident legal persons percent (treaty rates may reduce)  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 180(3)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **Withholding tax — royalties** — **15 percent**, on the same article 180(3) footing percent (treaty rates may reduce)  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 180(3)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **⚠ Withholding tax — vessel and aircraft leasing is 6% only where leased without crew** — Article 180(3) applies 15% to non-resident income generally, **excepting income from leasing sea and air vessels**, and sets **6%** for income from leasing sea and air vessels ***işgärsiz*** — **without crew**, i.e. bareboat. This guide gave a flat "ship and aircraft lease income — 6 percent"; a wet lease is not within the 6% percent  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 180(3)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **Non-resident withholding bites on gross, and reaches payments made abroad** — Article 180(1): where a non-resident legal person earns Turkmen-source income from activity **not creating a permanent establishment**, the tax agent withholds **from total income without applying deductions** (VAT excluded from the base), on **each payment**, ***including where those payments are made outside Turkmenistan***. **Cessation of a claim in any form counts as payment.** Article 180(4): where payment is made after the last day of the month in which income is treated as received under article 168, the tax agent pays the tax **from its own funds** and recovers it from later sums due to the taxpayer  _([Bitewi Kanun, arts. 180(1) and 180(4)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **Property tax on entities — 1%, accrued in quarterly steps** — Article 141 sets property tax at **1% of the tax period's tax base**, and fixes the computation amount for each reporting period: **0.25%** for the first quarter, **0.5%** for the half-year, **0.75%** for nine months and **1%** for the year. The guide's "(approx — confirm)" is resolved; the stepped schedule was absent percent  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 141](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **⚠ Annual return deadline — the Tax Code fixes no calendar date** — Article 176(1): the profit (income) tax declaration is filed **within the periods prescribed for submitting the financial statements**. It names no month or day, so the "31 March" stated here cannot be sourced to the Tax Code and depends on the financial-reporting rules instead. **Gambling income is the exception**: its declaration is due **by the 20th of the month following the reporting period**, at the tax office of registration  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 176(1)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **⚠ Advance payments are monthly, on the 25th** — Article 175(1): profit tax computed under article 174(2) is paid **as an advance payment on the 25th of each month of the tax year**, and on the totals for the **first quarter, first half-year, nine months and the tax year** **within five days after the day the declaration for that period is due**. Income tax under article 174(4) is paid **no later than the 25th of the month following the reporting period**. The guide's "(approx — confirm frequency)" is resolved: monthly advances, four period settlements, each five days behind its declaration  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 175(1)–(2)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
- **Tax agents report on dividends by the 20th** — Article 176(2): tax agents must, **by the 20th of the month following the month income was paid**, give their registering tax office information on the legal persons to whom dividends and other participation income were paid, and the amounts computed, withheld and transferred to the State Budget, on a form set by the Ministry of Finance and Economy. **Budget institutions file nothing** for reporting periods in which there was no taxable base  _([Bitewi Kanun, art. 176(2)](https://tax.gov.tm/pdf/ckeditor_assets/attachments/10/2.pdf))_
