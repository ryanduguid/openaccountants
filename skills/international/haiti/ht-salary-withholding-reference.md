# Haiti Salary Income Tax and Employer Withholding Reference

**Source-cited reference pending professional review. Affected engagement work is stopped.** This conditional reference explains selected wording of the Code Fiscal and supplies labelled fictional arithmetic for source review. Before actual classification, calculation or output preparation, load both declared bases, establish the requested period's operative law and satisfy their source and companion requirements. > **General reference only.** This material has not been reviewed for any person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residence, filing status or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend or take a position without review by a qualified professional in Haiti.

## Scope

Covers selected individual salary-income, employer-withholding, monthly-statement and penalty provisions of the Code Fiscal commencing 1 October 2026. It also compares selected June 2026 Rectificatif provisions as a separate dated source. It does not expressly establish the salary fiscal year, and does not establish the first return, transition treatment or a complete operative source chain for an engagement.

Outside this reference: business and professional income, company tax, capital gains, investment income and rental income except as cross-references; social insurance contributions; labour law; treaty relief.

Uniform administration across the country was not assessed.

## Salary base and exempt receipts

**Art 98 (p 36).** The base takes account of the gross amount of salaries, probation pay, salary supplements, overtime, traitements, function and responsibility allowances, boni, primes, paid leave, directors' fees, tantièmes, reimbursements, commissions paid to individuals, flat expense allowances and all cash or in-kind benefits. No valuation rule for in-kind benefits was found in Arts 98 to 104.

**Art 54 (p 20).** Exempt from income tax: civil and military pensions; retirement and invalidity indemnities; workplace accident indemnities; life and health insurance indemnities received by individuals; notice indemnities under the Code du Travail and the separation-indemnity branch under privileges legislation for *anciens grands commis*; trainee allowances during placement; court-ordered maintenance; and income of the State, territorial collectivities and public establishments, excluding income of public enterprises and autonomous bodies supplying commercial, industrial or financial goods or services. The printed lettering repeats (a, b, c, d, c, d, e, f), so cite by description as well as letter. This does not establish a blanket severance exemption.

### Method gaps in Art 100

S1 gives the 1/12 rule and no formula. The following were not found, and each holds under R-HT-METHOD:

- part-year employment, a pay change during the year, more than one employer or irregular pay;
- how the employer estimates 'annual income tax due', and any employee declaration of deductions;
- whether the employer may reflect Art 126 deductions in the monthly amount;
- whether commission, especially as sole pay, sits in Art 100 or in Art 103's 'other cash remuneration', and what 'salaire de base' means. **Inference:** Art 100(1)'s list is narrow and Art 103(1) catches 'toutes autres rémunérations en numéraire, additionnelles au salaire de base', so overtime and cash allowances point to Art 103;
- withholding on in-kind benefits. S2 treats a debit card as a non-withheld in-kind benefit 'pour les besoins des simulations', which is a modelling assumption;
- an employer year-end reconciliation, or an employer-issued certificate for employees, under S1;
- a required form or portal for the Art 101 statement. Arts 125 and 95(2) mention forms for their own filings only.

## Annual tax

**Art 125 (p 43).** Individuals file a general and detailed *Déclaration définitive* for all income on a form supplied free by the administration, signed by the taxpayer or a representative, by 31 January each year for the preceding fiscal year's income. **Conditional inference only:** if the 2026-27 salary year is established as 1 October 2026 to 30 September 2027, the corresponding date would be 31 January 2028. Neither these paragraphs nor this reference establishes that salary-period premise; R-HT-PERIOD stops actual use.

**Art 126 (pp 43-44).** Deductible from total income on supporting documents, where not already deducted in a category:

| Item | Deduction | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| a | Annual rent of the principal residence (defined in Art 275(2), p 84) | 25% of the rent |
| b | Mortgage interest paid to a financial or credit institution established in Haiti or a *prêteur patenté* on acquiring or building the principal residence, and its CFPB | None stated; establish the specific *patente* qualification rather than treating any licence as equivalent |
| c | Levies lawfully taken from salaries, other than income tax | None stated |
| d | Retirement or housing savings paid during the fiscal year to legally recognised institutions | None stated |
| e | Life and health premiums borne by the taxpayer and paid to a company fiscally resident in Haiti; Art 126(e) literally cites Art 60 | None stated. Complete Art 60 distinguishes residence from permanent establishment but supplies no affirmative insurer-residence definition. Haitian premises, representation, premium collection or a permanent establishment alone do not clear this condition; insurer residence remains held |
| f | Medical costs not covered by a life and health policy | None stated |
| g | Taxpayer's training and *perfectionnement* expenses, and school fees with the printed Haitian-establishment, minor-child and justification conditions | Printed limits: 100,000 Gourdes per child, 300,000 in total. Their reach over the taxpayer's own training remains held. |
| h | Meals and lodging on real, invoiced professional travel | None stated |
| i | Gifts to the listed foundations, charities, relief bodies, political parties subject to their legislation and specified recognised or registered institutions | 10% of income after items a to h; establish the complete recipient qualification and any political-party limit before applying it |

Art 126(2): a category's deficit can be set only against income of the same category.

Article 275(2), incorporated by the principal-home limbs, defines the home as the dwelling where members of the taxpayer's household reside. It does not import the separate CFPB age or financed-construction reductions in Art 275(1)/(3) into the income-tax deductions.

**Art 132 (p 45).** Applied to the taxpayer's total income, by fraction:

|---|---|

**Rounding.** Art 300 (p 93) rounds the tax base to the nearest Gourde, with a half Gourde counted up. A rounding rule for the tax or for the monthly withholding was not found.

**Band floors.** This reference computes each fraction from the previous ceiling (360,000, 540,000 and so on). The printed floors end in 001. S2's published results are consistent with this method, but after rounding to the Gourde they do not rule out the alternative.

**Excess withholding and claims.** Art 133 credits non-final withholding and provisional payments paid. It does not establish automatic repayment or carry-forward of a credit surplus. Complete Arts 502 to 508 (pp 157-159) establish a prior administrative claim route; Art 505(e) expressly addresses withholding and requires evidence of its date, paying establishment and amount. Application still requires substantive grounds, admissibility, evidence and representation. Art 504's deadline is 30 September of the second year following the specified payment, notification or motivating event; establish the applicable event and delivery rule rather than assuming a universal start. Art 513 (p 161) provides a 6-month decision period, with a possible 3 additional months notified in writing before expiry; silence at expiry is implicit rejection. Arts 514-515 permit the specified administrative compensation and substitution of legal basis. These are not automatic future-year carry-forward powers.

Arts 531 to 536 (pp 165-166) address relief and its consequences. Art 533 links repayment interest to qualifying relief after a claim or court decision, for sums already collected; it excludes gracious repayment and ex-officio relief from that interest. Art 536 concerns guarantee expenses, not the underlying salary-tax credit. Art 307 (p 95) permits discretionary notified set-off only where the specified existing, liquid and due claims support it. The ordinary salary-credit surplus entitlement, current implementation and complete judicial procedure remain unestablished under S1. S8 describes a 5-year carry-forward and a DGI certificate for employees, but it is undated, and its authority under S1 is not established. The selected payment-stay rules are set out below; they do not establish a refund entitlement. R-HT-METHOD stops actual refund, carry-forward or interest calculations.

**Payment stay, Arts 315 and 509 to 512 (pp 97, 160-161).** A claim ordinarily leaves the full tax and penalties payable within the legal deadline. A taxpayer may expressly request a stay in the claim for the contested assessment and associated penalties, identifying the relief amount or bases. Arts 315(2) and 509(3) condition suspension of exigibility and recovery-action prescription from presentation on grant by the competent Comptable Public. Art 509(6) separately makes the stay *de droit* where the claim concerns rights of 100,000 Gourdes or less. Preserve both provisions; neither a wholly discretionary rule nor an automatic stay from any complaint captures the selected text. Only one stay request is permitted for the same debt. Suspension lasts until a definitive administrative or court decision and extends to jointly liable co-debtors even without their own request.

Above 100,000 Gourdes of rights, Art 510 requires guarantees covering the contested rights, proposed in the stay request, subject to its paragraph 6 alternative. The threshold and guarantee amount concern rights, not an assumed rights-plus-penalties total. Listed forms include a deposit, bank guarantee, an enterprise's director/partner mortgage guarantee and the printed limb *des affectations hypothécaires d'immeubles appartenant en pleine propriété au propriétaire*. Do not infer a different ownership test. The Comptable Public has 15 days from lodging the offer to decide, notifying under Art 314; silence accepts the proposed guarantee. That does not itself establish an unrestricted stay. Where the claimant must constitute the guarantee, actual constitution and supporting evidence are due within 30 days from the Comptable Public's response. The selected units do not settle that trigger after silent guarantee acceptance. With insufficient or absent guarantees, the Comptable Public may grant a stay subject to a legal mortgage or conservatory seizure of tangible/intangible movable assets.

Under Art 511, depreciation or revealed insufficiency permits a further-security demand under Art 314. Failure within 30 days permits the specified bank-account-blocking authorisation route or summary referral to the Doyen du Tribunal de Première Instance for conservatory measures; it does not establish automatic unilateral account blocking. Art 512 permits the Comptable Public to admit replacement by another guarantee of at least equal value. Actual use requires the competent authority, notification and grant/guarantee records and the applicable receipt/day rules. Complete court and conservatory-enforcement procedures remain held. A stay does not establish an interest exemption: retain Art 534's separate taxpayer-interest conditions and Arts 535-536's guarantee-return/expense rules.

## Non-compliance

These are selected source provisions, not self-executing liability findings or a complete penalty workflow. Establish the actual obligation, taxpayer class, default, notice, procedure and operative law before any calculation. Article 471 (p 146) requires the specified inspector decision and visas, including the additional superior visa for Art 472 insufficiency penalties, factual/legal reasons and notification. Observation rights follow Art 435, including the 15-day extension where Art 435(2)'s request conditions are met. Missing required visa or reasons entails cancellation. The Art 435 response rules and Art 436 discretionary regularisation procedure have different silence effects; neither can be replaced by an assumed administrative acceptance.

**Amounts deducted but not remitted.** Art 476(1) treats withholding the employer must make as sums due, and Art 476(3) refers to 'sommes en principal'. Art 341(2) (p 103) makes co-perpetrators and accomplices jointly liable for 'ces retenues à la source', which presupposes the primary debt (inference). **Not found:** an express private sector rule making an employer liable for tax it never deducted, as distinct from Art 100(3)'s public sector liability. That exposure is held under R-HT-METHOD. Arts 335 to 341 and the audit and reassessment chapters were read only where cited.

Selected reassessment, office-assessment and abuse units were read in Arts 433 to 449. They preserve required rank, reasons, notices, response periods, proof and procedure-specific consequences. Arts 439-443 add distinct branches; do not apply Art 449's initiative/benefit test to all office assessments:

- Art 439 (p 136) addresses no response, an oral-only or late response to a request under Arts 361-362, or failure satisfactorily to complete a first response after the Art 362 demand. The underlying request and demand conditions remain necessary.
- Art 440 (p 136) distinguishes missing/incomplete required transfer-pricing documentation after the Art 425 demand during accounting verification/examination from absent/partial information after the Art 426 demand for taxpayers not required to maintain that documentation. Those incorporated procedures remain held.
- Art 441 (p 137) addresses rejected accounts on its specified grounds. It requires a 30-day first demand from receipt/delivery and, following no or unsatisfactory response, a 15-day second demand. Required rejection warnings apply under pain of nullity. Sending the demands suspends verification duration and the Arts 350-360 period until the taxpayer's response. Preserve the report, signature/copy and recorded-refusal formalities. The stated consequence is office assessment, the Art 472 fraudulent-conduct penalty of 80% of reassessed tax and loss of the Arts 414/420 duration guarantee, subject to the applicable procedural conditions.
- Art 442 (pp 137-138) requires the specified non-domiciled natural persons conducting activities or holding property in Haiti and foreign-seat enterprises with a Haitian permanent establishment to appoint a qualifying Haitian fiscal representative within 30 days from starting activity. The representative must have the prescribed Haitian domicile or corporate seat/residence and identify as such to the Administration. A failure requires a 60-day appointment demand before office assessment, without prejudice to other applicable penalties. The representative is jointly liable for tax payment with the appointing person. Do not extend this to every overseas person or representative.
- Art 443 (pp 138-139) addresses specified opposition to control, including by a person acting for the taxpayer. Preserve the report/signature/refusal formalities, 30-day extension of the stated control duration and ordinarily a demand to cease opposition within 3 days from receipt, served personally against discharge or by registered letter with acknowledgement. Serious violence is the express exception to the prior-demand requirement. Continued opposition after the demand, or the serious-violence branch, leads to office assessment with the stated 100% of reassessed tax, exclusive of other fiscal penalties, and loss of specified duration guarantees. Collective opposition carries 250,000 Gourdes per participant, without prejudice to criminal sanctions; retain its definition involving external participants obstructing normal control or preventing it.

The complete incorporated Arts 361-362, 425-426, 350-360 and control-scope/duration provisions remain unqualified where not separately cited. These selected branches do not establish a complete collection or assessment workflow.

**AMR notification, Art 314 (p 97).** Email requires the Administration's proof of actual receipt. Hand delivery requires effective delivery against discharge to the debtor or authorised representative; registered/express delivery normally uses withdrawal. The specified debtor-attributable delivery failure or non-collection within the carrier's period uses first presentation; bailiff service uses its service date. Before pursuing jointly or severally liable debtors, an AMR must be notified to each. Notification opens the contentious-appeal and recovery-action prescription periods; Art 314 alone supplies neither full period. Article 311's payment period requires a duly notified AMR. Apply the payment-stay conditions above rather than assuming a complaint suspends payment. Day periods under Art 298 use *jours francs* and specified expiry/receipt rules; do not automatically extend them to every fixed calendar date. Preserve Art 299's official communication and receipt predicates; an ordinary email alone proves no statutory notification.

**Factual ruling, Art 469 (pp 144-145).** A request to the Director General under Arts 298-299 must give the prescribed identity/address fields, fiscal provisions, precise and complete facts and legal arguments. Actual identifiers stay with the reviewer; the agent uses redacted references. The 3-month response period runs from receipt by the Director General, who informs the applicant; for an incomplete request it runs from receipt of the requested additional information. A response must follow Arts 298-299. Silence or an informal response is not a formal position. A formal rescrit is opposable only where the taxpayer follows the solution and the situation is strictly identical. The taxpayer concerned and, where applicable, participants in the act/transaction may rely on it. A changed administrative position operates only prospectively, with advance notice. This is a route for a specific evidenced situation, not a general payroll method or proof of an Art 540 decision.
