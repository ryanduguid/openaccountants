# Lesotho Corporate Income Tax

## 1. The company rates: Third Schedule

The following table reproduces the Third Schedule in the consolidation to
1 April 2012. It is historical evidence, not a verified current rate schedule.
RSL guidance supports 25% for ordinary company income and 10% for manufacturing
and commercial farming, but post-2012 amendments have not been reconciled.
Do not apply the historical 0% export rate without that review:

| # | Nature of income | Rate |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 | Manufacturing income from a manufacturing activity of an industrial, scientific or educational nature which promotes industrial, scientific, educational or other development within Lesotho | **10%** |
| 2 | Manufacturing income from manufacturing activities relating **exclusively to exports to any country other than a country within the Southern African Customs Union** | **0%** |
| 3 | Other manufacturing income | **10%** |
| 4 | Other income | **25%** |

_(Income Tax Act 1993, Third Schedule (section 10): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_

- **"Standard rate of tax" is a defined term** — the Act's interpretation section
  says *"'standard rate of tax' means a rate of 25%"*. It is the figure that drives
  the non-resident rate in s.12(1), the s.107 withholding rate and the advance
  corporation tax formula, so it is worth knowing that all four move together if it
  ever changes  _(Income Tax Act 1993, s.3, definition of "standard rate of tax": https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_
- **Commercial farming is 10%, and it sits in a different schedule**: the Fourth
  Schedule, item 2: *"Tax rate on persons undertaking commercial farming — the
  applicable rate is 10%"*, as amended by s.3 of Act No. 17 of 2008. **Subsistence**
  farming by a resident individual is exempt outright under s.29. The previous
  version bundled "manufacturing & commercial farming" into one 10% line, which
  gets the number right and hides that they come from different provisions  _(Income Tax Act 1993, Fourth Schedule item 2 and s.29: https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_

> **[CURRENCY NOTE: read before relying on the 0% band.]** The most recent
> consolidation RSL publishes is the Income Tax Act 1993 **updated to 1 April
> 2012**. RSL's own site does list an *Income Tax (Amendment) Regulations No. 24 of
> 2026*, but it is published as a **scanned image with no extractable text** and
> could not be read here. The 25% and 10% figures are separately confirmed by RSL's
> current website; **the 0% export band is not**, and no post-2012 instrument was
> checked against it. A reviewer must confirm the Third Schedule as amended.

## 2. RSL's own corporate-tax guide contradicts itself on these rates

RSL's *Guide on Corporate Tax* (PDF, June 2025) prints a rate table and, on the next page, explanatory notes that say the opposite.

| Source | Manufacturing for export outside SACU | Non-manufacturing / other |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| **Income Tax Act, Third Schedule** | **0%** | **25%** |
| RSL *Guide on Corporate Tax*, the table, PDF page 2 | 25% | 10% ("Other Income") |
| RSL *Guide on Corporate Tax*, notes (a) and (b), PDF page 3 | 10% | 25% |
| RSL website, *Corporate Income Tax* page |: (not addressed) | 25%, with manufacturing and commercial farming at 10% |

The table's rate column is inverted relative to the notes on the following page:
note (a) reads *"Corporate tax rate of 10% is levied on income from exporting
manufactured goods outside the Customs Union"* while the table row for that same
income says 25%; note (b) reads *"A corporate tax rate of 25% is imposed on
profits of a resident non-manufacturing company"* while the table's "Other Income"
row says 10%. **This is not a text-extraction artefact**: each rate is
positionally aligned with its own row in the PDF.

The Act governs as amended. The 2012 consolidation cannot settle a conflict
with later guidance without checking intervening amendments. Treat the export
rate as unresolved. The 25% ordinary company rate has support in the Act, the
explanatory notes and the RSL website.
_(Income Tax Act 1993, Third Schedule; RSL, Guide on Corporate Tax: https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2025-06/Guide%20on%20Corporate%20Tax.pdf; RSL, Corporate Income Tax: https://www.rsl.org.ls/corporate-income-tax)_

## 3. Branches of non-resident companies

- **Taxed at 25% on all Lesotho income**: *"A corporate tax rate of 25% is imposed
  on … branch profits of a non-resident company"* and *"the corporate tax rate is
  applied to all income of a Lesotho branch of a non-resident company"*. The
  previous version put "branch profits / remittance to non-resident" at 25% and
  marked it "(approx: confirm)". The rate is right; **it is not a remittance tax**
 : there is no separate charge on remitting the profit, it is the ordinary
  corporate rate on the branch's income  _(RSL, Guide on Corporate Tax, explanatory notes (b) and (c): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2025-06/Guide%20on%20Corporate%20Tax.pdf)_
- **And the manufacturing concession is closed to branches**: s.10(2): the special
  manufacturing rate applies to Lesotho-source manufacturing income *"but does not
  apply to a Lesotho branch of a non-resident company"*. A foreign manufacturer
  operating through a branch rather than a subsidiary pays **25%, not 10%**  _(Income Tax Act 1993, s.10(2): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_

## 4. Advance corporation tax: a whole obligation the guide omitted

- **A resident company paying a dividend prepays income tax at 33⅓%**: s.87(1)
  makes a resident company that pays a dividend liable to advance corporation tax,
  *"except to the extent that the distribution is made out of qualified income"*.
  s.87(2) sets the rate as **A × 100 / (100 − A)**, where A is the Third Schedule
  rate for income other than manufacturing income. At A = 25 that is **25/75 =
  33⅓%** of the gross dividend  _(Income Tax Act 1993, s.87(1)–(2): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_
- **"Qualified income" is manufacturing income net of its own tax, plus dividends
  from another resident company**: s.85. And s.87(3) provides that a dividend is
  treated as paid **first out of qualified income** and then out of other income, so
  a company with manufacturing profits shelters its distributions before the charge
  bites  _(Income Tax Act 1993, ss.85 and 87(3): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_
- **It is a prepayment, not a cost**: the liability may be satisfied by an
  instalment of tax already paid and not yet set off under s.150, and ACT itself may
  be set off against a later instalment or against the final income tax liability  _(Income Tax Act 1993, s.87(4)–(5): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_
- **The return is due within seven days of paying the dividend**: showing the
  dividends paid, the ACT paid, and any ACT satisfied under s.87(4). The trigger is payment of the dividend, not the board resolution  _(RSL, Advanced Corporation Tax Guide: https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-07/Advanced%20Corporation%20Tax%20Guide.pdf)_

> **The worked example in RSL's ACT guide does not compute.** It takes chargeable
> income of M100,000 and states the resulting company tax liability as M16,667 ;
> 25% of M100,000 is M25,000: then divides that by three to reach "M5,000 per
> quarter", where a third of M16,667 is M5,555.67. Neither step follows. The
> **rule** in that guide matches s.87 and is used above; its **arithmetic** is not
> relied on here, and a reader working through that example should not expect it to
> reconcile.

## 5. Withholding taxes

The previous version gave five withholding figures from a law firm's page and
compressed three different statutory regimes into one line. The Act separates them
by section.

| Payment | Section | Rate | Notes |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Dividend, interest, royalty, natural-resource payment or **management charge** to a **non-resident** | s.107(1) | **25%** (the standard rate) | Not specified |
| Dividend paid out of manufacturing income taxed at the concessional rate | s.107(2) | **nil** | No withholding at all |
| Interest, royalty **or management charge** used **solely** in producing concessionally-taxed manufacturing income | s.107(3) | **15%** | All three, not just royalties |
| Payment under a **Lesotho-source services contract** to a non-resident | s.108(1) | **10%** | Excludes management charges, which stay in s.107 |
| Payment to a **resident contractor** | s.157(1) | **5%** | See the thresholds below |
| Interest paid to a **person resident in Lesotho** | s.158(1) | **10%** | Not specified |
| Liquidator's payment to a **member** of a company | s.160 | **10%** | Not specified |

_(Income Tax Act 1993, ss.107, 108, 157, 158 and 160: https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_

- **The 15% manufacturing rate covers three payment types, not one**: s.107(3), as
  substituted by s.9 of Act No. 2 of 1994, reaches (a) interest on a loan whose
  funds are used solely in producing concessionally-taxed manufacturing income, (b)
  a royalty for technology used solely in producing it, **and (c) a management
  charge** for a management or administrative service used solely in producing it.
  RSL's own 2010 public ruling states the 15% limb for royalties only; the statute
  is wider  _(Income Tax Act 1993, s.107(3): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_
- **Management charges are never in s.108**: s.108(2) expressly disapplies the 10%
  services rate to a management charge withheld under s.107. The distinction decides
  whether the rate is 25% or 10%, so it is the first thing to settle on a services
  invoice from abroad  _(Income Tax Act 1993, s.108(2): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_
- **Withholding under s.107 or s.108 is a final tax**: unless the non-resident
  files a return electing to be taxed by assessment, in which case only otherwise
  deductible expenses may be claimed and the rate becomes the Fourth Schedule rate
  of **40%** on net assessable income. Compare the gross withholding with the net assessment for the taxpayer's actual deductible expenses  _(Income Tax Act 1993, s.109 and Fourth Schedule item 1: https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_
- **The resident-contractor withholding has three escape hatches**: s.157 does not
  apply to payments by an individual for the erection or improvement of that
  individual's **principal residence** (s.157(2)); it applies *"only commencing with
  the first month in which payments under the contract exceed M3,000 **in that
  month**"* (s.157(3)) ; withholding starts in that month; and it does not
  apply to a **complying contractor** holding a valid certificate of exemption from
  the Commissioner General, granted where the contractor has a fixed place of
  business, proper records, all tax paid and regular returns filed (s.157(4)–(7)).
  A "resident contractor" is defined by activity: vehicle, plant or equipment
  leasing, construction, transportation, painting or decorating, plumbing, drainage,
  irrigation, roofing or tiling, earthmoving or excavation, landscaping, demolition,
  site restoration and installation of services or appliances  _(Income Tax Act 1993, s.157: https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_
- **Resident interest: the M500 exemption is narrower than it sounds**: s.27(1)
  exempts the first **M500** of interest from **one savings account** of a resident
  individual who is not a minor, and only where the account has been **nominated**
  for the exemption, **bears the individual's TIN**, and is with a Lesotho-resident
  financial institution licensed under the Financial Institutions Act 1973. It is
  not available to companies. Where s.158 tax is withheld on interest paid to a
  resident individual (other than as trustee) or to an exempt organisation, the
  interest is excluded from gross income and the **withholding is a final tax**  _(Income Tax Act 1993, ss.27 and 158(2): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_
- **Withholding is on the gross amount before VAT**: and VAT is charged on that
  same gross amount, before the withholding is deducted. Where goods are supplied
  incidentally to a service, their cost is aggregated with the service and the whole
  is within the withholding. No withholding applies to utilities (electricity, water
  and sewage, telephone) or to rent for buildings  _(RSL, Withholding Tax Public Ruling (April 2010): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2025-06/Withholding%20Tax%20Public%20Ruling_0.pdf)_

> **That public ruling is dated April 2010 and shows its age**: it works its VAT
> example at **14%**, a rate Lesotho has since moved. Its statements of *structure*
> (which section catches which payment, what "gross amount" means, that incidental
> goods are swept in) are used above; its **rates** are taken from the Act instead,
> and its 15% limb is incomplete against s.107(3).

## 6. Filing, provisional tax and late-payment charges

- **The return is due 30 June**: every company files on or before 30 June each
  year, unless the Commissioner General has approved a **substituted accounting
  period**, meaning any twelve-month period that does not run 1 April to 31 March.
  With approval, the return is due on the last day of the **third month** following
  the financial year end. The previous version gave "30 June following the end of
  the 31 March tax year" and omitted the substituted-period route entirely  _(RSL, Corporate Income Tax: https://www.rsl.org.ls/corporate-income-tax; RSL, Guide on Corporate Tax: https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2025-06/Guide%20on%20Corporate%20Tax.pdf)_
- **Three instalments, and the statutory formula is not simply 30% of last year's
  tax**: s.150(1) sets instalments due **30 September, 31 December and 31 March**,
  each of **30% × (A − B)**, where **A** is the taxpayer's liability for the
  preceding year after any foreign tax credit but **before set-off of advance
  corporation tax**, and **B** is so much of A as was paid in the preceding year by
  **amounts withheld at source** under Part VII. RSL's own provisional-tax guide
  states the instalment as "30% of the Client's income tax liability for the
  preceding year" and **drops the − B**; for a company with substantial withholding
  credits the two produce materially different instalments. The previous version
  marked instalments "(approx: confirm instalment dates and number)"  _(Income Tax Act 1993, s.150(1): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf; RSL, Guide on Provisional Tax: https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2025-06/Guide%20of%20Provisional%20Tax.pdf)_
- **On a substituted period the instalments follow the period**: due on the last
  day of the **sixth, ninth and twelfth months** after it commences  _(Income Tax Act 1993, s.150(2): https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_
- **Additional tax for failing to file or to pay: 22% per twelve-month period**  _(RSL, Guide on Corporate Tax: https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2025-06/Guide%20on%20Corporate%20Tax.pdf)_
- **Additional tax for missing a provisional instalment: 3% of the amount due,
  compounded monthly** from the due date  _(RSL, Guide on Provisional Tax: https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2025-06/Guide%20of%20Provisional%20Tax.pdf)_
- **The Act's own penalty sections** are ss.193 (failure to file), 194 (failure to
  pay, including a s.150 instalment) and 195 (under-estimating by more than 10% where
  an extension to file was granted); each imposes additional tax "at the specified
  rate", which is why the percentages above are cited to RSL's guides rather than to
  the Act  _(Income Tax Act 1993, ss.193–195: https://www.rsl.org.ls/sites/default/files/2024-05/Income%20Tax%20Act%201993%20%20Updated%20up%20to%201%20April%202012_0.pdf)_

## Sources and review limits

The rates, withholding sections, advance corporation tax, instalment formula and
penalty sections were read from the Income Tax Act 1993 as consolidated and
published by Revenue Services Lesotho. Two things are deliberately left open. The
consolidation runs only to **1 April 2012**, and the *Income Tax (Amendment)
Regulations No. 24 of 2026* that RSL publishes is a **scanned image with no
extractable text**, so no post-2012 amendment to the Third Schedule was verified ;
the 0% export band in particular is unconfirmed against current law. And RSL's own
*Guide on Corporate Tax* contradicts both the Act and its own explanatory notes on
the rate table, a conflict recorded in §2 rather than resolved.
