## Position rules

Each rule states the reading of the source shown. Requested-period law and every unmentioned condition remain held.

| Topic | Reading |
|---|---|
| **Taxing area** | The territory of Sint Maarten under Art 1 of the Staatsregeling: S1 Art 1(1)(b). S2 Art 3(c) extends 'Sint Maarten' to specified seabed areas for the tax ordinances. |
| **Entrepreneur** | Anyone independently carrying on a business or profession, or exploiting an asset for lasting yield: S1 Art 1(1)(d). Art 1(2) excludes the specified offshore companies and banks only to the extent of qualifying offshore activities with the stated exemption under Arts 9–15 of the foreign exchange ordinance. Under Art 1(4), the specified persons' services to those bodies, acting as such, are not services for this purpose; this does not reach every service labelled offshore. Public bodies may be treated as entrepreneurs by ministerial rule: Art 1(3). |
| **Charge** | Residents and established entrepreneurs: turnover realised in the area. Non-resident entrepreneurs: only supplies made in the area and services enjoyed there: S1 Art 2. |
| **Turnover** | Total consideration an entrepreneur receives for supplies of goods and services within the business: S1 Art 1(1)(c). |
| **Goods and services** | Goods are physical items other than water, electricity and gas. Services are all non-goods performances for consideration: S1 Art 1(1)(e)-(f). Art 4 lists deemed supplies, including transfer under contract, hire purchase or finance lease, removal from business assets and commission sales. |
| **Consideration** | Everything received, excluding interest for making money available: S1 Art 1(1)(g). Non-cash consideration and consideration below market value use market value under Art 1(5)–(6). Own-use supplies under Art 4(d) use the amount that would be received if the goods were acquired or produced at the time of the supply, in their condition at that time, under Art 1(7). Historic cost is not the test. |
| **Base** | Turnover realised in the area: S1 Art 6(1). Amounts paid to a third party in the customer's name and for the customer's account are excluded: Art 6(2). |
| **Place** | Where the entrepreneur lives, is established or has a fixed establishment supplying from it: S1 Art 5(1). For goods, where transport starts, otherwise where the goods are at supply: Art 5(2). No separate general place-of-services rule was found in S1 beyond these and Art 2(b)(2). |
| **Chargeability** | Receipt of consideration, including partial receipts, is the timing rule under S1 Art 10(1). Art 10(2) permits invoice timing only on request and with the Inspector’s permission. An annual period or simplified-return permission does not supply an invoice-basis permit. |
| **Invoice basis relief** | With invoice-basis permission under S1 Art 10(2), obtained on a written request (S4 141976 Art 1, display), an entrepreneur may deduct the unreceived amount of an invoice written off as uncollectable, in the month written off ('in de maand waarin hij de factuur als oninbaar afboekt'). Later receipts are declared. Credit notes have their own rule. The deduction is capped at the period's turnover, with carry-forward only to the next period, and records are required. Hold until the permit and the current text are checked. |
| **Liable person** | Normally the entrepreneur: S1 Art 11(1). Under Art 5(3) the specified overseas supplier is deemed to have chosen domicile at the Inspectorate for supplies/services to a customer resident or established in the area. Art 11(2) separately requires that recipient to be an entrepreneur established in the area before liability moves to it, and requires either the joint written request submitted to the Inspector or the overseas supplier’s failure to pay on return. Residence alone is not the Art 11(2) eligibility test. |
| **Rate changes** | S1 Art 7(2) permits a decree to change the rate. Art 7(3) requires a ratifying bill to be offered within 6 months after the decree enters into force. Art 7(4) makes the decree lapse by law on the day after that period if no bill is submitted or on the day after the bill is withdrawn. If Parliament rejects it, the decree must be withdrawn without delay. If it becomes an ordinance, the decree is withdrawn when that ordinance enters into force. These are distinct branches; the 6 months are not a universal approval deadline. Existence and effect of any such decree remain held. |
| **Input tax** | No input credit is established by the qualified text. Do not model one. This does not establish that no other applicable relief exists. S3's memorandum says 'normaliter 90% van de betaalde BBO aftrekbaar', which is explanatory only; its legal basis was not read, and it is not a BBO credit. |
| **Imports** | No charge on importation was found in S1. The import ordinance was not read. |
| **Returns** | S1 Art 12 normally uses calendar months. The specified natural person exploiting an asset for lasting yield may request an annual period; it is not automatic. The Inspector may permit a simplified return. S2 Arts 6 and 8 require the complete signed return and requested documents, normally to the Inspector; filing with the Ontvanger requires the Director’s conditions. A missing form must be requested before payment is due. Art 9 permits conditional filing extension no later than 18 months after the debt arises and extends associated periods by the granted duration; it does not itself establish payment deferral. |
| **Longer periods** | Where the tax period exceeds 1 month, the Inspector may require monthly provisional payment within 15 days after the month: S2 Art 14(3). This is not a general permission to select a longer period. |
| **Departure** | S2 Art 9(3) concerns leaving Sint Maarten as a place of residence or transferring the establishment abroad. It requires immediate returns through the end of tax liability for the listed taxes, including BBO. Do not apply it merely to temporary travel. |
| **Additional assessment** | S2 Art 16 permits recovery of unpaid/underpaid tax on return, including its stated erroneous relief/refund branch. Under Art 17 the 5-year power runs after the end of the calendar year in which the debt arose or refund was granted; the 10-year branch requires bad faith for the specified facts. Filing extensions and other incorporated rules must still be checked. A supplementary assessment is collectable 1 month after its notice date under Art 5(4); collection exceptions remain relevant. |
| **Fines** | S2 Arts 18, 19 and 21 print ceilings ('kan'). S21 then sets the tariff above, which the Inspector may not depart from, subject to H1, H4, H6 and H7. Art 21 requires intent or gross fault and the causally affected unpaid amount. Art 22 requires notice of a proposed serious fine and at least 2 weeks to contest, but non-compliance 'heeft dat niet de nietigheid van de boete tot gevolg'. Art 26 requires a correct and complete return or information before the person knows or reasonably must suspect that the specified officials know or will learn of the defect; it prints a 15% maximum with no base, and S21 Art 8 supplies the percentages and base. Arts 18–28 procedures, time limits and safeguards remain required. No actual fine is determined here. |
| **Criminal** | S1 Art 53 prints a third-category fine for failure of Art 10(3)–(4) or Art 12 duties, consistent with S12 XLIII’s limited amendment. S2 Art 49(1) makes a late, incorrect or incomplete return, among other failures, an offence where prejudice to Sint Maarten could result: up to 6 months or a fourth-category fine or, if the under-levied tax exceeds that fine's amount, up to 1 times the under-levied tax, or both. Art 49(2), with intent: up to 4 years or a fifth-category fine or, if the under-levied tax exceeds that fine's amount, up to 2 times that tax, or both. Art 51 separately refers to duties under Arts 41(2), 42(1) and 44. Under Art 49(3), neither applies where a correct and complete return or information is given before the person knows or must reasonably suspect that the specified officials know or will learn of the defect; whether a case qualifies is not determined here. No criminal fine, category amount, culpability finding or stacking conclusion is determined here. |
| **Records** | S2 Art 43 applies to the specified business/professional natural persons, withholding agents and bodies. It requires records showing rights, duties and relevant tax data, in Dutch, English or Spanish, with the stated inspectability and identification duties and 10-year retention. Its force-majeure production provision is not a retention waiver. |
| **Invoices** | S2 Art 44 applies to the persons/entrepreneurs specified through Art 43(1)(a), (c), independently of BBO invoice-timing permission. Art 44(1) lists number, date, supply date, supplier name/address/registration, description, quantity, consideration and tax; (2) excludes only the BBO tax-amount item in 1(g). Paragraph 3(a) requires issue at supply for its literally referenced category; (3)(b) uses within 15 days after the supply month in other cases. Preserve its printed paragraph-5/category cross-reference anomaly rather than silently substituting paragraph 6. Under (4), agreed advance payments require an invoice before each payment becomes due. Under (5), make and retain invoice copies. The specified retail/hospitality/lottery/designated-category till alternative requires all stated per-transaction particulars and a customer copy. Preserve its other printed cross-reference anomalies. S1 Art 10(3)–(4) separately adds its invoice particulars, timing and copies for permitted invoice timing. |
| **Objection and appeal** | S2 Art 29 distinguishes the 2-month notice-date route for an assessment/appealable decision from the payment/withholding route. A reasoned objection goes to the Inspector. Under Art 30(6), where a required return was not filed or Arts 40–43 duties were not fully met, the assessment stands unless shown to be wrong. Art 31 normally permits appeal within 2 months after the date of the decision copy; its non-decision route has distinct 12-month windows linked to Art 30. Under Art 32 an assessment objection/appeal does not suspend payment. The separate tax-court procedure, extension and non-working-day rules remain held. |
| **Collection** | S1 Arts 41–52 contain collection rules, including allocation, collectability, acceleration, reminders, preferences, interest and liability. Art 51 limits the right to compulsory collection, not every tax right: the 5 years run from the start of the day after the assessment becomes fully collectable or, if later, the start of the day after the last enforcement act for that assessment is served on the tax debtor. Art 51(2) extends the period for payment deferral, the specified court-linked suspension of enforcement (from commencement by summons), suspension of payments and bankruptcy. No expiry date or extinguishment conclusion is calculated. Complete incorporated collection rules and legal interest remain held. |

## Inferences and unresolved readings

- **Base and separately charged tax.** S1 Art 1(1)(g) says everything received. No text read excludes BBO passed on to the customer. Whether 5% applies to an amount including charged BBO is unresolved, so examples use an assumed all-in amount.
- **Due date.** The ordinary 15-day wording gives 15 April after a March period, before testing applicable extensions. S15 identifies conditional non-working-day rules, but requested-period applicability, additional holiday designations and when a filing counts as received remain unqualified. Do not select an actual due date from this reference.
- **Commencement.** S11 separates ordinary commencement from delayed rate application. S23's 11 February 2011 statement and a reported 1 April 2011 date are not accepted as the historical 5% start. S12 has its own ratification-based formula. S3’s formula and CVDR’s 12 April 2023 label remain unreconciled. S15 Art 4(b) expressly excludes periods concerning publication, commencement or cessation of legislation, so its weekend and holiday rules cannot resolve these commencement formulas. No exact operative date is selected.

- **AB31 transitional provisions.** Complete Art II(1) applies the supply-time rate to tax becoming due after ordinance commencement for supplies/services performed before or on that date. Under II(2), where tax is due before commencement for supplies/services performed on or after that date, the additional amount that would arise at the supply-time rate becomes due as the provision states, 'op die datum'. Preserve that literal date linkage until the commencement, supply and tax-point rules have been established; do not select a calendar date here. These transaction-specific branches are separate from Art III(1)’s ordinance commencement and III(2)’s delayed rate application.

- **Currency references.** S19 Art 18 provides a 1:1 reading of existing Netherlands Antillean guilder references in covered instruments subject to the countries’ law from issuance. Preserve both exceptions: contrary nature or purpose and expressly contrary agreement by contracting parties. S20 appoints 31 March 2025 for issuance in SX. This is a currency-reference rule; it does not establish that a particular tax amount remained substantively unchanged, a rounding rule, a foreign-currency tax conversion or the cash withdrawal date. Verify the relied provision, requested period and later law under H1, H4 and H7.

- **Exportondernemer.** Not defined in S1. The 2003 circular was not read.
- **Exemption limit.** S4 141980 Art 1 says the Art 8(8) exemption 'vindt geen toepassing ten aanzien van leveringen en diensten aan toeristen'. Art 8(8) itself refers to the economic zones ordinance, so the title does not contradict it, and S23 also places the limit under Art 8(8). Apply it to Art 8(8) as printed. Whether it extends to Art 8(19) is not established, and neither is its current force: bill 186 proposes withdrawing it.
- **S1 numbering.** Art 27 is printed 3 times where Arts 27-29 would fall. All are repealed.
