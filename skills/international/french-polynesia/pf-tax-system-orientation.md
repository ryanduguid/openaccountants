# French Polynesia Tax System Orientation

Classify the income or transaction under French Polynesia's Code des impôts. The territory uses separate charges for wages and pensions, professional receipts, company profits and consumption. [DICP Code des impôts][CODE] · [French Polynesia investor tax guidance][ADE]

## Residence

Apply the domicile criteria for the relevant charge. The DICP's salary and multiple-income solidarity guidance treats an individual's home or main place of stay in French Polynesia, or a professional activity carried on there, as domicile links. Establish the kind and source of each payment and the applicable payer rules, especially when a payer is outside French Polynesia. [DICP multiple-income solidarity guidance][CSTM] · [Code des impôts, arts 193-1 and LP. 193-10–193-11][CODE]

## Income and territorial scope

French Polynesia has no general tax on all personal income. Salaries, pensions and similar payments can attract the territorial solidarity contribution (CST-S). Test separate charges on investment income and professional receipts for the applicable period. Company tax (IS) applies to profits of in-scope companies headquartered in French Polynesia and profits earned in the territory by local permanent establishments of non-resident companies. [French Polynesia investor tax guidance][ADE] · [DICP company tax][IS]

## Taxpayer type

Establish whether the taxpayer is a company within IS, a partnership with an applicable election or a business within transaction tax (IT). IT uses turnover and differentiated scales. An eligible small sole trader can fall within the separate simplified small-enterprise regime. Check the patente, any non-salaried solidarity contribution and the current thresholds separately. [DICP company tax][IS] · [DICP transaction tax][IT] · [French Polynesia investor tax guidance][ADE]

## Currency

Use Pacific francs (F CFP, currency code XPF). Establish the tax-specific conversion rule before reporting an amount in another currency. [Institut d'émission d'outre-mer][IEOM]

## Assessment periods

The ordinary IS return is due within 3 months of the close, with a 30 April deadline for a 31 December close and a before-1-April rule if no period closed in the year. DICP lists 31 March following the year of receipts or 3 months from an accounting close for the ordinary IT return. Use the dated calendar to check separate instalments, CST and TVA cycles for the actual regime. For salary solidarity reporting, verify the current annex timetable. Loi du pays 2026-9 removed a former electronic annual-annex exception. [DICP company tax][IS] · [DICP transaction tax][IT] · [DICP 2026 calendar][CAL] · [Loi du pays 2026-9, arts LP. 6 and LP. 12][LP9]

## Tax authority and headline rates

The Direction des impôts et des contributions publiques (DICP) administers these taxes. Its IS return identifies 25% as the normal company rate for periods closing on or after 31 December 2022. Review sector rates, the minimum tax and supplementary contributions separately. The ordinary TVA rates are 5%, 13% and 16% according to classification. A special 1% rate covers specified transactions involving archipelagos other than the Society Islands from 1 July 2026. Confirm actual supply locations, the customer's status, the tax point, any earlier amount on which tax was already due and statutory exclusions before using 1%. [DICP IS return, sheet 0-2][ISFORM] · [DICP TVA rates][TVA] · [Loi du pays 2025-38, arts LP. 1 and LP. 21][LP38] · [Loi du pays 2026-9, art LP. 11][LP9] · [DICP archipelago TVA guidance][ISLAND]
