# Oman Corporate Income Tax

> **A second corporate tax has been in force since 1 January 2025, and this guide
> did not mention it.** `om-tax-overview.md` in this same pack carried it; the file
> a reader opens for "what is corporate tax in Oman" did not. A group in scope that
> works from this guide alone will compute 15% under the Income Tax Law and stop,
> missing a separate charge under a separate law with its own return. The top-up
> tax is set out in section *Top-up tax on entities of multinational groups* below,
> read from the Official Gazette.
>
> It does **not** displace the Income Tax Law. Article 10 of the Top-up Tax Law
> makes the Income Tax Law apply residually wherever the new Law and its Regulation
> are silent, so the 15% / 3% / 55% rates below still govern the ordinary charge.

## Corporate income tax rates and base

| Item | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Standard rate | 15% of taxable income for an establishment, Omani company or permanent establishment | Income Tax Law, art. 112 |
| Petroleum | 55% of taxable income from petroleum sales for taxpayers engaged in petroleum exploration | Art. 114 |
| Withholding | 10% of the gross amount for the categories within arts. 40 and 52 | Art. 113 |
| Tax base | Taxable profit after statutory adjustments; resident companies are taxed on worldwide income | [PwC, income determination](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/oman/corporate/income-determination) |

The rate provisions are in the Tax Authority's [English Income Tax Law][om-law-en].

## Qualifying enterprises

Part Five bis applies to the forms defined in article 1(27 bis), subject to article 159 bis. Its basic conditions are:

- Commercial, industrial, vocational or service activity. Sea and air transport, banking, insurance and financial institutions, natural-resource extraction, public-utility franchises and activities excluded by an authorised decision are outside the regime.
- Registered capital at the beginning of the tax year no greater than OMR 50,000.
- Gross income at the end of the tax year no greater than OMR 100,000.
- Average employees during the tax year no greater than 15, counting all workers regardless of the work or pay arrangement.

These conditions appear in [article 159 bis][om-law-en] (English PDF p. 87; Arabic PDF p. 62). Article 159 bis 15 sets tax at 3% of taxable income. Articles 159 bis 12–14 govern income, deductible expenses and losses; recorded actual costs require supporting documents.

**Continuation above the basic limits.** Article 159 bis 5 and [Regulation article 164][om-reg-2019] allow an enterprise to continue within Part Five bis where capital exceeds the statutory limit by no more than 20%, gross income by no more than 50%, or average employees by no more than 10. These produce OMR 60,000, OMR 150,000 and 25 employees. Regulation article 165 calculates average employees as the opening and closing headcounts divided by two. The [Tax Authority's FAQ, question 4][om-faq] gives the larger limits as its general 3% eligibility test. The Regulation uses continuation wording. Confirm eligibility for a new entrant that meets only the larger limits; this guide does not resolve that difference by treating the basic statutory limits as absent.

**Exemption from the 3% tax.** Article 159 bis 15 exempts qualifying enterprises managed full time by their owner or a partner, and qualifying enterprises employing at least two Omanis. [Regulation articles 159–163][om-reg-2019] explain the alternatives: the managing owner or partner must have no employment with another entity; alternatively, at least two Omanis must hold permanent appointments for at least six months of the tax year, supported by employment records. Where the same owner or partner manages several enterprises, the owner or partner must identify the one for the exemption in the return; the Authority may select it if the notice is omitted. Claim the exemption through the return with evidence. It does not excuse filing.

**Industrial exemption.** Article 159 bis 16 applies article 118 to qualifying industrial income. Article 118 limits the exemption to five years from production commencement, excludes project-execution contracts and makes it subject to the applicable conditions and procedures. Article 119 requires an exemption decision. Industrial status alone does not establish a permanent exemption. [Income Tax Law, arts. 118–119 and 159 bis 16][om-law-en].

## Returns and payment

| Taxpayer | Return and payment deadline | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary taxpayer | Before four months expire from the earlier of the tax-year end and the end of the accounting period covered by the return, using the last accounting period where there are several | Art. 140 as replaced by RD 118/2020; payment under amended art. 150 |
| Qualifying enterprise under Part Five bis | Before three months expire from the tax-year end; submit electronically with the required income statement | Arts. 159 bis 17–19 and 22 |

For a calendar year ending 31 December, these ordinarily give 30 April and 31 March respectively. [RD 118/2020][om-rd-2020], decree article IV and attached amendments article II, applies the new article 140 to tax years starting on or after 1 January 2020. The [Tax Authority's FAQ, question 2][om-faq] confirms both deadlines.

**No separate provisional return under the amended ordinary regime.** RD 118/2020 replaces the old articles 138–140 and substitutes a single income return for the former provisional/final terminology. The replacement article 139 concerns penalties on an amended return, not a provisional filing. Article 138 requires an electronic amended return within 30 days after discovering an error or omission, within the article 157 time limit. Tax on the income return is payable at that return's filing deadline under article 150. [RD 118/2020, attached amendments arts. I–II][om-rd-2020].

**The old small-business filing exemption was repealed.** MD 30/2012 articles 134–136 formerly used OMR 20,000 capital, OMR 100,000 gross income and eight employees, with an application and a three-period test. [MD 14/2019, attached amendments article IV(3)][om-reg-2019] repeals the sections allowing exemption from filing the return and from attaching accounts. Use the current enterprise rules above. A tax exemption still requires a return and supporting information, as the [Authority's FAQ][om-faq] confirms.

## Withholding administration

Article 53 requires deduction from the gross amount when an article 52 amount is paid or credited to the recipient's account, whichever happens first. Remit within 14 days after the end of that month. Submit the prescribed statement and send a copy to the recipient. The obligation also applies to ministries, public authorities and other autonomous state bodies. Regulation article 133 identifies Income Tax Form No. 18. [Income Tax Law, art. 53][om-law-en]; [MD 30/2012, art. 133][om-reg-2012].

## Top-up tax on entities of multinational groups

Royal Decree No. 70/2024 issues the *Top-up Tax Law on Entities Affiliated to
Multinational Groups* (*قانون الضريبة التكميلية على الكيانات التابعة للمجموعات
متعددة الجنسيات*), Official Gazette issue 1578. The decree was signed 29 Jumada
al-Akhirah 1446, corresponding to **31 December 2024**, and article 4 of the
decree brings it into force **from 1 January 2025**. The Law is published by the
Oman Tax Authority on its
[Income Tax Law & Regulations page](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations).

- **Who is in scope** — A constituent entity that is a member of a multinational
  group whose revenue, per the ultimate parent entity's consolidated financial
  statements, reaches or exceeds the Omani rial equivalent of **EUR 750,000,000**
  in **at least two of the four** financial years immediately preceding the year
  assessed. Where a financial year in that test is longer or shorter than twelve
  months, the revenue threshold is adjusted proportionally for that year  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 2)_
- **Rate** — The tax is imposed at the rate required to bring the original tax
  imposed on the entity up to the equivalent of **15%**  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 4)_
- **Who pays — limb 1, the domestic charge** — The constituent entity **located in
  Oman** at any point during the financial year. This limb turns on the entity's
  own location, not on where the group's parent sits, and it is the limb that
  reaches an Omani subsidiary of a foreign-parented group  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 5(1))_
- **Who pays — limbs 2 and 3, the Income Inclusion Rule** — An entity located in
  Oman that is the group's **ultimate parent entity**, or an **intermediate parent
  entity** located in Oman, where it holds directly or indirectly an ownership
  interest in a constituent entity subject to a low tax rate. Article 8 confines
  these limbs to a low-taxed constituent entity that is **not located in Oman**  _(Top-up Tax Law, arts. 5(2)–(3) and 8)_
- **When the parent limbs stand down** — Article 5 does not apply where a
  **qualified Income Inclusion Rule** must be applied for the financial year in
  respect of the ultimate parent entity, or of another intermediate parent entity
  holding directly or indirectly a controlling interest in the intermediate parent  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 6)_
- **Partially-owned Omani parent** — Notwithstanding articles 5 and 6, a partially
  owned parent entity located in Oman holding an interest in a low-taxed
  constituent entity pays tax equal to the share allocable to that entity. This
  does not apply where it is wholly owned, directly or indirectly, by another
  partially owned parent entity obliged to apply a qualified Income Inclusion Rule
  for that financial year  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 7)_
- **Excluded entities** — State administrative units and other public legal
  persons; international organisations; non-profit bodies such as associations,
  federations and public-benefit private bodies; pension funds; an investment fund
  that is an ultimate parent entity; a real-estate investment entity that is an
  ultimate parent entity  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 3)_
- **Not in the Law — read the Regulation** — The mechanism for computing the tax,
  the provisions on **safe harbours**, and the rules treating permanent
  establishments as entities separate from the main entity are all delegated to
  the Executive Regulation, which the Chairman of the Tax Authority issues under
  article 2 of the decree. **That Regulation has not been read for this guide.**
  Where neither the Law nor the Regulation provides, the Income Tax Law applies  _(Top-up Tax Law, arts. 9–10)_
- **Separate implementing instrument.** The Top-up Tax Executive Regulation remains
  unread. MD 30/2012 and its MD 14/2019 amendments concern ordinary income tax;
  examining them does not resolve the top-up-tax computation or safe-harbour gap.
