# Oman Corporate Income Tax

> **A second corporate tax has been in force since 1 January 2025, and this guide
> did not mention it.** `om-tax-overview.md` in this same pack carried it; the file
> a reader opens for "what is corporate tax in Oman" did not. A group in scope that
> works from this guide alone will compute 15% under the Income Tax Law and stop,
> missing a separate charge under a separate law with its own return. The top-up
> tax is set out in section *Top-up tax on entities of multinational groups* below,
> read from the Official Gazette.
>
> It does **not** displace the Income Tax Law. Article 10 of the Top-up Tax Law
> makes the Income Tax Law apply residually wherever the new Law and its Regulation
> are silent, so the 15% / 3% / 55% rates below still govern the ordinary charge.

## Corporate income tax rates and base

- **Overview of corporate income tax** — Corporate income tax is Oman's principal direct tax, governed by the Income Tax Law. The standard rate is 15%, with a reduced 3% rate for qualifying Omani SMEs and a special 55% regime for petroleum income.  _(Income Tax Law (Royal Decree No. 28/2009))_
- **Standard corporate income tax rate — 15% confirmed from the Law** — **Article 112**: *"The tax stipulated in this Part shall be computed by applying the rate of **15 %** of the taxable income for any **establishment, Omani company or permanent establishment** for any tax year."* The article was **replaced by RD 9/2017** — the amendment that set this rate, and one this consolidation does incorporate %  _([Income Tax Law, art. 112](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **⚠ The 3% rate is real and sits in its own chapter, but the exclusion attached to it reads backwards** — The Law's small-business chapter provides that *"the tax stipulated in this chapter shall be calculated by applying a **3% rate** on the enterprise's taxable income for any tax year"*, computed on gross income under the general principles of arts. 54–60, 61(1)–(2), 62, 63 and 64(2)–(3), with actual expenses deducted where recorded in the art. 159 bis 9 books and supported by documents, and with losses relieved under arts. 68–69 and 71–74. **The same sentence then adds:** *"**Enterprises managed by their owners or one of their partners on a full-time basis and enterprises which employ at least two Omani persons shall be excluded from the provisions of this Article.**"* **That reads counterintuitively** — owner-managed businesses employing Omanis are the sort a small-business regime usually favours — and the Tax Authority's English text is expressly *"intended for general guidance"* with the **Arabic prevailing**. **Neither reading is asserted here**: it may exclude them from the 3% or from this Article because they are dealt with elsewhere. **A reviewer must settle it against the Arabic before advising**, because it decides whether the commonest kind of Omani small business gets this rate at all  _([Income Tax Law, small-enterprise chapter (Article 159 bis series)](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **Industrial enterprises are exempt, not rated at 3%** — The same chapter exempts an enterprise's income from **industrial activity** under the **GCC Law (System) of the Unified Industrial Organization**, applying **article 118** of the Law. An industrial small enterprise is therefore outside the 3% rather than inside it  _([Income Tax Law, small-enterprise chapter](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **Why no article number is given for the 3%** — Oman's published Law prints marginal article numbers in a **block after** the bodies they label. For arts. 139/140 and 159 bis 17/18 the numbering could be pinned from the Law's own internal cross-references; **for the 3% provision there is no such cross-reference**, and the surrounding marker block covers six numbers (159 bis 11–16) across fewer visible bodies. **Guessing one would be a fabricated citation**, so the chapter is cited and the article is not. The row's earlier hedge — *"registered with the authority for SME development; capital and turnover limits apply"* — finds **no support in the Law's text** read here; it may come from the Executive Regulation or from the SME-authority's own registration rules, neither of which states it in what was read  _(method note; [Income Tax Law](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **Petroleum income tax rate — 55% confirmed, and it is narrower than "petroleum income"** — **Article 114**: *"The tax rate for taxpayers engaged in **petroleum exploration** shall be **55 %** of the taxable income **in respect of any income derived from the sale of petroleum**."* Two limits the row did not state: the rate attaches to taxpayers **engaged in petroleum exploration**, and within those it reaches only income **from the sale of petroleum** — not every receipt of such a taxpayer %  _([Income Tax Law, art. 114](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **Tax base** — Net taxable profit per the financial statements, adjusted for tax purposes; resident companies taxed on worldwide income  _(Income Tax Law (Royal Decree No. 28/2009) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/oman/corporate/income-determination)_
- **Standard withholding tax rate — 10% confirmed, charged on the GROSS amount** — **Article 113**: *"The tax rate referred to in **Article 52** of this Law shall be **10 %** of the **gross amount**."* Article 52 is the charging provision for the listed categories of income paid or credited to a foreign person in the article 40 cases. **"Gross" is the operative word** — there is no deduction of costs against the 10%, so an effective rate on a net margin can be far higher than 10% %  _([Income Tax Law, arts. 52–53, 113](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **⚠ Withholding remittance — 14 days confirmed, but the trigger is "paid ***or credited***", not payment** — **Article 53** requires a taxpayer who pays **or credits to the account** any article 52 amount to deduct the tax from the **gross** amount and remit it *"not later than **fourteen days from the end of the month** in which that amount has been paid or credited, **whichever is earlier**"*. This row said *"the month in which the payment was made"*, which **omits the credit limb**: a book entry crediting a foreign person's account starts the clock even though no cash has moved, and the earlier of the two events governs. The article number is pinned by the Regulation's own cross-reference to *"the statement mentioned in the last paragraph of Article (53) of the Law"*  _([Income Tax Law, art. 53](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **Two withholding obligations the pack did not carry** — Article 53 also requires the remittance to be **accompanied by a statement in the prescribed form**, and *"a copy of that form shall be sent to the **recipient of the payment**"* — a positive obligation towards the payee, not just the authority. **Article 133 of the Executive Regulation** identifies the form: ***Income Tax Form No. 18*** annexed to the Regulation. And the same article extends the duty to **ministries, public authorities, establishments and other autonomous state entities**, so a government payer withholds on the same terms as a commercial one  _([Income Tax Law, art. 53; Executive Regulation (MD 30/2012), art. 133](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **⚠ A small Omani business can be excused the return entirely — three cumulative tests, a three-year look-back, and an application** — **Article 134 of the Executive Regulation** exempts an Omani establishment or company from **submitting the return** where **all three** hold: **capital at the end of the accounting period, as recorded in the Commercial Register, not exceeding RO 20,000**; **gross income for that period not exceeding RO 100,000**; and an **average of not more than 8 employees**, counting **all** workers *"whether occasionally or temporarily recruited — whatsoever nature, kind, location or duration of work assigned to them and the method adopted in determining their wages"*. **The conditions must be met in that period *and* in the two accounting periods ending with the two prior tax years.** **Article 136**: the exemption operates **only by a decision of the Secretary General on application**, which fixes the period and duration and any continuing obligations, and is **withdrawn or cancelled if it involved error**. **Article 135**: it does **not** apply at all where the article 17 notification was not filed. So it is a **three-year test, applied for, granted by decision, and revocable** — not a threshold a company simply falls under  _([Executive Regulation (MD 30/2012), arts. 134–136](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **⚠ These are probably the "capital and turnover limits" the 3% row was reaching for — and they attach to a different thing** — The SME row above hedged that the 3% rate needs *"registered with the authority for SME development; capital and turnover limits apply"*. **Capital and turnover limits do exist in the Omani materials** — RO 20,000 of capital, RO 100,000 of gross income and 8 employees — but they are **article 134's test for being excused the return**, not a condition of the 3% rate, which sits in the Law's Article 159 bis chapter and states no such figures. **Do not import one into the other.** Whether the SME-authority registration point has any source at all remains unestablished  _([Executive Regulation, art. 134; Income Tax Law, Article 159 bis chapter](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **⚠ Annual return deadline — "4 months" is supported by nothing, and Oman has *two* returns, not one** — This row gave *"return and payment due within 4 months of the accounting year-end (approx — confirm; some guides cite up to 6 months)"*. **There is no four-month deadline anywhere in the Income Tax Law.** The Law as the Tax Authority publishes it in English sets two separate filings: a **provisional return within three months** of the end of the tax year (or of the accounting period, whichever ends earlier) — **article 139** — and a **final return within six months** on the same measuring rule — **article 140**. The hedge named the right alternative and the row's own figure was wrong; more seriously, **a reader of this guide had no reason to know a provisional return exists at all**. **⚠ This is the rule for ordinary taxpayers and NOT for the small-*enterprise* regime** — see the next two rows, which run the other way  _([Income Tax Law (RD 28/2009), arts. 139–140, English version published by the Tax Authority](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **⚠ An "enterprise" files no provisional return at all, and its final return is due in THREE months** — The Law carries a separate small-business chapter (the **Article 159 bis** series). **Article 159 bis 17**: *"**The enterprise shall not be obliged to submit the provisional return of income** but the final return of income for any tax year shall be submitted **electronically** according to the form prepared by the Secretariat General"*. **Article 159 bis 18**: that final return *"shall be submitted **before the lapse of three months** starting from the date of the end of this year"*. **Article 159 bis 22**: the tax on it is payable **within the same time limit as the return**. So the enterprise regime inverts both halves of the ordinary rule — **no provisional return, and a three-month final return instead of six**. The article numbers here are pinned by the Law's own cross-reference (*"the final return stipulated in Article 159 bis 17"*), not by the PDF's offset marginal numbering  _([Income Tax Law, arts. 159 bis 17, 159 bis 18, 159 bis 22](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **Also in the enterprise chapter** — **Article 159 bis 19**: the final return must be accompanied by a **statement of income** on the Secretariat General's form, prepared from the books kept under article 159 bis 9. **Article 159 bis 20** applies the ordinary article 142 sample-investigation procedure to enterprises' returns, and **159 bis 21** applies the ordinary assessment machinery  _([Income Tax Law, arts. 159 bis 19–21](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **Payment follows each return's own filing date** — The tax shown on the **provisional** return is payable on the date that return is due; the tax on the **final** return is payable on the final return's due date, **after crediting the tax already paid on the provisional return**. Tax arising on an assessment runs on its own clock. So "return and payment" share a date in Oman, but there are two such dates in a year, not one  _([Income Tax Law, Part Five, Ch. Three, s. One (arts. 149 bis–151)](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **⚠ The six months may already be superseded, and the authority's own English materials cannot tell you** — Two things are true together. First, the English consolidation of the Law that the Tax Authority publishes **incorporates amendments only up to RD 9/2017** — it cites that decree 61 times and nothing later. Second, **Royal Decree 118/2020 expressly amends article 140**: its commencement clause singles the article out, providing that the decree takes effect the day after publication *"with the exception of article 140 … which comes into force from the tax year starting on 1 January 2020, or the tax years following it."* **The amendment's text is not published in English.** The Authority's English PDF of RD 118/2020 carries a translator's note saying it is an *"initial draft translation [that] includes only amendments made to Articles related to International treaties / Exchange of Information matters"* and directs the reader to the Arabic; its operative articles replace or add arts. 6(2), 16 bis, 18 bis, 23 bis, 28, 29, 70, 120 and bis variants, 131 bis, 181 and 185 — **article 140 appears in it exactly once, in the commencement clause**. The only Arabic decree on that page is RD 9/2017. **So: three months and six months are what the published Law says; article 140 is proven to have been amended in 2020; and what it now says is not established here.** Do not quote six months as the current rule without checking the Arabic RD 118/2020. [RESEARCH GAP — read the Arabic RD 118/2020 art. 140. Note the same caveat formally reaches **article 139** too, since the English consolidation predates the decree for both; only article 140 is *proven* amended.]  _([RD 118/2020, art. IV and Articles One–Two, as published in English](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **The Income Tax Executive Regulation has been read, and it is MD 30/2012** — The English *Executive Regulation of the Income Tax Law* the Authority publishes is **Ministerial Decision No. 30/2012**, and it requires the **auditor's report within six months following the end of the financial year**, consistent with the article 140 rhythm. **It is not the latest instrument**: the same page also carries **Ministerial Decision No. 14/2019**, whose Arabic title is *"issuing amendments to some provisions of the Executive Regulation of the Income Tax Law"* and which refers to MD 30/2012 — so 14/2019 **amends** 30/2012 rather than replacing it. **MD 14/2019 is Arabic-only and its text layer is a legacy Arabic font that extracts as Latin mojibake**, the same defect as Oman's Top-up Tax Law gazette PDF, so it has not been read. It amends the *Regulation*, not the Law, so it cannot be the source of any change to article 140. [RESEARCH GAP — MD 14/2019 should be rendered and read for changes to the Regulation's filing mechanics.]  _([Executive Regulation (MD 30/2012) and MD 14/2019](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_
- **Provisional return — the vagueness here was gesturing at something real, and it is a carve-out, not a repeal** — This row said a provisional return *"is historically required early in the year (the formal two-stage filing has been streamlined under recent practice — confirm current requirement)"*. **The two-stage filing has not been streamlined away.** It stands for ordinary taxpayers at **articles 139 and 140** — three months provisional, six months final. What is true is that **the Article 159 bis enterprise regime disapplies the provisional return entirely** (art. 159 bis 17) and shortens the final return to three months (art. 159 bis 18). So the guide had the right intuition attached to the wrong scope: not a general change in practice, but **a separate regime for small enterprises**  _([Income Tax Law, arts. 139–140 and 159 bis 17–18](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations))_

## Top-up tax on entities of multinational groups

Royal Decree No. 70/2024 issues the *Top-up Tax Law on Entities Affiliated to
Multinational Groups* (*قانون الضريبة التكميلية على الكيانات التابعة للمجموعات
متعددة الجنسيات*), Official Gazette issue 1578. The decree was signed 29 Jumada
al-Akhirah 1446, corresponding to **31 December 2024**, and article 4 of the
decree brings it into force **from 1 January 2025**. The Law is published by the
Oman Tax Authority on its
[Income Tax Law & Regulations page](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/portal/web/taxportal/income-tax-law-regulations).

- **Who is in scope** — A constituent entity that is a member of a multinational
  group whose revenue, per the ultimate parent entity's consolidated financial
  statements, reaches or exceeds the Omani rial equivalent of **EUR 750,000,000**
  in **at least two of the four** financial years immediately preceding the year
  assessed. Where a financial year in that test is longer or shorter than twelve
  months, the revenue threshold is adjusted proportionally for that year  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 2)_
- **Rate** — The tax is imposed at the rate required to bring the original tax
  imposed on the entity up to the equivalent of **15%**  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 4)_
- **Who pays — limb 1, the domestic charge** — The constituent entity **located in
  Oman** at any point during the financial year. This limb turns on the entity's
  own location, not on where the group's parent sits, and it is the limb that
  reaches an Omani subsidiary of a foreign-parented group  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 5(1))_
- **Who pays — limbs 2 and 3, the Income Inclusion Rule** — An entity located in
  Oman that is the group's **ultimate parent entity**, or an **intermediate parent
  entity** located in Oman, where it holds directly or indirectly an ownership
  interest in a constituent entity subject to a low tax rate. Article 8 confines
  these limbs to a low-taxed constituent entity that is **not located in Oman**  _(Top-up Tax Law, arts. 5(2)–(3) and 8)_
- **When the parent limbs stand down** — Article 5 does not apply where a
  **qualified Income Inclusion Rule** must be applied for the financial year in
  respect of the ultimate parent entity, or of another intermediate parent entity
  holding directly or indirectly a controlling interest in the intermediate parent  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 6)_
- **Partially-owned Omani parent** — Notwithstanding articles 5 and 6, a partially
  owned parent entity located in Oman holding an interest in a low-taxed
  constituent entity pays tax equal to the share allocable to that entity. This
  does not apply where it is wholly owned, directly or indirectly, by another
  partially owned parent entity obliged to apply a qualified Income Inclusion Rule
  for that financial year  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 7)_
- **Excluded entities** — State administrative units and other public legal
  persons; international organisations; non-profit bodies such as associations,
  federations and public-benefit private bodies; pension funds; an investment fund
  that is an ultimate parent entity; a real-estate investment entity that is an
  ultimate parent entity  _(Top-up Tax Law, art. 3)_
- **Not in the Law — read the Regulation** — The mechanism for computing the tax,
  the provisions on **safe harbours**, and the rules treating permanent
  establishments as entities separate from the main entity are all delegated to
  the Executive Regulation, which the Chairman of the Tax Authority issues under
  article 2 of the decree. **That Regulation has not been read for this guide.**
  Where neither the Law nor the Regulation provides, the Income Tax Law applies  _(Top-up Tax Law, arts. 9–10)_
- **⚠ Two different "Executive Regulations" — do not read this gap as closed** — The
  instrument named in the row above is the **Top-up Tax** Executive Regulation, issued
  by the **Chairman of the Tax Authority**, and it remains unread. It is **not** the
  ***Executive Regulation of the Income Tax Law*, Ministerial Decision No. 30/2012**,
  which this guide now cites at articles 133–136 and which the Tax Authority publishes
  in English. A reader who sees "the Executive Regulation" cited elsewhere in this file
  should check which of the two is meant. MD 30/2012 is itself amended by **Ministerial
  Decision No. 14/2019**, Arabic-only with a legacy-font text layer, also unread  _(method note)_
