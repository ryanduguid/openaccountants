# Bahamas Corporate Income Tax

The Department of Inland Revenue (DIR) describes The Bahamas as having no general corporate income tax in its 2026 Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax (DMTT) return guide. The [Bahamas tax overview](bs-tax-overview.md) covers the separate Business Licence, VAT and property tax regimes. [DIR return guide, section 8.2.2][RETURN-GUIDE]

## DMTT scope and first fiscal year

Under the incorporated GloBE Model Rules, a multinational enterprise group generally meets the revenue test if consolidated revenue is at least EUR 750 million in at least 2 of the 4 fiscal years immediately preceding the tested year. Adjust the threshold for periods of a different length and check the excluded-entity and location rules. Section 2(3) deems an entity other than a flow-through entity resident in The Bahamas if it was formed or is effectively managed there. [Act 58 of 2024, ss 2–4][ACT-2024] · [GloBE Model Rules, arts 1.1, 1.5 and 10.3][GLOBE]

The Act is deemed in force from 1 January 2024. For a fiscal year beginning in 2024, it applies only if an income inclusion rule (IIR) or undertaxed profits rule (UTPR) is required to be applied for that fiscal year in respect of each constituent entity located in The Bahamas. Apply the ordinary scope test to fiscal years starting from 1 January 2025. [Act 58 of 2024, s 1][ACT-2024]

The 15% GloBE minimum rate is used in the top-up calculation. Section 5 makes the Bahamian constituent entities of an in-scope group jointly and severally liable and uses the jurisdictional top-up calculation in GloBE article 5.2.3 with the modifications in section 5(2). Chapter 5 sets out the effective tax rate, excess profit and substance-based income exclusion calculations. Section 5 also addresses minority-owned entities and joint ventures. Use the GloBE rules and administrative guidance applicable to the fiscal year under the Act. [Act 58 of 2024, ss 3–5][ACT-2024] · [GloBE Model Rules, ch 5 and art 10.1][GLOBE]

## Reporting and registration

From 1 July 2026, an in-scope Bahamian constituent entity must file with the Financial Secretary, or have filed on its behalf, a GloBE Information Return and applicable notifications under articles 8.1 and 9.4, or a prescribed return. This duty applies even if no DMTT is payable. The Model Rules allow 18 months after fiscal year-end for the GloBE Information Return and applicable notifications in the transition year and generally 15 months otherwise. Confirm the form and deadline for a separately prescribed return. An entity asserting dual residence and location in the other jurisdiction under article 10.3.4 must notify the Financial Secretary and supply the evidence the Secretary requires. [Act 25 of 2026, ss 1–2][ACT-2026] · [GloBE Model Rules, arts 8.1.6 and 9.4.1][GLOBE]

The DIR's initial pre-registration instructions gave 31 March 2026 for form DMTT-24 for fiscal years beginning in 2024 and 30 April 2026 for form DMTT-25 for fiscal years beginning in 2025. Entities in scope for both starting years were instructed to submit both forms. The DIR's return guide describes a separate workflow in One Tax Bahamas. Check that registration and the user's filing role are active and the filing period is open. Validate and submit the return, then download the submission confirmation. [DIR notification instructions, 'Practical Tips'][NOTIFICATIONS] · [DIR return guide, 'Prerequisites' and 'Return Submission Confirmation'][RETURN-GUIDE]

## Payment and instalments

Section 7(1) gives an 18-month payment period from fiscal year-end for the group's transition year in The Bahamas and a 15-month period otherwise. From 1 July 2026, taxes, penalties, interest and other amounts under the Act are payable in Bahamian or US dollars into the Consolidated Fund. [Act 58 of 2024, s 7(1)][ACT-2024] · [Act 25 of 2026, ss 1 and 3][ACT-2026]

From 1 July 2026, section 7A sets the immediately preceding fiscal year's DMTT liability as the instalment base for constituent entities liable for that year. The instalments are payable in prescribed periods. If the amount paid falls short of the statutory instalments and the base is below the current year's DMTT liability, a penalty and prescribed interest apply to the deficiency. The balance after instalments is due by the fiscal year's filing due date. The Financial Secretary determines how an excess is refunded or applied against other liabilities under the Act. Confirm the prescribed instalment periods, interest and payment process before remitting. [Act 25 of 2026, ss 1 and 4][ACT-2026]

## Business Licence credit

Section 61A of the Business Licence Act, inserted by Act 20 of 2025 and deemed in operation from 1 January 2024, allows an eligible person to credit DMTT paid against Business Licence tax. The DMTT fiscal year must start in the Business Licence assessment year. The credit is capped at the Business Licence tax assessed for that year. An allowed credit above tax owing carries forward to succeeding years. The DIR also states that Business Licence tax is outside the GloBE Covered Taxes entries in its DMTT return guide. [Business Licence (Amendment) Act 20 of 2025, ss 1–3][LICENCE-2025] · [DIR return guide, section 8.2.2][RETURN-GUIDE]

## Evidence still needed

Confirm the prescribed return choice and deadline, the section 7A instalment calendar and payment procedures, the Business Licence credit claim process and current assessment, objection and appeal procedures before filing or paying. The guide covers DMTT and its Business Licence credit. Verify withholding and other cross-border tax obligations separately for the payment and period in question.
