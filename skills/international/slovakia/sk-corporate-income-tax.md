# Slovakia Corporate Income Tax

## Corporate income tax (2025)

- **Overview of CIT structure** — Slovak corporate income tax (daň z príjmov právnických osôb) moved to a three-tier rate structure from 1 January 2025, with a reduced 10% rate for small companies and a new top 24% rate for the largest taxpayers. Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income; non-residents on Slovak-source income.  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.))_
- **Reduced CIT rate (small companies)** — 10% percent (where taxable income does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the tax period)  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
- **Standard CIT rate** — 21% percent (where taxable income is between EUR 100,000 and EUR 5,000,000)  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
- **Top CIT rate (large taxpayers)** — 24% percent (where taxable income exceeds EUR 5,000,000)  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
- **Tax base** — Accounting profit adjusted for tax purposes (non-deductible items added back, exempt items removed)  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/income-determination)_
- **Minimum tax (tax licence)** — Five bands by **taxable revenues** (zdaniteľné príjmy), not by taxable profit and not "EUR 340 up to EUR 1,540": **EUR 340** up to EUR 50,000, **EUR 960** from 50,001 to 250,000, **EUR 1,920** from 250,001 to 500,000, **EUR 3,840** from 500,001 to 5,000,000, and **EUR 11,520** above EUR 5,000,000. The top band is new for 2026. The base matters: a company with high revenues and little or no profit still lands in a high band, which is the point of a minimum tax. Payable whether the company profits or loses; a newly formed company does not pay it in its first tax period, and an excess over the computed tax can be credited against the next three periods  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.); [Financial Administration of the Slovak Republic, methodological guidance on the minimum corporate tax, 18 August 2026](https://www.financnasprava.sk/_img/pfsedit/Dokumenty_PFS/Zverejnovanie_dok/Dane/Metodicke_pokyny/Priame_dane_uct/2026/2026.08.18_012_DZPaU_2026_MP_Minim_dan_PO.pdf) — the EUR 960 band and the taxable-revenues base come from this guidance, not from the secondary summary)_
- **WHT on dividends to companies/non-residents** — 0% to corporate shareholders in cooperating states and 35% to non-cooperating jurisdictions, which is what this line has always said and is right as far as it goes. **The full table is wider:** the source publishes **7%, 10%, 19% and 35%** as possible dividend rates depending on the recipient and the profit year, and dividends out of 2004–2016 profits are outside Slovak withholding altogether. Establish the recipient type and the year the profits arose before quoting 0%; the individual-shareholder rates in particular need confirming against the Income Tax Act, because they have moved more than once percent  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **WHT on interest** — 19% / 35% percent (19% standard; 35% to non-cooperating jurisdictions; reduced/0% under EU Interest-Royalty Directive or treaty)  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **WHT on royalties** — 19% / 35% percent (19% standard; 35% to non-cooperating jurisdictions; reduced/0% under EU Interest-Royalty Directive or treaty)  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **WHT on fees for services** — **19% / 35%**, on the same split as royalties and interest. This head was missing from the guide until September 2026, and it has its own source rule: a service fee is taxable in Slovakia **only if the service is provided in Slovak territory, or the payer is a Slovak resident**. Both limbs are alternatives, so a Slovak payer buying a service performed entirely abroad is still inside the charge percent  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **The 35% rate has three separate triggers** — 35% applies where there is **no double tax treaty** with the payee's state, **or** the state is on the EU list of non-cooperating jurisdictions, **or** the **beneficial owner cannot be identified**. The third is the one to plan for: it turns on the payer's evidence rather than on the payee's country, so a treaty partner can still attract 35% if the beneficial owner is not established percent  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **WHT remittance and notification deadline** — The Slovak payer remits the tax **within 15 days of the end of the month following the month of payment** and notifies the tax authority. The notification is a separate obligation from the payment percent  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **CIT return filing deadline** — Within 3 months after the end of the tax period (31 March for a calendar year); extendable by 3 months (6 for foreign income)  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/tax-administration)_
- **CIT payment deadline** — Tax due payable by the filing deadline; advance payments quarterly or monthly depending on prior-year tax  _(Income Tax Act (Act No. 595/2003 Coll.) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/slovak-republic/corporate/tax-administration)_
