# Ghana Corporate Income Tax

## Scope and periods

Identify the taxpayer, residence, permanent establishment, activity, year of assessment and accounting basis period before selecting a rate or filing date. This guide reconciles selected original Income Tax Act, 2015 (Act 896) provisions, selected Income Tax (Amendment) Act, 2023 (Act 1094) provisions and GRA guidance checked on 11 November 2026. It remains Tier 2 and pending qualified review. The complete amendment chain, specialist regimes and current operative application are not certified.

The original Act 896 text and the 2023 amendment must be read separately. GRA's minimum-chargeable-income note was issued on 20 March 2024 and states implementation from 1 May 2023. That is the note's implementation statement, not a finding that assent or issue date alone proves statutory commencement. [Act 896](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf); [Act 1094](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf); [GRA minimum-income practice note, cover and section 6](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Practice-Note-on-the-Application-of-Minimum-Chargeabe-Income-under-the-Income-Tax-Act-2015-Act-896.pdf).

## Tax base, residence and source

Original Act 896 section 2 defines chargeable income by assessable income less deductions allowed under the Act and requires separate source calculations. Section 3 includes a resident person's employment, business and investment income without the non-resident Ghana-source limitation. For a non-resident, section 3(2)(b) includes Ghana-source income and, where there is a Ghanaian permanent establishment, income connected with that establishment irrespective of source. Do not exclude foreign-source income merely because the company is non-resident, or assume every foreign receipt is connected with its Ghanaian establishment. [Act 896, ss 2 and 3, PDF p 9](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).

Original section 101(4) treats a company as resident if incorporated under the Companies Act, 1963 (Act 179), **or** if management and control of its affairs are exercised in Ghana at any time during the year. These are alternatives. The original incorporation reference must be reconciled with successor company law and later amendments; it is not silently replaced here with Act 992. Permanent-establishment definition, income attribution, treaty residence and foreign-tax credits require separate checks. [Act 896, s 101(4), PDF p 77](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).

For deductions, original section 9 requires the expense to be wholly, exclusively and necessarily incurred in producing the business or investment income. Capital expenditure is subject to its own treatment; section 14 and the Third Schedule govern capital allowances. Company tax accounting is on an accrual basis under section 19(3). These principles do not verify every asset class, financial-cost restriction, foreign-exchange item, related-party adjustment or documentary requirement. [Act 896, ss 8, 9, 14 and 19](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).

Act 1094 section 3 replaces section 17(1) with a deduction for unrelieved business losses from the previous **5 years of assessment**. Apply the relevant source, activity and other loss restrictions; this is not an unlimited loss carry-forward or a review of every remaining subsection. [Act 1094, s 3, PDF p 3](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf).

## Selected rates and source conflicts

GRA's corporate-income-tax page, checked on 11 November 2026, gives a general rate of **25%**. Original First Schedule paragraph 3 and the paragraph 3(1) replacement in Act 1094 section 13(b) must also be considered, with the relevant sector and concession rules. [GRA CIT guidance](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/corporate-income-tax/); [Act 896, First Schedule](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf); [Act 1094, s 13(b), PDF p 9](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf).

| Income or activity | Selected rate | Scope and source checked |
|---|---|---|
| General company income | 25% | Subject to the applicable sector, concession and other statutory rules |
| Trust income | 25% | Original First Schedule paragraph 2 and GRA CIT table; a trust is a separate taxpayer category |
| Company principally engaged in the hotel industry | 22% | Original paragraph 3(2) and GRA CIT table |
| Income from export of non-traditional goods | 8% | Original paragraph 3(3) and definition in 3(7); not every exported receipt |
| Financial-institution income from the specified farming or leasing-company loans | 20% | Original paragraphs 3(4) and (5) contain use-of-loan conditions; not a rate for all bank income |
| Income from petroleum or mineral operations | 35% | Original paragraphs 5 and 6 and GRA CIT table; separate operational/ring-fencing rules remain |
| Free Zone exports after the applicable concessionary period | 15% | Original paragraph 4; registration, concession period and export eligibility remain separate |
| Free Zone domestic-market income after the applicable concessionary period | 25% | Act 1094 paragraph 3(1) replacement and GRA CIT table; do not extend the export rate to domestic income |

Table sources: [Act 896, First Schedule paragraphs 2 to 6, PDF pp 116 to 118](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf); [Act 1094, s 13(b)](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf); [GRA CIT page](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/corporate-income-tax/), checked on 11 November 2026. These selected rates do not establish all exemptions, levies, eligibility or subsequent changes.

### Manufacturing location formula

Original First Schedule paragraph 3(6) gives **75% of the ordinary reference rate** for the regional-capital case and **50%** for the elsewhere case. With a 25% reference rate, those calculations are **18.75%** and **12.5%**. GRA's business-exemptions page identifies regional capitals other than Accra/Tema and locations outside regional capitals at those rates. The CIT page instead prints 18.5% for the regional-capital case; that figure does not match the reviewed statutory formula. [Act 896, First Schedule 3(6), PDF p 117](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf); [GRA business exemptions](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/tax-exemptions-for-businesses/); [GRA CIT page](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/corporate-income-tax/), both checked on 11 November 2026.

Paragraph 3(6) expressly refers to exclusions under subparagraphs (1) and (3). Its interaction with the broadly worded ordinary-rate provision, qualifying manufacturing activity, plant location and later amendments require confirmation. Do not treat a postcode alone as proof of a reduced rate.

### Sixth Schedule concession rate

Act 1094 section 13(c) replaces First Schedule paragraph 7 with **5% of chargeable income** for a person entitled to a Sixth Schedule concession. GRA's business-exemptions table also prints 5%, whilst that page's introductory prose and the CIT page say 1%. The reviewed amendment supports 5%; the conflicting 1% summaries do not establish entitlement or a later change. Verify the specific concession, duration, conditions, operative period and subsequent law before applying relief. [Act 1094, s 13(c), PDF p 9](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf); [GRA business exemptions](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/tax-exemptions-for-businesses/); [GRA CIT guidance](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/corporate-income-tax/).

## Minimum chargeable income

Act 1094 section 1 inserts section 2A. A person **may be required** to compute and pay tax on minimum chargeable income of **5% of turnover** where that person has declared losses for the previous 5 years of assessment. It excludes a person within the first 5 years of operations **or engaged in farming**. The 5% is an income base, not a flat corporate tax rate on turnover. [Act 1094, s 1, PDF pp 2 to 3](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf).

GRA practice note GRA/AG/2024/001, issued 20 March 2024, interprets the preceding period as 5 consecutive tax-loss years and gives implementation from 1 May 2023. Its examples distinguish tax losses from operating results and show that mixed tax-profit and tax-loss years do not satisfy the condition. Apply its complete facts before deciding eligibility; an accounting loss alone does not settle the tax-loss test. [GRA note, sections 2, 4 and 5.7, PDF pp 3 to 5 and 16](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Practice-Note-on-the-Application-of-Minimum-Chargeabe-Income-under-the-Income-Tax-Act-2015-Act-896.pdf).

Section 4.3 of the note says tax paid under this rule cannot be carried forward as a tax credit or set against another assessment year's income tax. Section 4.4 preserves loss carry-forward under section 17 despite the minimum-income rule; it does not renew an expired loss. Segmented activities, different rates, carried-loss interactions and the full later-law position need separate review. [GRA note, sections 4.3 and 4.4, PDF pp 5 to 6](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Practice-Note-on-the-Application-of-Minimum-Chargeabe-Income-under-the-Income-Tax-Act-2015-Act-896.pdf).

## Selected income withholding

The following are GRA income-withholding webpage positions captured on 11 November 2026. The page also contains older VAT material, which is not used here. Apply payer, recipient, source, exemption, treaty and permanent-establishment tests before choosing a rate. Complete statutory amendment and treaty checks remain open. [GRA withholding guidance](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/withholding-tax/).

| Payment | GRA rate | Important scope |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 8% | Resident and non-resident tables; finality concerns dividends paid by a resident company |
| Interest | 8% | Resident table excludes individuals and resident financial institutions; non-resident table excludes individuals |
| Royalties and natural-resource payments | 15% | Resident and non-resident tables |
| Management and technical service fees to a non-resident | 20% | Finality has the Ghanaian-PE exception below |
| Contract supplies to a resident payer other than an individual | Goods 3%; works 5%; entity services 7.5% | Payment to another resident person; relevant table rows say exceeding GHS 2,000 per annum |

The annual threshold is strictly **exceeding** GHS 2,000. The reviewed page does not resolve all aggregation or threshold-crossing mechanics and does not say tax applies only to the excess. Its separate general-services-by-an-individual row states 7.5% without that entity-services threshold. Do not apply the entity threshold to every service payment. [GRA withholding guidance, resident table and contract-supply discussion](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/withholding-tax/).

GRA requires the prescribed statement **and payment** of tax withheld within **15 days after month end**, with a withholding certificate for the recipient. For a contract with a non-resident to supply goods, works or services, it also describes notice to the Commissioner-General within **30 days of the contract date**. Current forms and complete statutory exceptions remain to be checked. [GRA withholding guidance](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/withholding-tax/).

Non-resident payments subject to withholding are described as final except payments derived through a Ghanaian permanent establishment. Non-final withholding can produce a credit; final tax must not automatically be treated as creditable. Treaty relief is not automatic: identify an applicable treaty and verify recipient eligibility, beneficial ownership where relevant, procedural requirements and the particular article before reducing a domestic rate. This batch verifies no treaty-specific rate. [GRA withholding guidance, final payments and non-final credit](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/withholding-tax/); [Act 896, ss 119 and 120](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).

## Company periods, estimates and returns

Original Act 896 section 18 distinguishes the calendar year of assessment from a company's accounting-year basis period. A change of company accounting year requires approval and affects payment timing. GRA's practice note DT/2016/007, issued on 6 October 2016, requires an application and transition calculations that avoid gaps or overlaps. [Act 896, s 18, PDF p 21](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf); [GRA accounting-date note, sections 4.2 to 4.5](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Practice-Note-on-Change-in-Accounting-Date.pdf).

Under original section 121(2)(a), a 12-month basis period starting at the beginning of a calendar month has instalments due on the last days of months **3, 6, 9 and 12**. Section 121(2)(b) has a separate rule for other cases. Original section 122 requires the estimate by the first instalment payment date, and a revised estimate affects later instalments under section 122(6). GRA's current CIT page gives March/June/September/December dates; those are not a universal timetable for every accounting period. [Act 896, ss 121 and 122, PDF pp 94 to 97](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf); [GRA CIT guidance](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/corporate-income-tax/).

Original section 124(1), subject to section 125, states a return deadline of **4 months after the end of each year of assessment**. It does not literally say 'accounting year'. Confirm the year/basis-period mapping, approved changes, current prescribed return and any applicable notice before calculating a non-calendar company's exact deadline. Company tax returns are separate from ORC annual returns. [Act 896, ss 124 and 125, PDF pp 97 to 98](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf); [GRA accounting-date note](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Practice-Note-on-Change-in-Accounting-Date.pdf).

Original Revenue Administration Act, 2016 (Act 915), section 30 permits a reasoned written extension application before the filing due date. Approval is discretionary, total extensions cannot exceed **60 days** and a filing extension does **not** move the payment date. Later administration amendments and any case-specific decision still need checking. [Act 915, s 30](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Revenue-Administration-Act-2016-.pdf).

## Worked checks

These are fictional comparisons with the identified source rules, not complete current-year assessments. Assume the stated rates and eligibility apply and separately verify levies, credits, restrictions and operative periods.

1. **Ordinary chargeable income:** GHS 1,000,000 assessable income less GHS 600,000 allowed deductions gives GHS 400,000 chargeable income. At 25%, tax is GHS 100,000 before other adjustments. [Act 896, s 2 and First Schedule paragraph 3](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).
2. **Manufacturing formula:** On GHS 100,000 chargeable income, a qualifying 75%-of-25% case gives GHS 18,750; the qualifying 50%-of-25% case gives GHS 12,500. This checks the formula, not eligibility. [Act 896, First Schedule 3(6)](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).
3. **Minimum income:** Following GRA's 2023 illustration, assume GHS 8,000,000 turnover, the required preceding tax-loss history, no first-5-year/farming exclusion and a 25% applicable company rate. Minimum chargeable income is GHS 400,000 and tax is GHS 100,000. GHS 400,000 is the income base. [GRA note, illustration 3, PDF p 8](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Practice-Note-on-the-Application-of-Minimum-Chargeabe-Income-under-the-Income-Tax-Act-2015-Act-896.pdf).
4. **Period check:** Under original section 121(2)(a), a 12-month basis period from 1 April 2026 has the relevant month ends on 30 June 2026, 30 September 2026, 31 December 2026 and 31 March 2027. This illustrates the original month rule only; confirm operative law, approval and deadline adjustments before filing or paying. [Act 896, s 121(2)(a)](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).
