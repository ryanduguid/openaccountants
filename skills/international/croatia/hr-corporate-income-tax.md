# Croatia Corporate Income Tax

## Corporate (profit) tax rates and base

- **Standard corporate profit tax rate** — 18% %  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
- **Reduced corporate profit tax rate (small taxpayers)** — 10% for taxpayers with annual revenue below EUR 1,000,000 %  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
- **Revenue threshold for reduced rate** — EUR 1,000,000 annual revenue EUR  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
- **Tax base** — Accounting profit (per Croatian/IFRS accounting standards) adjusted for tax-non-deductible items and tax incentives  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/income-determination)_
- **Corporate residence** — A company is resident if it is incorporated in Croatia or has its place of effective management/control there  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/corporate-residence)_
- **Withholding tax on dividends to non-residents** — 10% (general) % (may be reduced/exempt under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or a tax treaty)  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **Withholding tax on dividends to non-cooperative jurisdictions** — 25% where paid to entities in EU-blacklisted non-cooperative jurisdictions with no tax treaty %  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **Withholding tax on interest and royalties to non-residents** — 15% (general); 25% to non-cooperative jurisdictions % (may be reduced/exempt under EU Interest & Royalties Directive or a tax treaty)  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **Withholding tax on certain services to non-residents** — **Market research, tax consulting, business consulting and audit services belong to the 25% list, not the 15% one.** Those four are withheld at **25%** when paid to a non-resident established in an **EU non-cooperative jurisdiction** with which Croatia has no tax treaty. The general **15%** covers fees for the use of copyright and other intellectual property rights — reproduction rights, patents, licences, trademarks, designs and models, manufacturing procedures, production formulas, blueprints, plans, industrial or scientific experience — and interest, with exceptions. This line previously attached the four advisory categories to the general 15%, which over-withholds on an ordinary non-resident adviser (where no service withholding is due at all) and under-withholds by ten points on a blacklisted one percent  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **Tax loss carryforward** — Losses may be carried forward for up to 5 years; no carryback  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/deductions)_
- **Corporate profit tax return (Form PD) deadline** — Within 4 months of fiscal year-end (by 30 April for calendar-year filers)  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/tax-administration)_
- **Corporate profit tax payment deadline** — Final liability due by the return deadline (30 April for calendar-year filers); monthly advance payments during the year based on prior return  _(Corporate Profit Tax Act (Zakon o porezu na dobit) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/corporate/tax-administration)_

## The global minimum tax — a second charge this guide did not mention at all

Croatia has had a minimum global profit tax since **31 December 2023** and this guide
carried nothing about it. Everything below is read from the Croatian text in *Narodne
novine*, not from a summary. Three instruments are in force:

| Instrument | Gazette | Date |
|---|---|---|
| *Zakon o minimalnom globalnom porezu na dobit* (the Act) | **NN 155/2023**, no. 2362 | 22 December 2023 |
| *Zakon o izmjenama i dopunama…* (amending Act) | **NN 151/2025**, no. 2260 | 15 December 2025 |
| *Pravilnik o minimalnom globalnom porezu na dobit* (the ordinance) | **NN 53/2026**, no. 665 | 22 May 2026 |

- **The instrument and what it transposes** — *Zakon o minimalnom globalnom porezu na dobit*, passed by the Hrvatski sabor on 15 December 2023 and in force **31 December 2023** (art. 64). Article 2 states in terms that it transposes **Council Directive (EU) 2022/2523 of 14 December 2022**  _([NN 155/2023, no. 2362, arts. 2 and 64](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2023_12_155_2362.html))_
- **Who is in scope** — Constituent entities of an MNE group **or of a large domestic group** — a purely Croatian group of sufficient size is caught — whose annual revenue in the ultimate parent's consolidated financial statements is **EUR 750,000,000.00 or more in at least two of the four fiscal years immediately preceding** the tested year, **including** the revenue of entities excluded under art. 5(3). A year longer or shorter than twelve months is adjusted proportionally  _([NN 155/2023, art. 5(1)–(2)](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2023_12_155_2362.html))_
- **Minimum rate** — Article 4(1) point 15 defines *minimalna porezna stopa* as a rate of **petnaest posto (15 %)** — fifteen per cent, given in words and digits together. Article 5(6): the top-up is the amount that brings effective taxation of profit to at least 15%  _([NN 155/2023, arts. 4(1)15 and 5(6)](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2023_12_155_2362.html))_
- **All three charges are present, and the domestic one is the limb that bites first** — Article 13(1): *"U Republici Hrvatskoj se obračunava i plaća kvalificirani domaći dopunski porez"* — a **qualified domestic top-up tax** is calculated and paid in Croatia, reaching the Croatian parent of an MNE group, the parent of a large domestic group, and their constituent entities (art. 13(2)). Article 7 carries the **income inclusion rule** for a Croatian ultimate parent. Articles 14–16 carry the **undertaxed profits rule**. As in Oman, a Croatian subsidiary of a foreign-parented group is reached by the **domestic** limb, not by the IIR  _([NN 155/2023, arts. 7, 13 and 14–16](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2023_12_155_2362.html))_
- **Two commencement dates, not one** — Article 61(1): the Act applies to fiscal years beginning from **31 December 2023**. Article 61(2): articles 14, 15 and 16 — the undertaxed profits rule — apply only to fiscal years beginning from **31 December 2024**. A guide that gives a single "effective from" date for Croatian Pillar Two is wrong for one limb whichever date it picks  _([NN 155/2023, art. 61](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2023_12_155_2362.html))_
- **⚠ The top-up tax is not deductible against corporate profit tax** — The 2025 amending Act inserted a new article 5(7): *"Porezna obveza iz stavka 5. ovoga članka ne može umanjiti osnovicu poreza na dobit."* The top-up liability **cannot reduce the profit tax base**. This is the provision most likely to be modelled wrongly, because a new tax charge naturally reads as a deductible cost. The insertion pushed the old art. 5(7) — the top-up is revenue of the State Budget — down to **art. 5(8)**, so paragraph references to article 5 differ between the 2023 text and the text now in force  _([NN 151/2025, no. 2260, art. 1](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2025_12_151_2260.html))_
- **Filing — the information return and the two tax returns run on different clocks** — The **Top-up Tax Information Return** goes to the Porezna uprava electronically **no later than 15 months after the last day of the fiscal year** (art. 46(7)). The **domestic** top-up tax return is due within that same 15-month deadline (art. 49(1)), with the tax payable **30 days** after it expires (art. 49(4)). The **IIR/UTPR** top-up tax return is due **30 days after** the 15-month deadline expires (art. 48(1)), with tax payable 30 days after that (art. 48(4)). The domestic return is therefore the earlier of the two by a clear month  _([NN 155/2023, arts. 46(7), 48 and 49](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2023_12_155_2362.html))_
- **Joint and several liability** — Where several constituent entities of the same group must calculate and pay, their combined liability is treated as **one debt** and every constituent entity is jointly and severally liable  _([NN 155/2023, arts. 48(5) and 49(5)](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2023_12_155_2362.html))_
- **Penalties — EUR 3,000 to EUR 100,000** — The 2025 amending Act replaced article 52 with a fine of **EUR 3,000.00 to EUR 100,000.00** on the responsible or constituent entity for failing to file either return in the prescribed manner or time, failing to determine the top-up liability under art. 5(5), failing to pay within the art. 48(4)/49(4) deadlines, or failing to appoint a responsible entity within the art. 46(8) deadline  _([NN 151/2025, art. 8](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2025_12_151_2260.html))_
- **The forms exist, and the transition year gets three extra months** — The ordinance prescribes **Obrazac MGP – DP1** (domestic top-up tax) and **Obrazac MGP – DP2** (top-up tax; applies for periods beginning after 31 December 2023, and for the undertaxed profits rule after 31 December 2024). For the **transition year** under art. 53 of the Act, DP1 is due **18 months** after the last day of the reporting fiscal year instead of 15, and DP2 **30 days** after that. Note where that 18-month rule lives: it is in the **ordinance**, not the Act — the Act's only filing deadline is the 15-month one  _([NN 53/2026, no. 665, arts. 6(5), 7 and 12](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2026_05_53_665.html))_
- **The ordinance was about four and a half months late** — Article 62(1) of the Act required the Minister of Finance to adopt the article 13(9) ordinance **by 31 December 2025**. It was signed on 15 May 2026, published 22 May 2026 and in force on the eighth day after publication. For the first two charged years the detailed procedure, the forms and the payment mechanics did not exist in delegated form  _([NN 155/2023, art. 62(1); NN 53/2026, art. 13](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2026_05_53_665.html))_

> **Two drafting defects in the official text, recorded because a reader will meet them.**
> The enacted 2023 Chapter II heading read *"PRENISKO OPREZIVANOJ"* — a missing syllable
> — and article 2 of the 2025 amending Act exists partly to correct it to *"PRENISKO
> OPOREZIVANOJ"*. Article 49 of the 2023 Act is headed *"Članka 49."* rather than
> *"Članak 49."* And the amending Act cites the Act it amends **two different ways**: its
> article 1 says *"Narodne novine, br. 155/23"*, its article 11(2) says *"br. 156/23"*.
> The gazette index, the presidential proclamation and the ordinance's own basis clause
> all say **155/23**, so 156/23 appears to be a slip — but it is in the enacted text and
> is **not resolved here**.

> **What is not covered.** The safe harbours (arts. 33–34), the substance-based income
> exclusion, the effective-tax-rate computation in Chapters III–V, and the ordinance's
> *Podskupina RH* sub-group mechanics have **not** been read. The rows above are scope,
> rate, charging limbs, commencement, deductibility, filing, liability and penalties.
