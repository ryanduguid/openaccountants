# Bangladesh Company Formation & Entity Choice

> **The previous version was missing an entire company form, and one of its
> statements is false because of it.** Bangladesh has had the **one-person
> company** since the Companies (Second Amendment) Act 2020 inserted twelve
> sections into the Companies Act 1994. The guide listed neither the form nor its
> capital rule — and then said flatly that there is **no statutory minimum
> paid-up capital**. For an OPC there is: **BDT 2,500,000**, and a ceiling as
> well. Two further statements were right but incomplete in ways that change the
> answer.

## 1. Entity types — Companies Act 1994

- **Private company** — Formed by **two or more** persons  _(Companies Act 1994, s.5 — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-32025.html)_
- **Public company** — Formed by **seven or more** persons  _(Companies Act 1994, s.5 — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-32025.html)_
- **"Public company" is defined negatively** — any company incorporated under the
  Act, or under the law in force immediately before it, **that is not a private
  company**. So the private-company definition in §2 is what does the work: fail
  any of its three limbs and the company is public  _(Companies Act 1994, s.2(1), definition of "পাবলিক কোম্পানী" (clause ঞ) — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-32006.html)_
- **One-person company (এক ব্যক্তি কোম্পানী)** — *"a company whose shareholder is
  only one natural person"*. **A form the previous version did not mention at
  all** — see §3  _(Companies Act 1994, s.2(1), definition of "এক ব্যক্তি কোম্পানী" (clause খখ), inserted by the Companies (Second Amendment) Act 2020 — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-32006.html)_
- **Branch / liaison / representative office of a foreign company** — Available;
  registration runs through RJSC with BIDA permission ((approx — confirm the BIDA
  permission requirement and its conditions))  _(Companies Act 1994 (as described at [legalseba.com](https://legalseba.com/bd-services/company-incorporation-in-bangladesh/)))_
- **Partnership and sole proprietorship** — Registered with RJSC and the local
  authority respectively ((approx — confirm))  _(Companies Act 1994 (as described at [legalseba.com](https://legalseba.com/bd-services/company-incorporation-in-bangladesh/)))_
- **Registration authority** — Registrar of Joint Stock Companies and Firms
  (RJSC)  _(Registrar of Joint Stock Companies and Firms — https://www.roc.gov.bd)_

## 2. The two limits that are stated as one number each, and are not

- **Fifty members — but employees do not count, and joint holders count once** —
  A private company's articles must restrict share transfers, prohibit any
  invitation to the public to subscribe, and *"limit the number of its members to
  fifty, **excluding persons who are in the employment of the company**"*. And the
  proviso: where **two or more persons hold one or more shares jointly**, they are
  *"treated as a single member"* for this definition. The previous version gave a
  flat "maximum 50 shareholders". Both qualifications change whether a company is
  over the line  _(Companies Act 1994, s.2(1), definition of "প্রাইভেট কোম্পানী" (clause ট), limb (ই) and the proviso — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-32006.html)_
- **Two directors — unless the private company is a subsidiary of a public
  company, and then three** — s.90(1) requires **at least three** directors in
  every public company *and in every private company that is a subsidiary of a
  public company*; s.90(2) requires at least two in every other private company.
  **A foreign parent that is a public company makes its Bangladeshi private
  subsidiary a three-director company**, which the previous version's flat
  "minimum 2 directors" would have got wrong  _(Companies Act 1994, s.90(1)–(2) — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-32914.html)_
- **Only a natural person may be a director** — s.90(3): *"কেবলমাত্র প্রাকৃতিক
  ব্যক্তিস্বত্তা বিশিষ্ট একজন ব্যক্তি (natural person) পরিচালক নিযুক্ত হইতে
  পারিবেন"*. **There are no corporate directors in Bangladesh** — a group cannot
  put the parent company on the board and must nominate individuals  _(Companies Act 1994, s.90(3) — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-32914.html)_

## 3. The one-person company — Part X-A, ss.392A–392L

Inserted as a new Part X-A by **s.9 of the Companies (Second Amendment) Act 2020
(Act No. 24 of 2020)**. Twelve sections cover the memorandum and articles,
formation, share capital, registration, directors, meetings, alteration, share
transfer, the balance sheet, audit, borrowing and voluntary winding up.

- **One natural person, and only one company** — A natural person may form an OPC
  as a **limited** company for any lawful purpose by subscribing their name to the
  memorandum as promoter. *"A natural person may form **only one** one-person
  company."* Someone who already has one cannot form a second  _(Companies Act 1994, s.392B(1)–(2) — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-50102.html)_
- **A nominee is mandatory, named in the memorandum** — The memorandum must name a
  nominee, **with that person's written consent**, who becomes the shareholder if
  the sole shareholder dies or becomes incapable of running the company or of
  unsound mind. The consent must be recorded in the memorandum, the articles
  **and the register** at registration. The nominee may withdraw consent in the
  prescribed manner, and if the nominee predeceases the shareholder or becomes
  incapable, a replacement may be nominated with their consent  _(Companies Act 1994, s.392B(3)–(6) — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-50102.html)_
- **Paid-up capital: a floor *and* a ceiling** — Paid-up share capital must be
  **not less than BDT 2,500,000 (25 lakh) and not more than BDT 50,000,000 (5
  crore)**  _(Companies Act 1994, s.392C(1)(a) — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-50103.html)_
- **And a turnover band on the previous year** — Annual turnover for the
  **immediately preceding financial year** must be **not less than BDT 10,000,000
  (1 crore) and not more than BDT 500,000,000 (50 crore)**  _(Companies Act 1994, s.392C(1)(b) — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-50103.html)_
- **Outgrowing both limits converts the company** — Where paid-up capital exceeds
  the (a) figure **and** annual turnover exceeds the (b) figure, the OPC may,
  subject to the necessary conditions, be converted into a private limited or, as
  the case may be, a public limited company. Note the conjunction: the statute
  says **both**, not either  _(Companies Act 1994, s.392C(2) — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-50103.html)_
- **The sole shareholder is the director** — and a manager, company secretary and
  other employees may be appointed to run it  _(Companies Act 1994, s.392E — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-50105.html)_
- **One board meeting per half calendar year** — The director must hold at least
  one board meeting in each half of the calendar year  _(Companies Act 1994, s.392F — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-50106.html)_
- **Financial statements within 180 days of year end** — Filed with the Registrar
  with the accompanying documents, and every balance sheet and profit-and-loss (or
  income-and-expenditure) account must be **signed by the sole shareholder
  director**  _(Companies Act 1994, s.392I — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-50109.html)_
- **Registration and audit follow the private-company rules** — The registration
  procedure for a private limited company applies *"with necessary adaptations"*,
  and so do the Act's provisions on appointing an auditor and on the audit report  _(Companies Act 1994, ss.392D and 392J — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-50104.html)_

## 4. Capital, and the claim that needs qualifying

- **No statutory minimum for an ordinary private or public company** — correct as
  far as it goes, and it does not go as far as the OPC. **An OPC has both a
  minimum and a maximum** (§3). Stating "no statutory minimum" without naming the
  form it applies to is what made the previous version wrong  _(Companies Act 1994 (no minimum stated for private/public companies); cf. s.392C for the OPC — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-50103.html)_
- **Foreign-owned company — USD 50,000 inward remittance** — A practical
  requirement tied to work permits and expatriate sponsorship rather than a
  statutory share-capital floor ((approx — confirm against BIDA's current
  guidelines; not in the Companies Act))  _(BIDA work-permit practice (as described at [legalseba.com](https://legalseba.com/bd-services/company-incorporation-in-bangladesh/)))_

## 5. Incorporation steps and annual compliance

- **Step 1 — name clearance** — Apply through the RJSC portal; the clearance is
  valid **30 days** ((approx — confirm the current validity against RJSC; this is
  an administrative rule, not a provision of the Act))  _(RJSC name-clearance procedure — https://www.roc.gov.bd)_
- **Step 2 — bank account and encashment certificate (foreign capital)** — Open a
  bank account and remit the foreign shareholders' capital; the bank issues an
  encashment certificate for RJSC ((approx — confirm))  _(Companies Act 1994 registration practice (as described at [legalseba.com](https://legalseba.com/bd-services/company-incorporation-in-bangladesh/)))_
- **Step 3 — registration** — File the memorandum, articles and forms with RJSC,
  pay the registration fees and stamp duty, and obtain the Certificate of
  Incorporation ((approx — confirm the current fee scale))  _(Companies Act 1994 registration practice (as described at [legalseba.com](https://legalseba.com/bd-services/company-incorporation-in-bangladesh/)))_
- **Typical timeline** — Roughly 1 to 3 weeks for a straightforward local company
  ((approx — confirm; varies with foreign-capital remittance and bank steps, and
  this is a practitioner estimate rather than a statutory period))  _(Companies Act 1994 registration practice (as described at [legalseba.com](https://legalseba.com/bd-services/company-incorporation-in-bangladesh/)))_
- **A company's financial year need not be twelve months** — The Act defines
  "financial year", for a body corporate, as the period *"whether or not it is a
  full year"* for which its profit-and-loss account is laid before the annual
  general meeting. A first period or a changed year-end is therefore still a
  financial year. **The calendar year is the exception, not the rule**: the
  proviso applies it only to an **insurance company**  _(Companies Act 1994, s.2(1), definition of "অর্থ-বৎসর" (clause ক) and its proviso — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-32006.html)_
- **Core annual compliance** — Hold the annual general meeting; file the annual
  return and audited financial statements with RJSC; file the annual income tax
  return with the NBR ((approx — confirm the current RJSC and NBR deadlines; for
  an OPC the filing deadline is the statutory **180 days** in §3))  _(Companies Act 1994 (as described at [legalseba.com](https://legalseba.com/bd-services/company-incorporation-in-bangladesh/)))_

## 6. What this replaces

| The guide said | The Act says |
| --- | --- |
| Entity types: private, public, branch, partnership, sole proprietorship | Missing the **one-person company** — twelve sections, Part X-A, in force since 2020 |
| "**No statutory minimum** paid-up capital (effectively as low as BDT 1)" | True for a private or public company. **False for an OPC**: BDT 2,500,000 floor **and** a BDT 50,000,000 ceiling — s.392C(1)(a) |
| *nothing* | An OPC also needs prior-year turnover of **BDT 10,000,000 to 500,000,000** — s.392C(1)(b) |
| "Minimum 2, maximum 50 shareholders" | 50 **excluding employees**, and **joint holders count as one member** — s.2(t)(iii) and proviso |
| "Minimum 2 directors" | Two — **unless the private company is a subsidiary of a public company, and then three** — s.90(1)–(2) |
| *nothing* | **Only a natural person may be a director** — no corporate directors — s.90(3) |
| Public limited company listed without a number | **Seven or more** persons — s.5 |
| *nothing* | A financial year is the period the P&L is laid before the AGM, **whether or not a full year**; the **calendar year applies only to insurance companies** — s.2(1) |
| *nothing* | An OPC needs a **nominee** named in the memorandum with written consent — s.392B(3) |
| *nothing* | A person may form **only one** OPC — s.392B(2) |

Every provision above except those marked "(approx — confirm)" was read from the
Companies Act 1994 as published in the Ministry of Law's own legislative
database, which prints the amending act in a footnote against each inserted
section — here the Companies (Second Amendment) Act 2020 (Act No. 24 of 2020),
s.9. The lakh and crore figures in §3 were converted and then checked against an
independent English-language statement of the same amounts before being written
down, because a misread order of magnitude in that numbering system is a
hundred-fold error that inspection will not catch.
