# Bermuda Company Formation & Entity Choice

## Scope and period

This guide covers selected Companies Act formation, fee and reporting controls, not a recommendation of an entity, complete incorporation service or complete reporting result. The primary texts were captured on 14 November 2026. A downloaded consolidation does not establish that no later law applies. Check amendments, commencement and the actual action date before use; historical formations need the instruments then in force. Tier 2 and pending review remain.

The Beneficial Ownership Act 2025 commenced on **3 November 2025**. Its former s 16 approval provision was repealed on **30 December 2025**. The 2026 amendment's relevant beneficial ownership changes and separate Companies Act incorporation-consent provisions took effect on **14 September 2026**. These are distinct changes, not permission to omit incorporation consent. Sources: [2025 commencement notice, para 2](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Statutory%20Instruments/2025/Beneficial%20Ownership%20Act%202025%20Commencement%20Day%20Notice%202025), [2025 amending Act, ss 2-4](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2025/Beneficial%20Ownership%20Amendment%20Act%202025) and [2026 amending Act, ss 3-10, 15 and 22](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2026/Beneficial%20Ownership%20Amendment%20Act%202026).

## Choose the legal form and business permission first

The Companies Act permits companies limited by shares, companies limited by guarantee and unlimited liability companies; the guarantee route has the additional purpose or other conditions in s 5(3). A limited liability company (LLC), a partnership and a segregated accounts arrangement require their own legislation and classification. This guide does not supply their formation, liability, capital or officer rules. [Companies Act, s 5](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981); [Registrar's entity-registration overview](https://www2.gov.bm/roc/how-to-register-an-entity).

Local or exempted status is a separate company classification. For a local company carrying on business in Bermuda, identify the actual s 114 permission route. The Third Schedule Part I route requires Bermudian control, including at least **60% of voting rights** and at least **60% each of directors and beneficially owned shares**, subject to its qualifications. Section 114 also has other scheduled, licensed, subsidiary and listed-company branches. Do not treat the 60% tests alone as a complete permission decision. [Companies Act, ss 114-114B and Third Schedule](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

Exempted status is defined by s 127. Such companies remain subject to law except where expressly provided otherwise, and s 129 restricts business in Bermuda subject to its permissions and exceptions. It is not an exemption from beneficial ownership obligations, economic substance, all taxes or sectoral law. Check the proposed activity, location, ownership and applicable exchange-control, Ministerial or financial-regulatory permissions separately. This batch does not determine those permissions for a real entity. [Companies Act, ss 127-129](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

## Formation documents, capital and registration

1. Establish the company form, local/exempted classification, proposed objects and activity-specific permissions before selecting documents or estimating fees.
2. A name **may** be reserved not more than **3 months before the registration application**, for a reservation period not exceeding **3 months**, with the prescribed Government Fees Regulations fee. For a **local-company** application, s 6(2) separately requires an appointed-newspaper advertisement, no more than **3 months before the application**, stating the proposed name and objects. These are not universal processing times. [Companies Act, ss 6-6A](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).
3. Prepare the memorandum with the s 7 information, including name, objects or unrestricted objects, subscriber details, exempted status and any duration. For a company limited by shares, include share capital and its division into fixed-amount shares and the required subscriber agreement. Each subscriber signs before an attesting witness, unless an electronic memorandum is authenticated in the manner directed by the Registrar under s 7(4A). [Companies Act, s 7](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).
4. The memorandum's stated minimum capital must be **subscribed before business or borrowing starts**. Do not replace this with a universal BMD/USD 1 minimum, a claim of no capital requirement or an assumed 'typical' BMD 12,000 capital. Regulated and other forms may have additional requirements. Companies may not issue bearer shares under s 53. [Companies Act, ss 7, 53 and 64](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).
5. Supply the proposed relevant-activity information under s 5A and the Bermuda Constituent Entity/representative information required by s 5B. These formation disclosures do not decide the full economic substance or corporate income tax result. [Companies Act, ss 5A-5B](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).
6. Deliver the memorandum **with an application for the Registrar's incorporation consent**, in the required form and with the required documents. If satisfied as to compliance and consenting, the Registrar registers the memorandum and issues a certificate showing the registration date. Beneficial ownership filing is a separate requirement below. Do not substitute an assumed universal BMA beneficial-owner pre-clearance or infer that repeal of BO Act s 16 removed Companies Act s 14 consent or separate regulatory permissions. [Companies Act, s 14](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981); [2026 amending Act, ss 15 and 22](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2026/Beneficial%20Ownership%20Amendment%20Act%202026).

The public [registration overview](https://www2.gov.bm/roc/how-to-register-an-entity) is orientation, not evidence that a particular application, document set or payment was accepted. No processing-time commitment, complete fee quotation or actual incorporation is verified here.

## Registered office, officers and continuing company records

A company must maintain a physical registered office in Bermuda; a post office box is insufficient. Section 62 specifies initial notice and registered change mechanics. Company affairs require at least **1 director** and the directors appoint a secretary. [Companies Act, ss 62, 91-92](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

An exempted company must satisfy **one of the alternative resident-presence routes** in s 130(1): at least 1 director other than an alternate director ordinarily resident in Bermuda; a secretary who is an ordinarily resident individual or company; or a resident representative who is an ordinarily resident individual or company. This does not require all 3 resident roles and does not remove the general secretary requirement. Do not apply these company rules to an LLC or partnership. [Companies Act, s 130](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

Maintain the applicable registers and records, including members, directors/officers and directors' interests, with minutes kept under s 81. The ordinary AGM rule is at least once each calendar year. A company may elect by resolution in general meeting to dispense with AGMs under s 71A, for the specified period, but existing default liability continues and the provision preserves a qualified member right to require an AGM. A bare 'waive the AGM' entry is insufficient. [Companies Act, ss 65, 71-71A, 81, 92A-92B and 97(4)-(5)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981). The selected reporting controls below add accounts, audit, member disclosure and retention duties; they remain an incomplete operational and annual-return checklist.

## Beneficial ownership: identify, verify, record and file separately

Apply the BO Act's definitions, scope and exemptions first. Section 3's listed-entity and subsidiary exemptions have specified tests, including ownership/voting or control branches. An exempt legal person must still confirm that status and file proof with the Registrar, including the relevant stock exchange details. Being an 'exempted company' under the Companies Act is not itself a BO exemption. No actual entity exemption is determined here. [BO Act, ss 2-3](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/2025/Beneficial%20Ownership%20Act%202025).

For an in-scope legal person, identify beneficial owners and relevant legal entities. The s 6 test includes individuals with direct or indirect ownership/control of **25% or more** of the shares/interests, voting rights or partnership interests, ultimate effective control over management or control by other means. Only if no individual meets those tests does the senior-manager fallback apply. Trust/trustee cases have the separate s 6(3) and Trustee Act s 25B conditions; the solely-liquidator exclusion is in s 6(4). Do not stop at the percentage test or treat an intermediary entity as necessarily the ultimate individual. Trust cases need the additional trust-law sources before a result. [BO Act, ss 2 and 6-7](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/2025/Beneficial%20Ownership%20Act%202025).

Use the s 8 notice route where required, including its **30-day response period from receipt**, exceptions and false-response qualification. Before entering the minimum required information, take reasonable identity-verification measures using reliable independent documents, data or information and keep verification records. Maintain the s 10 register with the applicable individual, legal-entity or trust fields, nature/extent of interests and entry/cessation dates. Section 10 requires the register in the registered office or, after written notice to the Registrar, another qualifying place in Bermuda. These controls do not replace a full BO regulations/orders, privilege and information-access review. [BO Act, ss 8-10](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/2025/Beneficial%20Ownership%20Act%202025).

Keep these filing and change events distinct under the captured law, including the amendments operative from **14 September 2026**:

| Event | Selected statutory control |
|---|---|
| Initial registration, continuation, conversion or application for permission to carry on business in Bermuda | File minimum required information on registrable persons with the Registrar **at that event**, subject to the s 15(4) exemption-order power. This is not a general 30-day initial-filing allowance. |
| A beneficial ownership change affecting a register entry | Update as soon as practicable after the legal person is notified, no later than **30 days thereafter**, under s 11(1). The notice/confirmation rules in s 11(2)-(8) also apply; do not update until the relevant change is confirmed. |
| A change of beneficial owners requiring Registrar filing | File the minimum required information as soon as practicable after notification, no later than **30 days thereafter**, under s 15(2). Updating the company's register alone is insufficient. |
| Applicable listed shares/interests supporting the exemption, including a qualifying parent's, listed on a new exchange or delisted | Notify the Registrar as soon as practicable, no later than **30 days thereafter**, under s 3(5). |
| Exempt legal person no longer satisfies s 3(2) | Notify the Registrar as soon as practicable, no later than **30 days thereafter**, under s 3(6). |

Sources: [BO Act, ss 3, 11 and 15](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/2025/Beneficial%20Ownership%20Act%202025) and [2026 amending Act, ss 3, 9-10 and 22](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2026/Beneficial%20Ownership%20Amendment%20Act%202026). The s 11 confirmation-request response has its own **30-day period from receipt** under s 11(2); do not collapse every event into a single clock. The public limited-companies page updated **4 September 2026** still describes some **14-day** BO events. The later primary amendment controls these selected events; retain the actual event, notice and confirmation evidence rather than using the page's older deadline. [Official limited-companies overview](https://www2.gov.bm/limited-companies).

## Company-class fee and declaration clocks

| Company class | Initial and annual controls in the captured Companies Act |
|---|---|
| Local company, s 121 | Send the required capital/unit-trust declaration **within 1 month of filing the memorandum**, then **not later than 31 March each year**. Pay the applicable Part I Fifth Schedule fee, annual tax and separate corporate regulatory fee. The capital declaration uses intended issued capital at commencement or, if already in business, issued capital on 1 January of that year. |
| Exempted company, s 131 | Send the required business, relevant-activity, Bermuda Constituent Entity, BO compliance/exemption and capital declarations **at memorandum filing**, then **during January each year**. Pay the applicable Part II Fifth Schedule fee and separate corporate regulatory fee. Select the actual business/company branch and statutory measurement dates below. |
| Permit company, s 135 | Pay the appropriate fee and separate regulatory fee **before engaging in or carrying on trade or business in Bermuda**, then **during March each year**. Section 131(1)(a)(i)'s business/unit-trust declaration applies with the necessary modifications. At annual-fee payment, file the separate s 135(2A) activity, year-end, Bermuda Constituent Entity and BO compliance/listing/exemption statements. |

Sources: [Companies Act, ss 121, 131, 135 and Fifth Schedule](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981). The local-company after-31-August proviso halves the named fee, if applicable, and annual tax for that year. The exempted-company after-31-August proviso halves the s 131(1)(b) appropriate fee. A permit issued **after 31 October** engages s 135(1B)'s half appropriate fee for that year. These provisions do not automatically halve separate regulatory or additional charges. An annual fee is not established here as 'capital duty'.

## Select the statutory fee branch and capital base

The following dollar figures reproduce the selected captured statutory schedules. They are components, not an all-in invoice, a payment-currency or payment-channel decision, or a quotation for an actual company. First establish legal form, local/exempted/permit status, business at any time in the year, funds managed, capital and its measurement dates, currency, exemptions, accounts operated and actual licence/filing events. Do not apply an ordinary band before excluding a different statutory branch. [Companies Act, ss 121-126, 131, 135 and Fifth Schedule](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

### Local companies

For the ordinary local-company annual-tax scale, use **issued capital**, being the aggregate nominal value of shares actually issued, whether fully paid or not. For a mutual company use the reserve fund instead. Authorised capital plus share premium is not this local tax base. The year is a calendar year. [Companies Act, ss 121, 126 and Fifth Schedule Part I](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

| At least 500,000 but less than 1,000,000 | 3,385 |

Source: [Companies Act, Fifth Schedule Part I, para 2](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981). A local company trading wholesale in petroleum, other oils or liquefied petroleum gas has the separate **19,330 annual-tax branch**. Unit-trust management attracts the Part I fee of **3,050 per fund** at the statutory declaration date; distinguish that fee from capital-based tax. The local **corporate regulatory fee is 150**. [Companies Act, s 121 and Fifth Schedule Part I](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981); [Registrar regulatory-fees Act, s 3 and Schedule](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/2024/Registrar%20of%20Companies%20(Annual%20Corporate%20Regulatory%20Fees)%20Act%202024).

For a local company limited by guarantee other than a mutual company, Part I states that no fee is payable. Section 125 separately exempts annual tax if the company satisfies the Accountant General of its qualifying guarantee, charitable or non-commercial status. The non-commercial branch requires income in **each past year since incorporation not exceeding 500**. The charitable branch excludes the specified commercial powers/business, dividends, non-charitable interest and remuneration outside charitable or reasonable administrative purposes. A nonprofit label alone is insufficient. Neither the annual-tax exemption nor the Schedule's no-fee wording establishes exemption from the later separate regulatory fee or transaction charges. [Companies Act, s 125 and Fifth Schedule Part I](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981); [Registrar regulatory-fees Act, s 3 and Schedule](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/2024/Registrar%20of%20Companies%20(Annual%20Corporate%20Regulatory%20Fees)%20Act%202024).

### Exempted companies: capital and ordinary bands

For a shares company, assessable capital is **authorised capital plus share premium**. For a mutual fund as defined in s 156A, it is **authorised capital only**. For a mutual company, it is the **reserve fund**. Section 131(1)(a)(iii) sets the measurement date: memorandum-filing date on incorporation; that same date for the next January declaration if incorporated after 31 August in the preceding year; otherwise the preceding **31 August**, with the separate currency-conversion-date rules below. [Companies Act, s 131(1)(a)(ii)-(iii)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

For incorporation and the first January declaration after incorporation after 31 August, s 131(1)(a)(iii)(aa)-(bb) uses information as at memorandum filing, including the conversion-date information. In the **other-case preceding-31-August branch in (cc)**, the conversion date is instead the latest of filing and the last lawful authorised-capital alteration for a shares company, or the later of filing and the last lawful reserve-fund alteration for a mutual company. Use the Registrar-determined middle-market rate under the statutory method. **US currency converts at par for this capital calculation**; round a fractional dollar **up to the next whole dollar**. Do not substitute paid-up capital, an invoice date or a convenient live FX quote. This conversion rule does not establish which payment currency a service accepts. [Companies Act, s 131(1)(a)(iii)-(vii)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

### Additional charges, administration and transaction fees

- Where Part II para 2 applies, the lower fee is available if the business attracting the higher fee is not carried on. Carrying on that business **at any time in the year** attracts the higher fee for that year. Do not invent monthly proration. [Companies Act, Fifth Schedule Part II, para 2](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).
- A qualifying segregated accounts company registered under s 6 of the 2000 Act pays **295 for each operated segregated account, capped at 1,180 in aggregate**, in addition to its otherwise due annual fee or tax. Part II D separately prescribes **295 per operated incorporated segregated account, capped at 1,180**, for a qualifying registered ISAC, in addition to its annual fee. These are narrow Companies Act additions, not complete SAC/ISAC registration or fee rules. The actual 2019 amending/commencement instrument has not been checked for historical use. [Companies Act, Fifth Schedule Part II C-D](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).
- A licensed local company pays **1,050 on licence issue**, then **on or before 31 January** each following year whilst the licence subsists. This is distinct from its company-class March charges and licence-application fees. [Companies Act, s 114C](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981); [Miscellaneous Fees and Penalties Act 2023, ss 2 and 7](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2023/Miscellaneous%20Fees%20and%20Penalties%20Act%202023).
- A foreign corporation continued as an exempted company pays its appropriate fee and regulatory fee **within 1 month of registration of the memorandum of continuance**. Registration after **31 August** halves the appropriate fee only. A continuance-application fee is a different component. [Companies Act, s 132C(3A)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981); [2024 No. 2 regulatory-fees Act, s 2(3)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Document/%7B914f3404-82cd-4576-8458-230f59a221ff%7D).
- Local defaults under s 121 and exempted defaults under s 131 can attract default fines. For non-wilful non-compliance, the Registrar may accept late compliance/payment with the statutory **150 local penalty** or **300 exempted penalty**. This is discretionary, not an automatic extension. The stated appropriate-fee/tax non-payment rules require cessation of business after **3 months** until the specified sums are paid; contravention attracts **100 per day**. Section 135(3) imports s 131(2)-(6), (8) and (9), with the necessary modifications, for permit companies. It does not import s 131(7). This is not a complete regulatory-fee enforcement or permit-cancellation analysis. [Companies Act, ss 121, 131 and 135](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).
- The Accountant General may require an auditor's capital certificate under s 122. Failure without lawful excuse can deem the maximum annual tax unless the lesser liability is established. Annual tax/penalties are recoverable as civil debt under s 123. Fee/tax questions have the Minister determination routes in ss 121(6) and 131(6); schedule-variation orders under ss 121(7) and 131(7) require the stated affirmative procedure. Do not infer an accepted payment or good standing from these calculation controls. [Companies Act, ss 121-123 and 131](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

[Miscellaneous Fees and Penalties Act 2023, ss 6-7](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2023/Miscellaneous%20Fees%20and%20Penalties%20Act%202023); [BR32/2023, reg 2](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Statutory%20Instruments/2023/Government%20Fees%20Amendment%20Regulations%202023).

## Company records, statements and audit

### Establish which company rules apply

The following controls use the captured Companies Act, not a complete reporting framework or an audit exemption for an actual entity. Apply s 4 first: overseas companies are covered only so far as a provision requires; permit companies have the selected Parts in s 4(1A)(b), which do not include a blanket application of Part VI. A private incorporating Act has the qualified priority in s 4(2), but paragraph (c) makes Part VI, except s 91, amongst the mandatory Parts from 1 July 1984 notwithstanding the private Act. Check the incorporating Act, memorandum, bye-laws, class, licence and sector rules before applying these controls. LLC, partnership, liquidation, receivership and complete group reporting remain separate. [Companies Act, s 4](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

Share-capital company bye-laws must address accounts, making statements available and an audit at least yearly by an independent shareholder representative; guarantee-company bye-laws also include s 13(2)(c), (d) and (f). Read those provisions with the particular-interval waiver in s 88. They do not establish a universal small-company, dormant-company, exempted-company or charitable audit exemption. [Companies Act, ss 13 and 88](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

### Keep the right records, in the right place

Minutes of all general and director meetings must be entered in books, signed by the person presiding at the proceedings or their approval and kept by the secretary at the registered office. Section 82 gives members and directors inspection of **general-meeting minutes**, without charge for at least **2 business hours daily**, subject to reasonable restrictions, and copies within **7 days** of request for a reasonable charge. It does not create universal public access or a public right to board minutes. [Companies Act, ss 81-82](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

Section 83 account records cover receipts and expenditure and their purposes, all goods sales and purchases, assets and liabilities. Keep them at the registered office or another directors-chosen place, always inspectable by directors or the specified s 130 resident representative. If kept overseas, retain Bermuda-office records enabling reasonable-accuracy financial-position determination at the end of each **3-month period**. The stated interval is **6 months** for a company listed on an **appointed stock exchange**; a foreign listing alone does not prove the Ministerial appointment under s 2(9). This is local records availability, not quarterly remittance or annual accounts filing. [Companies Act, ss 2(9) and 83](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

Permit-company records under s 145 concern its Bermuda trade or business, including receipts/expenditure and their purposes, goods sales/purchases, assets and liabilities. Keep them at its principal Bermuda place of business; overseas records require local-office information enabling directors or the specified principal representative to determine its financial position reasonably accurately at the end of each **3-month period**. Do not import s 83's listed-company 6-month exception or all Part VI statement/audit rules. Home-country, sector and permit conditions may add obligations. [Companies Act, ss 4(1A)(b) and 145](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

The following retention duties have different documents, triggers and locations. Permanent storage may be nonlegible only with legible reproduction, and non-bound-book records need adequate precautions against falsification and for discovery. Other statutory, sector, access or litigation duties may require longer; these minima supply **no automatic destruction date or permission to delete records**. [Companies Act, ss 83(5), 145(3) and 273(1)-(4)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

| Selected document population | Captured retention rule |
|---|---|
| Section 83 account records, including applicable local records for overseas books | **5 years from preparation**, under s 83(5) |
| Section 145 permit-company account records, including applicable local records | **5 years from preparation**, under s 145(3) |
| Copies of s 81 minutes, s 84 financial statements and s 87A summaries | Preserve at the **registered office for 6 years from first required**, under s 273(4) |

### Prepare and approve member statements

Subject to ss 87 and 88, directors lay statements before the company in general meeting at the intervals and for the periods required by the Act and bye-laws. Section 84(1) includes results of operations, retained earnings or deficit, a balance sheet, changes in financial position or cash flows, notes and additional information required by the Act, incorporating Act, memorandum and bye-laws, together with the s 90(2) auditor report. A director signs the balance-sheet page before laying. [Companies Act, s 84(1)-(2)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

The notes describe the GAAP used. Section 84(1A) permits Bermuda, another country/jurisdiction or Minister-appointed GAAP, with foreign GAAP identified in the notes. Actual Ministerial appointments and applicable standards have not been reviewed. Sections 84 and 86 alone do not establish a universal IFRS mandate, a named framework's content, complete group-accounts requirements or a consolidation exemption. If required statements are not laid, the chairman may adjourn the meeting for up to **90 days**, or longer as members agree. This is a meeting mechanism, not an automatic filing extension. Section 84(4) has a reasonable-steps duty and its stated reliance defence. [Companies Act, ss 84(1A), (3)-(5) and 86](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

### Distinguish availability, summaries and an AGM election

Section 87(1), subject to its other provisions, makes the statements and required documents available to members. Its exclusions concern those without general-meeting notice entitlement, more than 1 joint holder and an unknown address. If documents are not sent **5 days before** the general meeting, any member may move a **5-day adjournment**. The provision also addresses default on an entitled person's demand beyond **7 days**, an already-satisfied demand and the defence of impossibility beyond the company's or officer's control. These member rights are not a universal Registrar or public financial-statements filing duty. [Companies Act, s 87(1)-(2)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

If an AGM is not convened in a financial year following a s 71A election, s 87(3) requires that year's s 84(1) statements to be made available to every member within **12 months of the end of the year in which the AGM was not held**; availability is deemed laying. On failure, member(s) may give notice requiring a general meeting held within **6 months of the failure**, not 6 months from their notice; s 84(2)-(4) apply. Read this with a valid s 88 waiver. An AGM election alone does not waive records, member statements or audit appointment, and the financial-year wording differs from s 71's calendar-year AGM rule. [Companies Act, ss 71-71A, 84 and 87(3)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

A company whose shares are listed on an appointed stock exchange may use ss 87A-87C's summary route. Summaries are derived from full statements and include the specified statement summaries, board-selected further information and a prominent warning that they do not provide the full understanding available from complete statements. The package includes the auditor's report on the summaries and notice of the member's full-statement election. Send it at least **21 days before** the general meeting; subject to s 88, send an otherwise entitled member full statements within **7 days of receiving the election**. The company using this route must make both summaries and full statements publicly inspectable at the registered office. This is a qualified listed-company alternative, not permission to omit full statements or a universal public-accounts rule. Actual exchange appointment, listing and election facts remain to be established. [Companies Act, ss 2(9) and 87A-87C](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

### Apply the particular-interval waiver exactly

Section 88 requires agreement of **all members and directors**, in writing or at a general meeting, for the particular interval and specified waiver: no laying of statements/auditor report, or no auditor appointment, as applicable. Members are deemed to agree at a meeting only if all are present in person and agree, or the members present in person agree and written agreements from those absent in person are produced. Directors-only approval, a majority vote, company size/status and an AGM election do not meet this test. If no general meeting occurs within **18 months of the agreement**, member(s) may give notice requiring a meeting within **6 months of that notice** to terminate the agreement. It does not waive s 83 records, s 273 retention or another regulator's requirements. [Companies Act, s 88](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

### Appoint, replace and assess the auditor separately

Subject to s 88, members appoint auditor(s) at the statutory meeting; on failure, directors must appoint forthwith. Read the initial term to the close of the next AGM in s 89(1) with the successor-continuity rule in s 89(2). Directors may fill a casual vacancy, with surviving/continuing auditors able to act meanwhile. Member-appointed remuneration is fixed by members or directors authorised by them; directors fix remuneration for their own appointments. Subject to s 88, the Registrar may appoint on a member's application where no auditor is appointed. These are distinct routes. [Companies Act, s 89(1)-(2), (4), (6)-(7)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

For a new nonincumbent general-meeting nominee, s 89(3) ordinarily requires written nomination notice at least **21 days before** the meeting, a copy to the incumbent and member notice at least **7 days before**, by the stated newspaper or bye-law route. The incumbent may waive that subsection by written notice to the secretary. A replacement must request and receive the outgoing auditor's written circumstances/reasons statement before accepting or consenting, with the **15-day no-response alternative** after the request in s 89(3B). Failure to request makes appointment **voidable by shareholder resolution**, not automatically void. The outgoing auditor has the specified meeting attendance, notice and hearing rights; the stated communications have qualified privilege. [Companies Act, s 89(3)-(3C), (5A), (11)-(12)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

Removal before term-end requires **at least two-thirds of votes cast** at a general meeting notified with that intention. The replacement for the remainder of the term requires a **majority of votes cast at that meeting**. Written notice of the proposed resolution must be given to both the incumbent and proposed auditor at least **21 days before** the meeting. A replacement majority does not reduce the removal threshold or satisfy the separate notice conditions. [Companies Act, s 89(5)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

Ordinarily, an officer/employee of the company or an affiliate, and the specified partner/employer/employee connections, cannot be auditor. The narrow s 89(9) route for a **nonmutual guarantee company** requires a unanimous vote of members present or represented at the appointment meeting and a person with neither personal responsibility for care of company funds nor day-to-day management or recording of its finances. The connection must be disclosed in the report. That meeting-vote test differs from s 88's all-members-and-directors agreement. Affiliate/control status uses s 86; other-law or sector qualification/licensing requirements remain unreviewed. [Companies Act, ss 86 and 89(8)-(10)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

The s 90 audit enables a report **to members** on the s 84 statements. Generally accepted auditing standards may be Bermuda, foreign or Minister-appointed; the report identifies foreign standards. Actual appointments and standards have not been reviewed. Sections 84/90 alone do not impose universal IFRS/ISA use or audited-account filing with the Registrar. Section 90(3A), for functions contemplated by this Act, limits actions against the auditor to the engaging company or persons expressly authorised by the auditor to rely on the work; this guide supplies no actual audit or reliance opinion. [Companies Act, s 90](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

### Keep Registrar and representative reporting distinct

The registered-office directors/officers register under s 92A is updated within **14 days** of changes in directors, officers or registered particulars. It has the specified individual/company and alternate-director details; a local company's register also states individuals' Bermudian status. Apply the defined officer scope in ss 92A(7) and 130(9). Public inspection is without charge for at least **2 business hours daily**, subject to reasonable restrictions; copies have their prescribed fee. The separate s 92B directors list goes to the Registrar in the prescribed form/with the prescribed fee, with director changes notified within **30 days**; the Registrar's register is publicly inspectable subject to conditions and fee. Do not turn the internal 14-day clock into 30 days or treat every officer change as a s 92B director notification. [Companies Act, ss 92A-92B and 130(9)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

The s 92A(8) exception requires objects **wholly and exclusively charitable**, no public fundraising solicitation, filed memorandum and directors/officers register, and Registrar confirmation of that filing. Confidentiality in s 92A(9) attaches to those filed documents. It is not a general s 92B, financial-statements, audit or charity exemption. Section 13(2A) separately requires companies to which s 13(2) applies to file specified bye-law information under (a), (e) and (f), and amendments within **30 days**; that information is nonpublic under (2B). It is not a wholesale filing of financial statements. [Companies Act, ss 13(2A)-(2B) and 92A(8)-(9)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

Where an exempted company uses a resident representative, s 130(7) requires that representative's Bermuda office to hold originals/copies of director/member minutes, required statements and auditor reports, and the s 83 account records required in Bermuda. The representative has the s 130(5) meeting, process and filing rights. On awareness of an Act/regulation breach with a material effect on company affairs **or** a share issue/transfer contrary to another regulating statute, the representative must report relevant particulars to the Registrar in writing within **30 days of awareness**, unless the company remedies it before the report. This is a duty owed to the Registrar, with the statute's liability protection and wilful-failure offence, not an annual audit-report filing requirement or a duty automatically imposed on every resident-secretary/director alternative. [Companies Act, s 130(5)-(12)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

The local, exempted and permit declarations described above have their own class, content and clocks under ss 121, 131 and 135. Section 122's written-notice capital certificate is distinct from a general annual audit. The specified s 131(8) BCE/representative information is nonpublic and shared with the Corporate Income Tax Agency; s 135(3) imports that provision with necessary adaptations. This does not make every declaration field universally private/public or establish CIT, BO or ES compliance. A qualified Gazette deferment under s 287A(2A) needs its actual notice; none is assumed here. Forms, directions, current fees/portal procedure and accepted submission remain unreviewed. [Companies Act, ss 121-122, 131, 135 and 287A(2A)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

### Check the special benefit-company branch

Part XIIB applies to a company whose memorandum states that it is a benefit company subject to that Part; its additional/different requirements prevail on conflict. Under s 156U, subject to its bye-law and statutory alternatives, members receive an annual benefit statement describing responsible/sustainable operation and pursuit of any public-benefit provision. Analysis ordinarily uses a third-party standard meeting the statutory comprehensive, independent, credible and transparent criteria, applied consistently or with reasons for a change/inconsistency. The section does not itself require an external certification engagement or universal public filing. [Companies Act, ss 156Q-156R and 156U(1)-(3), (6)-(7)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

Unless bye-laws provide otherwise, directors may choose less-frequent statements or no third-party standard. For an affected year, the requisite members may require that year's statement or standard by notice **not later than 3 months before year-end**. The requisite holding is **at least 2% of issued shares**; for a benefit company with shares listed on an appointed stock exchange, it is the lesser of that or shares of **at least $2,000,000 in fair market value**, under s 156R. The consolidation annotates 2025:21 and 1 December 2025; the actual amending/commencement instruments and constitution were not reviewed, so operational and historical reliance on this branch remains held. The benefit statement is separate from ordinary financial statements. [Companies Act, ss 156Q-156R and 156U(4)-(5)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

The incorporation information in Companies Act s 5A is distinct from ongoing Economic Substance Act duties. ## Manual fee control cases

These additional cases use the captured fee tables and hypothetical facts. They are manual source/arithmetic controls, not actual company, portal, accepted-invoice, practitioner or consuming-model execution. Sources are the Companies Act and selected fee instruments cited above.

1. For a local shares company to which the ordinary scale applies, issued capital of **49,999** gives **685** annual tax; **50,000** gives **1,020**. A petroleum-wholesale branch cannot use that ordinary result by assumption. Authorised capital and premium alone do not determine local tax.
2. An ordinary exempted shares company with **12,000 authorised capital + 3,000 premium = 15,000 assessable capital** uses the **4,275** band if that branch applies. A s 156A mutual fund instead uses authorised capital only; establish that status before changing the base.
3. Under the ordinary exempted scale, **100,000,000** maps to **10,980**, whilst **100,000,001** maps to **19,605**; **500,000,000** maps to **19,605**, whilst **500,000,001** maps to **32,676**. Apply statutory conversion/rounding first. These are fee-band results, not total charges.
4. Unit-trust management's **3,050 per fund** is additional under s 131(2A) or s 135(1A); it does not replace otherwise applicable appropriate/regulatory fees.
5. A qualifying small foreign sales corporation leasing aircraft uses para (bb)(iii)'s **2,079**, not the non-aircraft **615**. Establish the statutory US designation and evidence first.
6. Exempted memorandum filing on **31 August** is not after that date; **1 September** is. Permit issue on **31 October** is not after that date; **1 November** is. Halve only the specified appropriate fee, without automatically halving the separate **500** regulatory charge or unit-trust addition.
7. A licensed local company can have a **1,050 January licence fee** alongside March company-class charges. Permit-company March differs from exempted-company January. BR85/2024's October date applies only to 2024.
8. A permit company's affiliate or group member operating with Bermuda staff and management can satisfy the Schedule's physical-presence definition even without the company's own office. Without qualifying presence or a specified principal business, do not reduce the **25,000** catchall to **2,095** by assumption.
9. Carrying on the business attracting a higher Part II fee for even one day in the year cannot be prorated away under para 2.
10. For 5 qualifying accounts operated by one company, **5 × 295 = 1,475**, limited by the **1,180** aggregate cap. This Companies Act addition does not establish SAC/ISAC registration, account classification or all other charges.
11. A company incorporated on **1 September** alters its authorised capital on **1 October**. For the first January declaration after that incorporation, branch (bb) still uses memorandum-filing-date information. Do not substitute the later alteration date by importing branch (cc)'s conversion exception. This is a hypothetical check of the captured provision, not a historical or later-year fee quotation.
12. A missing name-reservation row does not prove free reservation. The enacted **100** and concurrent replacement remain unresolved for current pricing. The **340** consideration and **100** memorandum components do not establish an all-in formation price or accepted payment.

## Manual reporting control cases

These 19 additional groups check the selected captured provisions with hypothetical facts. They are manual source controls, not actual-company compliance, an audit, a court/Registrar decision, a consuming-model run, workbook execution or authenticated filing. Sources are the Companies Act provisions cited above.

1. A majority of shareholders and directors agree to no audit. Section 88 still requires **all members and directors**, for the particular interval and specified waiver.
2. All members agree but 1 director refuses. The s 88 test still fails; company size, dormancy or exempted status does not cure it.
3. A s 71A AGM election exists but no s 88 agreement. Do not erase s 87(3)'s member-statement procedure or its 12-month availability clock.
4. No general meeting occurs within 18 months of the s 88 agreement. A member may require a termination meeting within 6 months **of notice**. This differs from s 87(3)'s meeting within 6 months **of failure** to make statements available.
5. An account record is prepared on 1 July 2026 and a statement is first required on 31 December 2026. Apply the separate 5-year preparation and 6-year first-required populations/triggers; neither establishes a universal disposal date.
6. An unlisted company keeps accounts overseas and offers only an annual Bermuda balance. That does not meet s 83's local 3-month-end information condition. Its 6-month exception needs appointed-exchange listing.
7. A permit company listed on a foreign exchange uses only 6-month local information. Section 145 states the 3-month-end rule; do not import s 83's listed exception or infer Ministerial appointment from foreign listing.
8. A qualifying listed company sends summaries and suppresses full statements. The 21-day package, 7-day election response and public inspection of both summaries and full statements remain to be checked.
9. A person demands public board minutes under s 82. Its stated rights concern **general-meeting minutes** and members/directors, not universal public board-minute access.
10. A director change is entered internally on day 20 and notified to the Registrar on day 25. The separate 30-day notification clock does not cure the missed 14-day internal clock.
11. A nonmutual guarantee company unanimously elects its employed finance bookkeeper as auditor at the appointment meeting. The exception also requires no personal care-of-funds responsibility or day-to-day financial management/recording; the vote alone is insufficient.
12. A January company declaration is labelled publicly filed audited accounts. Its fields/recipient differ from member statements and the audit report; the BCE/representative field has its own nonpublic/CITA-sharing rule.
13. A CIT return-retention rule is used to destroy Companies Act minutes. Section 273's document population, location and first-required trigger need their separate check, along with other holds.
14. Universal IFRS or ISA use is asserted from ss 84/90. Those provisions permit the stated Bermuda/foreign/appointed limbs and require identification; a named mandatory framework needs its own applicable basis.
15. At the notified auditor-removal meeting, 60% of votes cast favour removal and a majority elect a replacement. The removal fails the at-least-two-thirds threshold; replacement votes do not cure it. Check the distinct notices to members, incumbent and proposed auditor.
16. A resident representative learns of a prohibited share transfer but assumes only financially material Act breaches are reportable. Section 130(6)(b) is a distinct trigger; apply its awareness/report/remedy conditions without importing (a)'s material-effect qualifier.
17. Benefit-company directors choose no statement for a year, but qualifying requisite members give the required 3-month-before-year-end notice. Check s 156U(5)'s override, actual bye-laws, status, member-holding facts and operative instruments; the directors' earlier decision alone does not settle the result.

18. An external lender has only the client's permission to rely on an audit. That does not establish the auditor's express authorisation under s 90(3A)(b); the engaging-company and authorised-other-person routes are distinct. No actual legal reliance or audit opinion is supplied. [Companies Act, s 90(3A)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

19. A company has debt securities listed on an appointed stock exchange but no listed shares. The debt-only listing does not establish the share-listing condition for ss 87A-87C summaries or the s 156R listed-share benefit-member threshold. Listed shares satisfy that listing condition only; the other requirements still need their own facts and evidence. [Companies Act, ss 87A(1) and 156R(1)](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1981/Companies%20Act%201981).

No whole formation, reporting or country completion is claimed.
