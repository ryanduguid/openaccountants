# Bermuda Payroll & Social Contributions

**Partial payroll reconciliation, 14 November 2026.** The rules below address the 2026-27 reference year, 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027, including the change for the October-December 2026 tax period. They establish rates and selected classification controls. A complete payroll calculation, relief application, social-insurance remittance or pension engagement remains **Needs Input** where the facts or procedure identified below are missing. Tier 2 and pending review remain.

Use the current [Payroll Tax Act 1995][payroll-act], [Payroll Tax Rates Act 1995][rates-act] and [Taxes Management Act 1976][management-act], read with the enacted [2026 amendment and validation Act][amendment-five] and [2026 rates amendment][amendment-thirty-one]. The legal-site snapshot reports laws amended through 31 October 2026; capture on 14 November does not prove the absence of later law. Recheck amendments for the actual period, including future periods.

## Inputs and classification

Record the tax period, employer/self-employed/employee role, statutory classification, verified basis of annual payroll, taxable remuneration for the period, annual cap history and any relief, written permission or tax-office notice. Payroll Act ss 3-7 govern the service connection, employee and deemed-employee definitions and remuneration. The label on a bank transaction, an employment contract or a company alone does not settle these facts.

Missing classification or pay-history facts are **Needs Input**. Do not choose the standard employer rate or apply the highest employee rate to all pay as a default. This guide does not establish how to annualise new, variable or monthly payroll, allocate caps between employers, settle overlapping classes or round a full payroll computation.

## Employer rates and effective periods

Payroll Act s 10 substitutes the prescribed class rate for an employer other than a statutory exempt undertaking. Rates Act s 3 sets the standard employer and self-employed rate at **9.75%** from 1 April 2026. That rate is not an answer for an unknown classification. Payroll Act s 2(3)-(6) defines an exempt undertaking, with exclusions and Commissioner directions; Companies Act exempted-company status alone does not establish the payroll classification. Employee-specific relief can still require a separate check.

For an employer whose eligibility for the ordinary payroll class has been established, apply the following Rates Act s 5 boundaries. These employer rates apply to the relevant taxable remuneration; they are not employee marginal bands.

| Verified annual payroll/class | Employer rate in the scoped period |
| --- | --- |
| Less than BMD 200,000, Class B(f) | 0.5% for April-June and July-September 2026; 1% for October-December 2026 and subsequent tax periods under the enacted amendment |
| At least BMD 200,000 and at most BMD 350,000, Class BB(a) | 2% |
| More than BMD 350,000 and at most BMD 500,000, Class BB(b) | 4.75% |
| More than BMD 500,000 and at most BMD 1,000,000, Class BB(c) | 7% |
| More than BMD 1,000,000, Class BB(d) | 9.5% |

[Act 2026:31][amendment-thirty-one] s 2 expressly applies the 0.5% to 1% substitution to October-December 2026 and subsequent tax periods. Its 28 September operative date does not prorate July-September. The government's [2026-27 calculation page][calculation-page], updated 29 June 2026, still displays 0.5%; that display does not override the enacted later-period rule.

Other prescribed classes require their own evidence, not a turnover-only shortcut:

| Class or relief route | Required distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Hotel or restaurant, Class A | 4% requires annual payroll **more than** BMD 350,000. The calculation page's inclusive boundary differs from the Act. |
| Qualifying retail store, Class AA | 5% requires store/group annual payroll above BMD 500,000 and a Commissioner determination on written application that at least 50% of period sales are clothing, shoes, jewellery and perfume, under Rates Act s 5A. |
| Bermuda Hospitals Board and Corporations of Hamilton and St George's, Class B | 3%; the statutory entries impose no payroll threshold. |
| Licensed residential care home, Class B(aa) | 3% requires the statutory licence and annual payroll above BMD 350,000. |
| Educational, sporting or scientific institution, Class B(d) | 1% requires the Minister's opinion that it operates otherwise than for gain by its individual members. |
| Self-employed horticulturist, Class B(e) | 1.75%, subject to the statutory business definition. |
| Self-employed farmer or fisherman, Class B(g) | 0%, subject to the statutory definitions, including the fishing-boat licence requirement. Do not treat landscaping or a livery stable as qualifying merely by label. |
| School, charity and other Class C entries | 0% depends on the particular entry, including approval, registration or Ministerial conditions. An organisation's description alone is insufficient. |
| BEDC-registered qualifying new small business, Class C(k) | 0% requires that the employer does not fall within Class C(j), and is limited to 4 tax periods beginning with the establishment period and the statutory small-business/registration conditions. |
| BEDC-registered business in a designated Economic Empowerment Zone, Class C(j) | 0% is limited to 9 tax periods beginning with the establishment period and the statutory location/registration conditions. |

Do not infer priority between competing classes from this table. Taxi, minibus, ridesharing, musician, disability, termination and other special regimes need their own statutory conditions; the ridesharing permit amount is not an ordinary employer rate.

## Employee bands, remuneration and relief

Rates Act s 3A applies each rate to its own annual remuneration slice, from 1 April 2026:

| Slice of annual remuneration | Employee/deemed-employee rate on that slice |
| --- | --- |
| First BMD 48,000 | 0.25% |
| Next BMD 48,000, up to BMD 96,000 | 7.75% |
| Next BMD 104,000, up to BMD 200,000 | 10.75% |
| Next BMD 300,000, up to BMD 500,000 | 11.5% |
| Next BMD 500,000, up to BMD 1,000,000 | 12.5% |

Do not apply the top reached band to all remuneration. Payroll Act s 3(3)-(4) disregards remuneration above BMD 1,000,000 per annum for the stated employer/self-employed and employee/deemed-employee charges. A complete pay-period annualisation, cap-allocation and rounding method is not established here. Do not assume each quarter is one quarter of annual pay.

Payroll Act s 7(1) includes cash and noncash remuneration, subject to the Act and the specific s 7(2) exclusions. Under s 7(2)(h), the specified exempt-undertaking and recognised-exchange company dividends have their stated employee/deemed-employee and group conditions. Under s 7(2)(i), the first BMD 20,000 per annum of the stated dividends from another company to its employee or deemed employee is excluded. This is not a blanket rule for every dividend from a nonlisted company. Verify the payer, recipient relationship and exclusions before an amount.

New-hire relief under s 9CB concerns the employer portion for qualifying periods from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2028. Check its exact employer definition, including the statutory 'exempted undertaking' limb and the conditions for another employer; do not silently substitute the payroll-specific 'exempt undertaking' definition. Check the March 2025 payroll submission baseline (or the stated zero-baseline case), at least 15 hours per week, qualifying hire dates and continued employment, no merger/acquisition, applications and supporting documents, arrears and other-relief conditions and maintenance of BT + QE throughout the period. Employee tax continues. The linked blank application refers to s 9CA, labels the baseline return January-March 2025 and abbreviates the maintenance condition. Preserve the Act's exact March 2025 wording and obtain clarification where needed; the form does not replace s 9CB or establish acceptance of an application.

Senior relief under s 9AA and Rates Act s 4AA concerns a qualifying Bermudian aged 65 or over: employer relief applies up to and including BMD 24,000 per tax period, excluding bonus payments, with excess at the applicable rate and employee tax continuing. The birthday period is included. The blank form's 15-hour wording does not establish an additional statutory condition. Application and part-time treatment remain **Needs Input**. Caregiver employer relief under s 8(b) and the self-employed Class C(m) entry are separate routes.

Payroll Act s 19 makes the employer responsible for the employee tax, permits deductions no greater than that amount and requires reasonably equal deductions where practicable when pay is made more than once in the period. A missed deduction cannot be recovered from a later payment; the employer assumes that undeducted liability. Employer tax cannot be passed to the employee, even by agreement.

Payroll Act s 19(5) separately addresses an employer who has deducted employee tax and fails to pay the full employee liability 'within fifteen days beginning on the last day' of the tax period. This wording differs from the general return deadline below, which counts after the period closes. Keep this separate statutory timing check; do not assume ordinary return dates settle deducted-employee-tax remittance. The precise counting interaction remains **Needs Input** until reconciled with the tax office for the affected period.

## Returns, payment and electronic filing

Ordinary periods are 3 months beginning 1 April, 1 July, 1 October and 1 January under Payroll Act s 21. Management Act s 7(1) requires the prescribed return within 15 days after the period closes; s 15 requires payment when the return is furnished unless the Taxes Acts require or permit another time. Normal quarterly return dates are therefore **15 January, 15 April, 15 July and 15 October**. These do not resolve the distinct s 19(5) deducted-employee-tax clock above. The calculation page's reference to a grace period does not add another 15 days after those dates.

Check unrevoked written return notices under Management Act s 7(2)-(3), conditional return-exemption certificates under s 8, assessment payment notices under s 16 and payment extensions/instalments under s 21. A return exemption does not exempt payment. Payroll Act s 22 permits written monthly hardship arrangements with its conditions. Management Act s 36A expressly does not apply to payroll.

The [official payroll landing page][payroll-page], updated 3 November 2026, says all exempted companies and companies with annual remuneration **greater than BMD 200,000** must submit online; the June calculation page says all taxpayers with payroll **BMD 200,000 or more**. The exact boundary and taxpayer-description differences remain unresolved. Obtain current official clarification for an affected case. Do not infer an exemption from online filing at exactly BMD 200,000. Start from the official landing page, which links www.etax.gov.bm; secure sign-in, submission and successful filing are untested. The former 15 March annual-reconciliation claim remains unverified.

## Manual boundary controls and remaining work

These are hypothetical rule checks, not executed payroll, taxpayer, consuming-model or workbook results:

1. A verified Class B(f) employer below BMD 200,000 uses 0.5% for July-September 2026 and 1% for October-December. Do not split September at the Act's operative date.
2. With ordinary Class BB eligibility established, exactly BMD 200,000 and exactly BMD 350,000 use 2%; more than BMD 350,000 up to BMD 500,000 uses 4.75%; more than BMD 500,000 up to BMD 1,000,000 uses 7%; above BMD 1,000,000 uses 9.5%.
3. A hotel at exactly BMD 350,000 does not meet Class A's strictly-greater test. It needs another established class; the business label does not supply a 4% rate.
4. A retailer above BMD 500,000 without the required determination cannot claim the qualifying-store 5% rate. The missing determination is **Needs Input**.
5. An employer with annual payroll BMD 600,000 and 10 employees but no classification or period-remuneration facts receives **Needs Input**, with no tax amount or standard-rate fallback.
6. Employee remuneration above BMD 48,000 enters a second marginal slice; it does not move the first BMD 48,000 to 7.75%. Missing annualisation, cap history or pay-period method prevents a complete payroll amount.
7. At exactly BMD 200,000, retain the official e-filing conflict and seek clarification. A written monthly permission, return notice and the separate s 19(5) deducted-employee-tax clock must be checked before a deadline is given; an unresolved timing interaction is **Needs Input**.

Annual-payroll measurement for new/variable/monthly cases, competing classes, employee annualisation and cent rounding, multiple-employer cap allocation, relief/form discrepancies, the distinct deducted-employee-tax timing interaction and the online-filing conflict remain open. Social insurance, occupational pensions, a full payroll return and practitioner assurance are separate work.

## Social insurance and occupational pensions: unreconciled

The following inherited contribution statements have not been reconciled to current law in this payroll-rate update. Do not use them for a contribution or remittance decision without separate primary-source and period checks.

- **Social insurance (contributory pension) total contribution** — BMD 75.30 per week (combined employer + employee)  _(Contributory Pensions Act 1970 (https://www.gov.bm/department/social-insurance))_
- **Social insurance split** — Employer pays 50% (BMD 37.65/week) and employee pays 50% (BMD 37.65/week) for workers aged 16-64  _(Contributory Pensions Act 1970 (https://www.gov.bm/department/social-insurance))_
- **Social insurance — employees aged 65+** — Only the employer portion is due; no employee contribution  _(Contributory Pensions Act 1970 (https://www.gov.bm/department/social-insurance))_
- **Social insurance remittance deadline:** the inherited monthly/last-Friday claim is **Needs Input**. The cited [Social Insurance landing page](https://www.gov.bm/department/social-insurance) identifies the administrator but does not reconcile this procedure. Verify the applicable remittance rule and period before giving a deadline.
- **Occupational (private) pension contribution** — Mandatory for Bermudians/spouses under the National Pension Scheme; typically 5% employer + 5% employee of pensionable earnings (approx — confirm current rate)  _(National Pension Scheme (Occupational Pensions) Act 1998 (https://www.gov.bm/bermuda-pension))_
