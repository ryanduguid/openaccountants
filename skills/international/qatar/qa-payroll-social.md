# Qatar Payroll & Social Contributions

> **Every rate the previous version gave is confirmed by the statute — and it was
> citing a law firm for all seven of them.** Reading Law No. 1 of 2022 as published
> by the General Authority for Retirement and Social Insurance confirms 21%, 7%,
> 14%, the QAR 100,000 ceiling, the contribution-salary definition and the
> fifth-of-the-month deadline. It also adds the **self-employed**, a **State
> subsidy** of the private-sector employer's share, a **2% monthly late fine**, a
> **10% under-declaration surcharge**, and the fact that the **GCC extension rests
> on a different law entirely**.

## 1. There is no income tax

- **No PAYE / payroll income tax withholding** — Qatar levies no income tax on
  employment income, so nothing is withheld from wages  _([Income Tax Law No. 24 of 2018](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/qatar/corporate/other-taxes))_

## 2. Who is covered — and it is narrower than "nationals"

- **The insured person must be Qatari** — art. 1 defines *المؤمن عليه* as *"every
  **Qatari** employed in the government sector, or a worker in the private sector,
  **or self-employed**, for whom the contribution is paid or who pays it for
  himself under the provisions of this Law"*. The previous version covered
  employees only; **the self-employed are inside the scheme and pay for
  themselves**  _(Law No. 1 of 2022 (Social Insurance Law), art. 1 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_
- **"Employer" is itself defined by reference to Qatari staff** — for the private
  sector, art. 1 defines *جهة العمل* as any natural or legal person who **employs
  one or more Qatari workers** on a regular basis for a wage. A private-sector
  business with no Qatari employees is outside the definition  _(Law No. 1 of 2022, art. 1 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_
- **Non-Qatari employees generate no social insurance** — there is no contribution
  for expatriate staff. Their end-of-service entitlement arises under the **Labour
  Law**, which is a separate obligation and not a payroll contribution  _([Labour Law No. 14 of 2004](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/qatar/corporate/other-taxes))_

> **The GCC extension is a different statute, and the previous version merged
> them.** Law No. 1 of 2022 covers Qataris. Insurance protection for **GCC
> nationals working outside their home country** in a member state comes from
> **Law No. 4 of 2007**, as amended by **Law No. 5 of 2021** — both listed
> separately in GRSIA's own legislation register. Citing Law 1/2022 for GCC
> coverage points at a law that does not contain it. [RESEARCH GAP — the GCC
> unified-system rates and mechanics were not read; the register serves those two
> instruments in Arabic only and they were not opened.]
> _(General Authority for Retirement and Social Insurance, Laws & Legislations register — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/English/Pensions/Pages/Laws-and-Legislations.aspx)_

## 3. The contribution — Law No. 1 of 2022, art. 11

Article 11 is short enough to state in full. The contribution is computed on the
contribution-account salary **not exceeding QAR 100,000**, and is paid at **21%**,
as follows:

| Component | Rate | Borne by |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Employee share — *"deducted from the contribution-account salary of the insured person"* | **7%** | Employee (deducted at source) |
| Employer share — *"paid by the employer on the contribution-account salary"* | **14%** | Employer |
| **Total** | **21%** | |

_(Law No. 1 of 2022, art. 11 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_

- **The contribution salary is defined, and the definition matters** — art. 1:
  *راتب حساب الاشتراك* is *"the **basic** salary or wage of the insured person,
  **plus the social allowance and the housing allowance**"*. Other allowances are
  outside it. The previous version had this right and cited a law firm for it  _(Law No. 1 of 2022, art. 1 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_
- **QAR 100,000 is a ceiling on that salary, not on the contribution** — the
  computation applies to contribution salary *"not exceeding one hundred thousand
  riyals"*  _(Law No. 1 of 2022, art. 11 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_
- **Payment is due by the fifth of the following month** — contributions are paid
  to the Fund *"within a period not exceeding the fifth of the month following the
  month for which those contributions are due"*  _(Law No. 1 of 2022, art. 11 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_
- **The State may pay part of the employer's 14%** — the final paragraph of art. 11
  permits the **public treasury**, with Cabinet approval, to **bear a proportion of
  the contribution due from a private-sector employer**, under rules it sets. This
  is a live subsidy mechanism and was absent from the previous version entirely  _(Law No. 1 of 2022, art. 11 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_
- **Only one job counts** — where an insured person works for more than one
  employer subject to retirement or social insurance law, the contribution is
  **limited to one job**, on conditions set by the Regulations  _(Law No. 1 of 2022, art. 10 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_

> **[UNVERIFIED — grandfathering.]** The previous version noted that "existing
> enrolments above this amount may be grandfathered". No transitional provision to
> that effect was found in the Law's text. It may sit in the executive regulations
> (Cabinet Resolution No. 3 of 2025), which were downloaded but not read in full.
> **This guide does not assert it either way.**

## 4. Penalties

- **Late payment: 2% per month** — art. 21 imposes a delay fine of **2% per month**
  on contributions and any other amounts due to the Fund that are not paid by the
  deadlines in the Law and the Regulations, running **from the date payment fell
  due until the date of payment**  _(Law No. 1 of 2022, art. 21 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_
- **Under-declaring salaries: a 10% surcharge** — art. 22 makes an employer that
  failed to register or to contribute for **all or some** of its staff, **or that
  paid contributions on something other than the real salaries or wages**, liable
  for an **additional amount of 10% of the total value of the real contributions
  payable**. Paying on an understated salary is treated the same as not paying at
  all  _(Law No. 1 of 2022, art. 22 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_

## 5. Two things not to confuse

- **A second QAR 100,000 appears in art. 57 and is unrelated.** That one caps the
  **aggregate** where a person combines more than one pension, or a pension and a
  salary. It has nothing to do with the contribution ceiling in art. 11, and the
  two are easy to conflate because the figure is identical  _(Law No. 1 of 2022, art. 57 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_
- **Law No. 24 of 2002 is repealed.** Art. 3 of the issuing law repeals it, so
  guidance written against the 2002 regime does not describe current law  _(Law No. 1 of 2022 (issuing law), art. 3 — https://www.grsia.gov.qa/Regulations%20and%20Policies/Law-1-2022.pdf)_

The rates, ceiling, definitions, deadline, subsidy provision and penalties were
read from the Arabic text of Law No. 1 of 2022 as published by the General
Authority for Retirement and Social Insurance. Three things are left open: the
**GCC unified system** (Law 4/2007 as amended by Law 5/2021) was not read; the
**executive regulations** (Cabinet Resolution No. 3 of 2025) were downloaded but
not read in full, so any detail they add — including the grandfathering point in
§3 — is unconfirmed; and the **end-of-service gratuity** for non-Qatari staff is
still cited to a secondary source rather than to the Labour Law.
