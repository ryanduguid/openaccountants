# Norway tax-system orientation

Load `workflow-base`. Record where the person lives or the business is established, where the income arises, the legal form, the tax period and any treaty. Check Svalbard's separate tax rules and any continuing mainland liability. Check the separate employment-income regime for Jan Mayen and the Norwegian Antarctic dependencies if it applies. [Norwegian Tax Administration, mainland taxation whilst on Svalbard](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/taxes/get-the-taxes-right/svalbard/brief-information-on-mainland-taxation-in-connection-with-periods-of-residence-on-svalbard/) · [Norwegian Tax Administration, Jan Mayen and Antarctic dependencies](https://www.skatteetaten.no/satser/skattesatser-pa-jan-mayen/)

## Territorial reach

Resident individuals and entities within the resident charge generally account for worldwide income. A resident individual can also owe wealth tax on worldwide taxable assets. An AS or ASA is exempt from wealth tax under § 2-36(1). Check that section and the rules for the legal form when assessing another entity. A person or entity with limited tax liability is taxable on specified Norwegian connections, including qualifying property, business and employment income. Check the statutory source categories, the relevant treaty and any double-tax relief. [Tax Act, §§ 2-1 to 2-3 and 2-36](https://lovdata.no/nav/lov/1999-03-26-14/kap2) · [Norwegian Tax Administration, company wealth-tax exemption](https://oppslag.rettskilder.skatteetaten.no/rettskilder2/type/handboker/skatte-abc/gjeldende/skatteabc-A-7/skatteabc-A-7.026/skatteabc-A-7.028)

## Residence

An individual generally becomes resident after staying more than 183 days in any 12 months or more than 270 days in any 36 months. Count whole and partial days. If the 183-day test is met in the first calendar year, residence generally starts on the first day of stay. If the count crosses the threshold in a later year, residence generally starts on 1 January of the year in which the threshold is exceeded. For someone who is tax resident on Svalbard only, the 270-day rule does not apply. They become mainland tax resident under this rule only after more than 183 days on the mainland in any 12 months. Check cessation separately when the person leaves Norway. A company covered by § 2-2 is generally resident if formed under Norwegian company law or effectively managed in Norway. Effective management considers both board and daily management and the circumstances of the organisation and business. Apply any treaty that treats it as resident in another country. [Tax Act, §§ 2-1 and 2-2](https://lovdata.no/lov/1999-03-26-14/kap2) · [Norwegian Tax Administration, commencement of residence](https://oppslag.rettskilder.skatteetaten.no/rettskilder2/type/handboker/skatte-abc/gjeldende/skatteabc-U-19/skatteabc-U-19.004/skatteabc-U-19.022)

## Taxpayer types

A sole proprietor accounts for business income under the individual tax rules. An AS or ASA is a separate taxpayer. For a partnership within § 10-40, compute the partnership result under the tax rules and take each participant's share into account under § 10-41. For a foreign trust, establish who controls the assets, what rights the trust instrument gives each party and the nature of any distribution. Check whether the facts bring an owner or beneficiary within Norwegian tax and whether the Norwegian-controlled foreign entity rules apply. [Tax Act, §§ 2-1, 2-2 and 10-40 to 10-41](https://lovdata.no/lov/1999-03-26-14/%C2%A710-40) · [Norwegian Tax Administration, trusts and NOKUS](https://www.skatteetaten.no/bedrift-og-organisasjon/skatt/skattemelding-naringsdrivende/selskap/nokus/) · [Tax Appeals Board, foreign trust wealth](https://www.skatteetaten.no/rettskilder/type/vedtak/skatteklagenemnda/skattemessig-behandling-av-trust-formue-i-utlandet/)

## Currency

Norway uses the Norwegian krone (NOK). Annual accounts generally use NOK or the functional currency and may be presented in NOK, EUR or the accounting currency under § 3-4. Check bookkeeping and tax reporting rules separately. The functional currency can be used for eligible bookkeeping, subject to NOK conversion or fixing rules for specific items such as VAT reporting and payroll. Retain the rates and conversion basis. [Accounting Act, § 3-4](https://lovdata.no/lov/1998-07-17-56/%C2%A73-4) · [Norwegian Tax Administration, bookkeeping currency](https://www.skatteetaten.no/rettskilder/type/handboker/skatte-abc/gjeldende/r-3-regnskap--foretak-med-bokforingsplikt/R-3.002/R-3.007/)

## Assessment periods

The ordinary income period is the calendar year. A business required to keep accounts that uses a different accounting year under the specific Accounting Act provisions cited in § 14-1(3) generally uses the last accounting year that ended before 1 January of the year of assessment. Check the legal basis for the different year and the applicable tax period together. [Tax Act, § 14-1](https://lovdata.no/lov/1999-03-26-14/%C2%A714-1) · [Accounting Act, § 1-7](https://lovdata.no/lov/1998-07-17-56/%C2%A71-7)

## Responsible authorities

Skatteetaten is the Norwegian Tax Administration and handles ordinary income tax returns and assessments. [Tax Administration Act, § 2-1](https://lovdata.no/lov/2016-05-27-14/%C2%A72-1) · [Norwegian Tax Administration, taxes and returns](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/taxes/)

## Fictional intake

An individual splits a long stay in Norway across 2 calendar years, trades through an AS and is named as a beneficiary of a foreign trust. Keep a day count for individual residence, determine the company's residence and period independently and obtain the trust deed and distribution records to classify the person's rights and receipts.

A Svalbard-only tax resident spends 273 days on the mainland over 36 months and at most 183 mainland days in any 12 months. They do not become mainland tax resident under § 2-1(2) on those facts. Check separately whether any earlier mainland residence continues.
