# Saint Lucia monthly National Insurance administrative reference

> **General reference only.** This Tier 2 draft illustrates a retained monthly NIC administrative profile. It does not establish the operative monthly legal ceiling, certify current law, authorise a wage deduction or provide complete payroll or a return. Guide presence and component checks do not establish comprehensive jurisdiction acceptance.

## Scope and unresolved ceiling authority

Load both workflow bases in full. Preserve all 4 universal outputs and 18 checks, all 5 payroll outputs and 12 checks, all 12 mandatory payroll country slots, shared refusals and the exact additional refusals below. Every required logical output must remain identifiable. This companion leaves the country contract incomplete; no incomplete prescribed financial output is permitted.

The fictional profile stipulates one ordinary registered domestic employee aged 30 throughout an established monthly contribution period, one employer, insurable employment under a contract of service and fulfilled applicable domicile/residence conditions. There is one complete ordinary monthly cash-wage payment at intervals longer than a fortnight. There are no other employers, foreign-service or diplomatic exemptions, public-service or own-account arrangements, benefit or death periods, age transitions, disputed pay categories, instalments, advances, arrears, late or cross-month payments, holiday amounts set aside, termination, abnormal practices or Director-approved alternative arrangements. These are stipulated fictional facts, not defaults for missing information. Selected additional earnings in A10–A11 are separately identified and classified by stipulation.

**The monthly ceiling's operative bridge is unestablished.** Act Schedule 2 states 5% for each party per monthly, fortnightly or weekly contribution period; regulation 11(1) repeats the rates subject to prescribed variations and maximum. Act Schedule 3 exempts the portion above 60,000 **per annum**, subject to another prescribed limit. Separately, NIC FAQ Q23 states the first 5,000 earnings **in a month**. The monthly administrative amount is not derived here by dividing the annual statute by 12. Do not call these different period rules interchangeable, declare the monthly practice invalid or infer annual exhaustion, payroll adjustments, credits or a refund from their numerical relationship.

Before an actual calculation, establish the complete applicable Act, amendment, commencement, Order, regulation, preserved earlier instrument, exemption, determination and direction chain. Resolve the precise authority and period application of the monthly ceiling with the reviewer. Act section 93 preserves specified instruments made under the earlier 1978 Act until revoked; a search limited to instruments after 2000 is insufficient. A retained consolidation, live FAQ, index, old annual report or absent search result cannot establish the complete chain. The labelled fictional administrative arithmetic below remains available whilst actual liability, deductions, recovery and filing stay held.

## Intake and administrative-profile mechanics

Reuse relevant redacted records before grouped questions. Establish legal parties, registration, insurability and residence conditions, employee age, employer and employment category, all wage components and dates, payment interval, ordinary period, exemptions and benefits, previous deductions/payments, other work, special arrangements, applicable authority, tools and reviewer identity. Present the profile and wait for confirmation before classification. Keep identifiers, bank information and signatures with the reviewer. Confirmation of facts cannot supply missing law.

1. **Insurability is independent.** Act section 26 has registration and prescribed domicile/residence or overseas-employment conditions, not a local-work-only test. Under section 26(4), casual, relative and partner employment is treated as insurable unless regulations otherwise provide; do not copy a categorical family exclusion from another country. Act Schedule 3 separately covers specified overseas-service, diplomatic and temporary noncitizen cases. Paragraphs 3–4 require the employer to satisfy the Director of the specified alternative coverage/benefits under paragraph 5. Foreign insurance alone is insufficient. Public servants, self-employment and voluntary insurance need their own branches.
2. **Period and earnings.** Act section 2 defines the contribution period by pay interval and the week, fortnight or month in which wages are paid; it is not automatically the service month. The illustration uses one normal monthly payment. Regulation 11 includes gross earnings received by or on behalf of the insured person, including overtime, allowances, commissions, shift payments and service charge. Holiday amounts set aside have their own related-period allocation; listed additional earnings paid later generally follow the payment period. Christmas bonuses, annual production bonuses, severance and terminal gratuities are specified exclusions, not permission to exclude every bonus or ordinary December salary. Abnormal practices remain subject to Director determination. Net bank pay is not a proved contribution base.
3. **Labelled monthly administrative arithmetic only.** Under the stipulated profile, let W be complete non-negative included earnings for the single ordinary monthly payment. Let B = min(W, 5,000), employee reference E = 5% × B, employer reference R = 5% × B and displayed total T = E + R = 10% × B. The monthly 5,000 is the separately observed FAQ profile, not an established monthly legal ceiling or an annual limit divided by 12. Display B, E, R and T separately. All actual contribution, deduction, recovery, payable, refund and financial-deliverable fields remain unaccepted and held.
4. **Boundaries and precision.** Keep independently established months separate. Do not cap their combined earnings as one month. Conversely, no general instalment aggregation rule is established here; split payments, advances, arrears, irregular intervals and cross-month payments require separate source resolution. Do not derive a weekly/fortnightly ceiling, annual exhaustion mechanism or multi-employer offset from this monthly profile. Preserve exact E and R separately, including fractions. Neither a 2-decimal input nor a rounded web example establishes lawful cash rounding. Unknown currency, conversion, sequence or rounding requires Needs Input. Do not import this ceiling into income tax, health/security levies, pensions, minimum wages or severance.
5. **Liability and permitted recovery differ.** Sections 28 and 33 place both shares on the employer for payment; section 32(5) prohibits shifting the employer's contribution to the employee. Section 32(2)'s six-month and employee-agreement conditions must be read with regulation 16's good-faith error, prior notification and six-month conditions. Neither an employee's consent alone nor correct profile arithmetic authorises recovery. Regulation 21 requires Director satisfaction and a written application for error refunds, controls offset and provides no interest. Actual recovery, refund and ledger allocation remain held. Do not create a cash debt, automatic set-off or net wage from the illustration.
6. **Exceptional periods and administration.** Section 32(3) has a specific employee-death-period exception. Regulation 20 distinguishes no work **and** no wages from unpaid apprenticeship; paid leave is not automatically exempt. Regulations 8–9 require C2/remittance to **reach** the Director within 7 days after month end and C3 to be **dispatched** within 7 days. Regulation 14 separately addresses advance deductions, accelerated payment at termination and Director-approved alternative arrangements; those cases cannot inherit ordinary timing. Regulation 17 describes a February annual return. Current forms, actual dates, holiday/weekend effects, receipt, channels, lawful rounding, surcharges and administrative acceptance remain held. The 2025 payment-method amendment does not prove a particular electronic portal or submission is accepted.
