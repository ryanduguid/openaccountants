# Gabon Tax Overview

## Tax system recorded for 2025

This section describes the DGI's **CGI 2025** compilation, whose header
identifies amendments through Loi n°033/2024 of 30 December 2024. Later
amendments and current administrative practice remain open. The corporate,
VAT and formation guides separately record selected provisions enacted for
**2026**. [DGI, CGI 2025](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)

- **Tax authority.** Direction Générale des Impôts (DGI).
  [DGI's official website](https://dgi.ga/)
- **Currency.** The Central African CFA franc (**XAF**), issued by the BEAC,
  circulates in Gabon. This currency reference was checked on
  **11 November 2026**. [BEAC, monetary role](https://www.beac.int/billets-pieces/mission-de-beac/)
  and [Banque de France, monetary cooperation](https://www.banque-france.fr/fr/banque-de-france/partenariats-afrique-france)
- **Tax year.** IS and IRPP normally use the calendar year's income as the
  base for the following budget year's assessment. A company starting in
  the final **6 months** before the mandatory closing date may close its
  first balance sheet at the end of the next budget year.
  [CGI 2025, articles 15 and 83](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Corporate territoriality.** Subject to international conventions, IS
  applies to profits from businesses operated or transactions carried out
  in Gabon. Resident companies are registered in Gabon or effectively managed
  there. Non-residents with a permanent establishment are taxed on profits
  attributable to it, similar sales and similar industrial or commercial
  activities in Gabon. Permanent-establishment definitions and exceptions
  require separate checks.
  [Article 7-I(1) to (3) and article 7-II](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Individual residence and source.** Subject to international conventions
  and the following code provisions, IRPP applies to individuals habitually
  resident in Gabon or present for at least **183 days** in the year.
  Habitual residence includes an available home as owner, usufructuary or
  tenant, or a principal place of stay or centre of interests or business.
  Individuals habitually resident abroad are liable on Gabon-source income,
  including salaries and pensions. Article 74 also covers state officials
  abroad who are exempt where they serve and income allocated to Gabon under
  a double-tax convention. Check household, exemption and treaty rules.
  [Article 74](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Corporate rates.** IS is **30%**, increased to **35%** for oil and mining
  businesses, on taxable profit rounded down to the nearest **CFA 1,000**.
  [Article 16](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Minimum corporate tax.** Subject to article 26 exemptions, IS cannot be
  less than either **1%** of the article 25 global-turnover base or the
  **CFA 500,000** minimum de perception. The corporate guide records the
  base, relief and comparison with ordinary IS.
  [Articles 24 to 26](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **VAT.** The standard rate is **18%**, with specified reduced and zero
  rates. Check the VAT guide's separate 2025 and enacted 2026 classifications.
  [Article 221](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Annual IS return.** File before **30 April of the following year**.
  [Article 20](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)

## Personal income scale recorded for 2025

Article 168 rounds taxable income down to the nearest **CFA 1,000**, divides
it by the applicable family parts and applies article 174 to income per
part. The resulting tax is multiplied by the number of parts before the
specified withholding and tax credits. The following table gives marginal
rates, rather than a single rate on the whole income. Family-part allocation,
special assessments and any business-income minimum tax require separate
checks. [Articles 168 to 175](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)

| Annual taxable income per part, CFA | Marginal rate |
|---|---|
| 0 to 1,500,000 | 0% |
| 1,500,001 to 1,920,000 | 5% |
| 1,920,001 to 2,700,000 | 10% |
| 2,700,001 to 3,600,000 | 15% |
| 3,600,001 to 5,160,000 | 20% |
| 5,160,001 to 7,500,000 | 25% |
| 7,500,001 to 11,000,000 | 30% |
| 11,000,001 and above | 35% |

[CGI 2025, article 174](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)

For example, assume a single person without dependants has **1 part** and
**CFA 12,000,000** of taxable income after deductions, without any special
assessment. Article 174's top-band formula is **35% × income per part minus
CFA 1,669,000**. Gross IRPP is therefore **CFA 2,531,000**, before crediting
withholding or other credits. This is an annual assessment illustration.
[Articles 168, 170 and 174](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
