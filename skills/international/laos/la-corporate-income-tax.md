# Laos Corporate Income Tax

## Profit tax rates

> **⚠ This guide rests on a law that has been replaced.** Every figure below is
> referred to the ***Law on Income Tax No. 67/NA of 18 June 2019***. That law is
> **repealed**. The Lao Official Gazette publishes the ***Law on Income Tax
> (Revised)*** — ກົດໝາຍວ່າດ້ວຍ ອາກອນລາຍໄດ້ (ສະບັບປັບປຸງ) — adopted by National
> Assembly resolution **No. 164/NA of 25 June 2025** and promulgated by
> **Presidential Decree No. 145/PO of 6 August 2025**, signed by the President of
> the National Assembly. The register lists its status as **ປັດຈຸບັນ (current)**.
>
> **Its article 74 (ປັບປຸງ) is explicit.** The Law takes effect **from 1 September
> 2025**, and *"ປ່ຽນແທນ"* — **replaces** — **Law on Income Tax No. 67/NA of 18 June
> 2019**, together with **Chapter III of Law No. 01/NA of 7 August 2021** amending
> certain articles of the tax laws.
>
> **⚠ Two dates that do not sit together, reported rather than reconciled.** Article
> 74 conditions commencement on promulgation *and* publication in the Official
> Gazette, and names **1 September 2025**. The Gazette's own register records the
> publication date as **19 June 2026** — after the date the Law names. Which date
> governs for a given period is a question for a Lao practitioner; this guide states
> both and resolves neither.
>
> **What this means for the rows below.** They were not re-verified against the new
> Law. The revised text runs to 74 articles over 34 scanned pages with no text
> layer, and its articles carry individual **(ປັບປຸງ)** markers showing which were
> amended — so some figures below may be unchanged and others superseded, and this
> guide cannot say which. **Treat every rate, threshold and deadline here as
> referring to a repealed instrument until a reviewer checks it against the revised
> Law.** [RESEARCH GAP — **the profit-tax rate articles 15 and 16 have since been read
> and are set out immediately below**; the rest of the guide — lump-sum tax, withholding,
> deadlines, deductions — has not been checked against the replacing Law.]
>
> _(Lao Official Gazette, Ministry of Finance register — [laoofficialgazette.gov.la](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/index.php?r=site/listlegistioncp&agencies_id=2&old=0); [Law on Income Tax (Revised), arts. 73–74](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/kcfinder/upload/files/88-25-6-2025_0001.pdf))_

> survived.** Articles 15 and 16 of the **Law on Income Tax (Revised)** were rendered and
> read at 200 dpi. Every figure below appears in the Lao text in **words and digits
> together**, which is the condition for accepting a figure from a scan. This does **not**
> lift the repeal warning above from the rest of the guide — only the rate rows were
> checked.
>
> **⚠ And article 15 contains a domestic minimum top-up tax that this corpus's Pillar Two
> sweep did not cover for Laos.**
>

- **Standard profit tax rate — 20%, confirmed in the replacing Law** — **Article 15**: *"ອັດຕາອາກອນກຳໄລ ກຳນົດອັດຕາ **ຊາວສ່ວນຮ້ອຍ (20%)** ສຳລັບວິສາຫະກິດ"* — the profit tax rate is set at **twenty percent (20%) for enterprises**. The headline rate came through the repeal unchanged percent  _([Law on Income Tax (Revised), art. 15](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/kcfinder/upload/files/88-25-6-2025_0001.pdf))_
- **⚠ Domestic minimum top-up tax — Laos has one, and no guide in this repository said so** — **Article 15**, second paragraph: for a legal entity in a **group of companies that is a member of a multinational company**, where the **effective tax rate actually paid** by all the legal entities is **less than fifteen percent (15%)** under international rules, that entity **must pay additional domestic minimum profit tax to make the shortfall up in full**. The **procedures and methods of implementation are left to separate regulations**, which were **not read**. Scope, the EUR 750m revenue test, commencement and safe harbours are all unestablished here — what is established is that the charge exists in the Law percent  _([Law on Income Tax (Revised), art. 15](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/kcfinder/upload/files/88-25-6-2025_0001.pdf))_
- **⚠ Increased rate — 22% on alcoholic beverages, with 2% to the health fund** — **Article 16(1.2)**: **twenty-two percent (22%)** for enterprises producing, importing and selling **alcoholic beverages** — white spirits, fortified spirits, whisky, wine and beer are named — of which **two percent (2%) must be paid into the health-promotion fund**. Absent from this guide entirely percent  _([Law on Income Tax (Revised), art. 16(1.2)](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/kcfinder/upload/files/88-25-6-2025_0001.pdf))_
- **⚠ Increased rate — 30% on casino business** — **Article 16(1.3)**: **thirty percent (30%)** for **casino business** (*ກິດຈະການກາຊິໂນ*). Also absent from this guide percent  _([Law on Income Tax (Revised), art. 16(1.3)](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/kcfinder/upload/files/88-25-6-2025_0001.pdf))_
- **⚠ The 13% listed-company rate is not in article 16** — This guide gives *"Companies listed on the Lao Securities Exchange — 13% for the first 4 years from listing"*. Article 16's reduced limb contains **only the 5% and 7% rates** below. The 13% may sit elsewhere in the revised Law, or in the Law on Investment Promotion, or it may not have survived the repeal — **this was not established, and the row should not be relied on until it is** [RESEARCH GAP]  _([Law on Income Tax (Revised), art. 16(2)](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/kcfinder/upload/files/88-25-6-2025_0001.pdf))_
- **Overview of profit tax rates** — Lao PDR levies profit tax (corporate income tax) at a flat standard rate with several sector-specific rates. The standard rate was reduced from 24% to 20%. Figures below are an unverified draft.
- **Standard profit tax rate** — 20% percent  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
- **Companies listed on the Lao Securities Exchange** — 13% for the first 4 years from listing percent  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
- **Tobacco — 22%, confirmed** — **Article 16(1.1)**: **twenty-two percent (22%)** for enterprises producing, importing and selling **tobacco products**, of which **two percent (2%) must be paid into the Tobacco Control Fund** under the Law on Tobacco Control. Confirmed against the replacing Law percent  _([Law on Income Tax (Revised), art. 16(1.1)](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/kcfinder/upload/files/88-25-6-2025_0001.pdf))_
- **Mineral / mining — 35%, confirmed, and it reaches exporters too** — **Article 16(1.4)** of the replacing Law: **thirty-five percent (35%)** for **mineral exploration and extraction concession business *and* mineral exporters** (*ຜູ້ສົ່ງອອກແຮ່ທາດ*). The "(approx — confirm)" resolves, and the charge is wider than "under concession" suggests percent  _([Law on Income Tax (Revised), art. 16(1.4)](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/kcfinder/upload/files/88-25-6-2025_0001.pdf))_
- **⚠ The 5% rate is wider than "education" and it is time-limited** — **Article 16(2.1)**: **five percent (5%)** for business connected with **innovation**, building **modern schools and hospitals**, **production factories and education equipment**, and **production and greening of urban areas** — **until the end of the profit-tax incentive period fixed by the Law on Investment Promotion**. This guide narrowed it to "education / training & research centres" and omitted the time limit, which is the part that decides whether it still applies percent  _([Law on Income Tax (Revised), art. 16(2.1)](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/kcfinder/upload/files/88-25-6-2025_0001.pdf))_
- **Green technology — 7%, confirmed, and also time-limited** — **Article 16(2.2)**: **seven percent (7%)** for business using technology derived from **innovation**, **environmentally friendly**, **preventing depletion of natural resources** and **clean energy** in production — again **until the end of the profit-tax incentive period under the Law on Investment Promotion**. The "(approx — confirm)" resolves; the time limit was missing percent  _([Law on Income Tax (Revised), art. 16(2.2)](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/kcfinder/upload/files/88-25-6-2025_0001.pdf))_

## Lump-sum tax for micro/SME businesses

- **Overview of lump-sum tax for micro/SME businesses** — Small businesses that do not keep full Lao accounting books pay a lump-sum (presumptive) tax on turnover instead of standard profit tax.
- **Annual turnover of LAK 50 million or less** — 0% (exempt from lump-sum tax) percent  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
- **Lump-sum tax — manufacturing/agriculture (turnover LAK 50m–400m)** — 1% of turnover percent  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
- **Lump-sum tax — commerce (turnover LAK 50m–400m)** — 2% of turnover percent  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
- **Lump-sum tax — services (turnover LAK 50m–400m)** — 3% of turnover percent  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_

## Withholding tax and filing

- **Overview of withholding tax and filing** — Lao PDR withholds on **six** heads of domestic income, not three, and runs a **separate deemed-profit regime for foreign contractors** on top. The annual return and instalment deadlines are below.
- **Withholding on dividends** — 10% (domestic-law rate; treaties may reduce, e.g. 5% for ≥10% holdings) percent  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **Withholding on interest** — 10% percent  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **Withholding on royalties (IP)** — 5% percent. Note the asymmetry: royalties are the **lowest**-rated head at 5%, while an ordinary consulting invoice is at 10%  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **Withholding on service fees** — **10%** on brokerage, consulting and service fees  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **Withholding on rent** — **10%** on the sale and lease of assets  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **Withholding on artist and athlete income** — **10%**  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **Withholding on share transfers** — **2%**  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/withholding-taxes)_
- **Foreign Withholding Tax (FWHT) on foreign contractors — a separate regime, and a final tax** — A foreign supplier without a Lao presence is not taxed on profit but on **deemed profit by activity**, and the FWHT bundles **profit tax and VAT** into one deduction. The deemed-profit tax element runs **1.4%** (agricultural manufacturing), **2%** (industrial production), **3%** (commerce and services) and **6%** (electricity and mineral supply), with **10% VAT** added on services. It is the **final** tax on the foreign supplier — there is no return and no refund of it — so the rate has to be right in the contract, not corrected later  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/withholding-taxes)_

> **The three heads this guide used to name were the first three rows of the
> source it cites.** PwC's Lao PDR withholding page carries six domestic rows and
> the FWHT deemed-profit table; the guide reproduced dividends, interest and
> royalties and stopped. A Lao payer engaging a foreign consultant, leasing an
> asset, or booking a performer would have found nothing here — and for a foreign
> contractor the FWHT is a final tax, so getting it wrong is not recoverable.
- **Annual profit tax return deadline** — By 20 January of the year following the tax year. Confirmed: the return and the financial statements have different dates, 20 January and 31 March, and the two filings draw on the same accounts  _(Tax Administration Law (Lao PDR); PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Lao PDR corporate tax administration)_
- **Annual financial statement filing** — By 31 March following a 31 December year-end  _(Accounting Law (Lao PDR) — https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report-international/laos-tax-agency-issues-notice-on-2025-financial-statement-filing-deadline-for-enterprise-account-holders)_
- **Tax base** — Domestic companies taxed on worldwide income; foreign companies on Lao-source income  _(Law on Income Tax (Law No. 67/NA, 2019) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lao-pdr/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)_
