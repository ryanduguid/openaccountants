# Grenada investment incentives

Identify the project, priority sector, relief sought and period in which the investment came into use. Check the legislation governing each relief, the Grenada Investment Development Corporation (GIDC) certificate and the Inland Revenue Division (IRD) or Customs requirements for claiming it. The Investment Act gives investors access to incentives for which they qualify under the relevant law. This guide traces the 2015 and 2016 statutory changes and identifies the checks needed for a live claim. [Investment Act 42 of 2014, ss 6–8][INVESTMENT]

## Registration and certificate

The Investment Act commenced on 29 April 2016. Section 6(2) requires each investment entity to register with the Agency. Section 16 repealed 5 named enactments and preserved certain licences and incentives in force at commencement through their original terms. Apply the separate rules for unused benefits and pending applications. Check the issue date, validity and original conditions before relying on a pre-commencement entitlement. [SRO 31 of 2016][INVESTMENT-START] · [Investment Act, ss 6 and 16][INVESTMENT]

Under ss 49–50 of the GIDC Act that commenced on 23 November 2016, a qualifying investor may apply for a certificate in the prescribed form. GIDC considers whether the investment is within a priority sector, the application satisfies the applicable requirements and the project is lawful and beneficial to Grenada. GIDC may request information for monitoring and its officers or designated agents may enter the premises at a reasonable time for that purpose. Section 51, as replaced in November 2016, provides for an appeal to the minister within 60 days after the person is informed of a decision. Confirm the current filing route and the conditions in the actual certificate. [GIDC Act 30 of 2016, ss 49–50][GIDC-ACT] · [Commencement Notice, SRO 64 of 2016][GIDC-START] · [GIDC (Amendment) Act 31 of 2016, s 15][GIDC-CHANGE]

GIDC's public fiscal incentives form refers to SRO 33 of 2016. It asks for business registration documents, the business plan, evidence of financing and title or lease, and lists tax compliance, NIS registration and project approvals for applicable cases. Retain the filed application, proof of receipt, certificate, conditions and any later amendments. Confirm with IRD what it requires to substantiate a separate income tax claim. [GIDC application form, pp 2–3][GIDC-FORM] · [Customs Duties (Exemptions) Order, SRO 33 of 2016][CUSTOMS]

## Qualifying investment allowance

Act 27 of 2015 replaced s 36A for property brought into use from 4 September 2015. It used the Sixth Schedule and allowed excess deductions to carry forward for up to 9 years of assessment. From 1 May 2016, Act 8 of 2016 substituted the Investment Act and regulations for that schedule reference and extended carry-over to 14 later years of assessment. Under the amended s 36A, the allowance is a deduction equal to 100% of qualifying costs of buildings, structures or equipment brought into use in the basis period. It reduces assessable business income and is additional to any annual allowance under the Second Schedule. [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 27 of 2015, ss 1(5) and 5][IT-2015] · [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 8 of 2016, s 2][IT-2016] · [Commencement Order, SRO 26 of 2016][IT-START] · [Priority Sectors Regulations, SRO 32 of 2016][SECTORS]

For assets brought into use from 1 May 2016, check eligibility in this order. Apply the Sixth Schedule for the earlier period described above. [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 27 of 2015, ss 1(5) and 5][IT-2015] · [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 8 of 2016, s 2][IT-2016] · [Priority Sectors Regulations, regs 1 and 3–5][SECTORS]

1. Identify the sector in reg 3. Information technology and telecommunications are on the general priority-sector list. Regulation 3(2) excludes those services specifically for s 36A.
2. Classify the investment under reg 4(1). The categories include new construction, expansion, reconstruction costing more than 40% of the building or structure's written-down value and specified installation of medical equipment. For a medical installation in an existing facility, the equipment cost must exceed 75% of the written-down value of the existing equipment.
3. Apply reg 4(2)–(3) to the sector combination. The listed agriculture, manufacturing and creative arts group must exceed XCD 1 million of cumulative qualifying costs. The second group in reg 4(2)(a) must exceed XCD 3 million. A facility combining sectors across both groups must also exceed XCD 3 million. For a mixed priority and other use, reg 4(4) applies the qualifying share of construction costs to the total cost of building materials and construction labour.
4. Reconcile costs to reg 5. Check construction materials and labour and project equipment against reg 5(1)(a)–(c). Exclude preparatory planning, permitting and design, legal and other professional fees, land, financing costs, office equipment and furnishings, vehicles, aircraft and boats. For reg 4(1)(a)–(d) investments, also exclude materials that do not become part of the building or structure. For qualifying medical investments, regs 5(1)(d)–(e) allow 10% of specified engineering, environmental impact and soil investigation costs if qualifying costs are at most XCD 30 million, and 12% above that threshold. Keep the asset register, invoices, labour records, commissioning dates and allocation basis.

For example, assume a new manufacturing facility brought into use in the 2017 basis period qualifies with XCD 2 million of qualifying costs and XCD 1.5 million of assessable business income before the allowance. The business uses XCD 1.5 million of the deduction in that year and tracks the XCD 500,000 excess for subsequent years under s 36A(2). This example assumes the business meets the sector, cost and activity requirements. [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 27 of 2015, s 5][IT-2015] · [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 8 of 2016, s 2][IT-2016] · [Priority Sectors Regulations, regs 3–5][SECTORS]

Flag a transfer within 5 years of bringing the asset into use. Section 36A(3)(a) can add fair market value to chargeable income, capped at deductions previously allowed 'for years of assessment preceding the year of the transfer or any subsequent year'. The printed carry-over cross-reference in s 36A(3)(b) points to subsection (5), whilst the carry-over appears in subsection (2). Confirm the cap's year cut-off and the effect on carry-over before calculating the transfer-year adjustment. [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 27 of 2015, s 5][IT-2015]

Section 36A(4), as amended from 1 May 2016, excepts transfers described in s 5A of the Property Transfer Tax Act from s 36A(3). Check the conditions for the villa or apartment transfer. Act 23 of 2015 preserves the former ss 5A and 5B for an approved tourism development that received Cabinet approval before that Act commenced. Confirm which provision applies before using the exception. [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 8 of 2016, s 2][IT-2016] · [Property Transfer Tax (Amendment) Act 23 of 2015, s 3][TRANSFER]

## Agricultural research, training and customs

Section 36AB provides a deduction of 50% of qualifying agricultural research and development costs. Routine testing, efficiency surveys or management studies, routine data collection, market research and sales promotion are excluded. From 1 May 2016, the defined agricultural activity must contribute to job creation or to generating income and foreign exchange in the national economy and be certified by the Ministry of Agriculture. Confirm which activity and period the certification covers. [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 27 of 2015, s 5][IT-2015] · [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 8 of 2016, s 3][IT-2016] · [Commencement Order, SRO 26 of 2016][IT-START]

Sections 37(1)(o) and 37(4) provide a deduction of 50% of qualifying costs for technical or vocational training of a resident employee. The qualifying cost base is capped at XCD 5,000 per employee per basis period, and the deduction at chargeable income from carrying on business in Grenada. Qualifying costs cover an external instructor, tuition and a subsistence stipend for up to 2 months per individual. This amendment applies to training expenses incurred from enactment on 4 September 2015. [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 27 of 2015, ss 1(6) and 6][IT-2015]

Customs duties relief under SRO 33 commenced on 1 May 2016. Check the goods and sector against the specific provision before placing an order. The Order sets conditions for relief on listed goods and different rules for particular sectors and vehicle categories. Confirm the applicable evidence at Customs, the eligible importer and any use or disposal restrictions. [Customs Duties (Exemptions) Order, regs 1 and 3–7][CUSTOMS] · [GIDC application form][GIDC-FORM]

## Before claiming

Confirm later amendments and orders for the actual claim period. IRD publishes general instructions for filing a return in GTAX. Confirm the live corporate income tax fields and evidence for the allowance with IRD. Verify the carry-over cross-reference, certificate conditions and any sector-specific arrangements for tourism, hydrocarbon activities or temporary relief. Keep written confirmation with the tax computation and source documents. [IRD GTAX video gallery][GTAX]
