# Grenada National Insurance contributions

Identify the contribution period, work location and contributor category. The National Insurance Board administers the fund. The National Insurance Scheme (NIS) publishes registration, returns and payment guidance. Amounts below are in Eastern Caribbean dollars (XCD). [National Insurance Act, Cap 205, ss 4, 9 and 24–32][CAP205] · [Parliament base edition][PARLIAMENT] · [Grenada debt strategy, p ii][CURRENCY]

## Employees and employers

For ordinary insurable employment, apply each share to gross insurable cash earnings for the relevant pay period, up to the applicable ceiling. The base can include overtime, commissions, employer-paid gratuities, service charges and holiday pay. Check regulation 4 for when to include those amounts and how to convert a different pay frequency. Check exemptions under section 27 and the Second Schedule before applying the ordinary rate. [Cap 205, ss 24, 26–30 and Second Schedule, and Collection Regulations, regs 4 and 7][CAP205]

| Rate step | Employer | Employee | Combined | Maximum insurable earnings |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 January 2025 | 7% | 6% | 13% | XCD 1,200 weekly or XCD 5,200 monthly |
| 1 January 2026 | 7.25% | 6.25% | 13.5% | XCD 1,200 weekly or XCD 5,200 monthly |
| 1 January 2027, scheduled | 7.5% | 6.5% | 14% | XCD 1,200 weekly or XCD 5,200 monthly under the published schedule |

Confirm any later changes before using the scheduled step. The ceiling has applied from 1 January 2024 for the ordinary rates in the table. [SRO 43 of 2023, reg 2 and Schedule Parts I–III][RATES] · [NIS rate schedule][ADJUSTMENTS]

Act 7 of 2023 links pensionable age to the date the person attains the age in its Third Schedule. The 2026 row specifies age 63 for an insured person who attains that age in 2026. Check the person's previous qualification and the contribution week when they attain age 16 or their applicable pensionable age. The NIS employer page describes a 1% employer-only employment injury rate outside the ordinary age range. Its main guidance and FAQ give different upper ages. Confirm the applicable category, base and current 1% rules with NIS before using that branch. [Act 7 of 2023, ss 2–6 and Schedules][AGE] · [Collection Regulations, reg 5 and Schedule][CAP205] · [NIS employer guidance][EMPLOYER]

## Registration, payment and records

- A new employer generally applies to register within 7 days of hiring the first employee. For a worker who has never registered, the Registration Regulations require the employer to apply immediately on hiring. NIS tells employers to ensure registration within 4 days of starting. Obtain the employee's NIS registration details and keep records of pay dates, amounts, deductions and the employment start and end dates. [Registration of Employers and Employees Regulations, regs 4–5 and Collection Regulations, regs 8 and 13][CAP205] · [NIS employer guidance][EMPLOYER]
- Deduct the employee's share when paying earnings and pay both shares into the fund. The ordinary remittance rule gives the employer 14 days after the end of the month. NIS directs employers to submit the earnings and contributions through its portal. Keep the submission and payment receipt. Regulation 14 also provides for an annual employee return within 14 days after the end of the year. Confirm the form and how NIS administers that return through the current portal. [Cap 205, ss 26 and 30–31 and Collection Regulations, regs 6, 11 and 14][CAP205] · [NIS employer guidance][EMPLOYER]
- Late employer payments attract a 10% surcharge on the unpaid amount and 1% interest on it for each month or part of a month after the prescribed payment time. The Board can remit part or all of the surcharge or interest. Regulation 11(3) gives a credit for good-faith employer overpayments, subject to accounting to the employee for any corresponding over-deduction. Check other refunds under Contributions Regulations reg 5. [Cap 205, s 32 and Collection and Contributions Regulations][CAP205]

## Self-employment and voluntary contributions

| Contributor | 2026 treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary self-employed person | Register with NIS. Regulations 6–8 apply to the ordinary branch for a person ordinarily resident in Grenada who meets the age conditions. The rate adds the employer and employee percentages, giving 13.5%. Apply it to insurable earnings from self-employment for the contribution year, subject to the sum of the monthly ceilings. For a full year with 12 ceilings of XCD 5,200, the annual base ceiling is XCD 62,400 and the maximum contribution is XCD 8,424. NIS allows payments as earnings arise or in annual lump sums. Confirm the separate threshold and timing when seeking a benefit. |
| Both employed and self-employed | The employer pays the contributions due for the job. Additional self-employment contributions count towards a shared annual contribution maximum under regulations 8–9. Establish total contributions already paid before computing the additional amount. Apply any excess under regulation 8(4) to the subsequent contribution year. |
| Approved voluntary contributor | An ordinarily resident person whose insurable employment ended may seek Board approval if they meet the age and other conditions. Regulation 5 sets the rate at 6.75% of insurable earnings. NIS publishes an XCD 5,200 monthly ceiling. Pay within 14 days after month end. Acceptance of insurable employment with a registered employer ends voluntary status and triggers payment of outstanding voluntary contributions within 15 days. |

The self-employment regulations have applied from 1 July 2023. Check the statutory contribution year, which normally begins on the first Monday of the calendar year, when reconciling to payroll or accounts. The voluntary pensionable-age amendment applies from 1 January 2024. [Self-Employed Persons Regulations, regs 2 and 5–12 and Schedules I–IV][SELF] · [Collection Regulations, reg 2 and Voluntary Contributions Regulations, regs 2–5][CAP205] · [Act 7 of 2023, s 1 and consequential amendments][AGE] · [NIS self-employment][NISSELF] · [NIS voluntary contributions][VOLUNTARY]

## Check the calculation

An ordinarily insured employee has XCD 5,500 of insurable cash earnings for a monthly period in 2026. Both shares use the XCD 5,200 ceiling. Withhold XCD 325, add the employer's XCD 377 and remit XCD 702 to NIS. Check liability separately for additional employment, mixed earnings, uninsurable pay, work abroad or a change of contributor category.

The NIS identifies CARICOM and Canadian reciprocal arrangements. Obtain the applicable agreement and the agency's determination for a cross-border case. Confirm special coverage, retirement or injury benefit eligibility, the portal's annual return procedure and qualified local review before relying on a calculation. Income tax withholding follows separate Inland Revenue Division rules. [NIS employer guidance][EMPLOYER]
