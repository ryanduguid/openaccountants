# Grenada corporate income tax

Use this reference for the ordinary Grenada trading income of a resident company. Confirm the company's residence, sources of income, basis period and any Inland Revenue Division (IRD) direction before calculating its tax. Incorporation in Grenada establishes company residence under the retained Act. A company incorporated outside Grenada is resident if it is managed and controlled in Grenada. The ordinary resident charge begins with assessable income from Grenada sources, subject to the Act's source, deduction and exemption rules. Review cross-border income, banks, insurers, other special regimes and reorganisation transactions separately. [Income Tax Act, Cap 149, ss 2, 7–12 and Parts IV–VI][TAX]

## Rate and chargeable income

The general company rate in the Fifth Schedule is **28% from 1 January 2019**. The 2014 Order set the company rate at 30%, the 2017 Order changed the individual rate entry and the 2018 Order replaced 30% with 28% from its commencement. The paper IR/IT:8 currently listed on the government website still prints 30% at line 150 and refers to the repealed section 125. Confirm the current approved form or GTAX return with IRD. [Cap 149, s 56][TAX] · [SRO 1 of 2014, para 5][ORDER2014] · [SRO 2 of 2017][ORDER2017] · [SRO 25 of 2018, paras 1 and 3][ORDER2018] · [Tax Administration Act 14 of 2016, Schedule 2 para 4(e)][ADMIN] · [Published IR/IT:8, pp 1–2][FORM]

Reconcile the final accounts to assessable income and then chargeable income. Section 36 generally allows qualifying expenditure wholly, exclusively and necessarily incurred in producing income from the relevant source, on claim and subject to evidence. Check specific requirements, related-party limits, capital allowances and exempt-income expenses before deducting them. The amended loss rule confines business or rental losses to business or rental income and distinguishes a current-year offset from a carried assessed loss. Determine the applicable period, ordering and treatment of a tax holiday before carrying a loss forward. [Cap 149, ss 36–42 and 72][TAX] · [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 27 of 2015, ss 1 and 7–9][AMEND2015]

Section 25(1)(ff) exempts business income taxed under Annual Stamp Tax at **0.5%**. The stamp-tax schedule effective from 1 January 2016 puts registered businesses below **XCD 300,000** annual gross receipts in the 0.5% band, with the first XCD 36,000 of receipts exempt. At exactly XCD 300,000 the separate upper band applies. Check whether the business income was actually taxed at 0.5%, including where the receipts exemption leaves no stamp tax payable. Assess the other grounds for a corporate return separately. [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 27 of 2015, ss 1 and 4][AMEND2015] · [Cap 149, s 65(2)][TAX] · [Annual Stamp Tax Amendment Act 22 of 2015, ss 1 and 6][AST2015] · [Annual Stamp Tax Amendment Act 7 of 2017, s 2][AST2017]

## Instalments and annual return

| Step | Ordinary rule under the cited law |
| --- | --- |
| Establish the basis period | The default is the 12 months ending 31 December in the assessment year. A business may use a 12-month substituted period under section 11(2). Changes to a previously adopted period require the Comptroller's written approval. Starting, changing or closing a business can affect its instalment and annual cycles. [Cap 149, ss 11 and 93][TAX] |
| Estimate and pay | For the ordinary calendar-year case, pay one twelfth of tax on estimated chargeable income at each calendar month-end. The default estimate is the prior year's chargeable income. If chargeable income is likely to be lower, the Comptroller may revise the estimate and tax on satisfactory evidence. If it is likely to be higher or has risen, section 93(7) requires instalments on an estimate that includes the increase. With a non-December basis period, read the separate commencement and month-end rule in section 93(4)(a). [Cap 149, s 93(1)–(7)][TAX] |
| File and settle | Within 90 days after the basis period ends, furnish the return and pay the final balance on filing. Attach the final accounts and reconcile their income to assessable income on the return. Section 72 has certification provisions for audited accounts and accounts prepared by another person. Check the relevant approved return, attachments and preparer signature under section 22 of the Tax Administration Act. [Cap 149, ss 65, 72 and 93(3)][TAX] · [Tax Administration Act 14 of 2016, ss 22, 44 and Schedule 2 para 4(b)][ADMIN] |
| Check relief before changing deadlines | Apply for a filing extension by the due date. A filing extension leaves the ordinary payment date in effect unless a payment extension is expressly granted. The IRD's 31 March 2026 announcement extended both corporate filing and payment to 30 April in that notice. Separately, the Beryl Act exempted qualifying companies registered and operating in Carriacou or Petite Martinique from advance monthly payments during the defined 1 June 2024 to 31 January 2025 period. Check its limits and savings before applying that historical measure. [Tax Administration Act 14 of 2016, ss 26 and 50][ADMIN] · [IRD 31 March 2026 notice][EXTENSION] · [Beryl Special Measures Act 6 of 2025, ss 1–2, 5 and 8][BERYL] |

### Worked example

Assume an established resident company has a December year-end and chargeable trading income of XCD 120,000 in both 2025 and 2026. It has no rate exception, tax credit or revised estimate. The 2026 tax estimate is XCD 33,600 (XCD 120,000 × 28%). Each of the 12 monthly instalments is XCD 2,800 (XCD 33,600 ÷ 12). If all 12 were paid and applied to 2026 and its final chargeable income is XCD 120,000, no balance is due. The ordinary return clock still runs. For the 31 December 2026 year-end the 90th day is 31 March 2027, assuming no applicable extension or non-working-day adjustment. [Cap 149, ss 11, 56, 65 and 93][TAX] · [SRO 25 of 2018][ORDER2018] · [Tax Administration Act 14 of 2016, s 26 and Schedule 2 para 4(b)][ADMIN] · [Interpretation and General Provisions Act, Cap 153, s 51][TIME]

## Registration, records and filing channel

- Register if liable or potentially liable for the covered taxes within 30 days after commencing business under section 12(1). From 31 December 2020, a body corporate issued a registration certificate must prominently display the original at its main place of activity and a certified copy at each other place of activity. Confirm the correct tax account and period with IRD. [Tax Administration Act 14 of 2016, s 12][ADMIN] · [Tax Administration Amendment Act 10 of 2020, s 2][ADMIN2020] · [SRO 75 of 2020][START2020]
- Retain the accounts, source documents, adjustments and evidence of instalments and allocations. Section 20(6) requires transaction records for 7 years from the transaction or the applicable assessment limit if that is longer. Check the requirements for the particular record and assessment. [Tax Administration Act 14 of 2016, ss 20 and 31][ADMIN]
- The January 2024 Finance Ministry announcement covers instalment filing and payments in GTAX. The government payments site directs corporate taxpayers to the tax services portal. The broader government directory pairs GTAX with instalments and lists annual corporate income tax separately. Confirm the current annual return, sign-in route, attachments, instalment allocation, amendment procedure and payment acknowledgement with IRD before filing. [GTAX launch][GTAX] · [Government payment portal][PAY] · [Government services directory][SERVICES]

Confirm the threshold and payment evidence for Annual Stamp Tax, the dating and treatment of any loss, the proper treatment of capital allowances and incentives, and any special filing or payment directions for the company's period. Obtain the relevant assessment and interest authority before dealing with a shortfall, repayment or dispute. The retained section 93 text still cross-references assessment and interest provisions that the Tax Administration Act repealed. [Cap 149, s 93(8) and (11)][TAX] · [Tax Administration Act 14 of 2016, ss 29–31, 54–58 and Schedule 2 para 4(c)][ADMIN]
