# Antarctica: US employee pay, sourcing and the foreign earned income exclusion v0.4

**Tier 2 source-cited draft, pending professional review.** This reference covers one question: how United States federal income tax law treats a US citizen employee's pay for work in Antarctica. It describes no Antarctic tax law: none was found in the sources read, and none is relied on.

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

## What applies in Antarctica, in short

- **No Antarctic tax law is relied on here.** The Antarctic Treaty freezes territorial claims (Article IV); this guide relies on no tax rule from the Treaty or from any Antarctic authority. Whether a person working in Antarctica owes tax, and to whom, depends on the law of each state that taxes them (for example by citizenship, residence or the employer's location) and on any law a claimant state applies to its claimed sector. This guide does not decide any state's claim or any non-US tax.
- **For a US citizen, Antarctic pay cannot be excluded as foreign earned income.** Antarctica is not a 'foreign country' for the foreign earned income exclusion, so that exclusion does not reach pay for work there.
- **Antarctic days do not count towards the 330-day test.** Time on an Antarctic station is not time in a foreign country.
- **Antarctic service income is generally US source.** The Code treats any activity conducted in Antarctica as a 'space or ocean activity', and that income of a US person is US source, except to the extent it is attributable to functions, resources or risks in a foreign country.
- **Why:** each rule turns on the US definition of a foreign country (territory under another government's sovereignty), and the US does not treat Antarctica as such. Section 863(d) names Antarctica expressly; the IRS instructions exclude 'the Antarctic region' from 'foreign country' in terms.

## Scope

Prepare a conditional worksheet that keeps Antarctic service-income sourcing separate from the foreign earned income exclusion under US law. The intended person is a US citizen employee performing their own services in Antarctica. Other persons (resident aliens, non-US persons, partnerships, corporations), self-employment, transport, communications and mixed transactions require specialist review. This is one Antarctic cross-border component; it makes no finding about any sovereignty claim or any other state's tax.

## Ask the client first

1. What citizenship, US tax status and tax year apply, and has a US professional confirmed them? Do not infer status from the employer, bank or station.
2. Where and when were the employee's own services performed, including Antarctic land and ice shelves, vessels, aircraft, other countries and US days? Obtain dated activity and travel records.
3. Who is the legal employer and payer, and what amounts relate to each service period? Distinguish a US government employee from an employee of a private contractor.
4. What establishes the tax home, abode, proposed qualifying period and each full foreign-country day? Identify any claimed bona fide residence route, waiver or combat-zone rule separately.
5. Are mixed services, subcontracted functions, transport, communications, foreign functions or resources, deductions or another country's tax involved? Obtain evidence of the transaction and any proposed allocation.
6. What returns, elections, prior claims, employer statements or tax assessments already exist? Route their correction and any filing to the responsible professional.

## The method, step by step

### 1. Fix the person and version [26 USC 7701(a)(30)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s7701/)

A US citizen is a United States person under section 7701(a)(30)(A). Record the verified person and year before applying section 863(d). The definition also covers other specified persons, but this employee worksheet does not determine their status or result. Establish the version and effective dates of each law, regulation and instruction for the proposed year. Missing version evidence is a stop, not an assumption that the captured edition is current.

### 2. Apply the Antarctic foreign-country screen [IRS Instructions for Form 2555 (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i2555)

The instructions state: 'The term "foreign country" doesn't include international waters and the airspace above them, any U.S. territories, or the Antarctic region.' The regulation defines a foreign country as 'any territory under the sovereignty of a government other than that of the United States' ([26 CFR 1.911-2(h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol12/xml/CFR-2025-title26-vol12-sec1-911-2.xml)). Do not turn a station's operator, another state's territorial claim or a long absence from the US into foreign-country qualification.

Section 911(b)(1)(A) limits foreign earned income to amounts 'from sources within a foreign country or countries' attributable to services in the qualifying period. Antarctic pay therefore does not become eligible foreign earned income because the payer is foreign or the employee spends a long time away. Pay from the United States or a US agency to its own employee is excluded from foreign earned income separately, by section 911(b)(1)(B)(ii), wherever the work is done. [26 USC 911(a), (b) and (d)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s911/).

The tax home test also fails for any period in which the employee's regular or principal post of duty is an Antarctic station: the instructions require the tax home to be 'in a foreign country, or countries' throughout the qualifying period, and Antarctica is not one.

### 3. Count the right days [26 USC 911(d)(1)(B)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s911/)

| Item | Amount or condition | Source and version |
|---|---|---|
| Physical presence route | Present in a foreign country or countries 'during at least 330 full days' in any '12 consecutive months', plus a foreign tax home | [26 USC 911(d)(1)(B) and (d)(3)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s911/), through PL 119-142 |
| Full day | 'a continuous period of twenty-four hours beginning with midnight and ending with the following midnight', with specific short-travel and transit rules | [26 CFR 1.911-2(d)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol12/xml/CFR-2025-title26-vol12-sec1-911-2.xml), revised 1 April 2025 |
| Antarctic presence | Not presence in a foreign country, so it supplies no qualifying day. The 330 days may be interrupted by such periods without breaking the count | [IRS Instructions for Form 2555 (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i2555), Physical Presence Test |

The regulation's short-travel rule treats under 24 hours of travel over areas not within any foreign country as not leaving a foreign country. Apply it to the actual itinerary and refer an ambiguous one rather than overriding the rule with a location total. The time test is only one requirement: bona fide residence, tax home, abode, elections and waivers need their own authority. Keep the statute's combat-zone exception to the abode restriction in section 911(d)(3); this worksheet makes no finding that a deployment meets it.

### 4. Determine source separately [26 USC 863(c)-(e)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s863/)

Section 863(d)(2)(A) defines a space or ocean activity and adds: 'Such term includes any activity conducted in Antarctica.' Under section 863(d)(1)(A), except as provided in regulations, that income 'if derived by a United States person, shall be sourced in the United States'. Section 863(d)(2)(B) excludes activities giving rise to transportation income, international communications income and certain natural-deposit activity.

The regulation applies 'notwithstanding any other provision, including sections 861, 862, 863, and 865'. Paragraph (d)(1)(ii)(H) lists 'Any activity performed in Antarctica' as ocean activity, and paragraph (b)(4) sources a qualifying service transaction under paragraph (b)(1) or (2). For a US person, paragraph (b)(1) starts with US source but makes the income foreign source 'to the extent the income, based on all the facts and circumstances, is attributable to functions performed, resources employed, or risks assumed in a foreign country or countries'. Paragraph (d)(2)(ii) treats a service as a space or ocean activity in its entirety when any part is performed there, unless the taxpayer demonstrates the value attributable to each location. Paragraph (a) excludes activity performed by another person. [26 CFR 1.863-8(a), (b) and (d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol11/xml/CFR-2025-title26-vol11-sec1-863-8.xml).

The source result in the simple worked case assumes the employee's own non-transport services occur wholly in Antarctica, with no mixed or separate transaction, no foreign-country functions, resources or risks and no exception. Refer any missing or contrary fact. A source classification is not a tax rate, a withholding instruction or a conclusion about another country's jurisdiction.

### 5. Preserve allocation and documentation requirements [26 CFR 1.863-8(c), (g) and (h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol11/xml/CFR-2025-title26-vol11-sec1-863-8.xml)

Gross income sourcing is not the whole taxable income calculation. Paragraph (c) sends expense allocation and apportionment to sections 1.861-8 to 1.861-14T, which are not qualified here. Where a taxpayer allocates gross income under paragraph (b), paragraph (g) requires documentation 'in existence when its return is filed' of the allocation, the methodologies and the reasons for them, made available 'within 30 days upon request', plus software access rules. That 30-day rule is not a filing deadline. Paragraph (h) applies the section to taxable years beginning on or after 27 December 2006, with later dates for specific provisions; match them to the year before use.

## Worked cases

### Antarctic pay only

Assume a US citizen employee of a private contractor receives USD 60,000 solely for their own non-transport services performed wholly in Antarctica during 2026. There are no mixed transactions, foreign-country functions, resources or risks, or exceptions. Section 863(d) and section 1.863-8 treat the USD 60,000 as US source. The section 911 screen supplies no foreign earned income from that pay, so the exclusion removes none of it. This does not compute taxable income, deductions, withholding, final tax or relief under any other provision.

```oa-check
name: Antarctic employment geographic screen, 2026
inputs:
  antarctic_pay: 60000
steps:
  antarctic_feie: antarctic_pay * 0
  pay_not_removed_by_feie: antarctic_pay - antarctic_feie
expected:
  antarctic_feie: 0
  pay_not_removed_by_feie: 60000
```

### Antarctic presence cannot repair a day shortfall

In a verified 12-month period, a US citizen has 329 full days in New Zealand, 1 full day on an Antarctic station and 35 other non-qualifying days. No short-travel rule, waiver or bona fide residence alternative applies on these facts. Counting the Antarctic day would give 330, but only 329 days are in a foreign country, so the physical presence test is 1 day short. Had that day been a further verified full day in New Zealand, the time requirement alone would reach 330. Neither variant establishes the tax home, eligible income, an election or an exclusion amount, and this comparison is not advice to change travel.

```oa-check
name: Antarctic day cannot repair the foreign-country shortfall
inputs:
  nz_full_days: 329
  antarctic_station_days: 1
  other_days: 35
  required_foreign_days: 330
steps:
  period_days: nz_full_days + antarctic_station_days + other_days
  foreign_days: nz_full_days
  shortfall: max(0, required_foreign_days - foreign_days)
  alternative_nz_days: nz_full_days + antarctic_station_days
  alternative_shortfall: max(0, required_foreign_days - alternative_nz_days)
expected:
  period_days: 365
  foreign_days: 329
  shortfall: 1
  alternative_nz_days: 330
  alternative_shortfall: 0
```
