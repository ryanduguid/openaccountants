# Sierra Leone Corporate Income Tax

> **The headline rate was right for the wrong year, and wrong for non-residents.**
> The previous version stated **30%** for both resident and non-resident companies
> and was labelled tax year **2025**. For 2025 the resident rate was **25%** — the
> Finance Act 2026 s.13(a) repeals *"the 25% relating to the rate of tax applicable
> to resident companies"* and replaces it with 30%. And **non-resident companies
> are still at 25%**: the Finance Act 2026 amends only Part II of the Second
> Schedule (withholding), leaving Part I untouched.
>
> This is the trap the corpus keeps meeting: **a rate cited to "the Income Tax Act
> 2000" gives no sign of which year's rate it is.** Sierra Leone amends its
> schedules by Finance Act almost every year, and the NRA publishes each one
> separately rather than as a consolidation.

## 1. The company rates, by year

| Taxpayer | To 2025 | From FA 2026 | Source |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **Resident** company | **25%** | **30%** | First Schedule Part III, as amended by FA 2026 s.13(a) |
| **Non-resident** company (income not within Second Schedule Part II) | **25%** | **25%** | Second Schedule Part I — **not amended** by FA 2026 |

- **The 25% is attested by the amending Acts themselves** — both the Finance Act
  2023 and the Finance Act 2024 insert paragraphs *"immediately after the line
  relating to the **25% rate of tax for companies**"* in Part III. The rate they
  were building on was 25%  _(Finance Act 2023, s.6 and Finance Act 2024, s.11(a) — https://www.nra.gov.sl/tax-laws)_
- **The Finance Act 2026 raises it to 30%** — *"Part III by repealing the 25%
  relating to the rate of tax applicable to resident companies in Sierra Leone for a
  year of assessment and replacing it with 30%"*  _(Finance Act 2026, s.13(a) — http://webtestcms.nra.gov.sl/uploads/The_Finance_Act_2026_121df05d9b.pdf)_
- **It applies from the 2026 financial year** — the Act is *"deemed to have come
  into operation"* for the financial year beginning in 2026  _(Finance Act 2026, s.1 — http://webtestcms.nra.gov.sl/uploads/The_Finance_Act_2026_121df05d9b.pdf)_
- **Non-residents stay at 25%** — Second Schedule Part I (s.7): *"Rates of tax on
  chargeable income of non-resident other than income to which Part II applies:
  25%"*. Finance Act 2026 s.14 amends **Part II** only. The previous version put
  non-residents at 30%  _(Income Tax Act 2000 as amended to Finance Act 2017, Second Schedule Part I — http://webtestcms.nra.gov.sl/uploads/Income_Tax_Act_2000_as_amended_to_Finance_Act_2017_1_83760e046c.pdf)_

## 2. The minimum alternate tax is 2%, and it carries a credit

The previous version described "a minimum tax of around **3%** of turnover … for
companies in a loss or low-profit position (approx — confirm)". Each part of that
is off.

- **The rate is 2%** — Finance Act 2024 inserts into Part III: *"Rate of minimum
  alternate tax applicable to companies reporting losses the previous year including
  companies under section 21, resident in Sierra Leone for any year of assessment.
  Rate of minimum alternate tax — **2% (two percent)**"*  _(Finance Act 2024, s.11(a) — https://www.nra.gov.sl/tax-laws)_
- **Non-resident companies have their own 2%** — Finance Act 2024 inserts into
  Second Schedule Part I: *"The rate of minimum alternate tax applicable to
  non-resident companies is **2% of turnover**"*  _(Finance Act 2024, s.12(a) — https://www.nra.gov.sl/tax-laws)_
- **It is paid as "the higher of", not as a top-up** — s.7A(1), as replaced by the
  Finance Act 2026: *"the income tax payable by a company in respect of any year of
  assessment shall be **the higher of** — (a) the income tax payable on the
  chargeable income as computed under this Act; and (b) the minimum alternate tax,
  which is the application of the percentage specified under Part III of the First
  Schedule that is applied to the company's **turnover** for the year"*  _(Finance Act 2026, s.5 — http://webtestcms.nra.gov.sl/uploads/The_Finance_Act_2026_121df05d9b.pdf)_
- **Paying it buys a credit** — a company that pays minimum alternate tax for a year
  is entitled to a **minimum alternate tax credit** equal to the amount paid, usable
  against any future excess of normal income tax over the minimum alternate tax, for
  a period **not exceeding ten (10) years**. The previous version treated the minimum
  tax as a pure cost  _(Finance Act 2024, s.8 (new s.7A(2)–(3)) and Finance Act 2023, s.6 — https://www.nra.gov.sl/tax-laws)_
- **"Turnover" is a defined term with exclusions** — s.7A(5): turnover takes its
  meaning from s.2 *"but excludes any amount—"*. The exclusions were not read here  _(Finance Act 2024, s.8 (new s.7A(5)) — https://www.nra.gov.sl/tax-laws)_

### The 2026 change broadened it from loss-makers to every company

- **Under the Finance Act 2024 it caught loss-makers only** — s.7A(1) as replaced by
  that Act: *"where a company (whether resident or non-resident) **makes an allowable
  loss under section 32A** for a year of assessment beginning on or after 1st day of
  January 2023, the company shall pay minimum alternate tax"*  _(Finance Act 2024, s.8 — https://www.nra.gov.sl/tax-laws)_
- **The Finance Act 2026 removed the loss condition** — the replacement s.7A(1) makes
  the tax **the higher of** the two computations for a company, full stop. **A
  consistently profitable company with thin margins is now inside the charge** where
  it was not before  _(Finance Act 2026, s.5 — http://webtestcms.nra.gov.sl/uploads/The_Finance_Act_2026_121df05d9b.pdf)_

> **The schedule was left behind by that change.** The Part III rate line still reads
> *"Rate of minimum alternate tax applicable to companies **reporting losses the
> previous year**…"* — the wording the Finance Act 2024 inserted, and which the
> Finance Act 2026 did not touch when it rewrote the operative section. **The
> charging section and the rate line now describe different populations.** The
> operative provision is s.7A(1) and this guide follows it, but a reviewer should be
> aware that the schedule reads narrower.

## 3. Withholding — both schedules were replaced wholesale in 2024

The Finance Act 2024 **repealed and replaced** Part IV of the First Schedule
(payments to residents) and Part II of the Second Schedule (payments to
non-residents). The Finance Act 2026 then amended four non-resident lines. The
previous version's figures predate both.

| Payment | Resident (FA 2024) | Non-resident (FA 2024) | Non-resident (FA 2026) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Employment income (s.116) | — | 25% | 25% |
| **Payments to contractors (s.117)** | **5%** | 10% | **20%** |
| Dividends (s.118) | 15% | 15% | **20%** |
| Interest (s.119) | 15% | 15% | **20%** |
| **Management/professional fees** | **15%** | 15% | **20%** |
| Rents | 10% | *(rents and royalties)* 15% | 15% |
| Royalties (s.120) | 15% | *(with rents)* 15% | 15% |
| Annuities (s.121) | 15% | 15% | 15% |
| Natural resource payments (s.122) | 15% | 15% | 15% |
| Real property (s.123) / non-resident beneficiaries | 15% | 15% | 15% |
| Lottery winnings of Le 1,000 and above | 15% | — | — |

_(Finance Act 2024, ss.11(b) and 12(b), and Finance Act 2026, s.14 — https://www.nra.gov.sl/tax-laws)_

- **The contractor rates were the furthest out.** The previous version gave **6.5%
  resident** and **11.5% non-resident**. The schedules say **5%** for residents and
  **10%** rising to **20%** for non-residents. Neither of the guide's figures appears
  anywhere in the Act or in any Finance Act read here  _(Finance Act 2024, s.11(b); Finance Act 2026, s.14(a) — https://www.nra.gov.sl/tax-laws)_
- **Management and professional fees only became a named line in 2024** — the
  pre-2024 Part IV had no such row. The previous version's 15% is right for residents
  and is now **20%** for non-residents  _(Finance Act 2024, s.11(b); Finance Act 2026, s.14(d) — https://www.nra.gov.sl/tax-laws)_
- **Royalties to residents fell from 25% to 15%** in the 2024 replacement — the
  pre-2024 Part IV had royalties at 25% and natural resource payments at 25%  _(Income Tax Act 2000 as amended to Finance Act 2017, First Schedule Part IV, and Finance Act 2024, s.11(b) — http://webtestcms.nra.gov.sl/uploads/Income_Tax_Act_2000_as_amended_to_Finance_Act_2017_1_83760e046c.pdf)_

## 4. Filing

- **120 days after the end of the year** — s.97(1): a taxpayer, or the nominated
  officer of a partnership or trust, must file a return of income for each year of
  assessment and arrange to pay any balance *"not later than one hundred and twenty
  days after the end of the year"*. The previous version had this right and marked it
  "(approx — confirm)"  _(Income Tax Act 2000 as amended to Finance Act 2017, s.97(1) — http://webtestcms.nra.gov.sl/uploads/Income_Tax_Act_2000_as_amended_to_Finance_Act_2017_1_83760e046c.pdf)_
- **Above a turnover threshold the return must carry certified accounts** — s.97(3)(a)
  requires a person carrying on business in Sierra Leone with annual turnover at or
  above the threshold to file *"a set of accounts certified by a firm of chartered
  accountants recognized by the **Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sierra
  Leone** and accepted by the Commissioner-General"*. Absent from the previous
  version  _(Income Tax Act 2000 as amended to Finance Act 2017, s.97(3)(a) — http://webtestcms.nra.gov.sl/uploads/Income_Tax_Act_2000_as_amended_to_Finance_Act_2017_1_83760e046c.pdf)_

> **[UNVERIFIED — the Finance Act 2026 amends s.97.]** Section 8 of that Act is
> headed *"Section 97 of the Income Tax Act, 2000 is amended as follows-"* and its
> sub-paragraphs could not be read: the published PDF interleaves the Act's body
> with schedule fee tables, and the amendment's content does not appear on the page
> that carries its heading or on the pages following. **The 120-day deadline above
> is stated from the Act and may have been changed.** A reviewer with the printed
> Gazette should check s.8 before relying on it.

## 5. Transfer pricing penalties are new

The Finance Act 2026 inserts a new s.150(4): a person conducting related-party
transactions who fails to comply with s.150(3) is liable to a penalty of **3% of the
value of the transactions** for each of — failing to disclose related-party
transactions within the return period; failing to submit the **local file**; failing
to submit the **master file**; and failing to submit the **country-by-country report**
on notification. For **multiple violations** the penalty *"shall not exceed three
percent (3%) of the value of the related party transaction"*.

_(Finance Act 2026, s.12 — http://webtestcms.nra.gov.sl/uploads/The_Finance_Act_2026_121df05d9b.pdf)_

## 6. A currency trap in the source documents

**Sierra Leone redenominated the leone in 2022, dropping three zeros**, and the
published instruments do not use one convention. The Income Tax Act consolidation
and the **Finance Act 2024** are written in old leones — FA 2024 uses `Le` 111 times
against a single `NLe` — while the **Finance Acts 2025 and 2026** are written almost
entirely in `NLe`. **The same nominal figure means a thousandfold different amount
depending on which instrument it comes from.**

For that reason this guide states **no leone threshold as a number**: the
audited-accounts threshold in §4 and the small-taxpayer band are given by
description only. The previous version's *"NLe 500"* contractor threshold is not
repeated — it was marked "(approx — confirm)" and no provision matching it was
located. **A converted figure would look exactly as authoritative as a read one.**

## 7. What this replaces

| The previous version said | The law says |
| --- | --- |
| Standard corporate rate **30%**, tax year 2025 | **25% for 2025.** 30% only from the **Finance Act 2026** (s.13(a)), for the financial year beginning 2026 |
| Non-resident company rate **30%** | **25%** — Second Schedule Part I, **not amended** by FA 2026 |
| Minimum/turnover tax "around **3%** … loss or low-profit (approx)" | **2%** for resident and non-resident companies. Under FA 2024 it caught only companies making an **allowable loss**; FA 2026 removed that condition and made it **the higher of** ordinary tax and the turnover computation for any company |
| *nothing* | Paying it buys a **minimum alternate tax credit**, usable against future excess normal tax for up to **ten years** |
| WHT contractors **6.5%** resident / **11.5%** non-resident | **5%** resident; non-resident **10%** under FA 2024, **20%** under FA 2026. Neither of the old figures appears in any instrument read |
| WHT dividends 15% "resident and non-resident" | Resident **15%**; non-resident **15%** under FA 2024, **20%** under FA 2026 |
| WHT interest 15% both | Same split — non-resident rises to **20%** |
| WHT royalties 15% both | Resident **15%** (down from 25% in the 2024 replacement); non-resident rents **and** royalties **15%** |
| WHT management fees 15% both | Resident **15%**; non-resident **20%** from FA 2026 |
| WHT rent 10% resident / 15% non-resident | **Confirmed** |
| Contractor WHT threshold "NLe 500 (approx)" | **Not located.** Not repeated — see the currency trap in §6 |
| Return within 120 days (approx) | **Confirmed** (s.97(1)) — but FA 2026 s.8 amends s.97 and its content could not be read |
| *nothing* | Returns above a turnover threshold need **accounts certified by an ICASL-recognised firm** (s.97(3)(a)) |
| *nothing* | **Transfer-pricing penalties of 3%** of related-party transaction value (FA 2026, new s.150(4)) |
| Rates cited to "Income Tax Act 2000", one via a **mail subdomain** (`mail.nra.gov.sl`) and six to a law firm | Every rule above is cited to the **Act or the numbered Finance Act** that made it, on the NRA's own *Tax Laws & Regulations* page |

The rates, schedules, filing rules and penalties were read from the Income Tax Act
2000 as amended to Finance Act 2017 — the NRA's own consolidation — together with
the Finance Acts 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 as separate instruments, all published at
`nra.gov.sl/tax-laws`. **The NRA publishes no consolidation later than 2017**, so
every rate after that date has to be assembled from the individual Finance Acts;
that is why this guide names the amending Act beside each figure rather than citing
the principal Act alone.
