# Jersey cryptocurrency tax

## Classification

Revenue Jersey's [cryptocurrency treatment guidance](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/IncomeTax/Technical/Guidelines/Pages/CryptocurrenciesTreatment.aspx) distinguishes trading from occasional activity. Small or irregular mining is generally not trading by itself; commercial mining combined with trading can require a different result. Exchange activity gives rise to income tax where the features of trading are met. An occasional non-trading exchange gain generally has no taxable profit and no deductible associated loss or expenses. Establish the facts before applying these descriptions.

Business receipts and payments in cryptocurrency must be reflected in the accounts under ordinary tax rules. Convert transactions to the accounts currency and express taxable profit or loss in sterling for Jersey assessment. Retain transaction timestamps, asset quantities, valuation sources, fees and the evidence used to establish trading status.

## GST

The [cryptocurrency guidance](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/IncomeTax/Technical/Guidelines/Pages/CryptocurrenciesTreatment.aspx) requires a sterling value for goods or services bought with cryptocurrency at the transaction date. Exchanging cryptocurrency for sterling, other currency or another cryptocurrency does not itself attract GST under that guidance. This does not exempt the underlying goods or services purchased with it.

Example: a GST-registered business makes an established standard-rated sale for a cryptocurrency amount worth GBP 100 excluding GST at the transaction date. The sale carries GBP 5 GST and GBP 105 total consideration. If the agreed sterling equivalent is instead GBP 105 including GST, the GST component is GBP 105 x 5 / 105 = GBP 5. Valuation and supply classification are assumed established. The [GST business guidance](https://www.gov.je/Industry/BusinessAdviceHub/BusinessTaxesSocialSecurity/Pages/GoodsServicesTaxBusinesses.aspx) supplies the 5% rate; retain evidence of the actual conversion used.

## Reporting and limits

The [CARF and CRS guidance page](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/InternationalTaxAgreements/IGAs/pages/cryptoassetreporting.aspx) identifies separate due diligence and reporting obligations for relevant service providers. It contains a reportable-jurisdiction list for the 2026 period and refers to the first reporting deadline of 30 June 2027. The page also retains conditional implementation wording. Verify the enacted regulations, current jurisdiction list and detailed guidance before giving an operational reporting instruction. A user's income tax exemption does not settle a provider's reporting duty.

Complete badges-of-trade analysis, staking, decentralised finance, token classification, mining GST exceptions, CARF/CRS implementation and licensing rules remain research gaps. No rate or exemption for those activities is inferred here.

## Checks and provenance

Establish trading status, value business transactions in the correct currency, and distinguish exchanging a token from selling goods for a token. Keep tax classification and international reporting separate. Primary guidance was read on 11 October 2026; its evolving-treatment caveat remains applicable. See `references.md`. This is a source-cited draft awaiting professional review.
