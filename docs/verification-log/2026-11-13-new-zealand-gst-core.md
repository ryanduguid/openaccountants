# New Zealand GST registration, timing and return boxes

Checked on 13 November 2026. This entry covers the selected ordinary resident small-business core in new-zealand-gst v2.1 and nz-gst-return v2.1. It does not establish complete consumption-tax coverage or practitioner approval. Both guides remain tier 2 with pending review. No catalogue, coverage figure or topic status changes are included.

## Corrections

The prior guides disagreed with each other and the current issuer-published GST101A. One instructed invoice-basis returns to use exclusive sales/purchases and a different tax fraction. Its purchase base and output/input totals were assigned the wrong boxes. The other put the net balance in Box 12 and included exempt receipts in Box 5. These are direct comparisons of the prior published prose with the official form, not an old executable tax engine. The new required-example check was also run against isolated copies of both base guides: it failed both because each had 0 blocks.

The corrected ordinary path uses GST-inclusive values on all bases: taxable sales in Box 5, zero-rated subset in Box 6, standard-rated sales base in Box 7, output GST in Box 8, debit adjustments in Box 9 and their total in Box 10. Eligible inclusive purchases are in Box 11, ordinary input GST in Box 12, credit adjustments in Box 13 and their total in Box 14. Box 15 uses the absolute difference and the payment/refund indicator. Exempt and non-supply receipts are reconciled outside Box 5. The workpaper keeps a signed balance and separates special credits from the ordinary tax-fraction base.

The basis controls timing. Ordinary invoice timing uses the earlier invoice or any payment, with the full fixed sale value recognised on an earlier part payment. Hybrid means invoice sales and paid purchases. Prior-period receipts must not duplicate invoice-basis sales already returned. Special time-of-supply rules and section 19D's individual-supply override remain separate analyses. Unknown registration, basis, period or material supply evidence prevents a filing-ready result; supplier names and bank narrations are prompts for evidence only.

Post-1 April 2023 taxable supply information replaces the old universal $50 invoice rule. The update cites the current records regime but does not certify every record-value band or special credit. GST101A is distinct from a combined GST103-series return. Ordinary filing includes nil returns, the March/November deadline exceptions and next-working-day adjustment. Actual approved periods, due dates, special variations and filing details still require confirmation.

## Threshold wording

The current Act is the version as at 30 June 2026. Section 51 uses strictly exceeding $60,000 for its ordinary past/expected turnover tests and excludes GST from values; IRD's public summary says at least $60,000. The guides preserve this discrepancy and require determination at the exact boundary. Charging GST while unregistered and registration exceptions also need separate resolution; low turnover does not itself authorise collection or stopping returns.

Section 19A includes exactly $2 million and requires the relevant eligibility/application conditions. Section 15 includes exactly $500,000 in its ordinary 6-monthly tests, whilst IRD's summary says under that amount. The guides require confirmation of approval at that boundary. Mandatory monthly filing applies above $24 million or where that amount is likely to be exceeded. The independent statutory check found the 2 unincorporated Acts affect selected section 5 supplies, not the reviewed registration, timing, basis or period provisions. The precise staged commencement of those unrelated amendments was not established.
