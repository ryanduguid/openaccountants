# New Zealand annual IR3 reconciliation and standard provisional handoff

Checked on 13 November 2026. This entry covers selected full-year resident individual calculations for the 2025-26 IR3 and a bounded 2026-27 standard provisional-tax handoff. It does not establish complete New Zealand coverage or practitioner approval. Both guides remain Tier 2 with pending review. No catalogue, coverage count or topic status changes are included.

## Annual return corrections

The previous IR3 flow calculated tax from self-employment net income whilst allowing PAYE and investment credits, without a complete annual aggregation. For synthetic salary of $60,000 and business net income of $20,000, following that displayed business-only path gives tax of $2,408 before subtracting $10,220.50 PAYE income tax. The corrected annual taxable income is $80,000, tax is $16,277.50, IETC is nil and RIT is $6,057. This is a direct trace of the old prose, not a previously executable regression test; the old guide had no worked-check blocks.

The revised guide separates salary, schedular income, sole-trader income, NZ interest and supported ordinary NZ dividends. It prevents duplicate schedular income/expenses, requires actual withholding and substantiated deductions and separates business profit from allocated provisional payments. It removes guessed withholding rates, expense percentages and bank-narration classifications. Other income, residency and assessment regimes remain explicit referrals.

The salary reconciliation uses IR3G’s $152,790 annual ACC-liable cap, 1.67% rate and $2,551.59 maximum, or the reconciled SOI income-tax field. Employer PAYE before payroll donation credits must first be reduced by extinguished donation credits, then by the annual ACC levy. The resulting income-tax credit is distinct from total payroll deductions. Full broken-term-deposit negative interest is handled at the supported timing; other negative-interest deductions wait until maturity.

The return applies supported brought-forward losses before taxable income, but calculates IETC from Box 30 net income before those losses. IETC requires the LC 13 whole-month facts, including the exact WFF entitlement-and-receipt conditions. Tax after IETC is floored at zero; imputation offsets only the remaining tax and excess carries forward. Refundable withholding can then create negative RIT. Provisional payments reduce Question 36B, not RIT. Question 37’s early payment discount and separate assessments/offsets require their own review before a final payable amount is certified.

Seventeen conditional executable cases cover mixed salary/business income, schedular and investment credits, imputation carry-forward, losses, IETC boundaries/months, the ACC cap, payroll giving and broken-term interest. Independent Decimal calculations matched all 17. The reviewer’s payroll-giving, interest-timing, early-discount handoff and Q24 citation findings were repaired and independently read back. The examples do not establish the eligibility facts.

## Dates and source discrepancies

The current issuer-linked IR3G is August 2026 and the IR3 form is July 2026. Selected native PDF pages had no encoding/OCR flags; the income/credit worksheets were also visually inspected. A whole-file IR3G encoding warning was retained, so this is not a claim that every PDF page was clean or reviewed.

IR3G p7 prints 31 March 2026 for the agent filing extension, which precedes the ordinary July 2026 filing date. The current IRD extension page and TAA 37 support the applicable extension up to 31 March 2027. RA 13 requires the qualifying notified agent/extension circumstances for the April terminal date; an engagement alone does not suffice. The nominal 7 February 2027 payment date falls on Sunday and 8 February is the observed Waitangi holiday. Applying IRD’s next-working-day rule gives 9 February 2027. This is an explicit calendar inference, subject to the account’s actual circumstances.

IR3G p43 prints $78,101 in the subtraction-threshold column. The operative individual table and IRD rate page put the 33% band above $78,100. Using the printed subtraction figure would reduce that band’s tax by $0.33. The guide uses the supported $78,100 threshold. The 2024-25 composite schedule is outside this full-year workflow.

## Standard provisional corrections and remaining calculation stop

The old provisional guide gave an unconditional 105% uplift and treated an extension alone as a 2-instalment trigger. RC 5 instead selects 110% only when all stated required-return, not-yet-due, unfiled and non-final-F conditions hold. A late return without an extension does not satisfy that test. Final F uses 105% of preceding-year RIT, with a supported preceding-year expectation where applicable; this differs from the separate optional expected-current-year RIT calculation.

The bounded workflow requires established full-year resident status, a 31 March balance date, fixed GST cycle, a required 2026 return and both 2025 and 2026 RIT above $5,000. A fresh review caught the separate RC 6(4)(a) case where no preceding-year return is required: defaulting to 105% there would give $15,750 rather than the stated $15,000 preceding RIT. The workflow now expressly stops that case and makes its default conditional on the required-return scope. The ordinary schedules are B/D/F for 3 instalments or C/F for 6-monthly GST. Low older-year RIT, initial liability, reassessments, changed cycles, other methods, interest and penalties are outside the calculation. Earlier liabilities retain their proper original bases; actual payments and arrears are reconciled separately. RC 10 subtracts earlier liabilities, not cash.

Ten executable blocks cover supported 105%/110% filing transitions, 2-instalment schedules, unpaid earlier liability, final-F expectations and whole-dollar truncation. One block is expressly a diagnostic for an unresolved rule, not a verified payment schedule.

RC 9(2)’s equal-instalment/final-remainder direction and RC 10’s cumulative formula with whole-dollar truncation give different timing for an unchanged annual amount of $15,752 over 3 instalments: $5,250/$5,250/$5,252 versus $5,250/$5,251/$5,251. An independent consultation did not settle that conflict. IRD’s 2020 legislative explanation confirms 2 truncation stages and uses the cumulative calculation, but its $130,795 remainder-1 example does not distinguish the readings. The guide therefore stops before preparing any 3-instalment schedule using an uplifted whole-dollar amount with remainder 2. This conservative stop also applies after a change of basis. Neither disputed schedule is represented as verified. Confirmation resolving the discrepancy is still needed. Negative instalments and payment-allocation disputes also stop for supported review.
