# Palau: healthcare and pension source boundaries

The retained Palau sources distinguish healthcare contributions, insurance
subscriptions, social security benefits and civil service pensions. Several
documents supply historical provisions without establishing current amounts.
These checks were performed on 12 October 2026 in Australia/Sydney, using
primary captures from 11 October UTC. They supplement the earlier
[codification-boundary record](2026-10-12-palau-solomon-tci-source-readiness.md).

## Healthcare contributions and subscriptions are separate provisions

The [original RPPL 8-14](http://rophcf.pw/download/documents/hcf_regulations/rppl_8_14_establish_msa_phi.pdf)
contains a legislative cover and 17 Act pages. All 18 physical pages were
processed with offline Small OCR and read. Its closing image confirms passage
on 16 April 2010 and signed approval on 7 May 2010. OCR omitted the handwritten
month. Section 5 links effectiveness to presidential approval or becoming law
without approval, subject to other law. The full commencement and amendment
chain remains unverified.

In both the original Act and the retained
[Title 41](https://www.palaulaw.gov.pw/view/rec1fbmec2XPeZ8fZ?source=documents),
section 918 concerns participation in the Medical Savings Fund, section 924
sets individual and employer contributions, and section 952 concerns insurance
subscriptions. Section 952 also allows rate changes by regulation after two
years of operations, subject to its conditions. Different contribution and
subscription percentages are not, by themselves, inconsistent rates. Their
coverage, remuneration definitions and later instruments must be checked
separately before implementation.

The [40-page Healthcare Fund (HCF) regulations](http://rophcf.pw/download/documents/hcf_regulations/hcf_regulations.pdf)
were extracted in native text, with selected provisions read. Section 504
addresses subscriptions; section 109 describes effectiveness through majority
committee approval consistent with the Administrative Procedures Act. The final
page prints committee adoption and approval on 30 August 2017. Its image also
shows presidential concurrence dated `10/27/2017`, or 27 October 2017, omitted
from native extraction. The two dates serve different labelled actions. This
review does not select an operative date or establish compliance with the
approval process, later amendments or current rates.

## The quarterly subscription amount remains unresolved

The [published quarterly National Health Insurance (NHI) form](http://rophcf.pw/download/documents/forms/quarterly_nhi_subscription_payment_form.pdf)
prints USD 49.73 in each of four cost-per-quarter rows. Its image confirms the
footer `ROPSSA 900 – 25 (rev. 03/25)` and the quarterly payment dates printed
on the form. The captured
[HCF Premium Contributions page](http://rophcf.pw/pgs/premium_contributions.html)
instead states USD 58.50 per quarter for voluntary participation. Neither source
explains the difference in the reviewed material. The form's revision label
does not establish that it overrides the website or the applicable law.

The HCF files were obtained through the HTTP route linked by the
[Social Security Administration](https://ropssa.pw/opf_module/opf_index.html)
after the HTTPS host presented a certificate-name mismatch. HTTP retrieval
lacks authenticated transport; publication identity
alone does not settle integrity, current applicability or the conflicting
amounts. A dated authoritative correction or the applicable rate instrument
remains required. No amount was selected for a taxpayer calculation.

## Social security amendments need their own period boundaries

The [government's RPPL 10-21 PDF](https://www.palaugov.pw/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/RPPL-No.-10-21-re.-To-provide-a-fifty-dollar-increase-in-social-security-payment-and-other-related-purpose.pdf)
has nine physical pages: signing statements on pages 1 to 4, a legislative
cover on page 5 and the four-page Act on pages 6 to 9. Complete OCR was read.
The closing image confirms passage on 26 March 2018 and approval on
29 March 2018, distinct from the January session heading.

Section 6 amends Title 41 section 741's self-employed contribution bases and
contains a separate retirement-age exception in subsection (c). This provision
must not be substituted into healthcare section 924 merely because both
concern self-employment. Section 2 separately describes a monthly benefit
supplement for the period from 1 October 2018 to 30 September 2019, with funding
conditions and further appropriation required for continuation. The Act alone
does not establish a current benefit or complete contribution-law chain.

## Civil service pensions depend on another instrument

Selected [Title 33](https://www.palaulaw.gov.pw/view/recFxXwAn0VYv1I6o?source=documents)
section 2030 provisions delegate the employee gross-pay percentage to the
Trust Fund Operation Plan and address government matching. Its initial ceiling
does not supply a current percentage. The applicable Plan, amendments and
coverage conditions remain unverified. Social security and healthcare source
rates cannot fill that gap.

## Temporary revenue allocations are not payroll rates

All six native pages of
[RPPL 11-36](https://www.palaulaw.gov.pw/view/recBhddY9MTikJMh2?source=documents)
were read. Its closing image confirms passage on 16 September 2024 and signed
approval on 30 September 2024; both native text and forced Small OCR omitted
the handwritten approval day and month. Section 3 says it adds subsection (h),
but its quoted text contains both (h) and (i). Those provisions allocate
alcohol and tobacco tax revenue for stated two-year periods. Preserve the
printed numbering and duration rather than converting the allocations into
employee or employer contribution rates.

Selected [RPPL 12-3](https://www.palaulaw.gov.pw/view/recNKaF61lBxqs6bp?source=budget-acts)
section 35 text and images, on physical pages 18 to 19, also describe healthcare
subscription funding from government revenue. Full later appropriation,
replacement and repeal chains remain open. The Code Commission's stated
exclusion of temporary provisions makes a code-only review insufficient.
