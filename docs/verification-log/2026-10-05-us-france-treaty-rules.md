# US treaties: France rules

The France section of `us-major-partners` cited ordinary-interest relief to
art 11(2), described interest and royalties as unconditional exemptions and
assigned technical services a blanket art 7 zero rate. It omitted conditional
parent-company relief and RIC/REIT restrictions. Its PE row said 12 months and
its summary implied reciprocal 5%/0%/0% treatment. This correction starts from
`aa8baee`, advances the guide to version 1.4 and covers US-source income of
French residents for tax year 2025. The guide remains tier 2 and pending review.

I checked the [1994 Convention](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/france.pdf),
[original explanation](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/francetech.pdf),
[2004 Protocol](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/131/Treaty-France-Protocol-8-12-2004.pdf),
[2009 Protocol](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/131/Treaty-France-Pr2-1-13-2009.pdf)
and [2009 explanation](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/131/Treaty-France-Pr2-TE-1-13-2009.pdf)
linked by the [IRS index](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/france-tax-treaty-documents)
on 5 October 2026. Protocol II replaces art 10, III replaces key art 12
paragraphs and renumbers retained provisions, I amends residence/derivation,
XIII amends the saving clause and XIV replaces art 30. Art 14 independent
services and its fixed-base rule remain. The arbitration memorandum was not
treated as an amendment to income classifications or rates.

| Earlier statement | Correction and primary pinpoint |
| --- | --- |
| No parent dividend category | Art 10(3) requires at least 80% voting power for the twelve months ending on entitlement, directly or through US/French-resident intermediaries, plus a stated art 30 route. |
| Substantial dividends always use 5% | Art 10(2)(a)(i) requires a company beneficial owner and direct ownership of at least 10% of US voting stock; art 10(5) excludes RIC/REIT dividends from that rate and the parent exemption. |
| Interest is completely exempt under art 11(2) | Ordinary relief is art 11(1); profits-linked interest under art 11(2) has a 15% gross ceiling through art 10(2)(b). Art 11(3) excludes dividends and late-payment penalties. Art 29(6) preserves REMIC excess-inclusion taxation. |
| Royalties and technical services always use 0% | Amended art 12 defines royalties and contingent alienation gains; the explanation distinguishes know-how from pure assistance/services. Services may use art 7 or retained art 14, with their respective PE/fixed-base rules. |
| PE starts at 12 months | Art 5(3) requires a building site or construction or installation project, or an installation or drilling rig or ship used for exploration or to prepare for extraction of natural resources, to last or be used for more than twelve months. General fixed-place and dependent-agent rules apply separately. |
| Reciprocal blanket summary | The US-source row points to the full conditions. Reciprocal French withholding was not reviewed. |

The section includes all three art 10(5)(b) REIT alternatives and the
art 10(5)(c) diversification definition: the value of no single real-property interest
exceeds 10% of total real-property interests, excluding foreclosure property
and attributing partnership real property proportionately. Exactly 10% passes
that bound; more than 10% does not. The explanation's 'gross value' wording
was not substituted for the operative definition.

Art 4(2)(c) requires current inclusion in the otherwise French-resident
member's taxable income and all four French qualified-partnership conditions.
Deferred inclusion alone does not satisfy this route. Art 11(5)'s
payer-residence and PE/fixed-base source rule is separate from the exchange
of notes preserving domestic or third-state treaty taxation for interest paid
by a PE located in a Contracting State; the note does not name a fixed base.
Current arts 12(4), (5) retain original paragraphs 6 and 7, renumbered by
Protocol III. Both the substantive original text and the renumbering are linked.

The English art 10(5)(b)(i) condition requires the pension trust or other
organisation to be maintained exclusively for the stated benefits and separately
established or sponsored by a resident. The French consolidation uses
*constitué exclusivement*; no reconciliation of the authentic texts is asserted.
The US-payer direct-voting-stock limb is art 10(2)(a)(i) in the English Protocol
and art 10(2)(a)(ii) in the French consolidation. The guide's US-source row
cites the English instrument and preserves its pinpoint.

The common gates cover residence/derivation under art 4, art 30 entitlement,
the art 29 saving clause, attribution under arts 10(7), 11(4), 12(3),
arm's-length limits under arts 11(6), 12(5), deferred attributable income
under arts 7(7), 14(2), and additional company tax under arts 10(8), (9).
Art 30(5)'s third-jurisdiction PE test uses combined actual tax below 60% of
the stated hypothetical residence-state tax. It substitutes a 15% gross
ceiling for affected dividends, interest and royalties, with the stated
self-developed-intangible and active-business exceptions. The unsupported
MFN royalty assertion was removed without inferring a replacement proposition.

Three interpretation gaps remain. The explanation's art 10(3) introduction
mentions pension-fund dividends, but the operative provision supplies only
a company-beneficial-owner route. The explanation's final copyright sentence
in its art 12(3) discussion appears inconsistent with the operative
PE/fixed-base attribution exception. Art 10(9)(b), (c) cross-reference items
in paragraph 7 although paragraph 8 imposes the additional tax. No pension
exemption or correction of those references is inferred, and the copyright
sentence is not used to override art 12(3).

Its art 10(9)(b), (c) also refers to paragraph 7. This comparison is not an
independent translation or complete comparison of the authentic signed texts.
The current [IRS competent-authority index](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/competent-authority-arrangements)
lists only FATCA material for France. That observation does not prove no
other arrangement exists. [Announcement 2020-6](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2020-23_IRB)
states the Treasury/IRS interpretation of NAFTA references after USMCA
replacement and an intention to seek counterpart confirmation; it does not
establish French agreement.

The [UN cumulative index](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/Cumulative%20Index/UNTS%20Volume%20No%202451-2500/cumindex.chrono.en.pdf)
records force on 30 December 1995 for the original Convention and
21 December 2006 for the 2004 Protocol. The
[2009 UN record](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%202659/volume-2659-A-33537.pdf)
at printed page 82 and the [Treasury announcement](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/tg504)
confirm force on 23 December 2009. Protocol XVI applies withholding changes
to amounts paid from 1 January 2009 and other tax changes generally to periods
beginning on or after 1 January 2010. The selected financial documents and
French consolidated text extracted natively with pdf-inspector, without OCR.
The UN force passages and 2009 publication footer were also read natively;
no financial claim relies on an OCR reconstruction or the rejected HTML
response from an attempted original-record PDF URL.

Manual primary-source comparisons distinguish below 10% from direct 10%
voting stock, below 80% from exact 80% voting power, a shorter holding from
the twelve-month period ending on entitlement, and ownership alone from the
required additional parent-relief route. They distinguish RIC from the three
REIT alternatives, dividend-classified profits-linked income from interest,
REMIC excess from ordinary interest, defined royalties from services, and
PE/fixed-base attribution from ordinary source relief. Exactly twelve
months does not meet the art 5(3) threshold; more than twelve months does
+for each stated site/project and installation/rig/ship category, including
+preparation for extraction.
Exactly 60% is outside the below-60% triangular test. These are treaty-text
comparisons, not taxpayer calculations.
