# South Africa: the reviewed 2026/27 table did not match SARS

> Entry of 2026-09-29 in the [verification log](README.md), written while folding the "Verified rates & thresholds" lists of the seven South African guides into their bodies (roadmap item 17); the methodology these entries apply is in [ACCURACY-METHODOLOGY.md](../ACCURACY-METHODOLOGY.md).

The South African income tax guide (`za-income-tax`, tier 1, reviewed by Werner Britz CA(SA) on 2026-06-12) carried a reviewer's list of verified facts that gave the 2026/27 individual tax table, the rebates and the tax thresholds "per the SARS Budget 2026 Tax Guide", and the body had been updated to those figures. The evaluation of 2026-09-28 had flagged the table as a specific error to verify against SARS before touching it, so before folding the list I read SARS's own tables for the 2027 year of assessment (1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027).

**They differ.** SARS publishes the brackets as R1 to R245,100 at 18%; R44,118 + 26% above R245,100 to R383,100; R79,998 + 31% above R383,100 to R530,200; R125,599 + 36% above R530,200 to R695,800; R185,215 + 39% above R695,800 to R887,000; R259,783 + 41% above R887,000 to R1,878,600; R666,339 + 45% above R1,878,600. The reviewed list had R245,200, R383,000, R887,100 and R1,878,300 as bounds and R44,136, R79,884, R125,516, R185,132, R259,739 and R666,131 as fixed amounts. The rebates differ too (secondary R9,765 not R9,768; tertiary R3,249 not R3,252), and with them the 65-plus thresholds (R153,250 not R153,278; R171,300 not R171,355). The primary rebate (R17,820), the under-65 threshold (R99,000), the medical scheme fees tax credits (R376, R752 for two, R254), the turnover tax bands and the R2.3 million VAT registration threshold all match SARS.

**How the list gave itself away.** The reviewed table was not internally consistent: R44,136 + 26% of (R383,000 − R245,200) is R79,964, not R79,884, and (R17,820 + R9,768) / 18% is R153,266.67, not R153,278. SARS's figures reconcile to the cent at every bound and every threshold. The most likely explanation is that the review worked from Budget-day material and transcribed or rounded a bracket, and the rest was carried along; the reviewer's citation was honest and the review was real, and the figures were still wrong. A "verified" label on a list is a claim about a process, not a guarantee about each number, which is the point of [What "verified" means here](2026-09-09-what-verified-means-here-and-what-it-does-not.md).

**What changed.** The table, the rebates, the thresholds and the worked example in `za-income-tax` now carry SARS's published figures with SARS named as the source, and the guide's provenance note says so; Test 1 of `za-provisional-tax`, which computed on the same table, is recomputed (R59,286 for the first period, not R59,282). Both guides are set to `review_status: pending_review`: they stay accountant-reviewed, and the reviewer has not seen the corrected text. The reviewed list also contained a page of rules the reviewer found missing from the guide (s 11A, s 12B to s 12L, s 13, s 18A, s 20A, tax residence, s 6quat, donations tax, estate duty, tax-free investments, s 7 and s 7C, dividends tax, SDL, withholding taxes, trusts, s 23H, s 23(e), personal service providers); those are now Section 10 of the guide, with each figure I could confirm on a SARS page (tax-free investment limits, donations tax, the small business corporation table, VAT registration) marked as confirmed and the CGT figures marked as the review's reading of Budget 2026, to be confirmed against the Eighth Schedule.

**What a checker could have seen.** `check-bracket-tables.py` recomputes tables whose rows carry a fixed amount and a rate, and the inconsistency above is exactly that computation; the table's layout ("R44,136 + 26% above R245,200" in a two-column table) is one the checker does not parse. Making it parse that layout is the mechanical follow-up in roadmap item 18.
