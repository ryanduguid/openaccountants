# Shared transfer-pricing method selection and evidence gates

I corrected the shared transfer-pricing workflow before using it for the
next Norfolk country batch. Its method step, hierarchy and completion
check could force a distributor into RPM/TNMM, a manufacturer into cost
plus/TNMM, and profit split into a fixed profile. Those instructions could
reject a more reliable method supported by the actual facts and country
law. The generic manufacturer and royalty ranges could also supply an
unsupported benchmark where evidence was missing.

Version 1.1 uses the applicable country rules and a documented assessment
of method reliability, functional analysis, comparability and adjustments.
It retains the OECD preferences when methods are equally reliable and
removes automatic assignments, an automatic TNMM fallback and the forced
less-complex tested-party rule. The chosen tested party needs reliable
application and comparable data. An interquartile range is now a supported
statistical choice rather than a universal requirement; a single reliable
result or another supported range remains possible.

I read selected passages in the OECD 2022 publication: paragraphs 2.2–2.5,
3.18–3.19 and 3.55–3.62, alongside the relevant method definitions. Native
text extraction supplied the substantive passages. This was not a review
of the entire publication. A rotated cover page required the separate OCR
route, which was unavailable; that page was excluded from evidence.

Country-specific entity, group, period, exemption and threshold conditions
now govern the Master File and CbCR obligation assessments. A foreign
parent's report alone does not clear another entity's local obligation.
The internal Local File output is distinguished from a statutory filing
duty. I checked the Australian counterexample in sections 815-355 to
815-380; it illustrates why a global revenue-only or parent-only shortcut
cannot establish every jurisdiction's obligation. The base supplies no
country threshold or fee. Country companions retain their own source gates.

The five named TP outputs and ten completion checks are preserved. I also
corrected the Australian/German companion pointers and the fork provenance.
Fourteen prepared synthetic cases cover the changed instruction branches,
missing evidence, local methods, range selection and preserved behaviour.
The comparison is static author self-review, not an independent model
benchmark or proof of runtime behaviour. No separate LLM upload trial,
performance measurements or taxpayer documentation package is claimed.

Repository checks cover regenerated shared references/index material,
metadata, sync, standing checker baselines and the documented test suites.
This remains Tier 2 pending professional review. The repair is scoped to
these workflow issues; it does not sign off every TP country companion,
complete the Norfolk pack or confer comprehensive-country acceptance.
