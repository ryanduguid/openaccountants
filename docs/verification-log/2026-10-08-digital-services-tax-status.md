# Digital services tax: repeal, cut-offs and UK mechanics

The DST matrix directed Canadian payments under a repealed Act, treated
India's specified-services levy as continuing and assigned the UK payment
deadline to the return. The UK quarterly percentage schedule had no support
in the checked payment provisions. These instructions also appeared outside
the country tables, in computations, outputs, self-checks and prohibitions.

I checked selected Canadian, Indian and UK propositions against the sources
below on 8 October 2026. The guide advances from version 0.1 to 0.2, retains
`tax_year: 2025`, tier 2 and `pending_review`, and records the limited subsequent
checks in `tax_year_notes`. The other countries' data remains a 2025 reference
requiring verification before operational use. The disclaimer and CTA are
preserved.

The Canadian guide cited SC 2024 c 17. The repeal identifies the former Act
as section 96 of SC 2024 c 15. Its deemed repeal date does not establish the
former Act's original commencement date. Former thresholds, attribution and
catch-up dates were withdrawn because they were not supported by this review.
Refund amounts require the prescribed-interest rules, rate history and
taxpayer payment facts.

India's service and consideration boundaries are separate. A later cash
settlement alone does not establish when consideration became receivable.
Historical rates, thresholds, payer scope, collection, remittance, statements,
corrections, income-tax disallowance and transition treatment require their
applicable provisions and facts. The guide withdraws those operational
details without inferring that all historical obligations were cancelled.
Two additional section-number lookups returned unrelated Acts and were
excluded after checking their statutory identity.

The UK ordinary 2% charge and elected 0.8 × operating margin × net revenues
are distinct computations. The alternative result is not multiplied by a
further 2%. The return and payment illustration for a period ending
31 December 2025 gives 31 December 2026 and 1 October 2026 respectively.
Removing the unsupported quarterly percentages does not establish that
instalments can never arise under an applicable arrangement.

The output specification now requires verified primary law for every
classification, exemption, attribution, calculation and deadline. An inherited
table cell cannot establish an operational conclusion. The former general
carve-out examples and VAT/GST collection, gross-revenue and no-netting
instructions are withdrawn from the verified subset; those matters require
their applicable primary provisions.

The sourcing-floor checker accepted two uncited rate-table findings for this
guide before the change. One authority link clears every table at guide
level, so removal of those findings records the added citations, not
verification of every country's figures. Any baseline removal must use the
existing checker and reproduce that explained cause.

Other jurisdictions' rates, thresholds, scope, attribution, deadlines,
replacement regimes and proposed-law status were not reverified. Complete
amendment histories, authentic-language completeness, Pillar One and trade
developments, claimant facts, taxpayer calculations, licensing and
professional sign-off remain unverified. Repository checks and a consultation
do not establish those results.
