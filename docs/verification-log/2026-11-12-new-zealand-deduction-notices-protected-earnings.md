The protected base subtracts PAYE income tax excluding ACC earners' levy. IR335 page 24's introductory prose is confusing, but its worked calculation and specification page 72 show the base explicitly: $600 gross less $84 income tax is $516, with maximum child support $206.40. IR335 page 25 makes ceased-employment code C take priority over P and requires the finish date; a protected-earnings shortfall is not automatically caught up later.

The current [District Court Act section 156](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2016/49/en/latest/sections/DLM6942500/), [section 157](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2016/49/en/latest/sections/DLM6942502/) and [section 154 definition](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2016/49/en/latest/sections/DLM6942492/) were captured and their relevant substantive provisions read. The displayed current-copy date is 17 February 2026. Section 157(3) requires reduction or cancellation of an ordinary attachment to preserve the higher of 60% of statutory net earnings and the order's minimum. Section 157(2) and (5) identify prior charges and other instruments. The guide requires their actual legal priority; it does not decide every competing-order case.

Specification page 72 step 5 prints the full other-attachment amount when it exceeds remaining capacity. This contradicts the protective floor and the current Act. I used the statutory clamp. The adjacent printed $2,000-pay example was not used as an arithmetic oracle. The synthetic cases test a single ordinary court attachment with the given base and valid prior deductions; they do not determine the validity of an actual order.

The [Tax Administration Act section 157](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1994/166/en/latest/sections/DLM356984/) is a different provision. The displayed current-copy date is 1 October 2026; I read subsections (1)–(8) relevant to salary/wage notices, not the entire Act. Subsection (3)'s ceiling is the greater of $10 per week and the lesser of 10% per week of notice-date tax debt or 20% of wages payable. Subsection (1) and the actual notice determine the required amount and payment time; the ceiling does not replace a lower required deduction. A notice may include daily interest, so an employer must reconcile its actual terms and outstanding amount.

The specification page 75 flowchart's $10 shortcut for weekly pay below $100 does not reproduce the statutory ceiling at $80: 20% is $16. The guide follows the Act and SPS, with conditional $80 and $30 worked checks and the stated below-$10 exception. Tax-arrears deductions are not SLCIR and are not on employment information; they are paid separately. The general month-end instruction is subject to establishing the actual notice's payment time.
