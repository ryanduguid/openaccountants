# US treaties: Canada and India rules

The Canada and India sections of `us-major-partners` misstated interest and
royalty categories and permanent-establishment (PE) tests. This source check
corrects those two sections and their PE rows for the guide's 2025 tax year.
The guide remains a tier 2 draft with `review_status: pending_review`.

The primary sources read on 5 October 2026 were Finance Canada's
[consolidated Canada-US convention](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/tax-policy/tax-treaties/country/united-states-america-convention-consolidated-1980-1983-1984-1995-1997-2007.html),
including the 2007 Fifth Protocol, and the IRS
[India convention and Protocol](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/india.pdf)
and [1989 technical explanation](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/inditech.pdf).
The [IRS document index](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/india-tax-treaty-documents)
links those Indian treaty documents. The
[Canadian publication record](https://publications.aws.tpsgc-pwgsc.cloud-nuage.canada.ca/site/eng/9.823645/publication.html)
supplies the original treaty's dates and series citation.

| Statement in the guide | Correction and primary pinpoint |
| --- | --- |
| Canada related-party interest is 10% | Art XI(1) exempts qualifying ordinary interest from source tax, including the arm's-length amount of related-party interest. Art XI(5) excludes a special-relationship excess. Arts XI(3) and (6) contain PE, contingent-interest and REMIC exceptions. |
| Canada's exempt royalties are in art XII(2), while patents are 10% | The specified exemptions are in art XII(3). Copyright production/reproduction has film and television exclusions; software use/right-to-use payments and patent/experience use/right-to-use payments are exempt. Contingent patent/experience alienation gains under art XII(4) require residual art XII(2) classification; non-contingent alienation needs separate gains analysis. Rental/franchise experience information is excluded from that exemption. Art XII(2) retains a 10% residual ceiling. |
| Canada has no services PE | Art V(9) has two tests using 183 days or more in any twelve-month period, with individual/revenue or connected-project/customer conditions. |
| India's patents and equipment both have a 10% rate | Arts 12(2)(a)(ii) and (3)(a) place patents and other specified royalties in the 15% category. Arts 12(2)(b) and (3)(b) place qualifying equipment royalties and included services ancillary/subsidiary to the enjoyment of that equipment in the 10% category. The service definition in art 12(4) is broader than that 10% category. |
| All Indian technical services are 15% and require making knowledge available | Art 12(4) has an ancillary/subsidiary limb and a separate make-available or technical-plan/design limb. Art 12(5) excludes specified services. Services outside that definition require art 7 or art 15 classification. |
| India construction PE starts at 90 days; services use a fiscal-year test | Art 5(2)(k) uses more than 120 days in any twelve-month period. Art 5(2)(l) uses more than 90 days in any twelve-month period or in-state services to a related enterprise, excluding included services. The latter limb has no day threshold. |

The revised tables identify US-source payments to residents of the named
partner. Reduced dividend rates require a company beneficial owner with the
stated voting-stock holding, and US RIC/REIT exceptions can alter the ordinary
caps. The sections also identify residence, beneficial ownership, entitlement,
saving-clause and PE/fixed-base conditions. Canada's broadcasting exemption
requires an applicable exchange of notes, so the table does not claim it.

India's 10% and 15% included-service figures are treaty tax ceilings.
Protocol IV describes US net-basis taxation under US law and any applicable
art 14(1) tax, capped together by the relevant percentage of the gross fee.
Treaty source under art 12(7) does not by itself establish domestic US
taxability or the withholding procedure. Protocol I's cross-year qualification
is stated for the art 5 time tests; the guide does not apply it to the separate
related-enterprise limb.

The India convention PDF is largely scanned. Selective extraction used
pdf-inspector OCR on PDF pages 4-6, 10-13, 22-31 and 54-60. OCR rendered some
digits as letters, including `l20`; the native-text technical explanation
independently states the 120-day construction threshold. The selected pages
had no hosted-review recommendation. The official
[IRS treaty tables](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/tax-treaty-tables)
still label Table 1 as the May 2023 edition. It corroborates the ordinary
Canada/India rate categories but expressly does not settle all eligibility
requirements or reciprocal foreign withholding.

The other 12 corridors, their domestic-law statements and the general LOB
overview were not reverified. Their recorded May 2026 dates remain historical
claims. The global date now states that limit, and the former count of 14
"treaties" now counts 14 covered partners, including Singapore's domestic-law
section. Current US withholding procedures and taxpayer-specific eligibility
were not reviewed. No qualified accountant has signed off on this guide.

The sourcing-floor checker treats the whole guide as its unit. An official
link anywhere clears all its rate-table findings. The new citations therefore
clear 15 reported tables represented by ten baseline fingerprints, including
unchecked corridors. Removing those stale fingerprints records that the guide
now contains primary links; it does not certify those other tables. The
arithmetic, bracket, expired-rule and fact-conflict queues require their own
checks.

The fact-conflict checker also stops reproducing the cross-border
`fees included services` fingerprint. Its former generic India row and an
unchanged Israel-US row shared that label but described different treaty
regimes. India's revised rows distinguish equipment-related included services
from other included services. The checker removed that one stale fingerprint;
this records the changed classification, not a source check of the Israel row.
