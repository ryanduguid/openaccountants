# Norfolk Island capital gains, losses and foreign-resident property

I added `nf-cgt` for selected 2026–27 capital gains and losses and linked it
from the Norfolk scope guide. It retains the Norfolk transition's four
conditions, identifies A1 event timing, separate proceeds and bases, current
loss ordering and discount gates, and selected home and asset-class rules.
It remains a Tier 2 source-cited draft pending professional review.

The live Register identifies C267 as the latest published assessment-Act
compilation, with an interval ending September 2026 and unincorporated
commenced amendments. I checked its first-page amendment list and read the
October Act's full foreign-resident CGT and renewable-energy schedules,
including commencement, application and transitional provisions. The new
property definition, principal-asset lookback, particular declarations and
conditional renewable-energy discount are not replaced with the old
consolidated wording.

The 2026 Tax Reform No. 1 Act separately applies its Division 102 changes to
assessments for the income year including July 2027 and later years. I read
the previous five-step net-gain method with that application clause and
compared thirteen selected old and latest sections. Eleven were identical;
the different net-gain and discount sections were checked with their period
rules. The guide does not project its ordinary 2026–27 examples into the
enacted 2027 regime. Tax Reform No. 2's selected residential extensions have
their own 2027–28 application.

I checked geographic Australia under section 960-505, which expressly
includes Norfolk, and the current transitional section 102-25. The Norfolk
rule substitutes a date in the CGT Parts; it is not the Cocos market-value
rebasing rule. A transfer fee, council valuation, beneficial ownership
change, actual CGT proceeds and reduced cost base have distinct tests.
The record exception is limited, not permission to discard all evidence.

The source review distinguishes loss before discount, collectable losses,
personal-use losses, inclusive asset thresholds, the non-portfolio threshold,
strict principal-asset comparison and inclusive renewable comparison. It
retains foreign/temporary-residence, main-residence and actual valuation
gates. The renewable concession is a particular foreign non-individual/trust
branch, not the ordinary individual discount or a general company rule.
