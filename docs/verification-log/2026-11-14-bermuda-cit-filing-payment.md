## Controls and remaining limits

Regs 6–10 distinguish registration, the Filing BCE and authority to act. Regs 12–14 distinguish payment exemptions and amounts from reg 10A's information duty. Nil payment or the small-tax payment exception does not itself erase information or return filing. Short-period and de minimis exceptions require their own facts. Reg 10A's deemed-filing and amendment rules preserve the earlier return-filing/original-deadline cutoff; a still-available web form does not confer permission to file after it.

Regs 17 and 23 distinguish the original return/final-payment deadline from later filing or assessment events. Reg 25(4) refers an amendment to Part 3 rather than freezing all overpayment dates: reg 20(4)(a)(ii) deems pre-deadline payments made on the later amendment filing date where the overpayment arises with respect to that post-deadline amendment. Numerical refund and interest computations remain unverified. The current primary instructions require Form 100 plus the relevant schedules, all simplified-route conditions and the substantive election consequences. Missing or inconsistent schedules can leave filing unsatisfied. Amendment requires the relevant complete return components and explanation even where summary values do not change. Regs 25–28 and 32 keep the taxpayer amendment, Agency correction/rejection, retention, assessment and notice-response clocks separate.

The guide has 8 grouped hypothetical manual controls covering nil tax, small tax, short years, the 2 dated examples, form cutoff/selection, simplified-route conditions, missing/amended schedules and distinct procedural clocks. These trace source rules and text only. They are not executed taxpayer computations, consuming-model evaluations or portal tests.
