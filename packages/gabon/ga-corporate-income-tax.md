# Gabon Corporate Income Tax

## Corporate income tax for 2025

The DGI's **CGI 2025** compilation identifies amendments through
Loi n°033/2024 of 30 December 2024. The rules in this section describe that
compilation for **2025**. Selected provisions enacted for **2026** appear
separately below. Later amendments, treaty application and current filing
practice still need checking. [DGI, CGI 2025](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)

- **Rates.** The standard IS rate is **30%**, increased to **35%** for oil
  and mining businesses. Taxable profit is rounded down to the nearest
  **CFA 1,000** before calculating IS.
  [CGI 2025, article 16](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Tax base.** Taxable profit is the net result of all operations in the
  period, including asset disposals during or at the end of the business.
  Article 8 also defines the net-asset calculation and accounting rules.
  [Article 8](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Reinvestment relief.** For a qualifying fixed-asset disposal during
  trading, article 9 permits deferral if the gain is recorded in a special
  reinvestment account and an undertaking is annexed to the return. Within
  **3 years from that financial year's close**, the business must reinvest
  in new fixed assets a sum equal to the **gain plus the original cost of
  the assets sold**. Buying or subscribing for shares or participation
  interests does not qualify. Portfolio assets must have been held for at
  least **3 years**. Timely reinvestment reduces the new assets' depreciable
  cost for depreciation and later capital-gain calculations by the deferred
  gain. Otherwise, the gain is brought back into profit when the deadline
  expires, or on an earlier sale or transfer or cessation of the business.
  [Article 9](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Residence and territoriality.** Subject to international conventions,
  IS applies to profits from businesses operated or transactions carried
  out in Gabon. Resident companies are those registered in Gabon or with
  their place of effective management there. Non-resident companies with
  a Gabon permanent establishment are taxed on profits attributable to that
  establishment, similar sales of goods in Gabon and similar industrial or
  commercial activities in Gabon. Article 7-II separately defines permanent
  establishments and their exceptions, which require a transaction check.
  [Article 7-I(1) to (3) and article 7-II](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Minimum tax.** IS cannot be less than either the **1%** impôt minimum
  forfaitaire (IMF) or the **CFA 500,000** minimum de perception, subject to
  article 26 relief. The IMF base is gross global turnover from the
  company's activities, including miscellaneous products and profits,
  ancillary income, financial income, exchange gains and income outside
  ordinary activities, rounded down to the nearest **CFA 1,000**.
  [Articles 24 to 26](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Minimum-tax relief.** Article 26 exempts entities exempt from IS under
  article 6 and newly registered businesses that have never conducted any
  activity in Gabon, provided their creation is more than a change of name
  or management with the previous activity continuing. It also exempts newly
  registered companies for their **first 2 financial years if loss-making**.
  That latter period is **3 years** for PME/PMI covered by article 17 of
  Law n°008/2018 of 8 February 2019. The 3-year benefit excludes businesses
  that started trading at least **2 years before registration**.
  [Article 26](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Investment-income withholding.** Article 109 prescribes a **20%** final
  IRPP levy on covered investment income, including dividends. Article 110
  provides **10%** for dividends and investment income received by venture
  capital companies during the holding period. Check the recipient's tax
  status and article 105 exemptions before applying these rules.
  [Articles 105, 106, 109 and 110](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Interest.** Article 109 expressly applies a **20%** final IRPP levy to
  income from claims, deposits and guarantees paid to persons without a real
  domicile in Gabon, subject to international conventions. Article 110 sets
  **15%** for bank bons de caisse and **10%** for bonds with a maturity of at
  least **5 years** issued in Gabon. Income classification and exemptions
  still require checking.
  [Articles 103 to 110](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Investment-income collection.** The paying establishment withholds
  before payment and remits the levy with a return within **30 days** after
  payment or making the income available to the beneficiary.
  [Article 111](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Covered payments to persons without a permanent professional
  installation in Gabon.** Article 206 requires a Gabon-established payer to
  withhold **25%** of the gross amount excluding turnover taxes on listed
  payments. These include independent professional activity in Gabon,
  inventors' income, copyright and industrial or commercial property income,
  services physically supplied or effectively used in Gabon and specified
  fixed-income receipts included in the recipient's professional revenue,
  excluding article 102 bond income. Remit with the prescribed return within
  the **first 15 days of the month after payment**. The article is limited to
  its listed payment categories and recipients subject to IRPP or IS.
  [Article 206](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Annual return.** File before **30 April of the following year**.
  [Article 20](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Instalments.** Pay **one quarter** of the previous year's tax on
  **30 November** and **one third** on **30 January**, without notice.
  A special declaration permits a proportionate reduction where the company
  expects a lower liability. An estimate later found inaccurate by more
  than **one tenth** attracts the article P-998 penalty on unpaid amounts.
  [Article 21](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)
- **Balance and credit.** Pay the balance when filing, by **30 April** at
  the latest. Tax paid above the amount due becomes a credit for later years.
  [Articles 21 and 21 bis](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)

For example, assume an ordinary **30%** company has **CFA 1,000,000** of
taxable profit after adjustments and a qualifying IMF base of
**CFA 120,000,000**, with no article 26 exemption or other relief. Ordinary
IS is **CFA 300,000**, the IMF is **CFA 1,200,000** and the fixed floor is
**CFA 500,000**. The company therefore owes **CFA 1,200,000** before crediting
payments. [Articles 16 and 24 to 26](https://dgi.ga/document/code-general-des-impots-2025/)

## Selected provisions enacted for 2026

**Loi n°041/2025**, signed on **29 December 2025** and published in
**Journal Officiel n°96 Quater of 30 December 2025**, amends the CGI through
article 4. The provisions below describe that law's **2026** rules. The
Gazette webpage's heading gives 30 December, whilst the signed closing text
gives 29 December. Later amendments and implementation measures still need
checking. [Official Gazette, articles 1 and 4](https://journal-officiel.ga/22265-041-2025-/)

- **Share-transfer gains.** Replacement article 23(6) requires the Gabonese
  company whose rights are transferred to collect the **25%** final levy on
  the gains covered by article 7(2), and remit it to the tax collector within
  **1 month of realisation**. Confirm article 7(2)'s scope for the transaction.
  [Loi n°041/2025, replacement article 23(6)](https://journal-officiel.ga/22265-041-2025-/)
- **Rental withholding.** Replacement article 178 bis imposes **10%** on
  taxable rents under article 85 where the tenant is a legal person, a person
  subject to business-profits tax recording the rent in their accounts, an
  estate agency or property manager, the state, a local authority or a public
  establishment. The withholding is inapplicable where the landlord is
  subject to IS in Gabon. Withholdings for a calendar quarter must be remitted
  within the **first 15 days of the following quarter**. Any withholding above
  the tax due is refundable by the Treasury.
  [Replacement article 178 bis](https://journal-officiel.ga/22265-041-2025-/)
- **Deductible costs.** Replacement article 11 adds a normalised electronic
  invoice condition alongside the other statutory deduction conditions.
  It provides a **6-month** exception from promulgation for a document proving
  payment of customs duties in place of that invoice. Later implementation
  measures, extensions and transaction-specific entitlement remain open.
  [Replacement article 11](https://journal-officiel.ga/22265-041-2025-/)
- **Invoice obligation.** Article P 832 ter(1) requires normalised electronic
  invoices for transactions by persons subject to IS, business-profits tax,
  the final synthetic tax or VAT. Devices and enterprise invoicing systems
  require tax-authority approval. The article leaves implementation details
  to ministerial regulations, which still need verification.
  [Article P 832 ter, paragraphs 1 to 3](https://journal-officiel.ga/22265-041-2025-/)

For example, assume a covered tenant pays **CFA 3,000,000** of taxable rent
to a landlord outside the IS exception during a calendar quarter. The
article 178 bis withholding is **CFA 300,000** at **10%**, remitted within
the first 15 days of the following quarter. This withholding is a prepayment
against the landlord's liability. [Replacement article 178 bis](https://journal-officiel.ga/22265-041-2025-/)
