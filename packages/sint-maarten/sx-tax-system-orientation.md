# Sint Maarten Tax System Orientation

Use the ordinances and forms of the country of Sint Maarten, the Dutch part of the island. The Tax Administration oversees income tax, wage tax, profit tax and the tax on business turnover (BBO). [Sint Maarten Tax Administration][AUTH]

## Residence

The individual income tax ordinance determines residence from the circumstances, with special rules for temporary absence, a return within a year and certain temporary stays by residents of other parts of the Kingdom. A body set up under Sint Maarten law is deemed established in Sint Maarten under the profit tax ordinance. Check the local and treaty positions where the person has links elsewhere. [Income Tax Ordinance, art 1][IB] · [Profit Tax Ordinance, art 1][WB]

## Income and territorial scope

Residents generally account for income from property, capital, business, employment and periodic payments within the ordinary income tax rules. Non-residents account for the local sources in article 17. A non-resident body or trust can be liable to profit tax for business through a local permanent establishment, local real estate and associated rights, or specified mortgage-secured receivables. For a Sint Maarten body, check the article 12 exemption for qualifying foreign business profit and its exceptions. Apply the BBO place rules separately for a sale or service. [Income Tax Ordinance, arts 3 and 17][IB] · [Profit Tax Ordinance, arts 1, 1A, 4 and 12][WB] · [BBO Ordinance, arts 4–6][BBO]

## Taxpayer type

Individual business profits ordinarily fall under income tax. Profit tax names separate rules for local companies, other legal bodies and trusts. Employer wage tax uses separate tables. BBO applies to an independent entrepreneur making taxable supplies, including some transactions of persons established outside Sint Maarten. Check special exemptions, applicable registrations and related-party classification before applying the ordinary rules. [Income Tax Ordinance, arts 3 and 6][IB] · [Profit Tax Ordinance, arts 1 and 1A][WB] · [BBO Ordinance, arts 1–2 and 4–8][BBO] · [2026 table regulation][TABLELAW]

## Currency

Use the Caribbean guilder (Cg, currency code XCG) for current returns expressed in guilders. The central bank introduced XCG on 31 March 2025 and notes that the former Netherlands Antillean guilder was legal tender through 30 June 2025. The conversion between the two guilders is one for one. Confirm the transaction date and the conversion basis for other currencies. [Centrale Bank van Curaçao en Sint Maarten][CBCS] · [2026 income tax table][TABLE]

## Assessment periods

Individual income tax uses the annual period. For 2025 returns, the Tax Administration set 1 June 2026 for the individual returns, 31 March 2026 for the provisional profit tax return and 30 June 2026 for the final profit tax return. Check the actual legal period, invitation and any extension for a different or non-calendar close. BBO generally follows a monthly cycle. [Income Tax Ordinance, art 12][IB] · [Tax Administration 2025 returns notice][NOTICE] · [BBO Ordinance, arts 10–12][BBO]

## Tax authority and headline rates

Use the annual table published by the Sint Maarten Tax Administration. Its 2026 income tax table tops out at 47.5% and states that anyone within the ordinary table with taxable income of Cg 27,281.28 or less owes no ordinary income tax. This income tax table already includes opcenten. The ordinary profit tax statute sets 30%. The separate 15 opcenten charge brings the combined ordinary rate to 34.5%, as shown on the 2025 final return. The published BBO ordinance lists 5%, subject to later rate instruments, exemptions and the facts. Confirm the operative legislation before treating draft reform as part of the period rules. [2026 table regulation][TABLELAW] · [2026 income tax table][TABLE] · [Income Tax Ordinance, art 24][IB] · [Profit Tax Ordinance, art 15][WB] · [Opcenten Ordinance, art 2][OP] · [2025 final profit tax return][PROFITFORM] · [BBO Ordinance, arts 7–8][BBO] · [Parliament tax reform docket][DOCKET]
