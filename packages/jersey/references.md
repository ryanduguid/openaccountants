## Material source decisions

- Pension deductions use Income Tax Law Articles 4, 130, 130C, 131H and 131I with the complete official pension-contribution guidance. The holder's deduction aggregates actual approved contributions and uses total income before all approved pension contributions. The official subscription example is reproduced using its stated expense assumption; it supplies no general subscription deduction rule. The illustrative zero floor represents no positive deduction when a ceiling is exhausted. Scheme approval, other pension provisions and professional sign-off remain separate.

- The general pension page also lists main-residence interest payments and ordinary annual contributions for its higher-income test; the captured Article 131I(3) expressly adds back approved pension contributions. Their treatment under the applicable Article 4 and deduction provisions remains unresolved. Cases involving those additional items require point-in-time statutory checks or Revenue Jersey confirmation before total income is established.
- Personal rates and allowances use the 2026 schedule, Budget summary and Income Tax Law Articles 92A to 92C. The child's and childcare amounts are conditional ceilings or additions. The examples assume only the full basic threshold and no foreign tax or specialist adjustments.
- Corporate residence uses Article 123. Articles 120AB and 120AC exempt relevant MNE group entities from ordinary income tax and disapply specified returns for qualifying years or periods; they qualify the generic company page's blanket annual-return wording. Sector classification uses Articles 123C to 123DA and the return notes. Retail eligibility and tax use Articles 123H to 123L, including aggregation and period provisions. The example is a standalone ordinary annual period; the tax amount is calculated before its effective rate.
- Monthly Class 1 uses the 2026 employer schedule and Social Security Law Schedule 1A. The generic migration page's 2% upper-band employer figure conflicts with the dated schedule and law's 2.5%; the guide uses the latter. Examples show percentage arithmetic and exclude operational rounding and exemptions.
- Class 2 uses Social Security Law Articles 3, 4 and 8 and Schedule 1B, Contributions Order Articles 12 to 13E, 16A and 16B, and Schedule 2, and the Classification, Residence and Collection Orders. The dedicated guide distinguishes 2024 ordinary income, estimated 2026 Article 12 income and actual 2026 startup-reconciliation income. Conditional examples use established E/T inputs, applicable relief and liability periods; operational penny assessments remain unverified. The published SEL amount differs from the statutory Social Security Fund 'standard contribution'.
- Articles 16A and 16B require written applications, supporting evidence and the general six-month application period, with discretionary late acceptance and extensions. These provisions were checked in the same complete S11.01 capture, reconfirmed on 11 October 2026 with the identical hash. The specific Article 13D startup cut-offs remain separate; acceptance does not establish an automatic retrospective period.
- The Class 2 page's GBP 72,248 and GBP 13,968 example figures conflict with the dated 2026 limits. Its total-income-only examples do not establish the statutory E/T branches. The Article 12 inclusive income test differs from the protecting-records page's strict/not-working wording; grant conditions remain unresolved. The Article 13C offset is limited to its specified classification. No broader top-up, benefit repayment or standalone LTC payable amount is inferred.
- Income Tax Article 70A supplies the Class 2 trading/professional expense percentages. Its liability-year wording and the 2025 return help's paid-amount instructions require reconciliation where timing differs. Raw turnover or tax profit does not establish every Schedule 2 income component, and the guide blocks that unsupported mapping.
- ITIS uses the employer guidance's valid-notice rule, 22% missing-rate default and inclusion of LTC. The payroll example's 12% is an assumed valid notice, not a published statutory rate.
- GST uses the domestic business and return guidance, place-of-supply sections and Direction 2026/03. The personal GBP 60 relief excludes business imports. Detailed mixed-supply and customs calculations remain open.
- Accounts use Companies Law Articles 103 to 105, 108 and 113 in the snapshot effective from 30 September 2026. The substance outline uses Articles 3 to 5A; published specialist guidance and partnership law still need investigation.
- Non-resident categories use Income Tax Law Article 118B. Broader generic non-resident wording does not override the statutory dividend-profit conditions or short-term visitor test. The landlord deduction example assumes an ordinary managing agent, established net rent and no clearance or special notice.
- Property examples reproduce and recompute the LTT guidance's ordinary and higher-rate share-transfer cases. The Budget summary and Stamp Duties Law's 2026 schedule establish the temporary higher-rate period. Freehold and probate charges remain separate, with no payable amount computed here.
- MCIT's introductory page has apparently inconsistent top-up wording; no ordering or effective-tax formula is taken from it. Its preliminary scope and payment framework do not close the missing technical provisions.
- Crypto classification uses the published tax guidance; it does not settle staking, DeFi or licensing. The CARF page combines a 2026 reportable list with conditional implementation wording. Enacted rules must be checked before an operational reporting instruction.
