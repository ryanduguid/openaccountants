# Netherlands 30% ruling: how I handle it

The 30% ruling (30%-regeling, now officially the **expatregeling**) lets a Dutch withholding agent pay a qualifying employee recruited from abroad a fixed tax-free allowance instead of reimbursing actual extraterritorial costs receipt by receipt. It runs for a maximum of 5 years, reduced by earlier periods of stay or work in the Netherlands. It is a payroll facility applied by the employer under a decision (beschikking) issued to employer and employee jointly, not something the employee claims alone on a return.

Two things make 2026 an unusual year. It is the **last year at 30%** for anyone who entered from 2024 onward (27% from 1 January 2027), and it is the **last year of the transitional partial non-resident status**, which was abolished for new use from 1 January 2025.

## Who this is for

**Qualifies:** an employee on a Dutch payroll who meets the definition of *ingekomen werknemer*, holds specific expertise evidenced by a salary above the annual norm, and for whom the Belastingdienst has issued a beschikking.

**Does not qualify:** anyone who lived within 150 km of the Dutch border for too much of the 24 months before their first Dutch working day. The Belastingdienst is blunt about the consequence: the facility cannot be used for employees from Belgium and Luxembourg. The same bar catches Northern France, large parts of Germany and a small part of the UK.

**No income norm needed:** scientific researchers at certain institutions and trainee medical specialists are carved out and need not meet an income norm.

**Send to a specialist when:** the employee held a Dutch ruling with a previous employer and is switching jobs (the ruling does not travel automatically); the employee has a doctorate obtained in or near the Netherlands (a separate PhD route exists); the employee has any Dutch history in the last 25 years that could shorten the 5 years; or the employee is relying on the pre-2024 transitional package.

## Before you start, ask the client

- **Where did the employee live in the 24 months before their first Dutch working day, and for how many months in each place?** The test is more than 16 of those 24 months at more than 150 km from the Dutch border.
- **Was the employee recruited from abroad, or already here?**
- **Any prior residence, work or even long visits to the Netherlands in the last 25 years?** These reduce the 5-year run, subject to small carve-outs.
- **Has the employee worked in the Netherlands before and is now returning?** A border-region exception exists where the earlier work period began at most 8 years ago.
- **What is the exact first working day?** This starts the 4-month application clock.
- **What is the contractual salary, and is the 30% treated as inside or outside that figure?** This decides whether the taxable remainder clears the norm.
- **Is there a Dutch doctorate in the picture?**
- **Did the employee use the ruling before 2024?** That governs both the percentage from 2027 and the partial non-resident transitional.

## The method, step by step

1. **Test the incoming-employee definition first.** More than 16 of the 24 months before the first Dutch working day at more than 150 km from the border. If this fails, stop. No salary level rescues it.
2. **Test the expertise requirement against the 2026 salary norm.** Taxable annual salary must exceed **€48,013** excluding the targeted exemption, or **€36,497** for an employee under 30 holding a Dutch academic master's or equivalent foreign degree. Check whether the researcher / trainee-specialist carve-out applies instead.
3. **Choose your route for the calendar year.** You either reimburse actual extraterritorial costs or apply the expatregeling. The choice is made in the first pay period of the calendar year and binds for the whole year. You cannot flip mid-year.
4. **File the request within 4 months of the first working day.** Employer and employee sign jointly. Expect a reply within 8 weeks.
5. **Apply the exemption in payroll.** Either up to 30% of salary including the allowance, or the equivalent 30/70 of salary excluding the allowance.
6. **Apply the cap.** In 2026 the tax-free allowance is capped at **€78,600**, reached at a salary of **€262,000** or higher for a full year. The cap is applied per withholding agent and pro-rated over the part of the year it applies, under article 31a(8) Wet LB 1964 by reference to article 2.3 Wet normering topinkomens.
7. **Re-test the income norm every year of the run.**
8. **Diarise the end date and the 2027 step-down.** Confirm which row of the transitional table your employee sits in before running 2027 payroll.

## The traps

- **Missing the 4-month window.** File within 4 months of the first working day and the facility runs from day one. File later and you lose that retroactivity. *The precise start date for a late-filed request is set out on the beschikking; confirm before filing.*
- **Dropping below the salary norm mid-year is not a partial loss, it is a full-year loss.** If the salary in a year is lower than the indexed norm, the facility lapses **retroactively to 1 January of that year**. Unpaid leave, a switch to part-time or a bonus that fails to materialise can wipe out the whole year. The defence is to reduce the tax-free allowance so the taxable remainder still clears the norm.
- **Forgetting the norm is indexed annually.** €48,013 is a 2026 figure. A salary that cleared the norm in 2025 may not clear it in 2026.
- **Assuming the ruling follows the employee to a new employer.** It does not transfer automatically. A new joint request is needed, with its own 4-month clock. On a company takeover the arrangement does transfer.
- **Ignoring the cap on high earners.** Above €262,000 the allowance stops growing at €78,600. Because the cap is applied per withholding agent and re-calculated pro rata, a mid-year employer change needs the cap split across both payrolls.
- **Silently losing years to Dutch history.** The 5 years is reduced by earlier stay or work in the Netherlands in the 25 years before arrival, with only narrow carve-outs: a maximum of 20 days per year worked in the Netherlands, and a maximum of 6 weeks per year (or once a maximum of 3 consecutive months) for holidays, family visits and other private reasons. A previous secondment surfaces here and shortens the run.
- **Getting the transitional rows wrong.** Employees whose beschikking applied by 31-12-2023 keep 30% and the current norm indefinitely. Employees who started in 2024 go to 27% at the current norm from 2027. Employees who started from 1-1-2025 go to 27% **and** the new, higher norm from 2027.
- **Assuming partial non-resident status is still available.** It cannot be elected from 1 January 2025. Only pre-2024 users keep it, and only through 2026. **2026 is the final year.** Box 2 and box 3 positions built on that status need reworking for 2027.
- **Relying on secondary summaries for the norms.** business.gov.nl publishes the income standard as **€46,107** and the under-30 standard as **€35,048**. Those are 2024 figures. The Belastingdienst 2026 numbers are **€48,013** and **€36,497**. Any tool built off business.gov.nl alone is currently wrong by about €1,900. The announced 2027 norms are likewise stated in 2024 terms and will be indexed.
- **Locking in the wrong ETK route.** The actual-cost versus expatregeling choice binds for the whole year.

## Worked example

Two employees, both with a valid beschikking running for the whole of 2026, both entered after 1 January 2025 (so 30% applies for 2026).

**Employee A, salary including the allowance of €100,000.** Tax-free allowance = 30% of €100,000 = €30,000. Taxable salary = €70,000, which exceeds €48,013, so the expertise requirement is met. The cap does not bite. Payroll runs €30,000 tax-free and €70,000 taxable.

**Employee B, salary including the allowance of €300,000.** Unrestricted, 30% would be €90,000. The cap applies: the maximum tax-free allowance for a full year 2026 is €78,600, reached at €262,000 or higher. So the allowance is €78,600 and taxable salary is €221,400, comfortably above the norm.

**The break-even worth knowing** (derived from the sourced figures, not itself a published figure): on a straight 30/70 split, taxable salary is 70% of the total, so the total package must exceed €48,013 ÷ 0.7, roughly **€68,590**, before the full 30% can be taken without breaching the norm. Below that, reduce the allowance so the taxable remainder still clears €48,013, rather than letting the year fail.
