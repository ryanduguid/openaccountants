# Saint-Martin Tax System Orientation

The Collectivité de Saint-Martin administers a territorial tax system for the French part of the island. Determine fiscal domicile and source first, especially for a move from a French department or business on both sides of the island. [Organic statute, art LO6314-4][CGCT] · [Territorial tax code][CGI]

## Residence and source

Under articles 4 A and 4 B, individuals domiciled in Saint-Martin are generally subject to local income tax on worldwide income. For individuals domiciled elsewhere, local tax generally applies to Saint-Martin-source income. The local domicile tests consider the household or principal place of stay, a non-incidental professional activity or the centre of economic interests. [Territorial tax code, arts 4 A, 4 B and 164 B][CGI]

Article LO6314-4 imposes a five-year residence condition on individuals who were fiscally domiciled in a mainland or overseas French department within the five years before settling in Saint-Martin. An entity with that prior French domicile generally needs five years of effective management in Saint-Martin, or effective management in Saint-Martin and direct or indirect control by individuals who have resided there for at least five years. French-department domicile and Saint-Martin-source taxation can coexist. Check which returns and relief apply under the France–Saint-Martin convention. [Organic statute, art LO6314-4][CGCT] · [France–Saint-Martin convention][CONVENTION] · [Territorial business tax booklet][BOOK]

## Taxpayer type

Individuals generally report taxable income by category in an annual household return. Article 206 identifies companies and other entities subject to local corporate income tax. Article 209 generally includes profits of enterprises operated in Saint-Martin, the specified local-source amounts under article 164 B and profits assigned to Saint-Martin by an applicable convention. Identify the business form and the applicable tax regime before choosing the return. [Territorial tax code, arts 6, 206 and 209][CGI]

## Currency and assessment periods

The official currency is the euro (EUR). Individuals report calendar-year income. The territorial filing notice for 2025 income specified 31 May 2026 and the local code permits the Executive Council to extend the normal 31 May deadline. For corporate income tax, article 223 generally requires a return within three months after the accounting close, with a separate timetable for a 31 December close or a year without a close. Confirm the current deadline and follow the local Centre des Finances Publiques instructions for professional declarations. [IEDOM, currency][CURRENCY] · [2025 income campaign notice][FILING] · [Territorial tax code, arts 12, 175 and 223][CGI] · [Tax authority, professional filing][AUTH]

## Headline taxes

- **Individual income tax.** For 2025 income, the ordinary resident scale applies by household quotient share. The first EUR 7,381 per share bears no tax under the scale. Marginal rates then run from 5.50% to 41%. Article 197 also provides a 40% reduction capped at EUR 6,700 for taxpayers domiciled in Saint-Martin. Check the other household and non-resident rules and the scale for the year being assessed. [Territorial tax code, arts 197 and 197 A][CGI] · [Rate deliberation CT 36-02-2025, art II][RATES]
- **Corporate income tax.** The ordinary rate is 20% for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 September 2014. Check any special rate, relief or separate capital-gain treatment. [Territorial tax code, art 219][CGI]
- **Turnover tax.** TGCA generally applies to taxable supplies of goods and services for consideration in Saint-Martin. The ordinary rate is 4%, with exceptions including a conditional 2% rate under article 259. Article 263 generally requires filing and payment by the 15th of the month following the period. The period is monthly, with a calendar-quarter option when annual tax payable is below EUR 2,000. Check the transaction's territoriality, exclusions and exemptions. [Territorial tax code, arts 250–254, 259 and 263][CGI]
