# Ghana Tax Overview

## Scope and source periods

Start with the taxpayer, residence, income category, source, activity, accounting basis period and transaction date. This overview reconciles selected original Income Tax Act, 2015 (Act 896) provisions, selected Income Tax (Amendment) Act, 2023 (Act 1094) provisions and GRA guidance captured on 11 November 2026. It remains Tier 2 and pending qualified review. It does not certify the complete amendment chain, exact commencement of every amendment or a complete current-year assessment.

The GRA announcement linked under Act 1178 is an implementation notice, not the original amending Act. Its September 2026 individual-tax position must not be taken as proof of every retained or replaced First Schedule provision. Original Act 1094 records assent and Gazette notification on 3 April 2023; those dates alone do not settle statutory commencement. [GRA 2026 announcement](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Amendments-To-The-Income-Tax-2015-Act-896_Updated-1.pdf); [original Act 1094](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf).

## Tax system and assessment periods

The Ghana Revenue Authority publishes the tax guidance cited here. Original Act 896 sections 2, 3, 5 and 6 distinguish chargeable income, assessable income and employment, business and investment sources. Resident income is not subject to the non-resident Ghana-source limitation. For a non-resident, section 3(2)(b) also includes income connected with a Ghanaian permanent establishment irrespective of source. Do not assume that foreign-source receipts are always outside Ghana's tax base, or that every foreign receipt is PE-connected. Residence, attribution, exemptions and treaty relief need separate checks. [Act 896, ss 2, 3, 5 and 6](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).

Original section 18 distinguishes the calendar year of assessment from a company's accounting-year basis period. An approved accounting-year change affects the period and payment calculations. The terms are not interchangeable. [Act 896, s 18, PDF p 21](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf); [corporate-income guide](gh-corporate-income-tax.md).

| Component | Selected position and period | Limits and source |
|---|---|---|

[GRA PAYE](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/paye/); [individual-income guide](ghana-pit.md) |

## Income-tax returns and payment clocks

Original section 124(1), subject to section 125, states an income-tax return deadline of **4 months after the end of each year of assessment**. It does not literally say 'accounting year'. Establish the assessment/basis-period mapping and current prescribed process before calculating a non-calendar company's exact deadline. Original section 125 contains exceptions for specified resident individuals and non-residents, preserves the Commissioner-General's power to require a return by written notice and permits an election to file. Do not infer that every person must file an annual return solely from the 4-month rule. [Act 896, ss 124 and 125, PDF pp 97 to 98](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).

Original section 121(2)(a) uses the last days of months **3, 6, 9 and 12** for its specified 12-month basis period beginning at the start of a calendar month; other cases have a separate rule. Filing, instalment payment, withholding remittance and transaction returns are separate obligations. Current notices, later administration amendments, forms and deadline adjustments remain to be checked. [Act 896, ss 121 and 122](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf); [corporate-income guide](gh-corporate-income-tax.md).

## Asset gains and realisation

Original sections 5 and 6 bring the specified business and investment gains into their respective income calculations. Section 35 measures an asset gain by consideration exceeding tax cost at realisation. Sections 36 and 37 govern cost and consideration. Qualifying acquisition, improvement and incidental costs have conditions. Tax cost excludes consumption and excluded expenditure, amounts directly deducted from income and amounts already included in another asset's cost. Accounting profit or sale proceeds alone do not establish the taxable gain. [Act 896, ss 5, 6 and 35 to 37, PDF pp 12 to 13 and 32 to 33](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).

Original section 38 covers more than a cash sale, including the stated transfers, changes of use and deemed-realisation cases. Reliefs and special transfer rules require their own conditions. Do not assume that a gift, death, reorganisation or replacement asset automatically produces either tax or exemption. The complete transfer/rollover, valuation, ownership-change and source rules remain open. [Act 896, s 38 and Part IV](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).

### Individual election and the GRA summary conflict

Act 1094 section 13(a)'s replacement First Schedule paragraph 1(3) allows an **individual** to elect a **25%** rate on the stated investment-asset gain less the specified investment-asset loss not charged elsewhere. It is not a universal tax on every property sale, every company gain or gross proceeds. Paragraph 1(5) addresses the remainder of chargeable income. Later amendments, election procedures and exact operative periods require confirmation. [Act 1094, s 13(a), PDF pp 8 to 9](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf).

GRA's capital-gains webpage captured on 11 November 2026 still describes a 15% individual option/isolated-transaction rate. That summary does not establish the operative election after the reviewed 2023 amendment. Use the identified statutory text and resolve later changes before selecting a rate. [GRA capital-gains page](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/capital-gains-tax/); [Act 1094](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf).

### Transaction return and withholding

Act 1094 section 5 inserts section 39A, requiring a person realising an asset or liability to submit the prescribed return within **30 days after realisation**. This is a transaction-return provision; it does not by itself establish every related payment deadline or remove annual-return obligations. [Act 1094, s 5, PDF p 4](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf).

Sections 8 and 9 respectively replace section 115(1) and insert section 116A. Section 115(1) concerns a resident payer, the stated payments and Ghana-source condition, subject to subsection (2). Section 116A covers a resident payer **other than an individual**, paying consideration for an asset/liability realisation outside section 115. It requires withholding on the gross payment. Section 13(e) and (f) add **3% of consideration received** for realisation by a resident person and **10%** for a non-resident person to the specified First Schedule payment categories. The payer and recipient tests, exceptions, source and finality must be checked together; the percentages are not rates on the computed gain. Section 13(g) also adds a payment category for section 116A, whose interaction must be verified before applying that residual rule. [Act 1094, ss 8, 9 and 13(e) to (g), PDF pp 5 to 6 and 10 to 11](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf).

## Rent, gifts and property charges

GRA's rent-income page, captured on 11 November 2026, gives **8% for residential** and **15% for commercial/non-residential** premises and states payment within **30 days after receipt**. Original First Schedule paragraph 8 contains 8%/15% rental-withholding rows. These summaries do not establish a final all-landlord tax regime. Original section 119(1)(b) and (c) has recipient/activity conditions and business-related exclusions for final rental withholding; non-resident finality also has a Ghanaian-PE exception. Identify the taxpayer and letting activity before treating a deduction as final or creditable. [GRA rent guidance](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/rent-income-tax/); [Act 896, s 119 and First Schedule, PDF pp 93 and 119](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf).

GRA's income-withholding guidance separately requires the prescribed statement and tax withheld within **15 days after month end**. That withholding-agent clock differs from the rent page's receipt-based summary. Determine the actual obligation and operative law rather than choosing a deadline from one webpage. This batch does not settle every landlord's current calendar. [GRA withholding guidance](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/withholding-tax/).

For gifts, Act 1094 section 13(a)'s paragraph 1(4) provides an individual's **25% election** for a gift outside business or employment. It is not proof that all gifts are taxable or that every recipient qualifies. The GRA domestic gift page gives 15%/25% resident/non-resident figures, whilst its portfolio page gives 25%/30%; both were captured on 11 November 2026. Their different relationship/exemption summaries also need reconciliation. Verify the income category, donor relationship, succession/transfer rules, exemption, valuation, source and operative election before assessment. No complete gift or inheritance regime is certified. [Act 1094, s 13(a), PDF p 9](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf); [GRA domestic gift page](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/gift-tax/); [GRA portfolio gift page](https://gra.gov.gh/portfolio/gift-tax/).

GRA describes stamp duty as an instrument-based charge and publishes generic rate ranges. Its implementation notice says Act 1109 increased stamp duties, with implementation from **1 January 2024**. The original amended schedule has not been verified here. Identify the instrument and obtain the applicable schedule, valuation and relief conditions; do not assess a conveyance from a generic webpage range. [GRA stamp duty](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/stamp-duty/); [GRA implementation notice](https://gra.gov.gh/implementation-of-new-tax-laws-and-amendments/).

GRA's property-platform notice describes an MMDA property-rate collection partnership from **1 January 2023**. It does not establish the collector, rateable value, local rate, exemptions or payment channel applicable to every property in 2026. Obtain the relevant assembly's operative resolution/valuation and current administration evidence. Municipal property rates, rental income tax, disposal income tax, stamp duty and VAT are separate checks. [Dated GRA property-platform notice](https://gra.gov.gh/commencement-of-implementation-of-unified-common-property-rate-platform-in-conjunction-with-the-metropolitan-municipal-and-district-assemblies-mmdas/).

## Worked comparisons

These fictional checks illustrate identified source rules only. They do not establish eligibility, an operative current rate, final liability or available credits.

1. **Gain and conditional election:** Assume GHS 200,000 qualifying consideration, GHS 120,000 tax cost and no relevant loss or adjustment. The gain is GHS 80,000; if the identified 25% individual investment-gain election applies, tax on it is GHS 20,000. [Act 896, ss 35 to 37](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/INCOME-TAX-ACT-2015-ACT-896.pdf); [Act 1094, s 13(a)](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf).
2. **Different withholding base:** On GHS 200,000 consideration, an assumed applicable resident-realisation withholding rule at 3% produces GHS 6,000. It is not 3% of the GHS 80,000 gain. Payer scope, statutory category, exceptions, finality and any credit must be established separately; this comparison does not net the two tax figures. [Act 1094, ss 8, 9 and 13](https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/INCOME-TAX-AMENDMENT-ACT-2023-ACT-1094.pdf).
3. **Standard-rate supply comparison:** On GHS 1,000 supply value, GRA's stated January 2026 same-base rates give VAT GHS 150, NHIL GHS 25 and GETFund GHS 25, totalling GHS 200. Classification and input deduction are separate questions. [GRA VAT](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/vat/).
