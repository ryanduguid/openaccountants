# Ghana Company Formation & Entity Choice

## Scope and periods

Identify the entity, activity, ownership and filing period before applying a formation or reporting rule. This guide covers selected Companies Act, 2019 (Act 992) provisions and ORC guidance checked on 11 November 2026. It also records selected provisions of the Ghana Investment Promotion Authority Act, 2026 (Act 1173), whose original records assent and Gazette notification on 15 July 2026. Those dates do not by themselves establish the legal commencement rule. The exact operative date, complete amendment chain and treatment of a particular existing investment remain to be confirmed. All sections remain Tier 2 and pending qualified review.

The former approximate general capital, stamp-duty and processing-time figures have not been verified against operative primary evidence. Do not use them as established minimums, charges or service promises. Private-company forms, fees and any sector capital rules remain outstanding.

## Company types and incorporation

Act 992 section 6 allows one or more persons to form an incorporated company. Section 7 distinguishes companies limited by shares, companies limited by guarantee, unlimited companies and external companies. A private company other than a company limited by guarantee restricts share transfers, limits members and debenture holders to 50 subject to the employee/former-employee exceptions and prohibits the specified public invitations. A company limited by guarantee may make profits only to further its objects under section 8. Banking, insurance and other specially regulated businesses retain their separate legislation under section 4. Sole proprietorship and partnership registration require their own legal routes; they are not additional company types in this section. [Act 992, ss 4 and 6 to 8](https://www.gipc.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/COMPANIES-ACT-2019-ACT-992.pdf).

For an incorporation under the original Act 992 text:

1. Choose the company type and a compliant name. Section 21 specifies the name endings, including Limited Company/LTD for a private company limited by shares and Public Limited Company/PLC for a public company limited by shares.
2. Complete the prescribed application. Section 13 requires the company type, address, subscriber and officer particulars, proposed director declarations and consents, secretary and auditor particulars, beneficial ownership information and the proposed stated capital/share information where applicable. The application may be delivered with or without a proposed constitution under section 13(5). Confirm the current form and attachments with ORC.
3. Provide the required directors. Section 171 requires at least 2 for a company incorporated after the Act's commencement, including one ordinarily resident in Ghana. Section 182 requires at least one resident director at all times. Residence is not a Ghanaian-citizenship requirement.
4. Pay the prescribed fee. Where the Registrar is satisfied that the application complies, section 14 provides for certification of incorporation; section 15 makes that certificate conclusive evidence of incorporation. The amount of a current private-company fee is not verified here.

Sources: [Act 992, ss 13 to 15, 21, 171 and 182](https://www.gipc.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/COMPANIES-ACT-2019-ACT-992.pdf), original 2019 text checked on 11 November 2026. ORC's currently linked incorporation page describes a public-company procedure and does not establish the private-company forms or fees: [ORC incorporation page](https://orc.gov.gh/service-incorporation-of-company-limited-by-shares.php).

## Investment capital: Act 1173 as gazetted

Keep the following statutory text separate from the unresolved commencement and transition checks. The Act's page 4 records assent and page 43 Gazette notification, both on 15 July 2026. Its section 1 also preserves compliance with other enactments. [Act 1173, s 1 and original date records](https://brr.gov.gh/acc/registry/docs/GIPA-ACT-2026.pdf).

| Provision | Text checked against the original | Application limit |
|---|---|---|
| Section 31(1), p 18 | A non-citizen may engage in a trading enterprise if that person invests at least US$500,000 in cash as equity capital and at least 75% of the enterprise's employees are skilled Ghanaians | Check activity, ownership, operative period and other legislation before applying it |
| Section 31(2) and (3), p 18 | The stated citizenship-loss circumstance and portfolio investment are excepted from the minimum capital requirement | These provisions do not expressly exempt the employment condition |
| Section 35(3), p 21 | A wholly Ghanaian-owned enterprise with a non-Ghanaian beneficial owner or director must satisfy section 31's minimum capital requirement | The text has no express trading limitation; its application to a non-trading enterprise needs clarification |
| Definition of trading enterprise, p 41 | Purchase and resale can be a principal activity or a material component of operations, with imported or locally produced goods in the same or substantially the same condition | Do not limit the test to enterprises whose sole activity is trading |

Primary text: [Act 1173, ss 31, 35(3) and 59](https://brr.gov.gh/acc/registry/docs/GIPA-ACT-2026.pdf). Section 31(2) concerns a Ghanaian citizen who lost citizenship by assuming citizenship of a country that does not allow dual citizenship. Do not extend it to every former citizen.

The Authority's [minimum-equity webpage](https://gipc.gov.gh/minimum-equity-requirements/), checked on 11 November 2026, says the general minimum applies only to trading enterprises. That summary does not resolve section 35(3)'s broader wording. A Ghanaian share register alone is insufficient to close the capital question where a beneficial owner or director is not Ghanaian. Do not infer that every non-trading investment is free of all capital requirements, including those under sector legislation.

## Existing investment registrations and savings

Section 60 repeals Act 865 and preserves specified matters. Existing registrations continue as registrations under Act 1173, subject to the savings terms; pending applications are treated as pending before the new Authority. Existing benefits and incentives continue alongside applicable new benefits. Existing expatriate quotas or work permits continue until expiry or renewal. It also saves L.I. 1547 and existing registered technology-transfer agreements. These rules do not establish that every previous capital threshold continues indefinitely. Verify the original registration, relevant benefit, application date and operative transition for the particular case. [Act 1173, s 60, pp 41 to 42](https://brr.gov.gh/acc/registry/docs/GIPA-ACT-2026.pdf).

## Accounting records and reports

Act 992 section 127 requires proper records of receipts, payments, sales, purchases, assets, liabilities and members' interests. Records must be kept at the registered office or another place in Ghana selected by the directors and be available to directors, the secretary and auditors. Its financial-statement requirements include a statement of financial position, comprehensive income, cash flows, changes in equity and accounting policies/notes. Standards must be those adopted or approved by the Institute of Chartered Accountants, Ghana; do not assume a single framework fits every entity. [Act 992, s 127](https://www.gipc.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/COMPANIES-ACT-2019-ACT-992.pdf).

Under section 128, directors must prepare and send the specified financial statements, directors' report and auditors' report to members and debenture holders within 18 months after incorporation, then at least once each calendar year at intervals no longer than 15 months. The section contains recipient exceptions and Registrar extension/waiver powers. First statements cover the period since incorporation under section 129, subject to its transitional provision. This dispatch timetable is distinct from the annual-return filing trigger and from any temporary ORC extension. [Act 992, ss 128 and 129](https://www.gipc.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/COMPANIES-ACT-2019-ACT-992.pdf).

## Annual returns and audit relief

Section 126 requires a return at least annually containing the prescribed member and beneficial-owner information. Its initial-year exceptions cover the year of incorporation and a year ending less than 18 months after incorporation, subject to the return following the first section 128 dispatch. The return must be completed within 36 days after the specified reports are sent and signed by at least one director and the company secretary. Section 298 sets private-company attachments and alternatives; do not assume every company has the same attachment package. [Act 992, ss 126 and 298](https://www.gipc.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/COMPANIES-ACT-2019-ACT-992.pdf).

ORC's notice dated 28 April 2026 extended the stated filing deadline from 30 April to 30 June 2026 **only for companies whose financial year ends on 31 December**. It is not a universal recurring statutory deadline. The notice also reports Directive No. 5 of 22 January 2025, providing eligible small companies a simplified auditor's review report in place of a full audit. Confirm classification, eligibility and required report before claiming relief. The directive's original text and operative conditions have not been verified here; the indexed PDF now returns 404. Section 382 authorises directives/guidelines but does not itself establish eligibility. [ORC 28 April 2026 notice](https://orc.gov.gh/single-blog.php?slug=extension-of-deadline-for-annual-returns); [Act 992, s 382](https://www.gipc.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/COMPANIES-ACT-2019-ACT-992.pdf).

## Tax registration and separate filings

ORC company returns and GRA tax returns are separate obligations. GRA's corporate-income-tax page, checked on 11 November 2026, requires taxpayer registration, annual tax returns, an estimated self-assessment within the first quarter and 4 quarterly instalments. Its calendar dates must not be assumed to resolve every non-calendar basis period. Full annual tax-return timing, payroll, VAT and withholding compliance require separate period-specific checks. [GRA corporate income tax](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/corporate-income-tax/).

## Worked scope checks

- **Resident director:** A Ghanaian citizen living abroad does not establish the residence requirement merely by citizenship. Check the ordinary-residence requirement and the ongoing resident-director requirement separately. [Act 992, ss 171 and 182](https://www.gipc.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/COMPANIES-ACT-2019-ACT-992.pdf).
- **Trading capital and employees:** As a comparison with section 31's gazetted text, US$500,000 satisfies the numerical cash-equity minimum; with 100 employees, 75 skilled Ghanaians meet the numerical 75% condition and 74 do not. This arithmetic does not establish the operative date, evidence of paid equity, sector approvals or overall investment eligibility. [Act 1173, s 31](https://brr.gov.gh/acc/registry/docs/GIPA-ACT-2026.pdf).
- **Ghanaian ownership:** A wholly Ghanaian-owned enterprise with a non-Ghanaian director still encounters section 35(3). Check its application before relying on the provider's only-trading summary. [Act 1173, s 35(3)](https://brr.gov.gh/acc/registry/docs/GIPA-ACT-2026.pdf).
- **Small company:** A small-company label alone does not establish audit relief. Verify the directive's classification and conditions and obtain the required auditor review report. The absence of the original from the live link does not establish that no relief exists. [ORC 28 April 2026 notice](https://orc.gov.gh/single-blog.php?slug=extension-of-deadline-for-annual-returns).
