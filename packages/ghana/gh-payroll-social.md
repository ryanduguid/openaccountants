# Ghana Payroll & Social Contributions

Selected ordinary Act 766 scheme components below were checked against SSNIT's provider FAQ on 11 November 2026. The FAQ is undated and is not a consolidated statutory text. Scheme-specific eligibility, legacy transitions, later amendments and qualified review remain open. The separate earnings notice expressly applies from 1 January 2026.

## Social security (SSNIT) contributions

- **Overview of SSNIT mandatory pension scheme tiers** — Ghana's mandatory pension scheme has a first (basic) tier managed by SSNIT and a second (occupational) tier, totalling 18.5% of basic salary split between employer and employee. A third, voluntary tier also exists.
- **Total mandatory SSNIT contribution (Tier 1 + Tier 2)** — 18.5% of employee basic salary (13% employer + 5.5% employee)  _(National Pensions Act, 2008 (Act 766) — https://www.ssnit.org.gh/faqs/)_
- **Employer contribution (of basic salary)** — 13%  _(National Pensions Act, 2008 (Act 766) — https://www.ssnit.org.gh/faqs/)_
- **Employee contribution (of basic salary)** — 5.5%  _(National Pensions Act, 2008 (Act 766) — https://www.ssnit.org.gh/faqs/)_
- **Tier 1 (basic, to SSNIT)** — 13.5% of basic salary, subject to SSNIT's insurable earnings limits. This allocates 13.5 percentage points of the total 18.5% contribution to Tier 1. _(SSNIT, FAQ, “What are the contribution rates and how are they distributed between the Employer and Employee?”, checked 11 November 2026 — https://www.ssnit.org.gh/faqs/)_
- **Tier 2 (occupational, to private scheme)** — 5% of basic salary is remitted to an approved Tier 2 occupational scheme, allocating the remaining 5 percentage points. The SSNIT earnings notice does not establish a Tier 2 cap; verify the applicable Tier 2 base separately above SSNIT's ceiling. _(SSNIT, FAQ, “What are the contribution rates and how are they distributed between the Employer and Employee?”, checked 11 November 2026 — https://www.ssnit.org.gh/faqs/)_
- **Insurable monthly earnings and published SSNIT contributions (Tier 1)** — From 1 January 2026, maximum earnings are GHS 69,000 (raised from GHS 61,000) and minimum earnings GHS 587.80. The notice publishes maximum and minimum monthly contributions payable to SSNIT of GHS 9,315.00 and GHS 79.40. It does not explain its rounding method: 13.5% of GHS 587.80 is GHS 79.353. Confirm the operative rounding instruction before calculating minimum contributions  _(SSNIT, Public Notice "Maximum and Minimum Insurable Earnings for 2026", under s 63(3) of the National Pensions Act, 2008 (Act 766) — https://www.ssnit.org.gh/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Public-Notice-Min-Max-Insurable.pdf)_
- **Tier 3 (voluntary)** — Voluntary provident fund/personal pension; contributions up to 16.5% of basic salary, paid by the employer, the employee or both, are deducted before PAYE is calculated  _(GRA, Pay As You Earn (PAYE) — https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/paye/ ; National Pensions Act, 2008 (Act 766) — https://www.npra.gov.gh/faq/)_

## PAYE withholding and remittance

- **Overview of PAYE and SSNIT remittance obligations** — Employers operate Pay As You Earn on employee emoluments and remit PAYE and SSNIT contributions to the GRA and SSNIT respectively on set monthly deadlines.
- **PAYE withholding** — Employer deducts income tax from employee emoluments each month using the graduated monthly bands; from 1 September 2026 the first GHS 588 a month is tax-free and the top 35% rate applies above GHS 50,000 a month (Income Tax (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act 1178))  _(GRA, Pay As You Earn (PAYE) — https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/paye/)_
- **PAYE return filing deadline** — Monthly PAYE returns are filed by the employer on or before the 15th day of the month following the month of deduction  _(GRA, Pay As You Earn (PAYE) — https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/paye/)_
- **SSNIT remittance deadline** — The provider FAQ requires the 13.5% SSNIT remittance within 14 days following the end of the month. Current statutory instructions and the separate Tier 2 payment deadline remain to be checked. _(SSNIT, FAQ, checked 11 November 2026 — https://www.ssnit.org.gh/faqs/)_

## Contribution allocation example and remaining work

Assume the ordinary Act 766 scheme applies to monthly basic salary of GHS 10,000, with no special adjustment and earnings within the SSNIT limits. Employer financing is GHS 1,300 and employee financing GHS 550, totalling GHS 1,850. Allocation is GHS 1,350 to SSNIT and GHS 500 to Tier 2. This uses the basic-salary percentages reported in the SSNIT FAQ and is not a PAYE, net-pay or complete payroll calculation. It does not extrapolate the SSNIT ceiling to Tier 2.

The consolidated law, scheme-specific coverage and legacy transitions, Tier 2 rules and deadline, above-ceiling treatment, rounding instructions, other employer obligations, full PAYE payment rules, later law and qualified review remain open. NPRA's undated FAQ discusses historical transition options; it does not establish a blanket current rule based only on age. _(NPRA, FAQ, checked 11 November 2026 — https://www.npra.gov.gh/faq/)_
