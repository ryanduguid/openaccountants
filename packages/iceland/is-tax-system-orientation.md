# Iceland tax-system orientation

Load `workflow-base`. Record where the person has their domicile or the entity is established, the source of income, the legal form, the income period and any treaty.

## Territorial reach

An individual within Article 1 and an Iceland-resident entity covered by Article 2 generally come within income tax on worldwide income. Limited liability under Article 3 covers specified Icelandic connections, including qualifying work, services, permanent establishment income and real estate income or gains. For Article 3, the territorial definition includes Iceland's territorial waters, exclusive economic zone and continental shelf, as well as areas where Iceland has taxing rights under law or a specific agreement. Check the applicable treaty and any special charging law. [Income Tax Act 90/2003, arts 1–3 a](https://www.althingi.is/lagas/nuna/2003090.html)

## Residence

An individual generally comes within full liability through Icelandic domicile or a stay of more than 183 days in any 12 months, including normal absences for holidays and similar reasons. Article 1 also covers qualifying work aboard an Iceland-registered ship or aircraft. After a person leaves and ends their Icelandic domicile, check the continuation of full liability for 3 years from the next New Year and the exception for a person who proves comparable resident tax liability and compliance abroad. A covered entity has domestic residence if it is registered in Iceland, is domiciled there under its statutes or is effectively managed there. Apply treaty residence rules separately. [Income Tax Act 90/2003, arts 1–2](https://www.althingi.is/lagas/nuna/2003090.html)

## Taxpayer types

A sole trader accounts for business income under the individual rules. A registered hf. or ehf. is a separate corporate taxpayer. Check whether a qualifying general partnership, limited partnership or partnership limited by shares requested separate taxpayer status at registration and meets the applicable conditions. When the partners are taxed instead, allocate income and assets by agreement, by ownership share if the agreement has no allocation and equally if ownership shares are absent or unclear. For a foreign trust, record the deed, control and economic interests. Check whether the low-tax ownership or control rules in Article 57 a apply on those facts. Skatturinn's RSK 17.31 instructions separately address the disclosure of trusts that directly or indirectly own an entity subject to the Land Act reporting obligation. [Income Tax Act 90/2003, arts 1–2, 57 a and 61](https://www.althingi.is/lagas/nuna/2003090.html) · [Skatturinn, RSK 17.31 instructions](https://www.skatturinn.is/media/rsk17/rsk_1731.en.pdf)

## Currency

Iceland uses the Icelandic króna (ISK). Annual accounts generally state amounts in ISK. An eligible company can obtain permission to keep books and prepare its accounts in an approved foreign functional currency. Article 91 requires the applicable return statement in ISK and sets conversion rules for a legal entity using foreign-currency accounts. Check the prescribed rates and any eligible alternative based on parallel ISK books. [Annual Accounts Act 3/2006, arts 7–9](https://www.althingi.is/lagas/nuna/2006003.html) · [Income Tax Act 90/2003, art 91](https://www.althingi.is/lagas/nuna/2003090.html)

## Assessment periods

Income tax generally uses income from the calendar year before assessment. Article 59 provides for the Director of Internal Revenue to authorise another accounting year where the industry ordinarily uses it or the taxpayer shows in the return that it has another accounting year. Record the operative income period and assessment year separately. [Income Tax Act 90/2003, arts 59 and 93](https://www.althingi.is/lagas/nuna/2003090.html)

## Responsible authorities

Skatturinn is Iceland Revenue and Customs. It handles ordinary income tax returns and assessments. Municipal income tax (útsvar) is a separate component of individual taxation. Check the municipality and how its charge is assessed with state income tax. [Skatturinn, Iceland Revenue and Customs](https://www.skatturinn.is/english/) · [Income Tax Act 90/2003, arts 90 and 93–95](https://www.althingi.is/lagas/nuna/2003090.html) · [Municipal Revenue Act 4/1995, ch IV](https://www.althingi.is/lagas/nuna/1995004.html)

## Fictional intake

A person domiciled outside Iceland takes up work there and operates through a general partnership. Count their days, identify any Icelandic-source income and check the partnership registration and agreement before allocating its result. Record the income year, the assessment year and any treaty.
