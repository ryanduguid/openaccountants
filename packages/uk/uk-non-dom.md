# UK Non Dom

## Section 1: Scope and effective date

The remittance basis ended for new income and gains from 6 April 2025. Earlier
unremitted amounts can still be taxed when remitted. Finance Act 2025, Part 2,
introduces separate claims for qualifying foreign income, foreign gains and
foreign employment income. Relief is not automatic and is not a general 0% tax
rate on every receipt from abroad.

This guide covers those enacted changes and the main transitional provisions.
Trust attribution, mixed funds, inheritance tax and later amendments need a
separate review before a computation is finalised.

## Section 2: Four-year foreign income and gains relief

Under ITTOIA 2005 section 845B, the first qualifying year follows ten consecutive
tax years of non-UK residence. The window includes that year and the next three
tax years, subject to UK residence in the claim year and statutory exclusions.
An intervening non-resident year does not extend the window. A first qualifying
year in 2022–23, 2023–24 or 2024–25 can leave part of the window available from
2025–26; arrival on or after 6 April 2025 is not required.

- Claim foreign income relief in the Self Assessment return under section 845A,
  identifying the qualifying income. Section 845H lists eligible categories,
  including qualifying foreign interest, dividends, property and trading income.
- Claim qualifying foreign gains under TCGA 1992 Schedule D1. Its asset definition
  excludes assets deriving at least 75% of their value from UK land. Pre-2025–26
  gains subject to the remittance basis do not become eligible FIG gains.
- The ordinary claim deadline is twelve months from 31 January following the
  tax year. For 2025–26 this is 31 January 2028. The return itself has its own
  filing deadline. Check the statutory restrictions on consequential claims.
- A foreign income claim, foreign gain claim or foreign employment election
  removes the personal allowance and CGT annual exempt amount for that year.
  It also restricts relevant foreign losses and other reliefs. Compare the
  consequences before claiming; UK-source income remains subject to normal rules.

Employment income uses the separate rules in Section 4. Foreign employer status
alone does not exempt earnings. After the four-year window, ordinary worldwide
taxation applies while the individual remains UK resident, subject to other
applicable reliefs and treaty provisions.

Sources: Finance Act 2025 sections 37–39; ITTOIA sections 845A–845H; TCGA Schedule
D1 and sections 1K and 16, as inserted or amended by that Act.

## Section 3: Existing unremitted amounts and rebasing

Finance Act 2025 Schedule 9 restricts remittance-basis claims to 2024–25 and earlier
years. It does not erase tax exposure on those years' unremitted income and gains.

The Temporary Repatriation Facility (TRF), in Schedule 10, permits an eligible UK
resident who previously used the remittance basis to designate qualifying
overseas capital in a return for one of three years:

| Designation year | TRF charge |
| --- | --- |
| 2025–26 or 2026–27 | 12% |
| 2027–28 | 15% |

The charge follows the designation year. Schedule 10 paragraphs 10 and 12
preserve the relevant remittance protection in later years for capital validly
designated during the facility. Check eligibility and the designation election.
Undesignated pre-2025 amounts remain subject to the applicable remittance rules.

Schedule 11 uses **5 April 2017** market value for eligible rebasing. Paragraph 1
requires the asset to have been held on that date, disposal on or after 6 April
2025, and no UK location during 6 March 2024 to 5 April 2025, subject to paragraph
2's exceptions. The individual must meet the historical domicile conditions,
including deemed domicile, and have made a qualifying remittance-basis claim
in at least one year from 2017–18 to 2024–25. It does not generally exclude
foreign real estate. Paragraph 3 allows an irrevocable election out for a disposal.

## Section 4: Overseas Workday Relief

Finance Act 2025 section 38 inserts ITEPA 2003 sections 41M–41V. A qualifying
individual needs a foreign employment election and a relief claim identifying
eligible foreign employment income. Ordinary relief is capped at the lower of
30% of relevant qualifying employment income and GBP 300,000 under section 41R.
Deferred earnings and relief claimed in later years require the statutory
allocation and cumulative-limit calculation.

Keep evidence of duties performed abroad and the earnings attributable to them.
A UK-workdays fraction alone does not calculate the final taxable amount once
the cap and other adjustments apply. The new relief has no requirement to keep
the relieved earnings offshore.

Schedule 8 Part 3 contains transitional rules for earlier arrivals, including
cases where the ordinary cap does not apply. Check paragraphs 5–8 before applying
the new-resident test or cap to someone who used the earlier OWR rules.

## Section 5: Historical remittance-basis charges

Before abolition, the remittance basis could involve a GBP 30,000 annual charge
after residence in seven of the preceding nine tax years, or GBP 60,000 after
twelve of the preceding fourteen. Deemed-domicile and other eligibility rules
could prevent a claim. Use the legislation for the historical tax year when
reviewing an earlier return. See [HMRC RDRM32330](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/residence-domicile-and-remittance-basis/rdrm32330).
