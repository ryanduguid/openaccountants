# Saint Kitts and Nevis income tax, payroll and Social Security

The [country reference](references.md) covers VAT, customs, property and company formation, with separate rules and evidence limits for each obligation.

> **General reference only.** This Tier 2 draft describes the dated tax and payroll rules below and compares selected Social Security source profiles. A qualified local reviewer must resolve the outstanding conditions before any actual deduction, recovery, payment or filing.

## Income tax scope and business obligations

For income accruing on or after **1 May 1980**, section 3(3) excludes persons other than companies from the general charge in section 3. This makes ordinary personal income tax inapplicable under that section. Read the old rates and allowances with the exclusion. The old employer withholding provisions in sections 68–71 concern emoluments assessable to income tax. For ordinary wages within the section 3(3) exclusion, those provisions supply no withholding amount or tax code. [Income Tax Act, Cap. 20.22, 2017 revision, ss 3 and 68–71](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Revised-Acts-of-St-Kitts-and-Nevis/Revised-Acts-of-St-Kitts-and-Nevis-2017/Ch-20_22-Income-Tax-Act.pdf) · [Inland Revenue, Corporate Income Tax](https://www.sknird.com/corporate-income-tax/)

Check unincorporated business tax, payroll levies and non-resident payments separately. The 2017 revision also retains section 37 for certain deductible payments to residents, including rent, royalties and specified interest. Confirm its current application, any rate rules and interaction with section 3(3) before treating a payment as subject to or free of withholding. The historical section 37 wording supplies no ordinary wage withholding rule. [Income Tax Act, ss 3, 36–37 and 68–71](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Act17TOC/Ch-20_22-Income-Tax-Act.pdf)

Act 12 of 2021, published 26 March 2021, inserted section 3A. A resident company's assessable income includes income wherever it arises. A non-resident company carrying on business through a local permanent establishment is assessed on income attributable to that establishment. Apply the Act's establishment tests and exceptions, and verify residence, deductions, loss relief and any special regime for the period. Inland Revenue's filing page uses broader language for non-resident filers. Confirm the return obligation for any non-company case. [Income Tax (Amendment) Act 12 of 2021, s 3](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Annual-Laws/2021/ACTs/Act-12-of-2021-Income-Tax-Amendment-Act-2021.pdf) · [Inland Revenue, Corporate Income Tax](https://www.sknird.com/corporate-income-tax/)

Section 32(1)(b) in the 2017 revision sets **33%** for income earned in 2013 and assessed in 2014 and for later years. Inland Revenue's published instalment FAQ uses 33%. The 2024 budget announcement proposed **25%** for registered corporations from 1 January 2024, and the government's economic summary describes that rate as established. The 2021 Inland Revenue advisory documented a separate conditional pandemic reduction for April 2020 through June 2021. The later rate instrument and its period, coverage and conditions remain unconfirmed here. Resolve the difference before selecting a rate for a live return. [Income Tax Act, s 32](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Act17TOC/Ch-20_22-Income-Tax-Act.pdf) · [Inland Revenue FAQs](https://www.sknird.com/faqs/) · [2024 budget announcement](https://www.sknis.gov.kn/2023/12/13/corporate-income-tax-rate-in-st-kitts-and-nevis-set-at-25-from-january-2024/) · [Government economic summary](https://www.gov.kn/your-jobs-economy/) · [Inland Revenue's March 2021 advisory](https://www.sknird.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Tax-Advisory-CIT-Tax-Rate-Reduction-to-25.Amended-March-2021.pdf)

Inland Revenue says locally incorporated businesses file even if inactive or on a tax holiday. It confirmed that companies with a 31 December 2025 year end had to file and pay by **15 April 2026**. For International Companies, Nevis international business corporations and Nevis LLCs, the September 2023 CIT-101 instructions describe a separate information return and keep responsibility with the registered entity even when an agent submits it. The March 2026 portal notice refers to 2025 filings ahead of the 15 April deadline. Check the applicable version, additional tax return and any filing extensions. [Inland Revenue corporate guidance](https://www.sknird.com/corporate-income-tax/) · [2026 filing notices](https://www.sknird.com/announcements-upcoming-events/) · [CIT-101 guidelines, September 2023](https://eservicesskn.sknird.com/ServiceProvider/Documents/CIT-101%20Guidelines%20Revised%20September%202023.pdf)

### Unincorporated Business Tax

The 2017 revision of Cap. 18.46 excludes employment from its business definition and excludes companies registered under the Companies Act and partnerships treated as companies and liable under the Income Tax Act. For other persons carrying on business in the Federation, sections 3–5 charge **4%** on the goods and services bases. The Act includes cash and accrued receipts and excludes properly accounted VAT and client money received in a representative capacity. Include the representative's own commission, fee or mark-up. Use VAT supply timing and value, including the hypothetical supply timing for someone outside VAT registration. Keep goods and services separate. For each calendar month the Act subtracts **EC$12,500** from goods and **EC$2,000** from services, flooring each base at zero. Section 5(5)–(8) permits one election to switch to Income Tax Act computation and treats the resulting tax as corporate tax. Section 6 makes partners jointly and severally liable and provides for a designated responsible partner. [Unincorporated Business Tax Act, Cap. 18.46, ss 2–6](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Act17TOC/Ch-18_46-Unincorporated-Business-Tax-Act.pdf)

The Inland Revenue guide and UBT-01 form instead show quarterly allowances of **EC$37,500** for goods and **EC$6,000** for services. They can produce different results from monthly floors. For example, fictional services of EC$0, EC$0 and EC$9,000 yield EC$280 under the monthly statutory reading and EC$120 under quarterly subtraction before any relief. UBT-01 also retains a COVID relief line. Confirm the accepted method and applicable relief authority for the return period. [Cap. 18.46, s 5(2)](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Act17TOC/Ch-18_46-Unincorporated-Business-Tax-Act.pdf) · [Inland Revenue guide, pp 3–5](https://www.sknird.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/200701012021.pdf) · [UBT-01, p 2](https://www.sknird.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/UBT-Return-Updated-3.0.pdf)

Section 7, as amended in 2014, requires a return and payment by **15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December**. Inland Revenue's guide maps these to December–February, March–May, June–August and September–November, moving a weekend or public holiday deadline to the next business day. Its current calendar lists **15 December 2026** for September–November 2026. The February 2024 launch included UBT and corporate tax filing and payment in SMARTS. Confirm registration, active forms, any deadline variation and acceptance before submission. [Cap. 18.46, ss 7–8](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Act17TOC/Ch-18_46-Unincorporated-Business-Tax-Act.pdf) · [Inland Revenue guide, p 3](https://www.sknird.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/200701012021.pdf) · [Inland Revenue tax calendar](https://www.sknird.com/tax-calendar/) · [Government SMARTS launch notice](https://www.gov.kn/st-kitts-and-nevis-inland-revenue-department-modernises-its-tax-administration-system-with-the-launch-of-smarts/)

### Withholding on payments to non-residents

Section 36 in the 2017 revision, reflecting the 2014 return amendment and 2016 charge amendment, imposes **15%** on covered gross payments, including listed interest, distributions, rents, royalties and service fees. Check residence, payment classification, statutory exceptions, deemed branch profit remittances and treaty relief. The payer supplies a withholding certificate and files and pays by the 15th of March, June, September and December. Inland Revenue's calendar shows 15 December 2026 for the September–November 2026 period. The ordinary wage income exclusion does not resolve a cross-border service payment, and the statutory headline rate does not settle treaty entitlement. [Income Tax Act, s 36](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Act17TOC/Ch-20_22-Income-Tax-Act.pdf) · [Inland Revenue withholding guide](https://eservicesskn.sknird.com/UserFiles/FormGuide/Sam170217075715.pdf) · [Inland Revenue tax calendar](https://www.sknird.com/tax-calendar/)

## Wage levy, severance funding and minimum wage

The Housing and Social Development Levy, called the Social Services Levy by Inland Revenue, applies to wages of covered employed persons in the Federation. Cap. 20.21 sections 2–4 refer to the Social Security Act's employment classification and include specific office, religious, diplomatic and prescribed exemptions. The 2024 amendment, published 29 February 2024, set the lowest band ceiling at **EC$23,400 per year (EC$450 weekly)**. The 2025 amendment, published 11 July 2025, raised it to **EC$27,040 per year (EC$520 weekly)**. On covered wages in that band the employee pays **0%** and the employer pays **3%**. The 2025 Act specifies no different commencement date. Check the July transition against official instructions for pay periods spanning the change. [Cap. 20.21, ss 2–4](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Act17TOC/Ch-20_21-Housing-and-Social-Development-Levy-Act.pdf) · [Act 1 of 2024, s 2](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Annual-Laws/2024/ACTs/Act-1-of-2024-Housing-and-Social-Development-Levy-Saint-Christopher.pdf) · [Act 14 of 2025, s 2](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Annual-Laws/2025/ACTs/Act-14-OF-2025-The-Housing-and-Social-Development-Levy-Amendment.pdf) · [Constitution, s 42(4)](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Annual-Laws/The-Constitution-of-St-Christopher-and-Nevis.pdf)

In the 2017 revision, section 3(2)(b)–(d) sets a **3.5%** employee rate in the next band and states **10%** and **12%** for upper wage portions around the **EC$78,000** and **EC$96,000** annual boundaries. The employer rate is 3% of covered wages in each band. The 2024 and 2025 amendments replace the lower boundary in paragraph (b). The upper bands use inconsistent cent endpoints and refer to a portion up to EC$78,000. Establish the accepted treatment at and above the thresholds, including whether the low-wage exclusion affects the upper-band calculation, how to apply the bands to the pay frequency and the correct wage base. The Social Security comparison ceiling cannot be substituted for a levy wage base. [Cap. 20.21, s 3](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Act17TOC/Ch-20_21-Housing-and-Social-Development-Levy-Act.pdf) · [Act 14 of 2025, s 2](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Annual-Laws/2025/ACTs/Act-14-OF-2025-The-Housing-and-Social-Development-Levy-Amendment.pdf)

Under sections 5–6, the employer accounts for the employee and employer amounts and furnishes a statement of wages and levy paid. The Social Security Board collects for the Comptroller. Inland Revenue waived **both portions on bonuses actually paid in December 2025**, regardless of the period earned. Regular pay remained subject to ordinary levy rules. Confirm prescribed remittance cut-offs and pay-component treatment for other periods. [Cap. 20.21, ss 5–6](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Act17TOC/Ch-20_21-Housing-and-Social-Development-Levy-Act.pdf) · [Inland Revenue, 18 December 2025 waiver](https://www.sknird.com/government-of-saint-christopher-and-nevis-announces-temporary-waiver-of-social-services-levy-on-december-2025-bonuses/)

Severance funding is a separate employer charge. In the 2017 Protection of Employment Act revision, sections 13–15 set **1% of normal wages** subject to prescribed variations, with monthly accounting, a wage statement and collection by the Board on behalf of the Labour Commissioner. The Board's public guidance still describes the 1% employer contribution. Verify the current prescribed remittance deadline and normal-wage definition for the case. Contribution liability and the employee's entitlement on termination require separate determinations. [Protection of Employment Act, Cap. 18.27, ss 13–15](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Act17TOC/Ch-18_27-Protection-of-Employment-Act.pdf) · [Social Security Board, Protection of Employment Act](https://socialsecurity.kn/protection-act/)

The government confirms a national minimum wage of **EC$12.50 hourly from 1 July 2025**, equivalent to **EC$500** for 40 hours. The Prime Minister's Office restated the hourly and weekly figures in November 2026. The 2024 order documents the earlier EC$10.75 rate, with commencement on 10 January 2024 in the instrument. Obtain the 2025 operative order and any category rules before resolving coverage, overtime or pay periods spanning the change. [Government task force recap, December 2025](https://www.skncgtoronto.gov.kn/2025/12/15/2025-review-of-cost-of-living-measures-and-economic-impact/) · [Prime Minister's Office, 6 November 2026](https://www.sknis.gov.kn/2026/11/06/this-is-real-support-prime-minister-drew-says-as-government-investment-in-step-programme-reaches-54-million-in-2026-alone/) · [S.R.O. 1 of 2024, ss 1 and 3](https://lawcommission.gov.kn/wp-content/documents/Annual-Laws/2024/SROs/SRO-1-of-2024-LABOUR-MINIMUM-WAGE-ACT-AMENDMENT-OF-SCHEDULES.pdf)

## Intake and comparison mechanics

Reuse relevant redacted records before grouped questions. Establish legal parties, registration and classification, employer location, employee age throughout the contribution periods, complete earnings and payment dates, normal monthly period, exemptions and benefit weeks, other employment, prior deductions/payments, special directions, applicable source profile, required tools and reviewer identity. Present the profile and wait for confirmation before classification. Keep registration identifiers, bank information and signatures with the reviewer. Taxpayer confirmation cannot supply missing law.

1. **Insurability is independent.** Act s 19 and First Schedule Part I paragraph 1 concern local employment under a contract of service. Classification and self-employment rules can change the result for agency, partnership and spouse arrangements even where a contract exists. Establish the employer's local business presence and every diplomatic, unpaid-family or other exclusion. A foreign-worker exemption requires the prescribed determination; foreign insurance or reciprocity alone is not an automatic exemption. The retained Exemption Regulations expressly keep the separate Social Services Levy outside their exemption.
2. **Wage base and period.** Contributions regulation 6 uses gross cash earnings, with enumerated inclusions such as overtime, housing allowance, commission, employee liabilities paid by the employer and holiday pay. Its gratuity exclusions concern retirement lump sums and December payments; they do not exempt all ordinary December salary. Resolve the precise payment category and allocation before using W. Regulations 4–6 address benefit weeks, paid leave, age transitions, abnormal pay practices and nonmonthly periods. Those branches remain held here. A net bank payment is not a proved contribution base.
3. **Two retained reference profiles.** Under the stipulated monthly comparison, let B = min(W, 6,500), with W non-negative. The principal Schedule 2 labels 6,500 per month from 1 January 1998; the Board guide corroborates it. For the **2017 schedule profile**, employee E = 5% × B and employer S = 5% × B; their displayed subtotal T = E + S = 10% × B. T is the sum of the two retained rows, not an accepted complete liability. For the **2018 Board profile**, display E, employer main scheme C = 5% × B and employer injury I = 1% × B separately; employer R = C + I = 6% × B and combined displayed L = E + R = 11% × B. This reproduces the Board profile conditionally and supplies no missing charging authority. The displayed difference L − T is I; do not post it as an assessed underpayment or refund.
4. **Monthly limits and precision.** Aggregate the stipulated instalments of the same established monthly salary before applying this monthly reference ceiling. Keep separate established months separate. Do not convert the table to a weekly, fortnightly or annual ceiling, exhaust an invented annual maximum, or apply this Social Security ceiling to separate levies. Regulation 9's annual-maximum wording needs reconciliation before an annual exhaustion rule is implemented. Preserve exact E, S, C, I, R, T and L separately. Round figures in a source illustration, or one rounded combined example, do not establish a legal share/aggregate rounding sequence. Unknown currency, conversion, sequence or rounding requires Needs Input.
5. **Liability versus employee deduction.** Act s 23 initially places both shares on the employer and prohibits passing the employer's own share to the employee. Contributions regulations 7–8 restrict recovery to authorised wage deductions and address timing and wages in advance. The profile comparison is not proof that any amount was lawfully deducted, paid or allocated. Do not deduct the employer injury or main-scheme share, or replace a missed employee deduction with an invented cash debt. No actual net wages or employer cost follows from the examples.
6. **Recovery, refund and payment routes.** Regulation 8(4) permits qualifying good-faith under-deduction recovery only with prior notification, the same-year condition and the additional-deduction limit; establish every condition rather than applying an unrestricted catch-up. Act s 47(5) prevents recovery of the employee part of sums paid through that prosecution route. Regulation 14(7) has separate same-year overpayment and employee-accounting conditions; regulation 23 has its own application, period and allocation rules. These are distinct paths, not an automatic offset or refund. Actual recovery/refund and the meaning of the applicable year remain held.
7. **Administration and other programmes.** Regulation 14 describes payment at the end of the wage-payment month or within one month thereafter, with special arrangements; this is not a universal 30-day rule. Regulations 10–12 and 25–26 cover statements, omissions, records and returns. Actual due dates, Board arrangements, current forms, electronic acceptance, penalties and annual reporting remain held. Housing/Social Development or Social Services Levy, severance, other employment charges, income-tax treatment and any island-specific rules require separate complete sources. They do not become zero, exempt or capped because a Social Security reference is available.
