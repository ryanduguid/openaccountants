# Grenada domestic private company formation

Use this guide to identify the incorporation documents for a Grenadian private company limited by shares or check how a legacy international company could continue under the Companies Act. Confirm the current forms, fees and supporting documents with the Corporate Affairs and Intellectual Property Office (CAIPO) before filing. External companies, public companies, non-profits, regulated businesses and business name registration need separate checks. [Companies Act, Cap 58A][ACT] · [2014 Companies Amendment Regulations, SRO 36][FORMS] · [Act 2 of 2021, s 2][CONTINUATION] · [Act 15 of 2023, ss 2–7 and Schedule][VALIDATION] · [CAIPO][OFFICE]

## Incorporation documents

In the 2010 revised edition, sections 3–4 of the CAIPO Act establish the office and assign Companies Act functions to its Registrar. The published 2014 regulations identify the same office's Registrar for the forms below. [Corporate Affairs and Intellectual Property Office Act, Cap 69A, ss 3–4][REGISTRAR] · [SRO 36 of 2014, reg 2][FORMS]

| Step | Requirement in the 2010 revised edition |
| --- | --- |
| Check the incorporators and corporate name | One or more eligible persons may sign the articles. Check the individual disqualifications in section 4(2). Check the name ending, restrictions and existing reservations. [Cap 58A, ss 4, 10–11 and 514–516][ACT] |
| Prepare the articles | Specify the proposed name, authorised share classes and any maximum numbers, applicable class rights, transfer restrictions, the number or permitted range of directors and any limits on business activities. [Cap 58A, s 5][ACT] |
| Give notice of the directors and office | Send the directors' names and the address of the registered office with the articles. The registered office must be in Grenada. [Cap 58A, ss 7, 69(1) and 175–176(1)][ACT] |
| File with the Registrar and keep the certificate | The default legal route uses signed duplicate originals, the other required documents and the prescribed fees. The date on the certificate is the date the company comes into existence. [Cap 58A, ss 7–9 and 503][ACT] |

## Forms gazetted on 29 August 2014

The 2014 amendment made Form 1 serve as the articles and notice of registered office address. With Form 9 and a Form 9A for each director, it also served as the notice of the directors' names. Form 9A is the director's consent. The amendment specified a classification consistent with ISIC for proposed or actual business activities. For a statement of proposed activities to restrict the business, the incorporators must make it a restriction when applying, or the company must later amend its articles by special resolution under section 213. [SRO 36 of 2014, regs 3–4 and Third Schedule forms][FORMS]

Under the 2014 amendment, the Registrar may share application information with Inland Revenue to register the company as a taxpayer. The Registrar may share it with National Insurance to register the company as an employer if the application states that the company will have employees. Confirm any required registrations separately. [SRO 36 of 2014, reg 3 inserting reg 27C][FORMS]

## Example

An eligible sole incorporator proposes a private company with a Grenadian registered office. The articles identify the proposed name, share class and director arrangements. The 2014 filing set combines the articles and office notice in Form 1 and pairs it with Forms 9 and 9A for the directors. Check the forms and applicable fee with CAIPO on the intended filing date. Once issued, use the date shown on the certificate to record when the company came into existence. [Cap 58A, ss 4–9, 69(1) and 175–176(1)][ACT] · [SRO 36 of 2014, regs 3–4][FORMS]

## Incorporation fee amendment gazetted on 28 February 2023

The 2023 amendment reduced the fee for the certificate of incorporation under regulation 28(1)(a) from XCD 1,200 to XCD 600. Confirm the applicable fee, other charges and filing requirements with CAIPO on the intended filing date. [Companies Regulations, Cap 58A, reg 28(1)(a)][ACT] · [SRO 15 of 2023, reg 2][FEE2023] · [Extraordinary Gazette No. 12 of 2023][ISSUE2023]

## Legacy international companies

New incorporation under the International Companies Act, Cap 152, ended on 31 December 2018 when the repeal took effect. The repeal preserved earlier incorporations and intervening lawful acts until the applicable operative date. For an international company that was a trust corporation under Cap 152C, check the date the international trust terminated under section 9 of that Act. For other legacy companies, the original rule used the earlier of winding-up or dissolution under Part VIII of Cap 152 and 31 December 2021. The 2021 amendment saved rights and benefits until the operative date. It preserved obligations and penalties an international company incurred whilst Cap 152 was in operation and allowed related investigations, proceedings and remedies. [Act 17 of 2018, ss 1–3][REPEAL] · [Act 2 of 2021, s 2][CONTINUATION]

A company carrying on business under Cap 152 and in good standing on 31 August 2021 was deemed to continue under the Companies Act, Cap 58A, from 1 September 2021. It had to operate in accordance with Cap 58A and was subject to that Act's liabilities and penalties for non-compliance. Check the entity's standing and continuation records. [Act 2 of 2021, s 2 inserting s 3(6)][CONTINUATION]

Act 15 of 2023, published on 22 December 2023, provided a separate route. The original schedule lists 22 companies. Section 2 also requires incorporation under Cap 152 and failure to comply with the earlier transition provisions. A company meeting that definition qualified for deemed continuation under Cap 58A if it paid all outstanding fees and penalties determined by the Grenada Authority for the Regulation of Financial Institutions to the CAIPO Registrar within 6 months of commencement. The Authority must notify the company of the section 3 requirements in writing at commencement. A continuing company had to operate in accordance with Cap 58A and was subject to that Act's liabilities and penalties for non-compliance. [Act 15 of 2023, ss 2–3 and Schedule][VALIDATION] · [Government Gazette No. 63 of 2023][ISSUEVALIDATION]

Section 4 validates acts and omissions of a company deemed to continue under section 3 that would otherwise have been valid and lawful but for its failure to be in good standing on 31 August 2021. Section 5 applies Cap 58A section 368 with the necessary modifications where the company failed to comply with section 3. Section 6 preserves legal remedies for acts or omissions of a company deemed to continue under section 3. Section 7 allows the Minister to amend the schedule by order. Check the applicable schedule, notices and payment records before treating an entity as continued. [Act 15 of 2023, ss 4–7][VALIDATION]

## Confirm before filing

- Gazette No. 28 of 13 May 2022 lists SRO 23 of 2022. The published cover identifies an amendment to the Third Schedule. Obtain the operative text and check later amendments before using the 2014 forms. Check any later schedule order under the 2023 validation Act and obtain registry evidence for the entity concerned. [Government Gazette No. 28 of 2022, Notice 23][ISSUE2022] · [SRO 23 of 2022 cover][COVER2022] · [Act 15 of 2023, s 7][VALIDATION]
- Ask CAIPO for the current articles and notice forms, fee schedule, filing channel, processing time and any required attorney involvement. The public eService portal lists business registration. Confirm with CAIPO which service accepts company incorporation. [CAIPO][OFFICE] · [Grenada eService portal][PORTAL]
- Check the current beneficial ownership forms, declarations and deadlines separately. The 2022 amendment published on 14 April 2022 inserted a beneficial ownership part. Routine annual returns, financial statements, tax and employer registration also require separate checks. [Companies Amendment Act 5 of 2022, s 4][ACT2022] · [Cap 58A, ss 149, 194][ACT]
