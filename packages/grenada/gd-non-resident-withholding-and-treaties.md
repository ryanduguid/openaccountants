# Grenada withholding tax on payments to non-residents

Identify the payer, payee, payment or credit date, contract, gross amount and any Grenada permanent establishment before deciding whether to withhold. Obtain evidence of the payee's residence and check where the income is attributable. A payment to an address outside Grenada creates a rebuttable presumption of non-residence under the published Income Tax Act. [Income Tax Act, Cap 149, ss 2, 7 and 50][CAP149] · [Act 27 of 2015, s 11][ACT27]

## When to deduct

1. Apply s 50(1) to a resident payer or a non-resident payer making the payment or credit in relation to a Grenada permanent establishment. Check that the payee is non-resident and that the amount is within the Third Schedule. Section 50(1) excepts income attributable to the payee's Grenada permanent establishment. Section 50(6) treats a foreign company's local branch separately from its head office and other non-resident branches and reaches specified accruals and transactions that give the local branch a deduction. [Act 27 of 2015, ss 11 and 18][ACT27]
2. Classify the amount under the Third Schedule. The 2015 amendment lists interest or discount, rent, lease premiums, franchise fees and licences, royalties, management and technical fees, directors' fees and similar commissions or fees, and annuities or similar periodic payments. Third Schedule para 1(2) also covers independent personal services performed in Grenada by a non-resident. Apply ss 51 or 54 to employment and s 52 to the specified payments to related residents. For non-resident partnership income, s 51A refers to the Fifth Schedule as amended. Confirm the applicable rate for that partner and period separately. Check the separate treatment of qualifying bank deposit interest under s 53. [Cap 149, ss 46 and 50–54 and Third and Fifth Schedules][CAP149] · [Act 27 of 2015, ss 11–13 and 18][ACT27]
3. Check exemptions, investment relief and the applicable treaty before deducting. The published Third Schedule para 3 gives a default of 15% of the gross payment and treats the deduction as the final liability for that income where the provision applies. For XCD 10,000 of rent subject to that rate with no relief, withhold XCD 1,500. [Cap 149, s 50 and Third Schedule paras 2–3][CAP149] · [OAS Grenada Business Formalization Toolkit, Inland Revenue Division section, 2023][TOOLKIT]

From 1 May 2016, s 50(4A)–(4C) introduced relief for qualifying investments in tourism accommodation or health and wellness, subject to the Investment Act and regulations. For specified financing interest and royalties associated with a person who is not related within the meaning of s 5 of the Value Added Tax Act, the enacted waiver is 50% for qualifying costs above XCD 30 million and up to XCD 80 million and 100% above XCD 80 million. If the costs fall short when the investment is completed or the investment agreement period expires, whichever happens first, repay the waived tax within 2 months of that event, subject to an extension. The Comptroller may extend the relevant period by up to 12 months in consultation with the responsible investment agency. Check the approval and qualifying costs before claiming relief. [Act 8 of 2016, s 4][INVESTMENT] · [SRO 26 of 2016][INVESTMENTSTART]

## Remittance and records

The s 50(1) replacement enacted in 2015 calls for payment to the Comptroller within 7 days of payment or credit to the payee. The 2023 Grenada Business Formalization Toolkit gives the payment-based rule for non-resident rent. Cap 149 s 96(a) separately states 7 days from the end of the month in which the tax was deductible. For an ordinary payment or credit within s 50(1), plan to remit within 7 days of that event as a precaution whilst obtaining written confirmation from the IRD of the applicable remittance and return deadlines. [Cap 149, ss 50 and 96 and Third Schedule para 1][CAP149] · [Act 27 of 2015, ss 11 and 18][ACT27] · [OAS toolkit, 2023][TOOLKIT]

For an amount accrued and still unpaid at the accounting date, s 50(2) provides for an advance payment when the business return is submitted. Third Schedule para 1(3) treats specified unpaid expenses in accrual accounts as paid on the last day of the basis period. Section 50(6) also treats certain branch accruals as payments. Confirm the applicable timing for the accrual with the IRD. [Cap 149, s 50(2) and Third Schedule para 1][CAP149] · [Act 27 of 2015, ss 11 and 18][ACT27]

The published Third Schedule requires a payee certificate showing the gross amount and deduction, supporting records, a remittance return with copies of certificates and a return for the calendar year within 2 months of year end, subject to extension. It also sets out liability where the payer fails to deduct. Verify current form, period, submission and payment allocation with the IRD, including any GTAX changes, before filing. The Tax Administration Act commenced on 1 May 2016. Check the legislation applicable to the period before relying on an older interest or penalty provision. [Cap 149, Third Schedule paras 4–6][CAP149] · [Tax Administration Act 14 of 2016, Part VII and Schedule 2][ADMIN] · [SRO 34 of 2016][ADMINSTART] · [Government Online Services][GOVPORTAL]

## Treaty decisions

For a lower deduction, confirm the payee's treaty residence, the covered tax and income type, source, applicable dates, any permanent establishment and the relief procedure. Section 43 of Cap 149 provides for agreements to be published by order in the Gazette. Section 50(5) allows the Comptroller to direct a lower deduction where a qualifying agreement provides a lower rate. Obtain the evidence and confirm the direction or claim procedure before deducting a reduced amount. [Cap 149, ss 43 and 50(5)][CAP149]

- **CARICOM agreement.** For a resident of an eligible contracting state, the agreement specifies a zero rate on ordinary dividends and a separate limit for preference shares. The source-state ceilings for interest, royalties and management fees are generally 15% within their respective definitions. Check exemptions, related-party excess and the payment-specific rules. Article 16 governs independent professional services and looks to where the services are performed and the amount attributable there. For Grenada and other later ratifying states, art 28 ties the withholding start to deposit of the instrument of ratification. Use the published status and verify the applicable deposit and payment dates for a historic claim. [CARICOM agreement, arts 1–2, 11–16 and 27–28][CARICOM] · [CARICOM Secretariat status record][CARICOMSTATUS]
- **United Kingdom arrangement.** HMRC records application in Grenada from the assessment year beginning 1 January 1949 and entry into force of the amending arrangement on 14 December 1968. Paragraph 6 addresses dividend tax imposed in addition to the tax on company profits. Paragraph 7 addresses qualifying royalties and limits exempt consideration to the fair and reasonable amount for the rights. Both have recipient taxation and permanent establishment conditions. Check the company shareholder exception in para 6 and the evidence of residence. HMRC calls for a residence certificate for a claim for relief from Grenada tax. Confirm the current Grenada claim procedure. [Consolidated arrangement, paras 6–7 and 15][UK] · [HMRC, agreement status][HMRCSTATUS] · [HMRC, relief from Grenada tax][HMRCCLAIM]

Obtain the operative instrument and exact payment-period treatment for South Africa, Switzerland or any other agreement before applying relief. The South African Revenue Service lists the Grenada agreement, and the Swiss Federal Council records the extension of its 1954 income tax convention to Grenada. Verify other treaty and domestic exceptions, including foreign tax credits, transfer pricing and specialist payments, separately. [South African Revenue Service, treaty index][SARS] · [Swiss Federal Council, 2015 dispatch, s 1.1][SWISS]
