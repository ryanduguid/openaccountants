# Mauritius payroll and social contributions

Use the pay period and employment category to identify the applicable rules. This reference covers ordinary private-sector payroll and the standard self-employed CSG regime through 21 November 2026. All amounts are in Mauritian rupees (Rs).

## Employee payroll

Keep separate bases for pay as you earn (PAYE), the contribution sociale généralisée (CSG), the National Savings Fund (NSF) and the training levy.

| Charge | Employee | Employer | Base and period |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| CSG up to and including Rs 50,000 | 1.5% | 3% | Apply to the whole monthly CSG remuneration. Calculate the basic-wage component of a statutory end-of-year bonus separately. |
| CSG above Rs 50,000 | 3% | 6% | Apply to the whole monthly CSG remuneration. For a statutory bonus paid in instalments, use the MRA's cumulative bonus rule. |
| NSF, ordinary private employee | 1% | 2.5% | Basic wage or salary, including salary compensation and excluding bonuses and allowances, subject to the applicable pay-period limits. Round contributions to the nearest rupee. |
| Training levy, ordinary liable employer | 0% | 1.5% | Full basic wage or salary, excluding overtime, bonuses and allowances. The NSF ceiling does not apply. |

Sources for employee rates and bases. [Social Contribution and Social Benefits Act 2021, ss 2–5 and First Schedule](https://www.mra.mu/download/SocialContributionAndSocialBenefitsAct2021.pdf). [National Savings Fund Act 1995, ss 2 and 5 and First Schedule](https://www.mra.mu/download/TheNationalSavingsFundAct1995.pdf). [Human Resource Development Act 2003, s 18 and First Schedule](https://www.mra.mu/download/TheHumanResourceDevelopmentAct2003.pdf). [MRA joint monthly return guidance](https://www.mra.mu/index.php/employers?id=278).

| Monthly NSF base | 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026 | From 1 July 2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Minimum, private household employees | Rs 2,795 | Rs 2,910 |
| Minimum, other employees | Rs 4,400 | Rs 4,580 |
| Maximum, all employees | Rs 28,570 | Rs 29,710 |

Use the MRA's matching table for daily, weekly, fortnightly and half-monthly payroll. Check household, public-sector, non-citizen, pension-age and other special classifications before applying ordinary rates. For qualifying domestic service with aggregate monthly remuneration of up to Rs 3,000, s 4(1)(c) sets CSG at 0% employee and 3% employer. The NSF contribution and training levy have their own exclusions. [MRA 2025 payroll notice](https://www.mra.mu/download/PayrollTaxes.pdf). [MRA NSF pay-period limits from 1 July 2026](https://www.mra.mu/index.php/employers/npf-nsf-contributions). [Social Contribution and Social Benefits Act 2021, s 4](https://www.mra.mu/download/SocialContributionAndSocialBenefitsAct2021.pdf).

The Portable Retirement Gratuity Fund (PRGF) has a separate employer return and payment. For an eligible worker, the ordinary contribution is 4.5% of monthly remuneration as defined for PRGF, including basic wages and specified bonuses and extra work. The ordinary deadline is one month after the end of the contribution month. Check eligibility and documented exceptions, including qualifying pension schemes, migrant or non-citizen status and basic monthly pay above Rs 200,000. [Workers' Rights Act 2019, ss 87–95](https://www.mra.mu/download/WorkersRightsAct2019.pdf). [MRA PRGF guide, sections 3–5](https://www.mra.mu/download/PRGFGuide.pdf). [MRA PRGF monthly return](https://www.mra.mu/index.php/eservices1/corporate/prgf?tmpl=component).

Example for a standard private-sector employee paid a basic monthly salary of Rs 25,000 in October 2026, with no bonus or allowance and eligible for PRGF. The employee's CSG is Rs 375 and NSF is Rs 250. The employer's CSG is Rs 750, NSF is Rs 625, training levy is Rs 375 and PRGF contribution is Rs 1,125. Compute any PAYE separately using the employee's taxable emoluments and declaration.

## PAYE and monthly remittance

Register as an employer with the MRA within 14 days of becoming an employer. Obtain the Employer Registration Number and maintain the payroll details required for the joint return. Give employees their Statement of Emoluments and Tax Deduction and submit the annual Return of Employees by the ordinary 15 August deadline. The MRA listed 17 August 2026 for the electronic Return of Employees for the year ended 30 June 2026. Check the current season and any exception, including the separate return for private households. [MRA employer obligations](https://www.mra.mu/index.php/media-centre/20-employers). [Income Tax Regulations 1996, reg 22](https://www.mra.mu/download/ITaxRegulationsGN78of1996.pdf). [MRA 2026 Return of Employees](https://www.mra.mu/index.php/eservices1/individual/return-of-employees?highlight=WyJhbGxvd2FuY2UiXQ%3D%3D).

Withhold PAYE when taxable emoluments are paid or made available. Check the definition of exempt person in s 2 and the exceptions in ss 92 and 93, including special treatment for directors and certain other payments, before applying the Rs 38,462 monthly threshold. Apply the employee's declaration and the rules for the period. The 2026 Finance Act limits the individual Fair Share Contribution to the income year starting 1 July 2025. Use the 2026/27 EDF for the year ending 30 June 2027, including the 35% ordinary marginal band above Rs 12 million of chargeable income. [Income Tax Act 1995, ss 2, 16C and 92–100](https://www.mra.mu/download/ITAConsolidated.pdf). [Finance Act 2026, ss 7(b), 7(v) and 28(12)](https://www.mra.mu/download/FinanceAct2026.pdf). [MRA employee declaration notes for 2026/27](https://eservices.mra.mu/edf/helpEDF28IT.html).

The MRA's ordinary joint monthly PAYE/CSG/NSF return and electronic payment are due by the end of the following month. For May and November, use the MRA's dates 2 days before the end of June and December respectively, excluding Saturdays and public holidays when counting. Private households use the contributions-only return. Check the MRA calendar and any published extension for the specific pay period. [Income Tax Act 1995, s 100](https://www.mra.mu/download/ITAConsolidated.pdf). [MRA joint monthly return and deadline](https://www.mra.mu/index.php/eservices1/individual/monthly-paye-csg-nsf-return?highlight=WyJhbGxvd2FuY2UiXQ%3D%3D).

## Self-employed CSG

Use the monthly net-income basis in s 4. Where eligible, a participant may use one twelfth of the immediately preceding financial year's net income instead of actual monthly net income. The return fixes the chosen basis for the period. An individual with employment remuneration and self-employed net income applies the respective rules to both.

| Monthly net income | Contribution |
| --- | ---: |
| Up to Rs 10,000 | Rs 150 |
| Over Rs 10,000 and up to Rs 50,000 | Higher of Rs 150 and 1.5% of 90% of net income |
| Over Rs 50,000 | 3% of 90% of net income |

The usual monthly return and payment are due by the end of the following month. The May and November monthly returns and payments are due 2 days before the end of June and December respectively, excluding Saturdays and public holidays when counting. Where making an annual election, check ss 6–8 for the filing and remittance timing. The MRA separately describes an advance-payment option due 15 October from financial year 2025/26. Exclusively passive income is outside the Act's definition of self-employed. [Social Contribution and Social Benefits Act 2021, ss 2, 4 and 6–8 and First Schedule](https://www.mra.mu/download/SocialContributionAndSocialBenefitsAct2021.pdf). [MRA contributions guidance](https://www.mra.mu/index.php/eservices1/individual/monthly-paye-csg-nsf-return?highlight=WyJhbGxvd2FuY2UiXQ%3D%3D).
