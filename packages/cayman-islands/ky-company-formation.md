# Cayman Islands Company Formation & Entity Choice

## 1. Choose the structure and register it

Identify where the business will operate, whether it will have a Cayman
place of business or hold Cayman land, the ownership and control chain and
any activity requiring a licence. An incorporation certificate and a
business or sector licence serve separate purposes. The fees below are in
Cayman dollars. [Companies Act, ss 26, 163–165, 174 and 183–185][companies] ·
[Trade and Business Licensing Act, ss 3 and 17–19][trade]

| Structure | Registration route in 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary resident or non-resident company | One or more subscribers file the memorandum and any articles with the Registrar, pay the registration fee and maintain a Cayman registered office. Non-resident status is a separate designation with limits on business in Cayman. [Companies Act, ss 5–9, 22–27, 50–51 and Schedule 5][companies] · [Local Companies (Control) Act, ss 2–5][lcc] |
| Exempted company | File the company documents and a subscriber declaration that operations will be mainly outside Cayman or carried on under a licence within s 174. Section 174 limits local activity and permits specified activity in furtherance of overseas business. [Companies Act, ss 163–165 and 174][companies] |
| Foreign company | Where Part 9 applies, file formation and constitutional documents, evidence of good standing, director particulars and the names and addresses of Cayman residents authorised to accept service. File within 1 month of becoming a foreign company for the Act. Registration is also relevant to overseas companies holding Cayman land. [Companies Act, ss 183–187 and Schedule 5 Part 5][companies] |
| Cayman LLC | At least one member. File the signed registration statement with the Cayman registered office and initial member details, pay the fee and put the LLC agreement in place. [Limited Liability Companies Act, ss 5–8 and 16][llc] · [Registry LLC fee schedules][llc-fees] |
| Exempted limited partnership (ELP) | Use at least one general and one limited partner and check the qualifying Cayman connection for a general partner. The general partner files the statutory statement and registration fee, including the Cayman registered office and the declaration limiting business with the Cayman public. [Exempted Limited Partnership Act, ss 4 and 6–10][elp] · [Registry ELP fee schedule][elp-fees] |
| Limited partnership or limited liability partnership (LLP) | Register a limited partnership under Part 8 of the Partnership Act. For an LLP, use the registration statement under its own Act and record the Cayman registered office and required partner information. [Partnership Act, ss 43–50][partnership] · [Limited Liability Partnership Act, ss 4, 8 and 18][llp] |
| Foundation company or segregated portfolio company (SPC) | Apply for foundation company status with the constitution and a qualified secretary. A Cayman SPC is an exempted company registered under Part 14, with company and portfolio fees and any applicable sector approval. [Foundation Companies Act, ss 4–5 and 16][foundation] · [Companies Act, ss 212–214 and Schedule 5 Part 7][companies] |

A general partnership can arise from persons carrying on business together
with a view to profit. A sole trader uses the applicable licensing route
for their business. For trusts and non-profits, check the separate register
and the activity before choosing a company form. Trustees can apply to
register an exempted trust subject to the beneficiary residence and domicile
conditions in the Trusts Act. The Registry
accepts applications for NPO registration separately from company
incorporation. [Partnership Act, ss 3–4][partnership] ·
[Trusts Act, ss 73–78][trusts] · [Registry NPO registration][npo] ·
[Trade and Business Licensing Act, ss 3 and 17–19][trade]

### Local trading permissions

Check the Trade and Business Licensing Act exclusions in s 3 before applying
to the Department of Commerce and Investment (DCI). Where a licence is
required, the application and renewal are governed by ss 17–23. For local
company control, the statutory Caymanian route generally requires Caymanian
control, at least 60% Caymanian beneficial share ownership and at least 60%
Caymanian directors. A company outside that route must establish another
permitted basis under s 4, which may require both a Local Companies (Control)
licence and a trade and business licence. Sector permissions may apply.
[Local Companies (Control) Act, ss 4–5 and 10–11][lcc] ·
[Trade and Business Licensing Act, ss 3 and 17–23][trade]

Use the updated trade and business fee schedule from 28 April 2026. DCI
offers qualifying locally owned and operated businesses licences of up to
5 years. Its published conditions include applying for renewal 90 days
before expiry and submitting annual returns and relevant documents by
31 January for that route. [SL 21 of 2026, regs 1–2][trade-fees] ·
[DCI multi-year licensing guidance][multi-year]

### Exempted company registration and annual fees

| Registered capital | Registration in 2026 | Annual fee in 2026 | Annual fee from 1 January 2027 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| None or up to CI$42,000 | CI$700 | CI$925 | CI$1,025 |
| Over CI$42,000 up to CI$820,000 | CI$1,000 | CI$1,225 | CI$1,325 |
| Over CI$820,000 up to CI$1,640,000 | CI$1,984 | CI$2,209 | CI$2,309 |
| Over CI$1,640,000 | CI$2,568 | CI$2,793 | CI$2,893 |

The table is for the ordinary bands. Schedule 5 has separate fees for
ordinary companies, foreign companies, portfolio registrations and other
transactions. The March 2026 erratum corrects the fee for filing a merger
or consolidation plan to CI$1,000 per constituent entity under Part 6(e)(i).
SL 46 of 2026 replaces the earlier scheduled order from 1 January 2027.
The separate LLC annual fee changes to CI$1,200 on the same date.
[Companies Act, s 26(4), s 169 and Schedule 5][companies] ·
[March erratum][company-erratum] · [SL 46 of 2026, paras 1–3][company-fees-2027] ·
[SL 40 of 2026, regs 1–2][llc-fees-2027]

## 2. Identify and maintain beneficial ownership

1. Establish whether the entity is a legal person under ss 2–3 of the
   Beneficial Ownership Transparency Act. Cayman companies, LLCs, LLPs,
   limited partnerships, foundations and ELPs are listed. The statutory
   definitions exclude specified foreign registrations, including foreign
   companies under Part 9.
2. Identify individuals with ultimate direct or indirect ownership or
   control of **25% or more** of shares, voting rights or partnership
   interests. Test ultimate effective management control and control by
   other means independently. Check the professional capacity exclusion
   and trustee and senior managing official contact rules in s 4. Identify
   reportable legal entities where required.
3. Provide particulars and category confirmation to the corporate services
   provider. Listed entities and qualifying subsidiaries, licensees,
   registered funds and Cabinet-exempted entities may use the applicable
   alternative route under s 12. A registered fund using that route must
   identify the qualifying Cayman contact. An ordinary resident company
   without a third-party provider can itself be the provider for this Act.
4. Keep the information current. For a relevant change, s 14 requires
   notice to the owner as soon as reasonably practicable and within 30
   days of knowledge or reasonable cause to believe there was a change.
   Regulation 3(3) requires written confirmation of amended particulars
   to the provider within 30 days of awareness that information ceased
   to be current. The provider normally deposits data or a no-change
   indicator at least monthly. Regulation 8 sets 365-day deposits for
   ordinary resident companies and 90-day deposits in liquidation, subject
   to the applicable change rule. Retain departed owners' records for
   5 years as required by s 16.

[Beneficial Ownership Transparency Act, ss 2–16][bo-act] ·
[Beneficial Ownership Transparency Regulations, regs 3 and 8][bo-regs] ·
[General Registry 2026 guidance][bo-guide]

## 3. Keep records and file on the right calendar

| Entity or obligation | 2026 treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Share-capital companies subject to s 41(1) | File the statutory list and capital summary in January after the year of incorporation. This includes licensed exempted companies with share capital. Ordinary companies pay the separate January fee under s 41(2). [Companies Act, ss 40–42][companies] |
| Exempted companies | Every exempted company pays the annual fee in January after the year of registration. The s 168 declaration applies to those without the s 174 licence. Check s 169(3) for late return or fee penalties in the second, third or fourth quarter. [Companies Act, ss 168–170][companies] |
| Foreign company | Pay the January fee under s 184(2) and report changes to the filed particulars under s 187. [Companies Act, ss 184 and 187][companies] |
| LLC, ELP and LLP | An LLC files its signed return and pays the fee in January. An ELP files the general partner's declaration and pays the annual fee in January. An LLP files the return and pays by 31 January after its registration year. [Limited Liability Companies Act, s 57][llc] · [Exempted Limited Partnership Act, s 39][elp] · [Limited Liability Partnership Act, s 20][llp] |
| Limited partnership | Pay the annual fee by 16 January. The 2025 revision sets the fee at CI$475. [Partnership Act, s 50][partnership] |

Keep the applicable member, director, manager or partner registers. A
company's accounts must explain its transactions and include material
underlying records such as contracts and invoices. Companies, LLCs and
ELPs have a minimum 5-year retention rule. Check the registered-office
availability requirements if the books are held elsewhere. A company with
books outside Cayman must also check the prescribed reporting rule in
s 59(2B) and the regulatory filing exception in s 59(2C).
[Companies Act, ss 40, 50, 55 and 59][companies] ·
[Limited Liability Companies Act, ss 61–63][llc] ·
[Exempted Limited Partnership Act, ss 21, 29–31][elp] ·
[Limited Liability Partnership Act, s 11][llp]

### Accounts, audits and tax cooperation

The Companies Act's general books requirement and January company filing
apply under ss 41, 59 and 168. For audit obligations, check the
entity's constitution and any regulatory law separately. CIMA-regulated
mutual and private funds generally submit audited financial statements and
a Fund Annual Return to CIMA within 6 months of financial year end. Use
CIMA's reporting instructions and check any granted exemption or extension.
[Companies Act, ss 41, 59 and 168][companies] ·
[CIMA investment fund reporting][cima]

- **Economic substance.** An entity files the annual notification under
  s 7(1), even where it reports no relevant activity. A relevant entity
  carrying on a relevant activity and required to satisfy the test files the
  report within 12 months after financial year end. The notice and report
  use separate submission rules. Check the tax-resident-outside-Cayman
  evidence requirements where applicable. [Economic Substance Act, ss 2, 4 and 7][es]
- **CRS and FATCA.** These are separate reporting regimes for financial
  institutions within scope. For the 2025 reporting year, DITC specified
  30 April 2026 registration, 31 July 2026 reporting and 15 September
  2026 for the CRS compliance form. The principal Cayman point of contact
  and the date the entity became a financial institution have a 31 January
  2027 notification deadline under the cited advisory. The revised CRS
  due diligence provisions apply from 1 January 2026. The new return and
  compliance form provisions commence 1 January 2027 and set a 30 June
  deadline for the preceding calendar year. [DITC 2026 deadlines][ditc] ·
  [SL 50 of 2025, regs 1, 3–6][crs]
