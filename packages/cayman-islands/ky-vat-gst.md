# Cayman Islands VAT, imports and visitor charges

## General consumption tax in 2026

Cayman has no general VAT, GST or retail sales tax. VAT rates, registration,
returns, input credits and a VAT reverse charge on imported services are
inapplicable because there is no general VAT system. Goods can attract
customs duty. Tourist accommodation, timeshare use and departures have
their own statutory charges. See the [tax-system overview](ky-tax-overview.md)
for the agencies that administer these and other charges. [Government
revenue overview][revenue] · [Legislation index][index]

## Customs duty

### Classification, value and filing

1. **Identify the goods and the entry purpose.** Classify each item under
   Schedule 1 using the Schedule 6 rules and the relevant section and chapter
   notes. Use the rate in force when the entry is presented, when goods are
   removed from warehouse for home use, or when they are imported if no entry
   is made. The rate may be a percentage of value or an amount per unit.
   Check Schedule 2 relief separately. [Tariff Act, ss 2–3 and Schedules
   1, 2 and 6][tariff] · [Customs Act, ss 17 and 43–44][cbc-act]
2. **Work out the value.** For a percentage duty, use the normal open-market
   price between independent parties at entry for home use or, where there
   is no such entry, at importation. Include delivery to the import point,
   including freight and insurance. For uninsured goods, include notional
   insurance of 1% of cost and freight. The Act sets conditions for
   buying commission and invoice discounts and authorises CBC to obtain
   valuation records. CBC instructs importers to convert the invoice at
   the Cayman dollar exchange rate in force on arrival.
   [Customs Act, ss 44–45][cbc-act] · [CBC entry instructions][cbc-entry]
3. **Declare and pay.** For commercial imports, register for an import
   account and trader identification number and use the Customs Online
   System (COLS). Section 17 requires entry before or within 7 days of
   landing, counted from the report date. Enter each tariff code separately.
   Provide the invoice and bill of lading or airway bill, and evidence of any
   approval or relief claimed. Pay duty on entry for home use and before
   delivery or removal unless a statutory release arrangement applies.
   Retain the declaration and its supporting evidence for CBC inspection.
   [Customs Act, ss 9, 17–18, 21, 43 and 45][cbc-act] ·
   [CBC entry instructions][cbc-entry] · [COLS registration][cols]

Use the exact code for the goods. These are examples from Schedule 1 of the
2026 Revision. [Tariff Act, Schedule 1][tariff]

| Goods | Code | Duty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laptops within the statutory description | 8471.30.00 | 22% |
| Wooden office furniture | 9403.30.00 | 22% |
| Malt beer | 2203.00.00 | CI$1.95 per litre |
| Personal-use electric vehicle within the CI$29,999 band | 8703.80.10 | 0% import duty. Check the separate environmental tax. |

### Relief and additional charges

- **Passenger allowance.** Returning residents can receive duty relief on
  accompanying personal and household goods at the Director's discretion.
  Merchandise is outside the allowance. The preceding limit was CI$500.
  The limit is CI$1,000 from 1 July through 31 August 2026 and again from
  23 November 2026 through 7 January 2027. The CI$800 limit applies from
  1 September 2026 outside that second period. Check the separate baggage,
  alcohol and tobacco conditions. [Tariff Act, Schedule 2 para 5][tariff] ·
  [SL 33 of 2026, paras 1–2][allowance]
- **Temporary imports.** Apply to the Director before the goods are
  released. Under s 22 of the 2024 Revision, the Director can allow duty-free
  retention for up to 6 months or approve an extension on prior written
  request. CBC may require security. Failure to re-export in time can
  trigger duty. Section 2 of Act 2 of 2025 provides for a 2% rate but is
  outside the provisions commenced by SL 34 of 2025 on 1 October 2025.
  CBC's published guidance still describes the duty-free approval route.
  Check for a further commencement order before applying the new rate.
  [Customs Act, s 22][cbc-act] · [Act 2 of 2025, s 2][cbc-amend] ·
  [SL 34 of 2025, para 2][cbc-start] · [CBC import relief][cbc-imports]
- **Other relief.** Check Schedule 2 for conditional exemptions and the
  Customs Act for re-imports, drawback, refunds for qualifying returned
  or destroyed goods and individual Cabinet waivers. Keep earlier duty
  receipts, export or destruction evidence and any written permission.
  Section 6 of the Tariff Act gives Cabinet a separate power to reduce
  or waive specified duties in Cayman Brac and Little Cayman.
  [Tariff Act, ss 3 and 6 and Schedule 2][tariff] ·
  [Customs Act, ss 46–52][cbc-act] · [CBC import relief][cbc-imports]
- **Other border charges.** Check package tax at first entry under s 51
  and Schedule 3, including the statutory port and other exceptions.
  Schedule 4 sets handling and storage charges. Schedule 5 sets inspection
  fees and, from 1 October 2025, a separate environmental tax on qualifying
  motor vehicles. The statutory bands are CI$1,000 for cost, insurance and
  freight of CI$79,999 or less and CI$2,000 from CI$80,000. Check vehicle
  exclusions and the transition for documented earlier import arrangements.
  [Customs Act, s 51][cbc-act] · [Tariff Act, ss 3–5 and Schedules 3–5][tariff] ·
  [SL 36 of 2025, paras 1 and 3][vehicle-order]

## Tourist accommodation and timeshares

The proprietor, including a manager, accounts for the tax. A tourist under
the Act is a visitor who is not ordinarily resident and stays continuously
for no more than 6 months. The following rates apply in 2026.
[Tourist Accommodation Act, ss 2–3][tourist] · [Tourism Department guidance][tourism]

| Stay | Charge |
| --- | --- |
| Tourist staying in ordinary overnight accommodation | 13% of the accommodation and connected service charges. Exclude food, drink and other consumables from the service base. The Department uses the gross room rate before deductions for agents or operating costs. |
| Tourist using a timeshare as its owner, the owner's guest or by exchange | Equivalent of US$10 per occupied room for each day or part of a day. |
| Other tourist staying in a timeshare | The ordinary 13% charge. |

Give the guest a statement on departure identifying the accommodation,
services and tax. Submit copies of the statements and pay the Collector of
Taxes within 28 days after the month of supply. Use the Department of
Tourism's signed accommodation or timeshare return and retain booking,
billing and residency evidence. Its guidance calls for a nil return for
vacant months, on-island copies and delivery before the deadline if the
office is closed on the due date. Use the Department's payment instructions
for remittance and the conversion rules for the payment method. For stays
crossing months, reconcile the Department's checkout instructions with
the statutory month-of-supply rule before deferring payment.
[Tourist Accommodation Act, ss 4–7][tourist] · [Department forms and payment
instructions][tourism]

Failure to submit the statement or pay in full by the deadline triggers a
20% surcharge on the tax due for the month. The Collector can assess
missing or incorrect amounts and statutory interest can also apply to
underpayments. Confirm the prescribed interest rate before calculating it.
[Tourist Accommodation Act, ss 7–10][tourist]

## Departure and environmental protection charges

The Travel Act (2026 Revision) gives a CI$26 departure charge for a covered traveller
on aircraft or a vessel other than a cruise ship. The aircraft agent accounts
for CI$10 to the Government and CI$16 to the Civil Aviation Authority. The
non-cruise vessel agent accounts for CI$26 to the Government. The cruise
charge is CI$6 per statutory tourist, payable through the agent. The
traveller definition excludes qualifying crew, diplomats, children under
12 and air transit under 24 hours. Section 6 separately provides
environmental protection fees, with distinct vessel, aircraft, seasonal
cruise and year-round cruise provisions. Check the route, any applicable
regulations and Sister Islands relief before calculating the total.
[Travel Act, ss 2–7][travel]
