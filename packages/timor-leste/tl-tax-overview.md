# Timor-Leste Tax Overview

## Tax system at a glance

This guide covers the ordinary domestic rules for the 2026 tax year under
[Law No. 8/2008, as republished by Law No. 5/2019, annex I][tax-act].
Petroleum operations and projects governed by special laws or agreements can
have different rules. Practitioner review is pending.

- **Territory.** The national territory includes the eastern part of Timor,
  Oe-Cusse Ambeno, Ataúro, Jaco and the maritime areas and airspace within the
  national boundaries. Special administrative arrangements can apply to
  Oe-Cusse Ambeno and Ataúro. [Constitution, arts 4–5][constitution].
- **Individual residence.** The test counts 183 days in aggregate in any
  12-month period that starts or ends during the tax year, with an exception
  for a permanent home outside Timor-Leste. A Timor-Leste Government employee
  posted abroad at any time in the year is also resident. [Law No. 8/2008,
  art 1(nn)][tax-act] and [ATTL annual return guidance][annual-return].
- **Entity residence.** A legal person formed, created, organised or
  established in Timor-Leste is resident. This includes the undivided estate
  of an individual who was resident immediately before death. [Law No. 8/2008,
  art 1(kk)][tax-act].
- **Income base.** Residents include domestic and foreign-source amounts in
  gross income. Non-residents include income from Timor-Leste sources.
  Article 49 sets the source rules. Article 50 allows a resident to credit
  foreign income tax on foreign income included in taxable income, subject
  to a country-by-country limit based on Timor-Leste tax on that income.
  [Law No. 8/2008, arts 28(4), 49 and 50][tax-act].
- **Taxpayers.** The income tax provisions cover individuals, undivided
  estates treated as a unit and legal persons, including companies,
  partnerships and trusts. A non-resident's permanent establishment is
  a branch or other permanent local representation registered in the local
  commercial register through which the non-resident demonstrably carries on
  business in Timor-Leste. Article 52 governs profit attribution.
  [Law No. 8/2008, arts 1(jj), 27 and 52][tax-act] and [Law No. 17/2023,
  art 8, amending art 1(u)][pe-amendment].
- **Currency and periods.** The official currency is the United States
  dollar (USD). The ordinary tax year is 1 January to 31 December, with a
  substitute year where authorised. File a required annual income tax
  return and pay the balance by the last day of the third month after year
  end, ordinarily 31 March. [Central bank currency guidance][currency] and
  [Law No. 8/2008, arts 1(e), 62(2) and 63][tax-act].
- **Authorities.** The Autoridade Tributária Timor-Leste (ATTL) administers
  domestic taxes. The Customs Authority collects applicable import duties
  and taxes. [Decree-Law No. 31/2019, arts 2–3][authority] and
  [Customs Authority guidance][customs].

## Other domestic taxes and deadlines

- **Annual income tax.** The ordinary resident individual rate is 0% on the
  first USD 6,000 of annual taxable income and 10% on the excess. The ordinary
  rate on taxable income of non-resident individuals and legal persons is
  10%. Article 72(1) sets a separate 30% rate for contractors within its
  petroleum regime. [Law No. 8/2008, arts 26 and 72 and annex VI][tax-act].
- **Domestic indirect taxes.** The ordinary system uses a services tax on
  designated hotel, restaurant, bar and telecommunications services and a
  sales tax on imports. The services tax rate is 5% of designated receipts
  when monthly turnover from those services reaches USD 500. The government
  described VAT as a proposal for introduction from 2028, subject to
  parliamentary approval and implementation preparations, in November
  2026. [ATTL services tax guidance][services], [Customs Authority
  guidance][customs] and [government budget presentation][vat-proposal].
- **Periodic filing and payments.** Services tax and wage income tax
  withholding returns and payments are generally due on the 15th day after
  the end of the month. Ordinary income tax instalments are due 15 days
  after the month or quarter ends. Article 64 uses the prior year's turnover
  to determine monthly or quarterly instalments. [Law No. 8/2008,
  arts 9, 23 and 64][tax-act] and [ATTL instalment guidance][instalments].
- **Social security.** For employment covered by the general contributory
  scheme, the published rate is 10% of the contribution base, made up of
  6% from the employer and 4% from the employee. [Social security authority
  guidance][social-security].
