# St Helena island ordinary annual employment income reference

> **General reference only.** This is a bounded Tier 2 draft for professional review. It does not certify current law, calculate payroll deductions or authorise a return, refund, payment or complete tax computation. Guide presence and component checks do not establish comprehensive jurisdiction acceptance.

## Scope and source gate

Load both bases in full. Preserve all 4 universal outputs and 18 checks, all 5 income tax outputs and 12 checks, all 13 mandatory income tax country slots, shared refusals and every additional refusal below. A selected reference does not satisfy the complete country contract or permit an incomplete financial deliverable.

This branch is one individual whose full-year tax residence for all 12 months is independently established under the applicable St Helena island law and whose only income is ordinary cash employment income. Establish the actual year, territorial source, classification and complete gross income from every employer. The reference assumes no special exemption, other income, contribution, deduction, credit, relief, prior-year adjustment or contrary direction. Those are established premises in fictional examples, not actual missing-fact defaults. Ascension and Tristan da Cunha have distinct regimes; the SH catalogue code does not apply this island's rules to them.

Before actual calculation, establish the complete direct and indirect Act, amendment, commencement, regulation, Legislative Council resolution, extra-statutory concession and Commissioner direction chain for the period. Sections 6 and 10(1) permit rate and allowance resolutions before their incorporation into the consolidated text. Section 81(2) permits concessions and s 82(4) saves earlier instruments and resolutions. An unchanged public page, a title search or a failed Bill does not establish that no other operative instrument exists. Missing law remains a source refusal.

Preserve these source conflicts and limits:

- **Residence.** Guide 1's 90/183-day boundaries differ from s 2. The statute includes more-than-90, aggregate-more-than-183 and contract-not-less-than-183 wording, travel discretion and preceding/following-year conditions. No residence classifier or part-year allowance is implemented here. Immigration residence is not tax residence.
- **Non-resident allowance.** Guides 1 and 12 deny it categorically, whilst s 10(3)(b) has a St Helena employment-income branch. Non-residents are held; do not turn this reference into a nil-allowance rule.
- **Flat 26% shortcut.** Guide 12's self-assessment step applies 26% without the higher band, although its later summary and Schedule 1 state 31% above the first £18,000 of ordinary chargeable income. Retain the band slices.
- **Filing dates.** A published 31 July date does not replace s 39(1)(a)'s actual certificate-receipt condition. Guide 3 also generalises that condition beyond the refund-only branch. Its extracted calendar cells have not been visually reconciled and are not used. Special notices and departure cases remain held; do not silently repair the published s 39(1)(c)/s 37 cross-reference.
- **Payroll.** Regulations 5–7 require Commissioner tables and directions, including multiple-employer directions and variations. Regulation 15 has qualifying small-employer exceptions to ordinary monthly reporting/remittance. Annual tax divided by 12 or 52 is not a demonstrated PAYE method.

## Intake and selected annual mechanics

Reuse redacted records before grouped questions. Establish the taxpayer reference, actual period, island, full residence decision, source and nature of every receipt, complete gross certificates, all other income and reliefs, tax actually withheld, remittance/authority evidence where relevant, certificate receipt dates, returns/notices, approved reviewer and required calculation/output tools. Present the profile and wait for confirmation before classification. Keep identity, account details and signatures with the reviewer. No legal gap can be answered by asking the taxpayer to guess the law.

1. **Period and terminology.** Section 2 defines the tax year as 12 months commencing on 1 April. The example period is 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027, conditional on established law. Under ss 7–8, local 'taxable income' precedes allowances and 'chargeable income' follows them. Map the generic base's taxable-income rung to local chargeable income explicitly; do not deduct the allowance twice.
2. **Gross employment, s 12.** Use established taxable gross employment income, not net bank wages. Combine ordinary salaries from every employer before the single annual personal allowance and bands. Money paid after 1 April for the immediately preceding tax year has a specific deeming rule; arrears, termination, benefits, pensions and classification disputes require separate analysis. A salary paid abroad does not by itself establish foreign-source income or exemption.
3. **Ordering and limits, ss 7 and 10.** Preserve the statutory deduction sequence and income-category ordering. For the selected salary-only premise with all other relevant items independently nil, let S be established annual taxable salary and A the established full 12-month personal allowance. The retained reference amount is 7,000 source pounds. Deduct at most min(S,A); C = max(S − A,0). An unused allowance does not make C negative or become a cash credit in this component. No contribution, gift or expense is silently set to nil for an actual taxpayer.
4. **Ordinary rate slices, Schedule 1.** The retained reference has 26% on the first 18,000 of ordinary C and 31% on the excess. T = 0.26 × min(C,18,000) + 0.31 × max(C − 18,000,0). With the full 7,000 allowance, the gross-salary boundary is 25,000, not 18,000. A lower primary-production business rate does not automatically apply to an employee working for such a business. Other sources and mixed-rate allocation remain held.
5. **Annual tax and settlement differ.** T is only a conditional annual reference liability before any independently applicable credit or adjustment. A certificate or bank credit does not alone establish a refundable amount, remittance, accepted return or payment. Section 44 credits and s 51 refunds/set-off must be reconciled with s 16's final-PAYE branch and actual evidence. Keep gross income, tax, withheld amount, remitted amount and any accepted credit separate.
6. **Return exception, ss 16, 37–38.** The selected s 38 condition requires employment-only taxable income and tax correctly deducted and paid to the Commissioner. Unknown remittance or incorrect deduction does not satisfy it. Section 16 treats PAYE as final where no annual return is filed pursuant to s 38, including its no-refund condition. Do not infer exemption from filing merely because every bank receipt is labelled salary. Notices, other income and separate duties require their own checks.
7. **Refund-only timing, s 39.** A return filed solely because PAYE exceeds what should have been deducted uses 3 months from actual receipt of the s 15(2) certificate. Section 39(2) limits the further right after that deadline. Other ordinary returns use the separate tax-year-end rule; statutory notices/departures need separate treatment. An extension under s 39(3) is not a general extension of the refund-only branch and s 43(2) preserves the original payment deadline after a qualifying filing extension. Actual dates and calendar interpretation remain held.
8. **Currency and precision.** Section 2A requires the currency of St Helena and states a foreign conversion date tied to the sum's due date to the Commissioner. Do not substitute a bank rate or assume that a foreign payment is already in the filing currency. CU below means a fictional source-unit pound. Keep exact intermediate amounts and distinguish an unrounded mathematical result from a lawful filing amount. No actual rounding, currency conversion or financial deliverable is cleared by these examples.
