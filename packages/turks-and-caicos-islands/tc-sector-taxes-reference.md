### Dated DFSST charge and registration

Use these rules only after establishing the requested period's applicable text and intervening law:

- **Institution:** s 2 identifies a person licensed under the Banking, Investment Dealers (Licensing) or Money Transmitters Ordinance. A business name or ordinary business licence does not establish that status. T3's shorthand mentioning banks and money transmitters does not delete the investment-dealer limb.
- **Charge:** s 4 charges the customer **12% of service fees paid or payable** for an outward transfer from a party using a financial institution within the Islands to a party outside the Islands. The base is the actual fee or a fee assessed under the Ordinance. The amount transferred is not that fee. Domestic transfers and unrelated bank fees need their own applicable provision.
- **Exemptions:** s 5 identifies a company registered as an exempted company under the Companies Ordinance and a person not resident in the Islands. Establish the exact period's category and residence rule. Nationality, an overseas address or a modern company label settles neither limb. Reconcile the historic company reference with applicable Companies law and transition. Do not invent a residence day count or extend customer exemption to all institution obligations.
- **Variation:** s 6 permits rate Orders, including differentiated services/classes; s 19 permits exemption regulations. The omitted Orders and later chain remain unclosed. The compiled rate is not a requested-period currentness certificate. [T1 ss 2, 4–6, 19]

Section 3 requires written registration, register particulars, written changes within **30 days** and cancellation where the institution ceases to be within scope. Its original registration date is **12 October 2011**, not a new-business deadline in 2026. T3 describes mandatory registration and the prescribed form. Acquire the current form, complete instructions and applicable procedure. The changes deadline does not substitute for initial registration. [T1 s 3; T3]

### Dated DFSST assessments, appeals and enforcement

Section 11 permits best-judgement assessments for specified return/record failures and requires notice stating amount and reason. Recovery does not prejudice a further assessment. Assessed tax is payable within **15 days after service of the notice**, even whilst an appeal is pending. Section 11(4) says no assessment under that section after expiry of **5 years**, without specifying the starting event in that sentence. Do not invent that start or import another procedure. [T1 ss 10–11]

Section 12 provides a Supreme Court appeal within **1 month from the date the assessment was made**, on payment of tax in conformity with it. That trigger differs from the payment service trigger. The Court assesses tax, orders repayment of excess where its assessment is lower or confirms and dismisses where the assessment is not excessive; costs are discretionary. No appeal, standstill or extension is filed or assumed here. [T1 s 12]

Section 13 increases unpaid tax or its unpaid part by **10% for each month or part of a month** during which it remains unpaid. Preserve this literal period rule rather than T3's monthly shorthand. No actual penalty, compounding assumption or remission is accepted. Sections 14–15 provide Crown civil-debt recovery and the institution's lien/customer-debt recovery routes. Section 16 restricts disclosure subject to its stated exceptions. Sections 17–18 create specified offences and conditional personal liability, including consent, connivance or neglect and the deemed-director rule. Criminal exposure requires professional review. [T1 ss 13–18]

## Worked cases

### Two conditional arithmetic checks, not complete country positions

Both invented cases assume the selected 31 March 2018 T1 rule applies for a stipulated period, after independent establishment of every omitted-Order, later-law and commencement gate. The institution has the required licence, the transfer is outward from inside to outside the Islands and the service fee is paid and recovered. Exact mathematical dollar units are used; actual currency acceptance, rounding, forms, payable, filing and professional review remain Held. No tax is computed from transfer principal. [T1 ss 2, 4–6, 9 and 19]

E1: an invented transfer of 1,000 has a separate paid/recovered service fee of 25. For the independently established resident, non-exempt customer, 25 × 12% = 3. The transfer principal does not enter this base.

E2: for a paid/recovered fee of 50, compare 2 separately established fact patterns: a resident non-exempt customer gives 6; a customer independently established as non-resident under the stipulated T1 s 5 rule gives 0 under that exemption. The difference is 6. Residence is an evidenced classification, not an election or a day-count test invented here. The zero base is a stipulated qualified exemption outcome, never the default for unknown residence. These checks do not supply the required 5 fully worked positions or execute the 4 outputs.

### Dated DFSST records and monthly returns

The institution accounts for and pays the customer's tax. Keep accounts detailed enough to calculate tax for each customer and retain them for **5 years**. Inaccurate or incomplete accounts require the requested information and evidence to correct them. Section 10 permits specified information, production and copying requirements; a compliance statement may be used in evidence. This retention rule does not shorten separate company-record or other tax duties. [T1 ss 7–10]

Within **15 days after each month ends**, s 9 requires the institution's return in the determined form and remittance of total customer tax paid or payable. Section 9(3) removes the institution's tax liability for services where it has been unable to recover the service fee. It does not establish an unrestricted bad-debt deduction, automatic refund or exemption merely because a customer has not yet paid tax. Establish the facts and current adjustment procedure. [T1 s 9]

Reconcile the fee ledger, customer categories, exemptions, paid/payable amounts, recoverability, return period, remittance and ledger balances. Keep fee and transfer-principal fields separate. Establish currency, precision and payment acceptance from applicable law and the approved procedure. A percentage alone does not qualify rounding. Keep real customer documents and identifiers out of agent evidence.
