# Foreign currency forex realisation gains and losses under Division 775 in Australia

Division 775 of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1997* (ITAA 1997) contains rules under which foreign currency gains and losses are brought to account when they have been "realised", and the ATO states this applies even if the monetary elements of the transaction are not converted to Australian dollars. It applies to any taxpayer — individual, trust, partnership, company or fund — who holds foreign currency, holds a foreign currency denominated account, or has rights or obligations to receive or pay foreign currency, and it operates on an income-year basis. Figures are for tax year 2026. The ATO pages cited below state these amounts without attaching them to a particular income year, so no year-specific variation is shown on the pages read.

## Who this is for

This Guide is for taxpayers who make gains or losses attributable to fluctuations in a currency exchange rate, or to an agreed exchange rate differing from an actual exchange rate, where one of the forex realisation events listed in the table below happens. The ATO notes that the forex measures are contained in Division 775 and Subdivisions 960-C and 960-D of the ITAA 1997, and that common forex transactions include those made through foreign currency denominated accounts, shares and hedging transactions.

It is not for financial arrangements subject to Division 230: the ATO states that "Division 775 does not apply to financial arrangements that are subject to Division 230 of the ITAA 1997". It is also not a guide to the functional currency rules in Subdivision 960-D. For crypto assets, see the stablecoins section below — section 995-1 excludes digital currency, which the ATO says includes bitcoin, from foreign currency, with effect for income years starting 1 July 2021.

## The method, step by step

- **Step 1 — Confirm the amount is foreign currency** — Confirm the amount is "foreign currency". Section 995-1(1) provides that foreign currency means a currency other than Australian currency, digital currency or anything prescribed by the regulations for that paragraph, and that digital currency has the same meaning as in the GST Act. The terms "currency" and "Australian currency" are not defined in the Assessment Acts, so they take their ordinary meaning: see the reasoning in TD 2014/25, which itself applies only to income years before 1 July 2021.  _([s 995-1](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/995-1); [https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=TXD%2FTD201425%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=TXD%2FTD201425%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001))_
- **Step 2 — Check Division 775 applies at all** — Check Division 775 applies at all — it does not apply to financial arrangements subject to Division 230 (TOFA): Foreign exchange gains and losses.  _([https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses))_
- **Step 3 — Identify which forex realisation event happened** — Identify which forex realisation event happened, from the list in the first table above, and confirm the gain or loss is attributable to a currency exchange rate fluctuation or to an agreed rate differing from an actual rate: Foreign exchange gains and losses. A mere fluctuation between currencies without a forex realisation event does not generate an assessable gain or a deductible loss.  _([https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses))_
- **Step 4 — Apply the counting rules where several events happen** — Where several events happen at once, apply the counting rules: generally only one of the five main forex realisation events is counted, using the specific rules in subsections 775-65(1), (2) and (3) for options and forward contracts, and otherwise the residual rule in subsection 775-65(4): Forex realisation events.  _([https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/forex-realisation-events](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/forex-realisation-events))_
- **Step 5 — Work out the gain or loss in Australian dollars** — Work out the gain or loss in Australian dollars by comparing the A$ amount received or paid with the forex cost base or proceeds, following the worked examples on Common forex transactions.  _([https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/common-forex-transactions](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/common-forex-transactions))_
- **Step 5A: receivables, payables and the quotation convention** — For a trade receivable or payable, identify its tax recognition time and Australian dollar amount, compare the Australian dollar amount at settlement, and include only the component attributable to the exchange rate. Use one quotation convention consistently: a rate expressed as foreign currency per Australian dollar requires division to convert foreign currency into Australian dollars.  _([ATO, Forex realisation events — https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/forex-realisation-events](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/forex-realisation-events))_
- **Step 6 — Foreign currency denominated accounts** — For foreign currency denominated accounts, note the ordinary operation of the measures: deposits to, and withdrawals from, those accounts may give rise to a realised gain or loss, and withdrawals from an account with a credit (positive) balance will also generally have a capital gains tax consequence: Forex elections.  _([https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/in-detail/forex-elections](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/in-detail/forex-elections))_
- **Step 6A: withdrawals are usually event 2, not event 4** — A bank depositor holds a right to receive foreign currency from the bank, so a withdrawal from a credit-balance account is forex realisation event 2, and a deposit that reduces a debit balance is event 4. Paying a foreign currency trade payable can involve event 4 for the obligation and a separate event for the currency used to pay it. Apply the ordering rules in section 775-65 where events overlap.  _([ATO, Forex realisation events — https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/forex-realisation-events](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/forex-realisation-events))_
- **Step 7 — Test the exceptions before returning an amount** — Test the exceptions before returning an amount: the private or domestic exception in subsection 775-15(2) with the cost base test in the fourth table above, the pre-commencement rules for currency, rights and obligations acquired or incurred before the date in the third table, and the 12 month rule for short-term transactions involving CGT assets and depreciating assets referred to in the first table.  _(subsection 775-15(2); tables above)_
- **Step 7A: gains and losses have different exceptions** — The private or domestic exclusion and the links to CGT events differ between section 775-15 for gains and section 775-30 for losses. A personal account label does not make every movement private, and an assessable gain does not prove that a comparable loss is deductible.  _(ITAA 1997 (Cth) s 775-15 — https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/775-15; s 775-30 — https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19970038/775-30)_
- **Step 8 — Consider the elections** — Consider the elections. For a qualifying forex account — an account denominated in a particular foreign currency that is either a credit card account or an account held for the primary purpose of facilitating transactions — the limited balance election under Subdivision 775-D uses the limit in the second table; the ATO's page sets out the form of words for a written election and the buffering rule: Forex elections. An account whose primary purpose is to hold money as an investment for a period of time, rather than to facilitate transactions, is not a qualifying forex account.  _([https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/in-detail/forex-elections](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/in-detail/forex-elections))_
- **Step 8A: limited balance election mechanics** — The election is opt-in, made in writing under section 775-230 and kept with the tax records. It covers the specified event 2 and event 4 gains and losses on qualifying accounts, not every disposal of currency, and event 1 on a deposit is unaffected. Aggregate credit balances and debit balances separately across the elected accounts, each within A$250,000 at the prescribed translation rate; the buffering rule allows breaches up to A$500,000 for no more than two periods of no more than 15 days in an income year.  _([ATO, Forex elections — https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/in-detail/forex-elections](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/in-detail/forex-elections))_
- **Step 9 — Consider whether a retranslation election is available** — Consider whether a retranslation election is available or already in place: an entity meeting the common requirements can elect to apply the foreign exchange retranslation method to all financial arrangements and to arrangements subject to Subdivision 775-F, or alternatively to one or more of its qualifying forex accounts: Elective methods.  _([https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/taxation-of-financial-arrangements-tofa/guide-to-the-taxation-of-financial-arrangements-tofa/elective-methods](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/taxation-of-financial-arrangements-tofa/guide-to-the-taxation-of-financial-arrangements-tofa/elective-methods))_
- **Step 9A: retranslation is a separate election** — A retranslation election under section 775-270 brings the account's exchange movement to account through forex realisation event 8 and a periodic closing-less-opening calculation. Do not apply it because the accounting system revalues balances; retain the election, commencement date, account coverage and calculation.  _([ATO, Forex elections — https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/in-detail/forex-elections](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/foreign-exchange-gains-and-losses/in-detail/forex-elections))_
- **Step 10 — Return the result** — Return the result: forex realisation gains are included in assessable income and forex realisation losses are deductible under sections 775-15 and 775-30. The ATO pages cited in this Guide do not name a separate forex form, label or lodgment deadline, and do not set out forex-specific record-keeping requirements — check the taxpayer's normal return lodgment obligations separately.  _(sections 775-15 and 775-30)_
- **Step 11: record and reconcile each item** — For each recognised item record the event, date, foreign currency amount, exchange rate source, Australian dollar calculation, exclusions or elections considered and the final treatment. Reconcile opening balances, deposits, withdrawals, settlements and closing balances to statements, and explain differences between book exchange gains and taxable amounts; an unrealised accounting entry is not itself a forex realisation event.

## Stablecoins and other crypto assets

Section 995-1(1) of the ITAA 1997 now excludes digital currency from foreign currency, and gives digital currency its GST Act meaning. Schedule 2 to the Treasury Laws Amendment (2022 Measures No. 4) Act 2023 made that change, with effect for income years starting 1 July 2021. TD 2014/25 concluded that bitcoin is not a "foreign currency" for the purposes of Division 775, reasoning that bitcoin is not a monetary unit recognised and adopted by the laws of any other sovereign State as the means for discharging monetary obligations. Paragraph 2 of the determination now records the amendment and says it applies for income years before 1 July 2021. The ATO's crypto asset guidance lists TD 2014/25 alongside TD 2014/26 (bitcoin is a CGT asset) and TD 2014/27 (bitcoin as trading stock), and states that the most common use of crypto is as an investment, in which case the crypto asset is a capital gains tax asset.

Because section 995-1 adopts the GST Act meaning, the GST criteria are the statutory test for Division 775 as well. Under section 195-1 of the GST Act, digital currency means digital units of value that are designed to be fungible; can be provided as consideration for a supply; are generally available to the public without substantial restrictions on their use as consideration; are not denominated in any country's currency, or are denominated in a currency not issued by or under the authority of an Australian or foreign government agency; do not have a value that depends on, or is derived from, the value of anything else; and do not give an entitlement to receive, or direct the supply of, a particular thing unless the entitlement is incidental to holding or using the units. The definition also has an exclusion for certain financial supplies, so read the [full text](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=PAC/19990055/195-1). A crypto asset that meets the definition is not foreign currency.

The official pages read for this Guide do not address stablecoins by name for Division 775 purposes. Inference, not an ATO statement: a stablecoin pegged to a fiat currency may fail the value limb, so the digital currency exclusion would not settle it, and whether it is a "currency" in the ordinary sense would still need separate analysis.

For a crypto asset or stablecoin, establish the asset's legal character first and apply the crypto guidance; a price linked to a foreign currency does not by itself settle the Division 775 analysis. A published private ruling binds only its applicant and stated arrangement, so it can identify issues but cannot establish another taxpayer's entitlement.

## Ask the client first

- Is the account a credit card account or held for the primary purpose of facilitating transactions, or is it held to hold money as an investment? This decides whether a limited balance election is even available.
- Has the balance of the account, converted to Australian dollars, ever gone above the limit in the second table, and if so how many times in the income year and how quickly was it brought back?
- When was the currency, right or obligation acquired or incurred — before or after the applicable commencement date in the third table — and was any transitional election made under s 775-150?
- Are the funds private or domestic in character, and what is the first element of the cost base of the relevant CGT asset, measured against the threshold in the fourth table?
- Is the arrangement a financial arrangement subject to Division 230 (TOFA), or covered by a fair value, reliance on financial reports, hedging or retranslation election?
- Is the asset a foreign currency at all, or a crypto asset such as a stablecoin held on a trading platform or wallet?

## When to refuse or refer

- Financial arrangements subject to Division 230 (TOFA) — the ATO states Division 775 does not apply to them.
- Functional currency elections under Subdivision 960-D, and the currency translation rules in Subdivision 960-C, which this Guide does not work through.
- Stablecoins and other crypto assets that may fall outside the GST Act definition of digital currency: the digital currency exclusion does not settle them, and the pages relied on here do not state a Division 775 outcome for them.
- Forex on shares, hedging transactions, options and forward contracts beyond the counting rules in section 775-65 — these need the worked treatment on the ATO's common forex transactions page and, usually, specialist review.
- Reliance on the edited private rulings cited above for a different taxpayer: they bind only their applicant and their facts.
- GST treatment of foreign currency or digital currency transactions.
- Any question needing a reporting label, form name, payment date or lodgment deadline: the pages cited here do not state them.
