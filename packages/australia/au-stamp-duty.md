## Scope

### Australia Stamp Duty (Transfer Duty) -- State & Territory Skill v1.5

## Ask the client first

- In which jurisdiction is the land or other dutiable asset, and who is the acquirer: individual, company, trust or SMSF?
- What are the contract date, transfer date and settlement date? Rate years, concessions and VIC CIPT entry all key off them.
- How is the land classified for duty purposes (SA qualifying/residential land and its land-use code, ACT commercial vs non-commercial purpose, NSW premium residential land)?
- Is any acquirer a foreign person for surcharge purposes, including through a discretionary trust?
- Does an entity acquisition engage a landholder regime (significant-interest threshold, aggregation of prior acquisitions)?
- Is first-home, PPR, pensioner or off-the-plan relief in play, including spouse prior ownership and residence conditions?

#### Core Principles and Conservative Defaults

**Core Principles and Conservative Defaults**

| Principle / ambiguity | Rule / default |
| --- | --- |
| Who pays | The transferee/purchaser, in almost every jurisdiction |
| Dutiable value | The **greater of** consideration paid and the **unencumbered** market value -- a mortgage, lease or caveat does not reduce it |
| Liability event | Identify the statutory transaction and liability date. Contract dates commonly matter; an ordinary TAS land transfer usually occurs at settlement (§3) |
| Concession vs exemption | A **concession reduces** duty; an **exemption eliminates** it (often replaced by nominal duty of $20--$100) |
| Not deductible | Duty on a capital acquisition is a **CGT cost base** item, not an income-tax deduction (§8.2) |
| Jurisdiction not stated | STOP -- duty is state-specific; ask where the land/asset is |
| Transaction dates unknown | Do not compute until the relevant contract, transfer, settlement and concession dates are established |
| Related-party transfer, no valuation | Do not compute -- duty is on unencumbered value, not the stated price |
| Foreign person status unclear | ASK -- never record an unconfirmed status as a determined legal classification. For conservative planning, model the surcharge as a labelled conditional assumption, kept separate from the determined position |
| Landholder/corporate structure involved | Refuse and escalate (R-AU-SD-2) |
| GST treatment of the price unclear | ASK -- an unknown GST treatment is not a default classification. For conservative planning, the GST-inclusive amount may be modelled as a labelled conditional assumption only |

Mortgage duty is abolished in every jurisdiction. Direct transfers of shares and units are, on the retained verification below, no longer dutiable in any Australian jurisdiction -- land held through an entity is instead caught by the **landholder** regimes (§7.1). That abolition row rests on the revenue-office summaries verified 20 August 2026 and the guide's retained provenance, not on a fresh read of each jurisdiction's current amending Act this cycle; for a large or unusual transaction confirm the current Act for the relevant jurisdiction before relying on it. Never tell a client "there is no duty on the share sale" without running the landholder test.

**SA's qualifying-land carve-out**: South Australia abolished duty on **non-residential, non-primary-production** land ("qualifying land" -- commercial, industrial, institutional, most vacant non-residential lots, per s 71DC *Stamp Duties Act 1923*), decided by land use code. An Adelaide warehouse purchase attracts **nil** conveyance duty; a Sydney purchase is ordinarily dutiable. For Melbourne, first check whether the property has entered Victoria's commercial and industrial property tax (CIPT) reform and the transfer qualifies for relief. Confirm the land use code before quoting SA duty.

**SA residential exclusions and vacant-land refund/exemption** (*Stamp Duties Regulations 2013*, version 15.9.2026, rr 4A, 5A, 10C, 10D, inserted by Act 10/2026 commencing 15 September 2026; read 4 November 2026):

- **Reg 4A** excludes purpose-built student accommodation (off-campus, fit-for-purpose with communal areas and facilities, developed and managed exclusively for students enrolled at an educational institution) and land assigned Valuer-General codes 1800 (hotel and motel), 1810 (hotel), 1820 (motel), 1825 (serviced apartments), 1831/1832 (hotel/motel community/other), 1834 (short-term accommodation, multiple units) or 5830 (sanatoria, nursing homes and health centres) from land taken to be used for residential purposes (qualifying-land definition, s 2) and from residential land for ss 72 and 102AB. Land does not fall within reg 4A(1)(b) merely because it is rented for short-stay accommodation.
- **Reg 5A** lists characteristics merely **indicative** that land is capable of being occupied as a residence or accommodation: a room or area designed for overnight occupation, bathroom facilities, a dedicated food-preparation and storage area, laundry facilities or plumbing for them, or any other characteristic the Commissioner considers indicative. Not an all-required checklist.
- **Reg 10C (refund of duty)**: where duty was paid on a conveyance or transfer of land the Commissioner determines was vacant, or vacant with only minor improvements, at the time of transfer, the payer may apply within the relevant period, in the determined manner and form, with evidence that the land was developed for a predominant purpose that would make it qualifying land. The development must be **completed** within the period and be the **first new predominant purpose** after the transfer. A change of use or a division of land is not development. Prescribed period: 3 years, or 5 years where the Commissioner so determines on application.
- **Reg 10D (exemption)**: for land the Commissioner determines is vacant, or vacant with only minor improvements, at the time of the conveyance or transfer, a liable party must apply **at or before** the conveyance or transfer and declare that the land will be developed within the relevant period for a predominant purpose that makes it qualifying land; the Commissioner must be satisfied that it will be so developed and that this will be the first new predominant purpose following the conveyance or transfer. Land counts as so developed only if the development is completed within the relevant period (r 10D(2)). An exempt assessment extends to every instrument giving effect to, acknowledging, evidencing or recording the conveyance. The applicant must notify the Commissioner in writing if the development will not meet the conditions or if information given was false, misleading or incomplete. The Commissioner may revoke at any time; a revocation after the transfer restores duty from the transfer date, assessed on the circumstances at that date using instrument-date rates, treats the determination as the s 20 charging trigger, and can carry discretionary interest and penalty tax as if the non-payment were a tax default. The relevant period ends early where the whole of the land is conveyed onward. There is no automatic exemption for vacant land and no ungranted 5-year extension.

**Victoria CIPT relief**: A standard subsequent transfer can be exempt where the property has a qualifying use and meets the entry-interest or timing conditions. These include a 100% entry interest, qualifying combined or matching interests, or a contract at least 3 years after entry. Partial relief can apply to some interests. For example, SRO's 100% entry on 1 January 2025 followed by a qualifying-use resale on 1 January 2026 is exempt; a 3-year wait is not required in that case. A $1 million resale on those facts has nil transfer duty, instead of the ordinary $55,000. Check the actual entry and transfer history, and the separate rules for non-standard transactions. Relief does not eliminate future CIPT liabilities. See [SRO dealings with properties in the reform](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/commercial-and-industrial-property-tax/dealings-properties-have-entered-reform).

#### 2.2 Related-Party and Non-Arm's-Length Transfers

There is **no market-value relief**. A transfer to a spouse, child, family trust or related company is assessed on the **unencumbered value** even if the stated consideration is $1 or nil. Revenue offices require formal valuation evidence where parties are related, there is no selling agent, consideration is non-monetary, or the same firm acts for both sides (Revenue NSW Ruling DUT 012 v4). Statutory exceptions: matrimonial/de facto property settlements, spouse transfers of the principal place of residence (several jurisdictions), and deceased-estate transfers under a will (§11.1).

### Section 3 -- The Assessment Event

- **Liability date**: Identify the jurisdiction and the statutory transaction. For an ordinary NSW sale agreement, liability generally arises on first execution, usually exchange. That is not a national rule: in Tasmania, an ordinary land transfer usually occurs at settlement. The contract can still determine valuation or concession eligibility. See [Revenue NSW's transaction overview](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/property-professionals-resource-centre/guides-rulings-cpns/topics/contracts-for-sale-of-land-and-transfers/overview) and [SRO Tasmania](https://www.sro.tas.gov.au/property-transfer-duties).
- **Rate year**: Check the relevant jurisdiction's commencement and transitional provisions against the contract, transfer and other required dates. Do not assume settlement selects the rate, or that a concession announced after signing applies to an existing contract.
- **Lodgement and payment are separate**: The timing below concerns ordinary land transfers and was checked against current official sources on 1 November 2026. Special transactions, approved arrangements and extensions can change it. Arrange payment for settlement or registration where required; a later statutory default date does not remove that requirement.

**QLD route checks**: Under s 455A(3), an agent self-assessor ordinarily lodges by the later of 30 days after liability arises or 7 days after receiving all documents needed to assess the transaction. Conditional agreements and approved extensions can alter lodgement. Early lodgement does not itself postpone the 14-day self-assessment payment period. Do not substitute the lodgement period for the payment deadline. See [QRO's lodgement requirements](https://qro.qld.gov.au/duties/self-assessors/essentials/lodging/).

**QLD paper lodgement reference -- home, first home or first home vacant land concession (current public forms, read 4 November 2026)**. *Purpose*: lodge an ordinary QLD residential transfer claiming a concession directly with QRO on paper. *Concession route*: claim on [Form D2.1 v22](https://www.publications.qld.gov.au/dataset/34f50b85-b959-4fe7-af22-f16174507a9d/resource/4f1c115c-f744-438e-9a40-f2292f65bf2a/download/form-d2.1_v22_october2026.pdf) (home, first home or first home (new home)) or [Form D2.7 v17](https://www.publications.qld.gov.au/dataset/93f3d27b-b2af-429e-a8fc-952e026cb125/resource/ff4d8f37-4b66-4def-979c-05ebeca3d255/download/form-d2.7_v17_october2026.pdf) (first home vacant land), both effective 22 October 2026; the concession form replaces Form D2.2 (a D2.2 otherwise accompanies every transfer duty lodgement). *Inputs*: [TR1 Document Lodgement Form (Jan 2026)](https://www.publications.qld.gov.au/dataset/4e9d7256-b8a3-46af-a061-939dc52426fe/resource/b71abd31-86b6-4bab-8589-bf2ccec50215/download/tr1-document-lodgement-form-jan2026.pdf); the concession form; the transfer instrument and the contract; Titles Queensland Form 1 Transfer and Form 24; valuation evidence where required (below); a covering letter listing the documents, your name and return address. TR1 Part A takes the client number and file reference and updated identity/organisation details if there is no client number or details have changed; Part B the correspondence address if needed; Part C **exactly one** primary transaction type ('residential land transfer' for an ordinary home); Part D is office use only. *Evidence*: for related or associated parties, a market appraisal or valuation less than 3 months old, and for residential property one including 3 recent comparable sales (ruling DA505.1; [QRO, what to lodge](https://qro.qld.gov.au/duties/transfer-duty/lodging-paying/what-to-lodge/)); for a new home claim, the vendor statement and GST invoice as applicable per the [QRO first home new home page](https://qro.qld.gov.au/duties/transfer-duty/concessions/homes/first-home-new-home/) (no off-the-plan vendor statement requirement is invented here); evidence of any debt assumed, if relevant. *Field mapping*: D2.1/D2.7 Part A -- property address; lot number, plan type and number, title reference; document date (date the document establishing the transaction was signed); transfer date (normally settlement); related-parties yes/no (yes requires evidence of value); consideration vs unencumbered value and the dutiable value (the higher); D2.1 also asks whether the home is a new building or off-the-plan purchase, the value of any non-residential property, and any existing lease and its expiry; D2.7 asks (from 1 May 2025) whether all the vacant land will be used for residential purposes and, if not, the dutiable value of the residential vacant land portion; D2.7's printed instruction that a pre-1 May 2025 transaction of $500,000 or more cannot use the form is the **historical** cap only. Parts B/C -- each seller's identity, trust name if trustee, and non-Australian yes-box. Parts D/E -- each buyer's identity and trust details, contact details, occupation date (D2.1), claim type (D2.1: home / first home / first home (new home) / no concession, select one; D2.7: first home vacant land / no concession), the specified-resident question for transactions from 1 August 2026 where asked, interests held before and after (whole or fraction), the non-Australian entity yes-box triggering the identity annexure (§4.3), the AFAD foreign-person questions (including agent-for-principal) and, on D2.1, the specified foreign retiree question. Attach an annexure page for additional transferors/transferees beyond the printed two. Part F states the notification obligations; Part G is the declaration -- **all claimants** sign, each signature independently witnessed (not by a transferee, their spouse or a related person), and an administrator under the *Guardianship and Administration Act 2000* may sign for claimants with impaired capacity. A self assessor lodging for a client adds the client/transaction numbers where the form asks. *Submission and payment (direct route)*: post original documents or email scanned/photographed copies; QRO accepts neither payment nor documents in person, and a document lodged directly **cannot settle through the electronic conveyancing network (ELN)**. Sequence: lodgement → QRO assessment notice → pay the duty → QRO returns the endorsed documents (registered post or email, per lodgement method) → register the stamped Form 1/Form 24 with Titles Queensland; duty must be paid and documents stamped **before** registration and before any settlement requirement that needs them. Urgent assessment is requested by covering letter or email at lodgement -- not assumed. *Qualified self assessor route*: a registered self assessor (most solicitors and settlement/lodging agents) assesses and stamps through QRO Online and, where applicable, the ELN, including nil-duty transactions, under its own registration conditions; the actual logged-in portal screens were not reviewed, so no full portal verification is claimed ([QRO, how to lodge](https://qro.qld.gov.au/duties/transfer-duty/lodging-paying/how-to-lodge/); [QRO, self assessor lodging](https://qro.qld.gov.au/duties/self-assessors/essentials/lodging/)). *Deadlines (sources beside them)*: liability for an ordinary agreement arises when the agreement is made (s 16 and [Schedule 2](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/current/act-2001-071)); lodge within 30 days (s 19(3)); agent self assessors by the later of 30 days after liability or 7 days after all documents are received (s 455A(3)); pay a standard self assessment within 14 days after the transaction statement is lodged (TAA s 30(1)(b)) -- early or conditional lodgement does not defer it; pay a Commissioner assessment by the notice date, which must be at least 30 days after the notice is given (TAA s 30(2)), an earlier date being possible on recoverability grounds (TAA s 33) and hardship payment arrangements being conditional and revocable (TAA s 34) ([TAA 2001 ss 30-35, read 4 Nov 2026](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/current/act-2001-072)). *Records and reassessment*: keep the assessment notice, contract and stamped documents for the cost base (§8.2); if circumstances change after claiming, notify QRO within 28 days on Form D2.4 (the concession is then reassessed, with possible penalty tax and interest); retention conditions continue -- occupy as home within 1 year of the transfer date (residential land) and for vacant land build and occupy the residence within 2 years, plus the other printed conditions. The foreign-retiree position stays separate: the first home owner **grant** rules allow renting a room or granny flat whilst the residence condition is met, but entering a lease arrangement can disqualify the transfer duty **concession** depending on timing -- do not collapse either into a general room-rental permission.

**WA later payment periods**: Section 25(2) can allow 12 months after liability arises for a conditional agreement or specified transactions requiring registration; s 25(3) can allow 3 years for an agreement conditional on subdivision or issue of title. These are conditional statutory rules, not permission to leave duty unpaid when settlement or registration requires it. Check the assessment notice and [RevenueWA's payment instructions](https://www.wa.gov.au/service/financial-management/taxation-and-duty/pay-duty).

Thresholds are CPI-indexed each 1 July. Premium duty applies to **residential** land only; on land over 2 hectares it applies to the first 2 hectares proportionally, remainder at general rates. The bands above are Revenue NSW's **2026-27 indexed amounts**: s 32 fixes the rate column and the thresholds are annually substituted amounts under ss 33AC-33AE, so the unadjusted figures printed in s 32's statutory text differ from the current indexed bands. Because each band charges for every $100 **or part** of $100, any fraction of the excess rounds up to a whole $100 (a ceiling). Worked boundary (§10 Example 1): at $800,001 ordinary duty is $11,602 + ceil(($800,001 − $387,000) ÷ $100) × $4.50 = $11,602 + 4,131 × $4.50 = **$30,191.50**, which equals the official FHBAS calculator's saving of $30,171.50 plus the $20 payable; at exactly $800,000 the excess is a whole 4,130 hundreds and duty is **$30,187**.

**QLD eligibility from 1 August 2026**: For transactions entered into on or after 1 August 2026, the home, first home and first home vacant land concessions use the 'specified resident' test in s 90A of the *Duties Act 2001* (Qld). **QLD mixed eligibility**: Under ss 93-93B, specified-resident status is tested for each buyer on the day liability for transfer duty arises. For an ordinary sale agreement, that is generally when the agreement is made. An eligible buyer can receive concession relief on their qualifying interest even if a co-buyer is ineligible. Apply the statutory mixed-claim formula; do not simply apply the progressive scale independently to each buyer's share. In QRO's example, Fiona (citizen, 75%) claims the home concession; Mark (not eligible, 25%) receives no concession and AFAD also applies. Use QRO's calculator for the mixed transaction, entering the ineligible interest as 'no concession claimed'. For an ordinary transfer where AFAD applies, s 244(2)(a) imposes 8% on the transaction's dutiable value to the extent of the foreign acquirer's interest in AFAD residential land, rather than on the concession-adjusted duty amount. **QLD trustee claims**: The exception is limited to trusts other than discretionary or unit trusts whose beneficiaries are all individuals under a legal disability. **QLD new home and vacant land concessions from 1 May 2025 (ss 92A, 92B, 687, actually read 4 November 2026)**: for liability arising on or after 1 May 2025 the first home (new home) concession (s 92A) and the first home vacant land concession (s 92B) have **no value cap**, and the consideration must be at least the unencumbered value (ss 92A(1)(d), 92B(1)(c)). The uncapped concession reaches only the eligible residential portion or interest, not all mixed land: s 92A operates by deducting the Schedule 3 rate applied to the dutiable value of the **residential land** (the land, or part of land, on which the residence is constructed, with qualifying curtilage -- s 86A, s 90), and s 92B by reference to the **residential vacant land** (the part of the vacant land on which the residence is to be constructed, with qualifying curtilage -- s 86C(2), s 90). A mixed-use purchase is therefore assessed on its parts, not given the concession on the whole. Transactions with pre-1 May 2025 liability keep the former capped provisions, and s 687(3) preserves the former provisions for post-1 May transfers that replace a pre-May transfer or land, exercise a pre-May purchase option, or implement a pre-May arrangement whose sole or main purpose was deferring the transaction to obtain the new provisions -- those exceptions are not eligibility loopholes for new transactions. A 'new home' under s 86 includes a home previously sold only as a place of residence under a builders' terms arrangement (s 86(4A), with s 86(6)); s 692 of the *Duties Act 2001* (Qld) (inserted by the *Home Ownership and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2026* (Qld) No. 25 of 2026, s 11) applies those subsections retrospectively to liabilities arising on or after 1 May 2025 ([current Act, ss 692-693](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/current/act-2001-071)). An established (not new) first home uses s 92 and the Schedule 4A stepwise deduction in §5.1, not s 92A.

**QLD vacant land with existing buildings from 22 October 2026 (ss 86C(1A), 87(2)-(3), 693, actually read 4 November 2026)**: land acquired with a building or part of a building on it can still be vacant land under s 86C(1)(b) where each building or part is an **eligible building**. Under s 86C(3) an eligible building means a building or part of a building **other than** a building or part of a building that (a) is fixed to land **and** (b) is either (i) designed, or approved by a local government, for human habitation by a single family unit or (ii) a class 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9 building or part of a building under the Building Code of Australia -- the exclusion requires the complete conjunction of (a) and (b): a fixed ordinary class 1 house is **not** an eligible building, whilst a fixed class 10 shed that is not designed or approved for human habitation is not excluded by that clause and is an eligible building. This definition classifies eligible buildings only; it does not by itself decide the concession. But s 87(2)-(3) provides that a building comprised, wholly or partly, of such an eligible building is **not** the residence for the land -- the residence to be constructed must be a separate building, so constructing the residence wholly or partly by converting the pre-existing shed does not qualify, and the other concession conditions still apply. These new sections apply only where liability arises on or after commencement (22 October 2026); under s 693(2) the former ss 86C and 87 still govern a transfer or agreement made on or after commencement that replaces a pre-commencement transfer, exercises a pre-commencement option, or gives effect to a pre-commencement deferral arrangement. The QRO first home vacant land page's summary that 'there must be no building (or part of a building) on the land' when acquired is **stale** against the current Act and Form D2.7 v17 -- treat it as an outdated summary, not the statutory test ([current Act, ss 86C, 87, 693](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/current/act-2001-071); [QRO vacant land page](https://qro.qld.gov.au/duties/transfer-duty/concessions/homes/first-home-vacant-land/)). The s 246(2) specified foreign retiree definition feeds only the s 90A specified-resident test; the s 246(1) AFAD exemption keeps its own separate property, ownership and occupation conditions, so do not merge retiree renting and occupation rules into the general concession conditions.

**QLD identity details annexure -- confirmed source conflict (current forms read 4 November 2026)**: Form D2.1 v22 and Form D2.7 v17, both effective 22 October 2026, instruct that **each non-Australian transferor and transferee** must complete an identity details annexure, and every transferor/transferee part of both forms carries a 'non-Australian entity' yes-box that triggers it; the guides state that non-Australian individuals **include permanent residents** -- a wider class than the foreign person / AFAD definitions, which ask separate questions on the same forms. **VIC first-home eligibility, residence and notice (ss 57H-57N, actually read 5 November 2026)**:

**Land and PPR transfer (ss 57H-57I)**: the relief runs on a PPR transfer -- an estate in fee simple in land that, in the Commissioner's opinion, carries a building designed and constructed primarily for residential purposes and lawfully usable as a place of residence, or vacant land on which a person intends to construct such a building (ss 57H(1), 57I(1)). The Commissioner **may** treat land as vacant land despite something remaining on it if satisfied the land is substantially vacant apart from the remnant of any building, or any other object or structure, preserved because of its heritage significance, or any building or structure intended to be demolished -- each preserved-or-demolition fact is itself a matter for the Commissioner's satisfaction, and not every old or damaged building makes land vacant land (s 57H(2)). Every transferee must be a bona fide purchaser for adequate consideration and must be one of the following: a natural person who is at least 18 years of age; or, if such a natural person is under a legal disability, a guardian of the person; or, if such a natural person is the principal beneficiary of a special disability trust, a trustee of that trust (s 57I(1)(a)(i)-(ii)). The 18-year condition attaches to the natural person -- the person under the legal disability or the principal beneficiary, as the case requires -- not to the guardian or trustee themselves, and the Commissioner may determine that a transfer is a PPR transfer despite a transferee, person under a legal disability or principal beneficiary being under 18 if satisfied the transfer is not part of a scheme or arrangement designed to take advantage of the benefit of the Division (s 57I(2)). At least one relevant person (transferee, person under the disability, or principal beneficiary, as the capacity requires) must intend to occupy the land as their principal place of residence, and the purchase contract must have been entered into on or after 1 January 2007 (s 57I(1)(b)-(c)).

**Grant context (ss 57JB(1), FHOG Act ss 7-13)**: the exemption and the concession each require that the s 57JB requirements are satisfied at the time of the transfer (s 57JA(1)(a) and (2)(a) respectively), and the first of those requirements, s 57JB(1), is a first home owner grant that is paid or payable under s 7 of the *First Home Owner Grant and Home Buyer Schemes Act 2000* (Vic), or that **would be** payable but for the home not being a new home under that Act and/or its s 13(1A) consideration cap, or -- for s 57H(1)(b) vacant land -- that would be payable if there were a new home on the land at the date of transfer and that Act's s 12 did not apply; an actually paid grant is not a mandatory condition. That Act's ss 7-13 supply the grant context (selected s 3 definitions and ss 4-13 read at version 053, 5 November 2026 -- Provenance): eligibility is assessed at completion and is subject to exemptions (s 7); the applicant must be a natural person at least 18, with a limited Commissioner age exemption (s 8); an applicant must be an Australian citizen, New Zealand citizen (at v053; the SRO page notes different pre-26 November 2025 NZ rules) or permanent resident, one qualifying joint applicant sufficing (s 9). Two prior-benefit bars then apply, each with its own exception:

- **Prior grant (s 10)**: an applicant is ineligible only if the applicant **or their partner** has both been a party to an earlier application under this Act or a corresponding law **and** a grant was paid on that application. The bar is lifted if the grant was paid back (s 10(2)), or if the Commissioner is satisfied that the person who was an applicant in the earlier application does not reside in the home to which it relates because another person subjected that person or a family member of that person to family violence **and** has not received and will not receive any financial benefit from that home (s 10(3)).
- **Prior ownership (s 11)**: both ownership bars are directed at Australian residential property only -- land in Australia with a building on it lawfully occupied as a place of residence or suitable for occupation as a place of residence (s 3) -- so always-vacant Australian land and residential property outside Australia do not trigger either bar. An applicant (or their partner) is ineligible who, before 1 July 2000, held a relevant interest in residential property in Victoria, or an interest in residential property in another State or Territory that is a relevant interest under that jurisdiction's corresponding law; no occupation is required for that bar (s 11(1)); for the purposes of that s 11(1) bar only, any deferment of the right of occupation (because the property was subject to a lease or for any other reason) is disregarded in working out whether a relevant interest was held (s 11(2)). An applicant (or their partner) is also ineligible who, before the commencement date of the relevant transaction, both held such an Australian residential interest **and** occupied the property as a place of residence for a continuous period of at least 6 months (s 11(3)). Despite anything to the contrary in s 11, an applicant is not ineligible where they or their partner held a relevant interest in residential property and the Commissioner is satisfied that the person who held it does not reside in the residential property because another person subjected that person or a family member of that person to family violence **and** has not received and will not receive any financial benefit from the residential property (s 11(4)).

'Partner' in that Act is its own s 3 definition subject to its s 6 non-cohabiting-spouse rule; the Duties Act applies its **own** s 3 'partner' (spouse or domestic partner) to the separate s 57JB(2) test, and the FHOG s 6 rule is not imported into it.

**Prior duty benefit and family violence (s 57JB(2)-(4))**: that s 57JB(2) second requirement is capacity-specific: a personal-capacity transferee or their partner must not have received an earlier s 57JA benefit; a guardian needs both the represented person or their partner clear **and** no earlier guardian-received benefit on the represented person's behalf; a trustee needs both the principal beneficiary or their partner clear **and** no earlier trustee-received benefit on the beneficiary's behalf. The s 57JB(3) exception lifts only the personal/partner bar in s 57JB(2)(a), and only where that person does not reside in the earlier home because another person subjected them or their family member to family violence (s 57JB(4) adopts the *Family Violence Protection Act 2008* s 4 meaning of 'family violence', which that Act sets out in its s 5; that meaning turns on 'family member', which that Act defines in its s 8, but s 57JB does not itself define the words 'family member' in s 57JB(3)(a)) **and** they have not received and will not receive any financial benefit from that home: repaying a grant does not clear this independent prior-duty-benefit test, and there is no general exception for any relationship separation or for the guardian/trustee limbs.

**Residence and Defence (s 57K)**: the transferee must occupy the land as the transferee's principal place of residence for a continuous period of at least 12 months commencing within the 12 months after the transferee became entitled to possession (s 57H(1)(a) land); for vacant land (s 57H(1)(b)), the 12 months must commence within the 12 months after the **earlier** of the date the transferee was lawfully first able to use the building as a place of residence or the date 24 months after entitled possession (the SRO page expresses that second limb as moving in within 36 months from the settlement date; the statute's 24-month date is a reference event measured from entitled possession, and settlement and entitled possession need not coincide). Joint transferees comply if the land is so occupied as the principal residence of at least one of them, whether separately or together with any of the others and whether or not the same transferee occupies it for the whole period; for a guardian or trustee, the represented person's or principal beneficiary's residence counts; and every place of residence of the transferee, in or outside Victoria, is taken into account (s 57K(2)-(3)). A limited exception applies in relation to an exemption or concession from duty under s 57JA only -- of the three s 57K(1A) matters, not the PPR concessional rate and not the s 21(3)/(4)/32V(3)/(4) consideration adjustment -- for members of the Permanent Forces (*Defence Act 1903* (Cth)), tested at the time of the PPR transfer: a single transferee must be such a member and enrolled on the register of electors; joint transferees need at least one member and **all** enrolled. Section 57K(3) applies throughout s 57K, so where a guardian or trustee holds, these tests look to the person under the legal disability or the principal beneficiary, not the guardian or trustee (s 57K(3)-(5)).

**Variation and death (s 57L)**: the Commissioner may, if satisfied there is a good reason, reduce the required period of residence, determine that a temporary absence does not break the continuity of residence, or extend the period in which residence must begin (s 57L(1)). The transferee is not entitled to a PPR concessional rate, s 57JA exemption or concession or s 21(3)/(4)/32V(3)/(4) adjustment in respect of any other PPR transfer during the period of a treated-continuous temporary absence unless the transferee pays the difference between the duty paid on the original PPR transfer and the duty recalculated at the s 28(1) rate without any s 21(3)/(4)/32V(3)/(4) adjustment (s 57L(2)-(2A)); a transferee, person under a legal disability or principal beneficiary who is occupying the land as their principal place of residence, or is temporarily absent under such a determination, and dies, is taken to have complied with the residence requirement (s 57L(3)).

**Failure and notice (ss 57M-57N)**: if the residence requirement is not complied with (s 57M), the transfer is chargeable at the s 28(1) rate without the s 57JA benefit but still subject to any other exemption or concession, and without any s 21(3)/(4) or 32V(3)/(4) consideration adjustment; the liability arises when the requirement is not complied with; the Commissioner **may** reassess accordingly with an allowance for duty already paid, expressly authorised even more than 5 years after the initial assessment; and a transferee who received a relevant benefit in the relevant capacity (personal, guardian or trustee, extending to the person themselves and any subsequent guardian or trustee in that capacity) is barred from a relevant benefit on any other PPR transfer until that duty has been paid (s 57M(4)). Notice (s 57N): a transferee who has received a PPR concessional rate, a s 57JA exemption or concession, or such a consideration adjustment must lodge a written notice with the Commissioner within 30 days after becoming **aware of any circumstances that may result in** the residence requirement not being complied with -- not 30 days after an actual failure -- and a failure to notify does not affect the Commissioner's s 57M reassessment power or s 57L discretion.

([*Duties Act 2000* (Vic) v141 ss 57H-57N, read 5 Nov 2026](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-06/00-79aa141-authorised.pdf); [*First Home Owner Grant and Home Buyer Schemes Act 2000* (Vic) v053 ss 4-13, read 5 Nov 2026](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-11/00-5aa053-authorised.pdf).) Selected primary reads only, 5 November 2026 AEDT: the immigration, relationships, Defence, Electoral and social security statutes those provisions incorporate, the FHOG regulations and other jurisdictions' corresponding laws were not read, so cross-law and procedural points still require verification and this guide's qualified-professional-review position stands.

**TAS established-home first-home exemption and historical concession (ss 57C-57J and 46A-46I, complete, read 4 November 2026)**: the scheme covers first-home **established** homes only -- an established home is a home other than a new home, and 'new home' and 'owner' take their *First Home Owner Grant Act 2000* meanings through Duties Act ss 3 and 57C (a new home is a home that has not previously been occupied or sold as a place of residence, or one that both would be considered new residential premises due to substantial renovations under s 40-75(1)(b) of the *A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999* (Cth) and has not been occupied or sold as a place of residence since those renovations were completed -- substantial renovations alone do not satisfy that branch -- so new homes sit outside this exemption; selected FHOG Act s 3 definitions and ss 4-6 complete were read as context, the GST Act only as FHOG s 3 states it, not read in full). Eligible first home buyer (s 57E): a natural person who has turned 18 -- the Commissioner may exempt a person from the age requirement where they will meet the residence requirement and the transfer is not a circumvention scheme -- who is an Australian citizen or permanent resident, except that under s 57E(3), where there is more than one transferee, one qualifying transferee suffices; the person is disqualified if the person or their spouse has previously owned a home in Tasmania, another State or a Territory, been paid a first home owner grant under the *First Home Owner Grant Act 2000*, attracted the 46E concession on an earlier transfer, or had an earlier transfer exempt under this Division. The Duties Act applies its own 'spouse' definition (s 3: includes a significant relationship under the *Relationships Act 2003*); the FHOG Act s 6 non-cohabitation exception is not imported into it. 'Permanent resident' (s 3) includes a New Zealand citizen holding a special category visa. Eligible transaction (s 57F): a transfer of land on which an established home is situated, occurring within the eligible period, with every transferee an eligible buyer intending to meet the residence requirement and the dutiable value within the cap; s 57F(2) lets the Commissioner waive the all-transferees requirement where satisfied there are good reasons a transferee is not an eligible buyer. Residence (s 57H): each transferee must occupy the home as their principal place of residence for a continuous period of at least 6 months starting within 12 months after the transaction, or a Commissioner-approved shorter occupation or later start -- the Commissioner may approve a shorter occupation under s 57H(2) only if satisfied there are good reasons why the transferee cannot comply with the 6-month requirement, and under s 57H(3) a shorter-occupation or later-start decision may be made at any time before a reassessment under s 57I; a non-complying joint transferee may be exempted where at least one other complies and good reasons exist; each transferee must retain their interest until the requirement is satisfied, and must give written notice within 14 days of a failure (fine not exceeding 100 penalty units). Reassessment is discretionary, not automatic: where the Commissioner becomes aware that the exempt transaction did not satisfy s 57F, or that a transferee failed to comply with s 57H, the Commissioner may reassess the duty under s 57I(1)-(2); s 57J then requires the Commissioner, once satisfied that no duty is payable under the s 57G exemption, to refund any duty paid, and the current SRO page offers refund applications for duty already paid. Expiry does not strip these historical rights, does not bar later applications or refunds as a class, and does not make every property under $750,000 duty-free -- the conditions above decide each case. The SRO page's 'any state or territory' prior-grant wording beside the s 57E(4)(b) literal Tasmanian Act citation is an unpromoted observation, not a proved defect (prior Australian home ownership separately disqualifies under s 57E(4)(a)); unknown prior-grant or prior-ownership facts are ASK/adviser cases.

#### 5.2 Off-the-Plan and Residence Concessions

- **NSW off-the-plan**: Eligible owner-occupiers can obtain an **additional 12 months** to pay. Payment is due at the earliest of 15 months after signing the contract, completion and settlement, or assignment of any part of the contract. Check purchaser status, the agreement and residence requirements; the deferral is not automatic. Deferral changes cash flow, not the duty amount. See [Revenue NSW, off-the-plan purchases](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/taxes-duties-levies-royalties/transfer-duty/understanding-transfer-duty/off-the-plan-property-purchases).
- **VIC off-the-plan** — Deducts outstanding **construction and refurbishment costs** from dutiable value. The temporarily expanded version (all buyers including investors and companies, strata apartments and townhouses, no value threshold) runs from 21 October 2024 and covers contracts entered into **on or before 20 April 2027** ([SRO, Tax Relief Amendment Act 2026](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/about-us/news-and-events/news/building-legislation-and-treasury-legislation-tax-relief-amendment-act-2026)). QLD, SA and NT have no dedicated off-the-plan concession -- ordinary rates apply to the full contract price, subject to any otherwise-applicable first-home or other relief the buyer qualifies for; the dutiable-value rules above (GST-inclusive consideration where GST applies, greater of consideration and unencumbered value) still govern.
- **WA off-the-plan** — Extended to 30 June 2028 for transactions entered into on or after 12 March 2026. The maximum concession rate applies up to $800,000, phasing down to the base concession rate above $900,000, and eligibility now includes new dwellings bought off the plan in survey-strata and community titles (land) schemes. Confirm the amount with RevenueWA's calculator ([WA, 2026-27 Housing Taxation Package](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/announcements/2026-27-housing-taxation-package); [WA, Off-the-plan Duty Concession](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/announcements/the-plan-duty-concession)).
- **ACT off-the-plan and new units** — From 1 July 2026 the price cap is removed from the off-the-plan unit duty exemption, and a Newly Unit Titled Duty Exemption covers owner-occupiers buying a newly constructed, unit titled property not sold off the plan ([ACT Budget updates](https://www.revenue.act.gov.au/about-the-act-revenue-office/news/act-budget-2026-27-updates)).
- **Residence concessions** — VIC PPR concession: dutiable value up to $550,000 with 12 continuous months' occupation, not combinable with the FHB exemption. VIC City of Melbourne: 50% concession or full exemption for new residential property in the LGA up to $1 million (different contract-date windows). QLD home concession: available to any eligible buyer occupying the property as a home, not just first home buyers (§4.3 scale and eligibility test).
- **Pensioner/concession-card relief** — VIC (to $750,000), SA seniors downsizing relief, and jurisdiction-specific equivalents. The ACT removed the price cap from its Pensioner and Disability Duty Concession Schemes from 1 July 2026 ([ACT Budget updates](https://www.revenue.act.gov.au/about-the-act-revenue-office/news/act-budget-2026-27-updates)). One benefit per transaction: an eligible pensioner who is also a first home buyer must choose.

#### 7.2 Business Asset Duty

- **NSW** abolished business asset duty, marketable securities duty and mortgage duty from **1 July 2016**. A NSW business sale is dutiable only to the extent it includes land.
- **QLD** still taxes Queensland **business assets** — goodwill, business names, IP, statutory business licences, debts and some personal property (s 35 *Duties Act 2001*).
- **NT** taxes tangible business property (land, buildings, plant acquired with other dutiable property, chattels, certain statutory licences) but **not** intangibles such as goodwill, trademarks, stock-in-trade, work in progress or livestock.
- **TAS** abolished duty on goodwill and other non-real business assets from 1 July 2008; **SA** and **ACT** have abolished it.
- In a multi-state business sale, apportion the price by jurisdiction and asset class before assuming a duty outcome.

#### 7.3 Motor Vehicle and Insurance Duty

- **Motor vehicle duty** -- NSW and QLD read 5 November 2026; other jurisdictions not read, and each has its own scale and EV settings. NSW (*Duties Act 1997* ss 261-266): charged on an application to register a vehicle, new or to a different registered person, and payable on registration. $3 per $100 or part of dutiable value; for a passenger vehicle of $45,000 or more built for not more than 9 occupants, $1,350 plus $5 per $100 or part above $45,000 (motor cycles, large buses, hearses and invalid conveyances excluded). Dutiable value is the greater of consideration and market value. QLD (*Duties Act 2001* ss 377-383, Sch 4C): charged on an application to register a vehicle, or to transfer one to a different person, and payable on application; a special vehicle bears a flat $25 (ss 377(3), 382(2)(a)). Per $100 or part: hybrid or electric $2; up to 4 cylinders, 2 rotors or steam $3; 5 or 6 cylinders or 3 rotors $3.50; 7 or more cylinders $4. Above $100,000 add $2 per $100 or part on the whole dutiable value, so $4, $5, $5.50 and $6, except vehicles over 4.5 t gross vehicle mass. Dutiable value is the list price with optional equipment for an unregistered vehicle that has one, otherwise the greater of consideration and market value (s 378).
- **Insurance duty** -- NSW, QLD, SA and ACT read 5 November 2026. Charged on the **premium**. NSW (*Duties Act 1997* Ch 8): Type A 9%, Type B 5% (motor vehicle, aviation, disability income, occupational indemnity, hospital and ancillary), Type C 2.5% (crop or livestock) (s 234). Crop and livestock insurance effected or renewed from 1 January 2018 is exempt (s 259(1)(o), (p)), as is commercial vehicle, commercial aviation, occupational indemnity (not medical indemnity), product and public liability cover for a CGT small business entity, on declaration (ss 259A-259C). Duty runs on the total consideration (s 231); that section does not mention GST, and GST's inclusion rests on Revenue NSW's insurance duty page. QLD (*Duties Act 2001* Ch 8): 9% of the premium for class 1 and class 2 general insurance (s 362(1)), 5% of the first year's premium for term life (s 363(1)); s 353 does not mention GST, and GST's inclusion rests on QRO's rates page and Public Ruling DA353.1.2. SA (*Stamp Duties Act 1923* ss 32, 34, 35): 11% general insurance, 1.5% of net premium for life insurance; the premium expressly includes an amount charged to reimburse the insurer's GST. ACT: no insurance duty. Ch 8 of the *Duties Act 1999* was omitted by A2017-11 s 4 from 1 July 2017; an ACT Revenue Office search summary says abolished 1 July 2016, a conflict not resolved here. Insurance duty is embedded in the premium and shows on the tax invoice, not as a separate assessment.

### Section 8 -- Interactions

#### 8.1 GST

- Dutiable value is the **GST-inclusive** consideration. A commercial property sold for $1.1m including GST is assessed on $1.1m, not $1m. The margin scheme does not reduce dutiable value.
- Where the sale is a **GST-free going concern** or **farmland**, there is no GST in the price, so dutiable value is the price -- but the greater-of rule still applies: dutiable value remains the greater of the consideration and the **unencumbered market value**, so a GST-free sale below unencumbered value is still assessed on the value.
- Duty **itself** carries no GST — it is an Australian tax, not consideration for a supply. Code duty payments as BAS-excluded/no-GST; never claim an input tax credit on a duty payment.
- The VIC Digital Duties Form requires GST-exclusive price, GST payable and total to be entered separately; duty is assessed on the total.

#### 8.2 CGT Cost Base -- cross-reference `au-capital-gains`

- Transfer duty on acquiring a CGT asset is an **incidental cost** forming part of the **second element** of the cost base (ss 110-25(3), 110-35 ITAA 1997).
- It is **not** an immediate deduction for a capital acquisition and not a rental property deduction (contrast land tax and council rates, which are deductible while the property is income-producing — see `au-rental-property`).
- Duty on a **leasehold** acquisition or a lease premium may be deductible or amortisable in narrow cases — escalate rather than assume.
- Keep the duty assessment notice with the contract for the life of the asset: it is primary evidence of the cost base, and CGT records must be kept until 5 years after disposal.

#### 8.3 Objections and Reassessment

- Every jurisdiction allows a written **objection** to a duty assessment, generally within **60 days** of the assessment notice, under its Taxation Administration Act; late objections require the Commissioner's discretion. Common grounds: valuation, misclassification of land use (critical in SA), incorrect application of a concession, and aggregation disputes.
- **Refunds/reassessment** — A cancelled contract, corrected concession claim, or a first home buyer who paid at settlement can usually be reassessed; SA allows FHB relief refund applications within 5 years of settlement. Statutory routes read in this cycle: SA reg 10C's vacant-land refund and reg 10D(6)'s revocation-restore rule (§2.1), and TAS s 30HC's developer reassessment (§6). Deadlines vary — verify before relying on one. Objection is administrative review, but drafting the objection is legal work (R-AU-SD-1).

### Section 11 -- Edge Cases

#### 11.1 Deceased Estates

- A transfer from a deceased estate to a beneficiary **in conformity with the will** or on intestacy is generally exempt or attracts only nominal duty. NSW charges a concessional **$100**; Victoria treats it as exempt under s 42(1) *Duties Act 2000* and extends the same treatment to the first transfer into a testamentary trust.
- The concession is fragile: a transfer for **valuable consideration**, an unequal distribution settled with cash between beneficiaries, or a transfer that does not conform to the will can be assessed at full ad valorem rates. Put the grant of probate and the will in front of the revenue office.
- CGT rollover on death is a separate question — see `au-capital-gains`.

#### 11.2 Trust Distributions and Trust Dealings

- An in-specie distribution of real property from a trust to a beneficiary is **generally a dutiable transfer**, even with no money changing hands. Some jurisdictions provide narrow relief for distributions to a beneficiary of a fixed trust where no consideration passes and the beneficiary's existing interest is being satisfied.
- Trust **resettlements**, deed variations, changes of trustee and changes of beneficial ownership are separate dutiable heads in several jurisdictions (NT charges nominal $20 duty on a trust deed). These need jurisdiction-specific advice **before** the deed is signed — after the fact there is usually no fix. Refuse and escalate (R-AU-SD-5).

#### 11.3 Corporate Reconstruction and Consolidation Relief

All jurisdictions offer some form of **corporate reconstruction/consolidation** relief for transfers within a wholly-owned group, but the tests (group composition, pre- and post-association periods, clawback on group exit) differ materially and several require an **application before** the transaction. Relief is discretionary in some jurisdictions and a statutory exemption in others, ranging from full exemption to a 90% reduction. Do not model a restructure assuming relief will be granted (R-AU-SD-3).

**Tasmania, current at 4 November 2026** (*Duties Act 2001* (TAS) ss 226C-226E): No 19 of 2026 s 18 omitted the former group-composition tests in s 226C(2)-(4), including the 90% composition limb, with effect from 9 October 2026; the reconstruction definition in s 226C(1) no longer carries those tests. Section 226E(1)(b) now also covers transactions undertaken to **establish** a corporate group (limb (iaa)), alongside changing a group's structure or asset holdings. The exemption operates **on application** by a party, with the Commissioner satisfied as to purpose and that no duty-avoidance purpose exists (ss 226E, 226F) -- it is not automatic. Earlier 90%-based eligibility assumptions must not be carried forward.

#### 11.4 Other Traps

- **Nominations and sub-sales** — Naming a different purchaser before settlement can create a **second dutiable transaction** (double duty), particularly under Victoria's sub-sale provisions. **Options**: granting or assigning a call option over land is dutiable in several jurisdictions (call option assignment duty in NSW).
- **Chattels** — Chattels sold with land are dutiable in TAS and several others; allocating price to chattels rarely reduces duty and invites reassessment. **Partnership interests**: acquiring an interest in a land-holding partnership can be dutiable as an indirect land acquisition.

### Section 10 -- Worked Examples

#### Example 1 -- NSW first home buyer at the exemption cap (decision case)

Contract 15 August 2026, existing home in Newcastle, price **$800,000**, both buyers eligible under FHBAS.

- General duty: $11,602 + 4.5% × ($800,000 − $387,000) = $11,602 + $18,585 = **$30,187**. FHBAS gives a full exemption at $800,000 or less → duty payable **$0**.
- **Saving: $30,187.** At $800,001 the concession continues: the [Revenue NSW FHBAS calculator](https://www.apps09.revenue.nsw.gov.au/erevenue/calculators/fhba.php), verified 4 November 2026 by an official calculator call for execution date 15/08/2026, existing home, purchase price $800,001 and no shared equity, returns **purchase duty $20** with a saving of **$30,171.50**. The calculator's output separately adds $20 for each duplicate and $20 for each transfer, so the displayed purchase duty is not every instrument's all-in cost.
- Ordinary duty at $800,001 is recomputed from the scale, not observed: $11,602 + ceil(($800,001 − $387,000) ÷ $100) × $4.50 = $11,602 + 4,131 × $4.50 = **$30,191.50**. The $1 over the cap pushes the band excess into a part-$100, which the 'or part' wording ceilings up to 4,131 hundreds. Cross-check: saving $30,171.50 + payable $20 = $30,191.50, agreeing with the official calculator. At exactly $800,000 the excess is a whole 4,130 hundreds and ordinary duty stays $30,187.
- Derived from the directly read s 78A home formula (§5.1): N − (((1,000,000 − V) ÷ 200,000) × D) with V = $800,001, N = $30,191.50 and D = $30,187 (ordinary duty at $800,000) gives $30,191.50 − (199,999 ÷ 200,000) × $30,187 = $4.650935. The concession does not take the payable amount below the s 273 minimum duty of $20, so duty payable is **$20** and the saving is $30,191.50 − $20 = **$30,171.50** -- derived and observed, matching the calculator output above. The calculator separately charges $20 per duplicate paper and $20 per transfer; those are separate charges, not part of purchase duty.
- **Decision**: the $1 price increase raises purchase duty by $20 while eligibility is unchanged, so keep the price at or below $800,000. Both positions are worked above to the figure that decides it.

#### Example 2 -- VIC first home buyer, tapered concession

Contract 3 July 2026, established home in Geelong, dutiable value **$650,000**, eligible first home buyer, principal place of residence, not a foreign purchaser.

- General duty: $2,870 + 6% × ($650,000 − $130,000) = $2,870 + $31,200 = **$34,070**. The phase-in fraction is exact: ($650,000 − $600,000) ÷ $150,000 = **1/3** (0.3333 recurring). It must not be worked as the 4-decimal 0.3333, which yields $11,355.53.
- Exact concession: $34,070 × 1/3 = $11,356.666 recurring. The SRO's official calculator, called with these exact inputs (contract 03/07/2026, established home, PPR, first home owner, not foreign; verified 4 November 2026), returns duty payable **$11,356**, a saving of **$22,714** ($34,070 − $11,356). Do not restate the result as $11,357/$22,713.
- The s 57JA(3) formula was directly read on PDF page 239 (printed page 221) of *Duties Act 2000* (Vic) version 141 on 4 November 2026: the concession is ((A − $600,000) ÷ $150,000) × B, where A is the dutiable value and B is the duty paid or payable but for s 57JA on the PPR transfer. What remains unestablished is the final rounding step: s 118(1)-(2) of the *Taxation Administration Act 1997* (Vic) (version 092, effective 2025-11-26, ss 3, 4, 7, 118 read 4 November 2026) permits the Commissioner to **decrease** a fractional amount of tax, single or aggregated, to an amount not below the nearest dollar -- a discretion, not a mandatory floor, and nothing read shows which rounding produced the observed $11,356. Keep the exact $11,356.666 recurring, the observed $11,356 and any universal rounding rule distinct; do not generalise to other concessions or jurisdictions, and treat the arithmetic checker's ROUND_HALF_UP as tool behaviour, not law. The s 28(1) whole-dollar rounding read above (nearest whole dollar, exact 50c down) applies to the **general** duty charge; whether and how it interacts with the final concession amount here remains this open point, not a settled rule.
- Nil duty at $600,000, full duty at $750,000.

  - phase_in_fraction: (dutiable_value - 600000) / 150000
  - exact_concession: general_duty * phase_in_fraction

  exact_concession: 11356.67
  duty_payable: 11356

#### Example 3 -- VIC aggregation across two lots

One purchaser buys two adjoining vacant lots from the same vendor under one arrangement, **$480,000 each**.

- Assessed separately: $2,870 + 6% × $350,000 = $23,870 each → **$47,740**. Aggregated at $960,000: $2,870 + 6% × ($960,000 − $130,000) = $2,870 + $49,800 = **$52,670**.
- **Extra duty from aggregation: $4,930.** Watch the notch immediately above: at $960,001 the flat 5.5% band applies to the *whole* value ($52,800 -- precisely, 5.5% × $960,001 = $52,800.055 assessed as $52,800 under the s 28(1) nearest-whole-dollar rounding, exact 50c down), so a small price rise costs more than the increment.

#### Example 4 -- QLD foreign purchaser, investment property

A foreign-owned company contracts on 10 August 2026 to buy a Brisbane residential unit for **$1,300,000**. No home concession (not a residence).

- Transfer duty: $38,025 + 5.75% × ($1,300,000 − $1,000,000) = $38,025 + $17,250 = **$55,275**. AFAD: 8% × $1,300,000 = **$104,000**.
- **Total duty: $159,275**. The $104,000 surcharge adds about **188%** of the $55,275 base duty; it represents about 65% of the total. An Australian citizen or permanent resident occupying the unit would instead use the home concession scale: $30,850 + 5.75% × $300,000 = **$48,100**.

#### Example 5 -- NSW landholder duty, 60% share acquisition (scale illustration only)

A private company holds NSW land with an unencumbered value of **$4,000,000** plus goods of **$500,000**. An investor acquires **60%** of the shares on 1 August 2026 — a relevant acquisition (50%+ in a private landholder; land above the $2m threshold).

- Dutiable value: 60% × ($4,000,000 + $500,000) = **$2,700,000**. Duty: $52,237 + 5.5% × ($2,700,000 − $1,290,000) = $52,237 + $77,550 = **$129,787** on the scale alone. Surcharge purchaser duty may also apply if the land is residential and the acquirer is foreign.
- **This illustrates the threshold test and how the scale engages only.** It is not a payable computation: real landholder calculations trace linked entities, prior acquisitions in the aggregation window and constructive interests -- refuse and escalate (R-AU-SD-2).

#### Example 6 -- QLD first home concession, $730,000 established home (published known case)

Contract 4 November 2026, established home in Brisbane, dutiable value **$730,000**, wholly residential, every buyer an eligible first home buyer and specified resident, consideration at least unencumbered value.

- s 91 home concession duty: $10,150 + $4.50 per $100 or part over $540,000 = $10,150 + 1,900 × $4.50 = **$18,700**. Schedule 4A row $730,000--$739,999.99: deduction **$12,145**. Duty payable: $18,700 − $12,145 = **$6,555** -- exactly QRO's published example ([QRO first home concession](https://qro.qld.gov.au/duties/transfer-duty/concessions/homes/first-home/)).
- The deduction is the fixed Schedule 4A step, **not** an interpolation: a linear taper would produce a different, wrong figure (§5.1). The deduction is from the s 91 home-concession duty, not from ordinary duty.
- First-row edges, same whole-property facts (wholly residential established home, whole interest, every buyer an eligible first home buyer and specified resident, consideration at least the unencumbered value, so each stated value is itself the s 11(7) dutiable value of the residential land, s 91 plus Schedule 4A only; statutory-rate result, no final administrative rounding proved): at **$700,001** the s 91 duty is $10,150 + ceil($160,001 ÷ $100) × $4.50 = $10,150 + 1,601 × $4.50 = **$17,354.50**, less the unchanged first-row deduction $17,350 = **$4.50**; at **$709,999.99** the s 91 duty is $10,150 + ceil($169,999.99 ÷ $100) × $4.50 = $10,150 + 1,700 × $4.50 = **$17,800**, less $17,350 = **$450.00**. Nil ordinarily ends at $700,000: the deduction stays $17,350 to $709,999.99 whilst the s 91 duty rises (§5.1).
- Value-type counterexample, known consideration above known unencumbered value (whole interest, no s 48 or apportionment case, no surcharge or other adjustment): eligible wholly residential established first home, consideration **$730,000**, unencumbered value **$650,000**. s 11(7) takes the consideration as the dutiable value (there is a consideration, it is ascertainable when the liability for transfer duty arises, and the unencumbered value is not greater than it), s 90 attributes it to the residential land, and the s 92(1)(c)(i) eligibility limb is met because the unencumbered value is not more than $700,000 -- yet the s 92(2) result is duty, **not nil**: $10,150 + 1,900 × $4.50 = **$18,700** s 91 duty, less the Schedule 4A $730,000--$739,999.99 deduction **$12,145** = **$6,555**, the same figure as the published case. Nil tracks the dutiable value of the residential land, never the unencumbered value (§5.1). Unknown consideration or unencumbered value, mixed interests, and s 48/apportionment cases are outside this example: ASK.

#### Example 7 -- QLD charging edge at $75,001 (or-part component)

The **Schedule 3 table application only**, assuming an ordinary dutiable transaction with dutiable value **$75,001**, no concession, QLD law current at 4 November 2026.

- Second band: $1,050 + $3.50 per $100 **or part** over $75,000. The $1 excess is one part of $100 (a ceiling), so the band adds ceil($1 ÷ $100) = 1 × $3.50: total **$1,053.50**.
- The naive pro-rata $1,050 + 3.5% × $1 = **$1,050.035** is wrong: Schedule 3 charges 'for each $100, or part of $100'. No final administrative rounding rule for QLD is proved by anything read (the WA whole-dollar floor and the NT 5-cent floor are other jurisdictions' rules), so none is applied to this component.

steps:

  - excess_over_75000: dutiable_value - 75000
  - charged_hundreds: ceil(excess_over_75000 / 100)

#### Example 8 -- QLD mixed use and unknown status (decision case: ask, do not invent)

A buyer contracts for a QLD property at **$640,000** described as 'house with a home business on part of the lot', one of two buyers is not an Australian citizen, and prior ownership is unclear.

- **Do not compute a nil or a split figure from assumed facts.** Ask for: the portion of dutiable value attributable to the residential land (s 90 attribution and the s 86/86C definitions); each buyer's claim type and specified-resident status on the day liability arises; whether it is every buyer's first home; the unencumbered value against the consideration (the s 92(1)(c) market-consideration condition applies above $700,000, including by cents); any foreign person / AFAD interest (s 244); and the agreement date against the 1 May 2025 (s 687), 1 August 2026 (s 90A) and 22 October 2026 (ss 86C(1A)/87 via s 693) boundaries.
- Then apply the correct route: the s 93-93B mixed-claim machinery for a part-eligible buying group (§4.3), the residential-portion attribution for mixed land (§4.3), and the Schedule 4A table only for an established-home first home (§5.1). A mixed, non-residential or unknown-status fact pattern is a fact-gathering step, never an invented nil.

## When to refuse or refer

### Section 12 -- Refusal Catalogue

- **R-AU-SD-1 -- Duty clause drafting** — Trigger: request to draft, review or amend a duty clause, special condition, objection letter or contract term. Message: "Drafting or reviewing contractual duty clauses is legal work. Refer to a property solicitor or licensed conveyancer in the relevant jurisdiction."
- **R-AU-SD-2 -- Complex landholder duty structures** — Trigger: multi-entity groups, linked or tracing interests, staged acquisitions, unit trust conversions, or any landholder analysis beyond confirming the threshold is engaged. Message: "Landholder duty across linked entities requires specialist state taxes advice. This skill can identify that the regime is engaged, not compute the liability."
- **R-AU-SD-3 -- Corporate reconstruction and consolidation relief** — Trigger: intra-group restructure, demerger, group consolidation, or a request to confirm relief eligibility. Message: "Corporate reconstruction relief has jurisdiction-specific pre-approval and clawback conditions. Escalate before executing any transaction."
- **R-AU-SD-4 -- Multi-jurisdiction and foreign share/unit acquisitions** — Trigger: an acquisition of shares or units where the entity holds land in more than one jurisdiction, a landholder regime is engaged across borders, or a non-resident acquirer requires concurrent analysis under each Duties Act plus FIRB. Message: "Multi-jurisdiction and cross-border acquisitions require concurrent analysis under each Duties Act plus FIRB. Escalate to a state taxes specialist." An ordinary domestic share or unit transfer with no landholder duty engaged is not by itself a refusal trigger; the abolition rule in §2.1 stands.
- **R-AU-SD-5 -- Trust distributions and dealings in dutiable property** — Trigger: in-specie distribution, resettlement, deed variation, change of trustee, or discretionary-trust foreign-beneficiary questions. Message: "Duty on trust dealings in dutiable property is jurisdiction-specific and depends on the deed. Obtain advice before the deed is executed."
- **R-AU-SD-6 -- Valuation disputes and objections** — Trigger: client disputes the Commissioner's valuation or land-use classification. Message: "Valuation objections require a qualified valuer's report and a formal objection within the statutory period. Escalate."
