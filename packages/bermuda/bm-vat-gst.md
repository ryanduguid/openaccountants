# Bermuda VAT / GST

## Scope and evidence periods

This guide preserves the unresolved VAT/GST question and supplies selected import-duty controls. The numerical tariff illustrations use the Government's posted First Schedule, effective from **1 April 2026**, with its stated goods codes and source pages. The linked Second to Seventh Schedules supply selected relief conditions. These are selected provisions, not a review of every tariff row. Later amendments and beneficiary-specific notices must be checked for an actual import. The guide's update date does not certify that no later law exists. [Posted schedules and currency notice](https://www.gov.bm/schedules-customs-tariff-act-1970) · [Act 2026:10, ss 2–4](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2026/Customs%20Tariff%20Amendment%20Act%202026)

The later retail amendment was assented to on **30 June 2026** and operates retrospectively from **1 April 2026**. It changes a relief deadline; it does not turn every computer into a duty-free import. Historical 2025 tariff amounts, actual importer facts and accepted filing remain separate work. [Act 2026:17, ss 2–3](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2026/Retail%20Shops%20%28Temporary%20Customs%20Duty%20Relief%20For%20Capital%20Investments%29%20Amendment%20Act%202026)

## VAT / GST evidence gap

The inherited guide reported no VAT, GST or general sales tax and therefore no registration threshold, return or reverse charge. Current affirmative primary evidence for those conclusions has not been established. Treat them as **Research Gap / Needs Input**, not verified inapplicability. The presence or amount of customs duty does not resolve that question. [Tax-system evidence boundary](bm-tax-overview.md#claims-still-requiring-evidence)

- **VAT / GST / general sales tax:** obtain affirmative current primary evidence before an absence conclusion.
- **VAT registration threshold:** the prior 'not applicable' conclusion depends on establishing that absence; do not invent a threshold or report zero.
- **VAT return / filing frequency:** the unresolved absence claim does not establish that no customs or other indirect-tax return exists.
- **Reverse charge:** the prior 'not applicable' conclusion remains conditional on resolving the VAT/GST evidence gap.

The [OTC inventory](https://www.gov.bm/department/tax-commissioner) and [Customs Department](https://www.gov.bm/department/customs) identify separate charges and responsible bodies. Neither departmental list proves that the VAT/GST absence claim is complete.

## Classification, route and source periods

1. Establish the import event and date, goods specification, ownership and intended use. Identify the legal tariff heading/subheading, Section and Chapter Notes and duty unit under the General Rules. An invoice label such as 'electronics', 'building materials' or 'general merchandise' does not establish a rate. The importer remains responsible when using an agent. [Customs Tariff Act 1970, ss 1–2](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/59bd7229-2025_customs_tariff_act_1970_consolidation_as_at_2025.06.27.pdf) · [First Schedule, General Rules 1–6, pp 1–2](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/e1311751-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_first_schedule_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf) · [Official classification guidance](https://www.gov.bm/classification-imported-goods)
2. Establish the route. 'Personal goods' is a statutory ownership/use category, including personal or professional use and excluding sale, lease or other commercial exploitation. Accompanied and unaccompanied goods have separate definitions, including baggage and duty-free-shop qualifications. Residency alone does not classify goods imported for resale as personal. [First Schedule, Chapter 98 note 1, p 447](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/e1311751-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_first_schedule_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf)
3. Where applicable, Special Rules 1–2 prefer headings **98.01 / 98.02** over another heading, unless the other heading's rate is **0%** or a **percentage greater than 25%**. A lower positive ordinary rate does not itself prevail. These rules do not establish a universal 25% ceiling. The Collector may require another applicable heading under s 2A(3). [First Schedule, p 2](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/e1311751-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_first_schedule_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf) · [Act, s 2A](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/59bd7229-2025_customs_tariff_act_1970_consolidation_as_at_2025.06.27.pdf)
4. Post or a courier licensed under Post Office Act s 6A can use heading **98.03** or another applicable heading, subject to the Collector's power to reject that choice under s 2A(2). Do not carry a simplified postal rate into an accompanied-personal or ordinary cargo example. [Act, s 2A](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/59bd7229-2025_customs_tariff_act_1970_consolidation_as_at_2025.06.27.pdf) · [First Schedule, pp 449–451](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/e1311751-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_first_schedule_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf)
5. Determine the statutory value, remaining allowance and any valid relief before calculating. Establish each approval, beneficiary, goods, end-use and date condition. Record separate charges separately; the examples below calculate customs duty only.

## Statutory customs value

Use **Revenue Act 1898, s 24 and its Second Schedule**, rather than assuming the invoice total or CIF amount is the duty base. The starting transaction value is the price actually paid or payable for goods sold for export to Bermuda, adjusted under Part 8 and subject to the conditions on restrictions, sale conditions, seller proceeds and related parties. If that method cannot apply, follow the statutory sequence for identical goods, similar goods, deductive/computed value and reasonable means; Part 4 permits the specified importer-requested reversal of the deductive and computed methods. Arbitrary, minimum or higher-of-two values are not substitutes. [Revenue Act, s 24 and Second Schedule Parts 1–8, pp 25 and 82–87](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1898/Revenue%20Act%201898)

Part 8 adds qualifying commissions/brokerage except buying commissions, packing/containers, apportioned assists, condition-of-sale royalties/licence fees and seller resale proceeds to the extent not already included. It requires objective and quantifiable data. Part 8(2) excludes transport to the port or place of importation, associated loading/unloading/handling charges and insurance. Missing sale, relationship or adjustment facts are **Needs Input**. Use the Collector-published exchange rate in effect at importation and retain the evidence reasonably required under s 24(2). The Schedule's interpretative Annex is integral; s 24(4) also permits additional or derogating regulations, which must be checked for an actual decision. [Revenue Act, s 24 and Second Schedule Parts 1–9 and 14](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1898/Revenue%20Act%201898)

## Personal allowances

Under the posted First Schedule, **9801.415** covers other accompanied personal goods imported by an **islander**, with one aggregate **BMD 300** allowance. An islander is a natural person entitled to reside under the Bermuda Immigration and Protection Act; a tax-residence label alone does not establish that definition. **9801.420** gives a qualifying visitor one aggregate **BMD 50** allowance; a visitor is a natural person not entitled to reside. Chapter 98 note 4 allows a part value up to the available limit, so a qualifying higher-value item is not automatically excluded from all allowance. [Act, s 1](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/59bd7229-2025_customs_tariff_act_1970_consolidation_as_at_2025.06.27.pdf) · [First Schedule, Chapter 98 notes 1–4 and 9801.415/.420, pp 447–448](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/e1311751-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_first_schedule_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf)

The specified alcohol/tobacco allowances require the importer to be **18 or over**: **1 litre of spirits, 1 litre of wine, 200 cigarettes, 0.5 kg of tobacco and 50 cigars**, subject to the applicable codes and note 2's one aggregate quantity per person under each code. Excess alcohol/tobacco has its own specific units and rates; do not apply 25% to every excess item. Awards, inherited effects, used personal effects, transfer of residence and re-export have distinct codes and conditions. [First Schedule, Chapter 98, pp 447–448](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/e1311751-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_first_schedule_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf)

The [allowance guidance](https://www.gov.bm/duty-free-allowances) also mentions baggage within 90 days and CPC 4413. Their precise legal route was not separately validated here. Establish the actual baggage, goods, purpose and available allowance before relying on that treatment.

## Conditional relief and later notices

For Fifth Schedule relief, establish the eligible beneficiary, exact goods, end use, restrictions, controls and authorisation. The eligible beneficiary applies using the customs declaration and specific CPC at import/removal or within the prescribed **12 months**. The Collector may grant or refuse relief, must be satisfied about the stated conditions and absence of duty avoidance and may impose tracing/checking conditions. Do not treat an 'all importers' beneficiary description as dispensing with the goods or end-use tests. [Act, s 5(5)–(6)](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/59bd7229-2025_customs_tariff_act_1970_consolidation_as_at_2025.06.27.pdf) · [Fifth Schedule general provisions, p 7](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/a0b14e6f-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_second_to_seventh_schedules_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf)

Selected posted relief branches include:

- **CPC 4247, 0%:** goods of headings **92.01–92.09**, used only by a local professional instrumentalist. The definition requires an islander who plays or teaches others to play a musical instrument for consideration; a hobby alone is insufficient. [Fifth Schedule, p 49](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/a0b14e6f-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_second_to_seventh_schedules_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf)
- **CPC 4248, 0%:** costumes/accessories and costume or float-making materials imported for and used by Bermuda Day Parade participants; **vehicles and tools are excluded**. [Fifth Schedule, p 50](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/a0b14e6f-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_second_to_seventh_schedules_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf)
- **CPC 4703 / 4705:** the retained Acts reduce the specified BELCO diesel/fuel-oil rates from **BMD 0.08 to BMD 0.04 per litre from 1 July 2025**, then to **BMD 0.00 from 1 April 2026**. Exclusive BELCO use and the other CPC conditions remain necessary. These are not rates for every fuel importer. Later restricted fuel notices require their own beneficiary, goods, bonded-removal and period checks. [Act 2025:7, ss 2–4](https://bermudalaws.bm/Document/%7B3d4d351a-3001-4ec0-b789-ac5f60bd4dc2%7D) · [Act 2026:10, ss 2–4](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2026/Customs%20Tariff%20Amendment%20Act%202026) · [Fifth Schedule, p 51](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/a0b14e6f-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_second_to_seventh_schedules_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf)

### Approved retail capital investment

The **Retail Shops (Temporary Customs Duty Relief for Capital Investments) Act 2008** supplies a separate relief route. A retailer applies in writing for a qualifying retail-shop scheme; the Minister may refuse, approve or attach conditions and communicates the outcome in writing. Section 6 provides **0% ad valorem** for goods to which an approval applies, notwithstanding a different First Schedule rate. The goods must be listed in the Act's Schedule, which expressly includes **heading 84.71**. The Minister decides Schedule-scope questions under s 6(3). Neither a computer label nor an ordinary tariff code proves approval or eligibility. Older codes in the retail Schedule must not be mapped automatically to current camera/TV codes. [Principal Act, ss 2–6 and Schedule p 8](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/2008/Retail%20Shops%20%28Temporary%20Customs%20Duty%20Relief%20for%20Capital%20Investments%29%20Act%202008)

As amended by **Act 2026:17**, s 7(2)(b)(i) requires import **on or before 31 March 2031**. Section 7(2)(b)(ii) requires the Collector to receive the written claim **not later than one calendar month after that statutory date**, or a later date the Minister may allow. The claim month does not run from each actual import date; the later-date permission does not itself extend the separate import cutoff. The claimant is the retailer or the person in Bermuda who supplied the goods to it, with the applicable s 8 duties and Collector directions. [Principal Act, ss 7–8](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/2008/Retail%20Shops%20%28Temporary%20Customs%20Duty%20Relief%20for%20Capital%20Investments%29%20Act%202008) · [2026 amendment, ss 2–3](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2026/Retail%20Shops%20%28Temporary%20Customs%20Duty%20Relief%20For%20Capital%20Investments%29%20Amendment%20Act%202026)

Section 2 defines the 'statutory period' by reference to the principal Act's commencement. The 2026 amendment extends the import cutoff; it does not establish a new rolling 3-year period from an import or from that amendment. The actual inspection/disposal application of ss 8 and 10, approval conditions, cancellation and Collector procedures remain **Needs Input** before an actual claim. [Principal Act, ss 2 and 7–10](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/2008/Retail%20Shops%20%28Temporary%20Customs%20Duty%20Relief%20for%20Capital%20Investments%29%20Act%202008)

Later notices can add named beneficiaries, specified goods and expiry dates without changing an ordinary tariff row. For example, **BR 131/2026**, operative **12 November 2026**, approves specified technology infrastructure and equipment for its named centre through **31 March 2028**, subject to its stated CPC 4237 and other conditions. It does not approve every technology importer. Different CPCs can retain positive rates; matching a beneficiary does not establish zero duty. Other temporary notices, valuation regulations and later law still require checking. [BR 131/2026, paras 2–4](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Statutory%20Instruments/2026/Customs%20Tariff%20%28Approved%20Businesses%20-%20CPC%204237%29%20Temporary%20%28No.%207%29%20Notice%202026) · [Fifth Schedule general and CPC conditions](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/a0b14e6f-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_second_to_seventh_schedules_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf)

## Declaration controls and other charges

**BR 109/2026**, operative **1 October 2026**, replaces traveller declaration rules, instructions and Form 98. 'Goods to declare' include the specified taxable accompanied personal goods, transfer-of-residence goods under **9801.411**, restricted/prohibited goods and cash, negotiable instruments, precious metals or precious stones with total value **greater than BMD 10,000**. The value threshold is not a cash-only rule. [BR 109, ss 2–6 and 8, pp 2–4 and 11–12](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Document/%7B68161f46-0415-4638-9f13-8aec2993708b%7D)

For air arrivals, use Form 98 in the red channel where available when goods must be declared; otherwise deliver it directly to an officer. With nothing to declare, use the green channel where available; without a dual-channel system, deliver Form 98 to an officer. Commercial-vessel masters/crew use Form 81, pleasure craft use Form 98 and commercial passengers follow their separate goods/channel rule. Departure has separate BR 109 rules; do not apply the arrival shorthand to every departure. [BR 109, pp 2–4 and completion instructions pp 7–11](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Document/%7B68161f46-0415-4638-9f13-8aec2993708b%7D)

The official allowance page still links **Form 98 version 4/16**, showing BMD 200. The Government's **2 October 2026** notice states that version 3/26 took effect on 1 October and that 4/16 was accepted only through **31 October 2026**. That old attachment is not a verified current filing form. The enacted form images at BR 109 pp 5–6 and a current standalone form have not been visually/OCR checked in this review; no form-field or filing-readiness assurance follows from the native instructions. [Official form-transition notice](https://www.gov.bm/news/new-customs-traveller-declaration-now-in-effect) · [Stale linked form](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/a5073889-customs-traveller-declaration_2.pdf)

The [declaration guidance](https://www.gov.bm/declaring-goods-customs) distinguishes accompanied personal goods, commercial or other persons' goods in baggage, post, courier and cargo, including separate BCD/CAPS requirements. Some guidance predates BR 109 and must be read with the enacted rules. Authenticated CAPS/WebTrader operations, current acceptance and payment have not been tested. The guidance also mentions wharfage and postal processing charges, but this review has not captured their charging authority, bases and exemptions. A customs-duty illustration is not a complete landed-cost total or evidence that other charges are zero.

## Manual boundary cases

These are conditional desk checks using the specified source periods. They do not classify actual goods, verify an approval or establish an accepted filing.

1. **Unspecified electronics, BMD 5,000:** **Needs Input**. Obtain import date, product/code, ownership/use, route, statutory value/currency, remaining allowance, relief/approval and separate-charge facts. Neither resident status nor an invoice amount supplies a default duty rate.
2. **Ordinary camera:** assume code 8525.890, statutory value BMD 5,000, ordinary cargo treatment, no relief and no later overriding instrument. The selected April 2026 row gives **BMD 500 customs duty** at 10%.
3. **Accompanied personal camera:** assume the same BMD 5,000 value, a qualifying islander, statutory personal/accompanied status, only qualifying other goods, BMD 300 allowance still available and no Collector requirement for another heading. The Chapter 98 25% route and part-value allowance give **BMD 4,700 dutiable value and BMD 1,175 duty**. A lower ordinary 10% rate is not a free choice under Special Rule 1.
4. **Ordinary specified computer or television:** assume code 8471.300 or 8528.720, statutory value BMD 5,000, ordinary route, no relief and no later overriding instrument. The selected 25% rows give **BMD 1,250 duty**. The computer example must branch again if approved retail relief is established.
5. **Zero-rate exception:** properly classified goods under the selected zero-rated 8523.512 row do not become 25% merely because they are personal goods. Establish the product and applicable code. An ordinary percentage greater than 25% also requires Special Rule 1's exception analysis; 25% is not a universal cap. [First Schedule, pp 2 and 388](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/e1311751-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_first_schedule_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf)
6. **Approval and cutoff:** heading 84.71 goods covered by a valid retail-scheme approval, an entitled claimant and all applicable claim/direction requirements can use s 6's 0% branch. An unapproved importer cannot use it from the heading alone. Import on 1 April 2031 fails the stated normal cutoff; the claim-date permission alone does not authorise that later import. Do not invent a claim month from each actual import or restart the statutory period.
7. **Purpose and ownership:** a resident's goods for resale do not become personal goods from residence alone. Goods owned by another person need their own declaration/classification path. Statutory personal or professional use still requires Chapter 98's ownership and non-commercial-exploitation conditions.
8. **Age and quantities:** a 17-year-old is outside the stated alcohol/tobacco allowances. At 18 or over, establish each code's quantity and the one aggregate allowance; excess goods need their applicable duty units and rates.
9. **One value allowance:** the islander's BMD 300 and visitor's BMD 50 are the respective one-person aggregate limits for qualifying other goods, with part-value use. Do not subtract a fresh allowance from every item or treat an exhausted allowance as available.
10. **Identified transport and insurance:** assume a qualifying transaction-value sale, a BMD 5,000 invoice including separately identified BMD 200 transport to the import port and BMD 50 insurance, no other required additions/adjustments, established ordinary camera classification and no relief. Part 8(2) leaves **BMD 4,750 customs value**; the selected 10% camera row gives **BMD 475 duty**. Without those assumptions, the invoice total or its reduction is **Needs Input**.
11. **Valuation method missing:** a gift, a non-qualifying related-party sale or unquantified royalty does not justify a guessed transaction value. Apply the statutory alternative sequence and evidence requirements; do not select an arbitrary minimum or higher-of-two base.
12. **Clothing, footwear or food parcel:** the selected 98.03 rows give 6.5%, 6.5% and 15% respectively only for an established applicable postal/courier choice, subject to Collector control. Those figures do not establish the accompanied-personal or ordinary-cargo rates.
13. **Unclassified car or building material:** **Needs Input**, not a standard-category percentage. Selected posted car rows use 75%/150% value bands and electric-car 8703.800 is zero; cement and gypsum entries also differ. Obtain exact classification, value, source period and relief facts. A January 2027 announcement does not establish enacted duty. [First Schedule, pp 88 and 402–405](https://www.gov.bm/files/media-library/20260413/e1311751-2026_customs_tariff_act_1970_first_schedule_final_2026.03.31v1r0.pdf)
14. **Specific CPC conditions:** a hobby musician does not satisfy CPC 4247's consideration test; parade tools/vehicles are outside CPC 4248; another fuel importer does not gain BELCO's exclusive-use treatment. A named approval must match its goods, beneficiary, end use, period and other conditions.
15. **Traveller declaration:** the BR 109 threshold is strictly greater than BMD 10,000 for the stated combined categories; restricted goods and transfer-of-residence goods have separate triggers. Exactly that amount alone does not satisfy the greater-than trigger. The allowance-page 4/16 attachment does not establish a current form after the transition ended.
16. **Other charges:** a supported duty calculation does not resolve wharfage, processing, other taxes or accepted payment. Missing charging authority and actual-route facts prevent a complete import-cost total.
17. **VAT/GST inference:** a zero-duty code or approved customs relief does not prove VAT/GST absence, a zero VAT rate or no registration/return/reverse-charge obligation. Keep the original absence question **Needs Input**.

## Arithmetic self-checks

The following 4 checks recompute only the assumed numbers in cases 2–4 and 10. Classification, source period, route, eligibility and acceptance require their separate evidence. Exact integer results here do not establish a general cent-rounding rule.

```oa-check
name: BM posted 2026 ordinary camera, established value and no relief
inputs:
  customs_value: 5000
  rate: 10
steps:
  duty: pct(customs_value, rate)
expected:
  duty: 500
tolerance: 0
```

```oa-check
name: BM posted 2026 accompanied islander camera, sole available allowance
inputs:
  customs_value: 5000
  available_allowance: 300
  rate: 25
steps:
  dutiable_value: max(0, customs_value - available_allowance)
  duty: pct(dutiable_value, rate)
expected:
  dutiable_value: 4700
  duty: 1175
tolerance: 0
```

```oa-check
name: BM posted 2026 ordinary specified computer or television, no relief
inputs:
  customs_value: 5000
  rate: 25
steps:
  duty: pct(customs_value, rate)
expected:
  duty: 1250
tolerance: 0
```

```oa-check
name: BM conditional transaction value excluding identified freight and insurance
inputs:
  invoice_total: 5000
  transport_to_import_port: 200
  insurance: 50
  rate: 10
steps:
  customs_value: invoice_total - transport_to_import_port - insurance
  duty: pct(customs_value, rate)
expected:
  customs_value: 4750
  duty: 475
tolerance: 0
```

## Required handoff and remaining gaps

Record the goods specification, code and duty unit; ownership/use and islander/visitor status where relevant; import event/date and route; source/version and later-law check; statutory valuation method, adjustments and exchange rate; available allowance; approval/CPC, beneficiary, goods, end use, claim/cutoff and Collector directions; declaration path and unresolved separate charges. Obtain qualified review before relying on an actual liability or filing.

This selected review leaves historical 2025 tariff reconstruction, uninspected temporary beneficiary notices, other-titled amendments and valuation regulations, actual relief/inspection/disposal application, the baggage/CPC 4413 legal route, current form images and authenticated operations, non-duty charging authority, affirmative VAT/GST absence evidence and practitioner execution open. It does not complete the customs/VAT topic or the country.
